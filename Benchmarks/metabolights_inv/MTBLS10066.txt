Study Identifier	MTBLS10066
Study Title	Alterations in gut microbiota composition, plasma lipids, and brain activity, suggest inter-connected pathways influencing malnutrition-associated cognitive and neurodevelopmental changes.
Study Description	<p>Malnutrition affects over 30 million children annually and has profound immediate and enduring repercussions, with nearly half of child deaths under five linked to malnutrition. Survivors face lasting consequences, including impaired neurocognitive development, leading to cognitive and behavioural deficits, impacting academic performance and socioeconomic outcomes. Despite extensive literature on malnutrition’s associations with infections, metabolism, microbiome and genomics, knowledge gaps persist around how these changes are mechanistically connected. The objective of this study was to identify gut microbiome mediated associations between Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and cognitive development. Despite limitations, this study provides evidence supporting the hypothesis that inter-connected pathways between the gut microbiome, microbial metabolism mediate plasma lipids, brain activity and cognitive functions. Causality between gut microbiome, plasma metabolite changes and MAM phenotype remains unclear, necessitating further research. However, these findings offer insights to optimize interventions for MAM-associated neurodevelopmental changes.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Peripheral venous blood samples were collected in EDTA Vacutainers, separated into plasma and RBCs and immediately frozen at -80 °C. Batches of blood samples were air-freighted on dry ice from Bangladesh to the Liggins Institute, New Zealand for processing and analysis (Lipidomics,DDA,20min,F5,ABCv10_FLUSH.meth)</p><p><br></p>	<p>Lipids were extracted according to method of Liu et al. (2016). Plasma samples were thawed and maintained on ice. A 10 µL volume of each sample was placed in an amber glass autosampler vial and 300 µL of a mixture of Type 1 water, butanol, methanol, chloroform and SPLASH Lipidomix in a ratio of 4:15:15:20:1 was added. The sample was immediately vortexed and then sonicated for 15 min at room temperature. The vials were centrifuged at 4500 RCF for 10 min at 4 °C to pellet protein and other precipitate, the supernatant was transferred to a new 1.8 mL amber autosampler vial with a low-volume insert and capped for negative ionisation LC-MS. Samples were diluted 5x with 75% IPA in a new autosampler vial with low-volume insert for positive ionisation LC-MS.</p><p><br></p><p>Liu, Z., S. Rochfort, and B. G. Cocks. 2016. Optimization of a single phase method for lipid extraction from milk. Journal of Chromatography A 1458:145-149.</p>	<p>Extracted lipids were resolved using a liquid chromatography [LC] system comprised of an Accela 1250 pump (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Austin, Texas USA), a TriPlus autosampler (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and a HotDog 5090 column oven (ProLab GmbH, Reinach, Switzerland) set to 45 °C. The mass spectrometer was a Q-Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Germany) equipped with a heated electrospray ionisation [HESI] source. The 3 mobile phases (A/B/C) were water, MeOH and IPA, all containing 0.05% (v/v) GAA and 5 mM NH4.</p><p><br></p><p>Gradients for positive ionisation:&nbsp;</p><p>min A B C</p><p>0 30 70 0</p><p>0.1 30 70 0</p><p>5 5 95 0</p><p>14.65 5 25 70</p><p>14.75 5 5 90</p><p>15.5 5 5 90</p><p>16 5 95 0</p><p>16.5 30 70 0</p><p>20 30 70 0</p><p><br></p><p>Gradients for negative ionisation:&nbsp;</p><p>min A B C</p><p>0 50 50 0</p><p>0.1 50 50 0</p><p>5 10 90 0</p><p>12 5 90 5</p><p>14.75 5 5 90</p><p>15.5 5 5 90</p><p>16 5 95 0</p><p>16.5 50 50 0</p><p>20 50 50 0</p>	<p>Quantitative and qualitative mass spectral data was acquired using Data Dependent Analysis [DDA]. For positive ionisation the full scan range was m/z 200-1000 from 0.0 to 8.0 min, and m/z 500-1250 from 8.0 min until the end of the run. For negative ionisation the m/z range was 135-1000 from 0.0 to 8.0 min. For both methods the HESI source was operated with a spray voltage of 3500 V, capillary temperature of 375 °C, sheath gas flow 40, aux gas 8, probe heater temperature of 200 °C, S-Lens RF level of 55 and a max spray current of 100 µA. AGC target was 1e6, maximum ion trapping time was 100 ms and Orbitrap resolution was 17,500. A total of 4 precursors were selected for DDA MS/MS scans every cycle with AGC 1e5, max IT time of 75 ms, isolation window of 4 m/z and an offset of 0.7 m/z with normalised collision energies of 20 and 30. Minimum AGC target was 2e4.</p>	<p>A Python script was used to correct retention time drift between batches (Pook, 2024). Automated peak picking, annotation, manual integration, and deletion of false positives and noise features was carried out using MS-DIAL v4.92 (Tsugawa et al. 2015, Tsugawa et al. 2020). An in-house Python script (Python v3.9.2) was used to blank subtract peak areas and normalise to the closest internal standard batchwise. Batch effects were removed using Principle Component Analysis to correct for the vectors of the quality control samples.</p><p><br></p><p>Pook, C. (2024). mzML_batch_RT_adjustment. Zenodo.&nbsp;https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11095186</p><p>Tsugawa, H., T. Cajka, T. Kind, Y. Ma, B. Higgins, K. Ikeda, M. Kanazawa, J. VanderGheynst, O. Fiehn, and M. Arita. 2015. MS-DIAL: data-independent MS/MS deconvolution for comprehensive metabolome analysis. Nature methods 12:523-526.</p><p>Tsugawa, H., K. Ikeda, M. Takahashi, A. Satoh, Y. Mori, H. Uchino, N. Okahashi, Y. Yamada, I. Tada, P. Bonini, Y. Higashi, Y. Okazaki, Z. Zhou, Z.-J. Zhu, J. Koelmel, T. Cajka, O. Fiehn, K. Saito, M. Arita, and M. Arita. 2020. A lipidome atlas in MS-DIAL 4. Nature Biotechnology 38:1159-1163.</p>	<p>LC-MS features were annotated using the spectral library internal to MS-DIAL v4.92&nbsp;(Tsugawa et al. 2015, Tsugawa et al. 2020).</p>

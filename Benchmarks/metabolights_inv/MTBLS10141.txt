Study Title	Akkermansia muciniphila attenuates obsessive-compulsive disorder-like behavior by producing L-tyrosine
Study Description	Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) ranks among the most prevalent psychiatric disorders globally. The impact of the gut microbiota and associated metabolites on OCD remains unclear. Here, we report a remarkable reduction of Akkermansia muciniphila (A. muciniphila) abundance in both patients and mice, and supplementation with this strain profoundly attenuated OCD phenotypes in mice. Our results reveal that A. muciniphila improves OCD symptoms primarily through its production of the metabolite L-tyrosine. L-tyrosine could alleviate OCD phenotypes by increasing angiotensinogen (AGT) expression, leading to elevated tyrosine hydroxylase and dopamine content in the cortex. Mechanistic studies demonstrate that this increase of AGT is attributable to enhanced N6-methyladenosine modification on its mRNA with reader protein YTHDC2 by downregulation of FTO. Overall, L-tyrosine generated by A. muciniphila improves the OCD phenotype in mice mainly by regulating the FTO-AGT-YTHDC2 axis and increasing dopamine content.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Collect the plasma from the QNP-treated mice and the control mice, and store it at -80 °C.</p>	<p>LC-MS detection was performed on the Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with Fourier-transform mass spectrometry system. An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 micro m; Waters, Milford, USA) was adopted. Mobile phase A was 95% water-5% acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was 47.5% acetonitrile-47.5% isopropanol-5% water containing 0.1% formic acid. The injection volume was 2 micro L and the column temperature was set at 40 degrees C. Gradient elution: The flow rate remained 0.4 mL/min from 0 to 5.5 min, with mobile phase A decreasing from 100% to 0% and B increasing from 0% to 100%. From 5.5 to 7.4 min, the flow rate was adjusted to 0.6 mL/min while B was kept at 100%. At 7.4-7.6 min, the flow rate was 0.6 mL/min, A rose to 48.5% and B dropped to 51.5%. During 7.6-7.8 min, the flow rate was changed to 0.5 mL/min, A returned to 100% and B fell to 0. From 7.8 to 10 min, the flow rate was set back to 0.4 mL/min and 100% phase A was maintained to finish the analysis.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into metabolomics software ProgenesisQI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention-time correction and peak alignment, generating a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity. Feature peaks were then identified by library searching within this software.</p>	<p>For sample pretreatment, accurately transfer 50 micro L sample into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, add 200 micro L extraction solution (methanol-acetonitrile = 1:1, v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine). After vortex-mixing for 30 s, perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min at 5 degrees C and 40 KHz, then keep the sample static at 20 degrees C for 30 min. Centrifuge for 15 min at 13000 g and 4 degrees C, collect the supernatant and dry it under nitrogen gas. Reconstitute the residue with 80 micro L reconstitution solution (acetonitrile-water = 1:1), vortex-mix for 30 s, and conduct low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 5 min at 5 degrees C and 40 KHz. Centrifuge for 10 min at 13000 g and 4 degrees C, transfer the supernatant into a sample vial with an insert for instrumental analysis. In addition, take 20 micro L supernatant from each sample and mix them to prepare quality-control samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed with electrospray ionization, and mass-spectrometric signals were acquired in both positive- and negative-ion scan modes. The scan range was 70-1050 m/z; the sheath gas flow rate was 50 arb, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 13 arb, the heater temperature was 425 degrees C, and the capillary temperature was 325 degrees C. The spray voltage was 3500 V for positive mode and 3500 V for negative mode. The S-Lens RF level was set at 50, and the normalized collision energies were 20, 40 and 60 eV. The resolution for Full MS was 60000, while the resolution for MS^2 was 7500.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into metabolomics software ProgenesisQI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention-time correction and peak alignment, generating a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity. Feature peaks were then identified by library searching within this software. MS and MS/MS spectral information were matched against metabolite databases with the MS mass error set to less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were annotated according to MS/MS matching scores. Main databases included public databases such as HMDB and METLIN as well as an in-house built database.</p>

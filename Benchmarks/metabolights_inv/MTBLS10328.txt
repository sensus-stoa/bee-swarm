Study Identifier	MTBLS10328
Study Title	Analysis of 3274 bacterial isolates by rapid evaporative ionisation mass spectrometry (REIMS)
Study Description	<p>Analysis of bacterial composition using rapid evaporative ionisation mass spectrometry (REIMS) allows for the detection of metabolites that may be considered specific to particular taxons. This database of 3274 isolates covering gram positive and negative bacteria across 5 phyla, 12 classes, 24 orders, 49 families, 85 genera and 239 different species was used to determine m/z features with a high degree of specificity and sensitivity to particular taxonomic levels. To do so, a subset of 597 isolates was identified, from which ~5000 m/z variables were derived, of which 359 were identified as taxon specific markers (TSMs) using this code [1]. Validation was performed using the remaining observations from this dataset. Human colorectal cancer samples analysed by mass spectrometry imaging were also analysed as part of the study to determine if the TSMs could be detected from clinical samples. These files can be found in MTBLS289 [2].</p><p><br></p><p>[1] https://github.com/jsmckenzie/bacterialTSM</p><p>[2] https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS289</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All bacteria were isolated during routine practice at the NWLP clinical microbiology laboratory at Charing Cross hospital, UK. Bacterial IDs were obtained using the Bruker Biotyper platform by Biomedical Scientist microbiology staff following manufacturers' protocols.</p>	<p>No extraction was performed on these samples. MS analysis was performed directly on the cultured bacteria using in-house developed rapid evaporative ionisation mass spectrometry (REIMS) instrumentation.</p>	<p>The Thermo Scientific Exactive MS system was operated in negative ion mode covering a mass range between 150-2000 m/z at a mass resolution of 50,000 (at 200 m/z). The injection time was 1000 ms and microscans was 1. The tube lens, capillary and skimmer voltages were -160, -50 and -24 V, respectively. The capillary temperature was 250 °C and automatic gain control was on.</p>	<p>Thermo RAW files were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard's msConvert [1], using version 3.0.22310.fb594fb. The command line for conversion is shown below, and the following options were selected in the GUI: Write index, TPP compatibility. The binary encoding precision was set to 64-bit.</p><p><br></p><p>''~\ProteoWizard 3.0.22310.fb594fb 64-bit\msconvert.exe' --mzXML --filter 'titleMaker &lt;RunId&gt;.&lt;ScanNumber&gt;.&lt;ScanNumber&gt;.&lt;ChargeState&gt; File:'''^&lt;SourcePath^&gt;''', NativeID:'''^&lt;Id^&gt;''''&nbsp;'~\PathToFile\Filename.RAW''</p><p><br></p><p>[1] Chambers, M., Maclean, B., Burke, R.&nbsp;<em>et al.</em>&nbsp;A cross-platform toolkit for mass spectrometry and proteomics.&nbsp;<em>Nat Biotechnol</em>&nbsp;30, 918–920 (2012). https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.2377</p>	<p>No metabolite identification has been performed on the raw spectra.</p>	<p>For rapid evaporative ionisation mass spectrometry (REIMS) analysis, 0.5-1.0 mg of freshly cultured bacterial biomass was analysed using a bipolar sampling probe (bayonet-shaped irrigated bipolar forceps by Erbe Elektromedizin, Tübingen, Germany) directly connected to a Thermo Scientific Exactive mass spectrometer via a polymer tubing. A power-controlled Valleylab Force EZc was used as a&nbsp;radiofrequency&nbsp;alternating power supply at a setting of 60 W.</p>

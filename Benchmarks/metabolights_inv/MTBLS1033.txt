Study Identifier	MTBLS1033
Study Title	Metabolomics Study of Serum and Urine Samples Reveals Metabolic Pathways and Biomarkers Associated with Pelvic Organ Prolapse
Study Description	Pelvic organ prolapse (POP) is a common medical condition among women and involves complicated diagnostics and controversial surgical management. The exact molecular mechanism underlying POP is poorly understood, especially at the metabolism level. To explore the metabolic mechanism underlying POP and discover potential biomarkers for POP diagnosis, we applied a non-targeted metabolomics approach using ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole-time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS). Metabolomics study of serum samples from patients with POP (n = 24) and controls (n = 22) revealed a total of 59 metabolites that are significantly different (VIP ≥ 1 and p ≤ 0.05) between the two groups. Between urine samples from POP patients (n = 45) and controls (n = 59), 33 metabolites differed significantly (VIP ≥ 1 and p ≤ 0.05). Metabolic pathways affected by these differentially expressed metabolites were analyzed. In both serum and urine samples, three pathways including arginine biosynthesis and purine metabolism were found to be significantly related to POP. Six metabolites including GPC, 1-methyladenosine, maleic acid, L-pyroglutamic acid, inosine, and citrate are significantly changed (VIP ≥ 1 and p ≤ 0.05) in both serum and urine samples from patients with POP. Receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve analysis showed that using these six metabolites as a biomarker could distinguish patients with POP from controls with good accuracy in both serum (AUC = 1) and urine samples (AUC = 0.854). Collectively, these results further extended our understanding of key regulatory metabolic pathways involved in the pathophysiology of POP, as well as provided some promising biomarkers for effective POP diagnosis.

Study Protocol Description	The study was endorsed by the local ethics committee of the Jiangxi Provincial Maternal and Child Health Hospital, and written consent document for each participating subject was obtained. A total of 150 women were recruited, including 69 patients with POP and 81 healthy individuals as control. </br></br> Serum samples were collected from 46 subjects, and urine samples were collected from the rest 104 participants (see Table S1 in the paper associated with this study). All collected samples were immediately stored at -80 °C prior to metabolomics analysis.	Metabolites in the serum and urine samples were extracted with 400 μl methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). The extraction mixtures were vortexed for 30 s, incubated at 20 °C for 10 min, and then centrifuged for 15 min (14,000 x g, 4 °C). Afterwards, the supernatants were transferred into a new Eppendorf tube for lyophilization. Lyophilized samples were re-dissolved in 100 μl acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). After another centrifugation for 15 min (14,000 x g, 4 °C), the supernatants were transferred into a sample vial for UHPLC-Q-TOF MS analysis.	For untargeted metabolomics analysis, an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC system (Agilent Technologies, Santa-Clara, CA, USA) coupled to an AB SCIEX Triple TOF 6600 System (AB SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA) was used for analyzing serum samples, and an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC system coupled to an AB SCIEX Triple TOF 5600+ System were used for analyzing urine samples. A volume of 2 μl for each sample was injected imto an ACQUITY BEH Amide 1.7 μm (2.1 mm x 100 mm) column for chromatographic separation. The delivery flow rate was 0.30 ml/min.	The TOF/MS was also operated on both positive and negative ion modes. The ion source gas1 was set 40 PSI, and the ion source gas was set at 80 PSI. The curtain gas was set 30 PSI, while the source temperature was set at 650 °C.	The raw UPLC-Q-TOF/MS files was converted to mzXML format via Proteo Wizard MS converter and then processed for peak alignment, feature detection and retention time correction using XCMS online software.	The metabolites were identified by accurate mass (< 25 ppm) and MS/MS data according to the established standards database.

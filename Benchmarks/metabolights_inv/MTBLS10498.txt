Study Identifier	MTBLS10498
Study Title	Metabolomics and Proteomics Reveal the Inhibitory Effect of Lactobacillus crispatus on Cervical Cancer Cells
Study Description	Cervical cancer remains a significant global health issue due to its high morbidity and mortality rates. Recently, Lactobacillus crispatus has been recognized for its crucial role in maintaining cervical health. While some studies have explored the use of L. crispatus to mitigate cervical cancer, the underlying mechanisms remain largely unknown. In this study, we employed non-targeted proteomics and metabolomics techniques to investigate how L. crispatus affects the growth of cervical cancer cells (SiHa) and normal cervical cells (Ect1/E6E7). Our findings indicated that the inhibitory effect of L. crispatus on SiHa cells was associated with various biological processes, notably the ferroptosis pathway. Specifically, L. crispatus was found to regulate the expression of proteins such as HMOX1, SLC39A14, VDAC2, ACSL4, and LPCAT3 by SiHa cells, which are closely related to ferroptosis. Additionally, it activated the TCA cycle in SiHa cells, leading to increased levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lipid peroxides (LPO). These results reveal the therapeutic potential of L. crispatus in targeting the ferroptosis pathway for cervical cancer treatment, opening new avenues for research and therapy in cervical cancer.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples were SiHa and Ect1/E6E7 cells. These cells were collected after 48 h of co-incubation in transwells with medium containing or not containing 6 x 10^6 CFU/mL of <em>Lactobacillus crispatus</em>. Then, cells underwent dissociation, centrifugation and washing. Each group contained 8 biological replicates, and the total number of samples was 32.</p>	<p>An 80% (v/v) methanol-water solution was added as the extractant to extract the metabolites. Subsequently, the samples were ultrasonically broken up for 5 min and then kept at -80 °C overnight, allowing for complete extraction of the metabolites. Then, the supernatants were separated by centrifugation again at 12,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C. The supernatants was then dried and stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>The metabolite samples were dissolved in a methanol:acetonitrile:water (1:1:2) solution, followed by centrifugation (15,000 rpm, 30 min, 4 °C) to remove insoluble compounds. Each sample was dissolved in 20 μL of solvent and a 4 μL aliquot was subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis. Additionally, 2 μL of each sample was combined to create a mixed sample serving as a quality control (QC) sample.</p>	<p>For non-targeted metabolomic analysis, metabolites were analyzed by using a LC-20A HPLC system (Shimadzu, Tokyo, Japan), equipped with a Waters XBridge BEH Amide column (150 x 2.1 mm, 3.5 µm, Waters, MA, USA). All samples were injected in a randomized order, with QC samples injected every 8 samples. The mobile phases A and B were water (containing 0.1% formic acid) and acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), respectively. The flow rate was set at 0.2 mL/min, and the oven temperature maintained at 25 °C. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0.0 min, 95% B; 0.0-3.5 min, 95-75% B gradually; 3.5-13.0 min, 75-45% B gradually; 13-18 min, 45-30% B gradually; 18-23 min, holding at 30% B; 23.01 min, transitioning to 95% B; 23.01-28.0 min, maintaining 95% B.</p>	<p>Analysis was performed using a TripleTOF 4600 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, CA, USA).&nbsp;The ESI-Q-TOF was set in information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode with the following parameter settings: ion spray voltage: +5500 V (positive-ion mode), -4500 V (negative-ion mode); m/z scan range: 50-1000 Da; precursor ions selected at a time: 10; declustering potential (DP): ±80 V; collision energy (CE): ±50 eV.</p>	<p>The metabolomics data were converted to to&nbsp;mzXML and mgf by MSConvert and subsequently processed with the R package XCMS to extract the corresponding m/z values, retention times and normalized peak intensities.</p>	<p>Identification of differential metabolites was performed using MetDNA2 (http://metdna.zhulab.cn/).</p>

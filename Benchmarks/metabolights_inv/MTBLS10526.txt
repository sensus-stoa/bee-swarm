Study Identifier	MTBLS10526
Study Title	Multi-Omics Unveils Strain-Specific Neuroactive Metabolite Production Linked to Inflammation Modulation by Bacteroides and Their Vesicles
Study Description	<p>18 Bacteroidetes isoaltes were analyzed for their neuroactive potentail using un-targeted and targeted metabolomics. The bacteria were grown to stationary phase, the supernatant was separated and purified using 0.22 um filter. The metabolome from the supernatant was extracted using standard Methanol extraction. The reconstituted supernatant samples were transferred into LC vials and loaded onto a sample tray in the nanoLC coupled to the Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Chromatographic separation of metabolites was performed on a Proxeon EASY nLC II System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Thermo Scientific Acclaim PepMap RSLC C18 column (P/N ES800A). The Xbridge BEH Amide (2.5 μm, 2.1 x 150 mm, Waters, Milford, MA, USA) column was used for metabolite separation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Single colonies of Bacteroidetes isolates were inoculated into 10 mL of sterilized Fastidious Anaerobic Broth (FAB) media and incubated at 37 °C in an anaerobic chamber for 48 h. Subsequently, 100 µL of this culture was transferred to fresh, sterilized McFarlane culture medium incubated at 37 °C in an anaerobic chamber until reaching the stationary phase over 72 h. The cell-free supernatant (CFS) was obtained from bacterial cultures by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 10-30 min, followed by filtration through a 0.22 µm filter.</p>	<p>To 100 μL of the Bacteroidetes supernatant, 900 μL of methanol (-20 °C) was added along with 1 mg/mL reserpine as an internal standard (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint-Louis, USA), and the mixture was vigorously vortexed for 1 min. The mixture was then kept at -77 °C for 15 min. Subsequently, the samples were thawed for 3 min at room temperature, thoroughly vortexed again, and centrifuged for 30 min at 12,000 x g at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and dried using a SpeedVac system. The dry extract was dissolved in 100 μL of a solution consisting of 50% (v/v) acetonitrile and 50% water, with 0.1% formic acid (FA) added to dissolve the extracted metabolites. The samples were then passed through a 0.2 μm PTFE or PVDF filter.</p>	<p>The reconstituted supernatant samples were transferred into LC vials and loaded onto a sample tray in the nanoLC coupled to the nanoLC coupled to the Q- Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Chromatographic separation of metabolites was performed on a Proxeon EASY nLC II System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Thermo Scientific Acclaim PepMap RSLC C18 column (P/N ES800A), 15 cm x 75 μm ID, 3 μm, 100 Å employing a water/acetonitrile/0.1% formic acid gradient. In total 1 mL of samples were loaded onto the column for 60 min at a flow rate of 0.25 μL/min. Compounds were separated using a linear gradient from 0 to 100% of acetonitrile for 35 min, followed by washing 10 min at 100% of acetonitrile, then using a gradient from 100 to 0% of acetonitrile for 5 min and washing for 10 min at 100% of water.</p>	<p>The extracted metabolites from the EVs were analyzed by nanoLC coupled to the Q- Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Chromatographic separation of metabolites was performed on a Proxeon EASY nLC II System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Thermo Scientific Acclaim PepMap RSLC C18 column (P/N ES800A), 15 cm x 75 μm ID, 3 μm, 100 Å employing a water/acetonitrile/0.1% formic acid gradient.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data were processed and analyzed by MS dial version 4.60 along with Massbank and Human metabolome database (HMDB) to facilitate the identification of target molecules. Default parameters were used for the identification of individual compounds in samples, and contaminations detected in the blank were excluded from the list of identified compounds.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data were processed and analyzed by MS dial version 4.60 along with Massbank and Human metabolome database (HMDB) to facilitate the identification of target molecules.

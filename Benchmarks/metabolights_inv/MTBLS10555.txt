Study Identifier	MTBLS10555
Study Title	Characteristics of myocardial structure and central carbon metabolism during the early and compensatory stages of cardiac hypertrophy
Study Description	Cardiac hypertrophy is a classical forerunner of heart failure and myocardial structural and metabolic remodeling are closely associated with cardiac hypertrophy. We aim to investigate the characteristics of myocardial structure and central carbon metabolism of cardiac hypertrophy at different stages. Using echocardiography and pathological staining, early and compensatory cardiac hypertrophy were respectively defined as within 7 days and from 7 to 14 days after transverse aortic constriction (TAC) in mice. Among mass-spectrometry-based metabolomics, we identified 45 central carbon metabolites. Differential metabolite analysis showed that six metabolites, including citrate, cis-aconitate and so on, decreased significantly on day 1 after TAC. Ten metabolites, including L-lactate, (S)-2-hydroxyglutarate and so on, were obviously changed on days 10 and 14. Pathway analysis showed that these metabolites were involved in seven metabolic pathways, including carbohydrates, amino acids and so on. Western blot showed the expression of ATP-citrate lyase, malate dehydrogenase 1 and lactate dehydrogenase A in myocardium changed markedly on day 3, while the phosphorylation level of AMP-activated protein kinase did not show significantly difference. Our study reveals the characteristics of myocardial structure and central carbon metabolism of cardiac hypertrophy at different stages, which may have a suggestive role in the early diagnosis of cardiac hypertrophy.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Wide-type C57BL/6J mice aged 8-10 weeks were subjected to transverse aortic constriction (TAC) to induce cardiac hypertrophy caused by pressure overload. The mice were divided into a sham group and five TAC subgroups on postoperative days 1, 3, 7, 10 and 14. After TAC or sham, mice were given an intraperitoneal injection of tribromoethanol to euthanized mice. Samples were obtained from myocardial tissue of TAC or sham mice. After the mouse heart was removed, it was immediately put into a cryopreservation tube and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for 30 seconds. The samples were subsequently stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>The tissue samples were precisely weighed to Eppendorf tubes. After adding 2 small steel balls and 500 μL&nbsp;of MeOH/H2O (3/1, v/v, precooled at -40 °C), the samples were vortexed for 30 s, and then were homogenized for 4 min at 40 Hz and sonicated for 5 min in ice-water bath. The homogenate and sonicate circle were repeated 3x times, followed by incubation at -40 °C&nbsp;for 1 h and centrifugation at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C&nbsp;for 15 min. The supernatants (400 μL) were collected and dried by spin. Then 200 μL&nbsp;water was added to the dried residue as a reconstitution solution. Reconstituted samples were vortexed before filtration through the filter membrane, and were subsequently transferred to inserts in injection vials for high-performance ion chromatography tandem mass spectrometry&nbsp;(HPIC-MS/MS) analysis.</p>	<p>The HPIC separation was carried out using an Thermo&nbsp;Scientific&nbsp;Dionex&nbsp;ICS-6000 HPIC System (Thermo&nbsp;Scientific), equipped with Dionex IonPac AS11-HC (4 μm, 2 mm x 250 mm; Thermo Scientific) column and a AG11-HC (2 mm x 50 mm) guard column. The mobile phase A was 100 mM NaOH in water, the mobile&nbsp;D was ultrapure water. Another pumping system was used to supply the solvent (2 mM acetic acid in methanol) and solvent&nbsp;mixed with effluent before entering ESI (flow rate of 0.15&nbsp;mL/min).&nbsp;The column temperature was set at 30 °C. The auto-sampler temperature was set at 4 °C&nbsp;and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p><p><br></p>	<p>An AB SCIEX 6500 QTRAP+ triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (AB Sciex), equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) interface, was applied for assay development. Typical ion source parameters were: IonSpray Voltage = -4500 V, temperature = 450&nbsp;°C, Ion Source Gas 1 = 45&nbsp;psi, Ion Source Gas 2 = 45&nbsp;psi, Curtain Gas = 30 psi. The MRM parameters&nbsp;for each of the targeted analytes were optimized using flow injection analysis, by injecting the standard solutions of the individual analytes, into the API source of the mass spectrometer. Several most sensitive transitions were used in the MRM scan mode to optimize the collision energy for each Q1/Q3 pair. Among the optimized MRM transitions per analyte, the Q1/Q3 pairs that showed the highest sensitivity and selectivity were selected as ‘quantifier’ for quantitative monitoring. The additional transitions acted as ‘qualifier’ for the purpose of verifying the identity of the target analytes.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to mzXML files.</p>	<p>The extracted ion chromatographs from a standard solution&nbsp;and a sample of the targeted analytes under the optimal conditions. All of the analytes showed symmetrical peak shapes, baseline separations were obtained, and the retention time and peak shapes for all of the analytes showed good correlation between the standard solution and the real sample.</p><p><br></p><p>The final concentration (CF,&nbsp;nmol/L) equals the calculated concentration (CC,&nbsp;nmol/L) multiplied by the dilution factor (Dil). The metabolite concentration (CM, nmol/g) equals the final concentration (CF,&nbsp;nmol/L) multiplied by the final volume (V, mL) the sample experiment concentration factor (CF), divided by the mass (m, mg) of the sample. N/A&nbsp;means that the targeted metabolites were not detectable in the corresponding samples.&nbsp;The results highlighted in blue represent that the concentrations&nbsp;were below the LLOQs, and those highlighted in red represent that the concentrations&nbsp;were above the ULOQs, both of which mean that the quantitative accuracy were relatively poorer.</p>

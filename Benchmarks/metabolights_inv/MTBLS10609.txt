Study Title	Metabolomics study for investigating the specific role of INOSITOL-REQUIRING ENZYME 1A and INOSITOL-REQUIRING ENZYME 1B under chronic endoplasmic reticulum stress in Arabidopsis thaliana
Study Description	<p>The Unfolded Protein Response (UPR) is a retrograde, ER-to-nucleus, signalling pathway which is conserved across kingdoms. In plants, it contributes to development, reproduction, immunity and tolerance to abiotic stress. This metabolomic dataset was produced from 14-day-old Arabidopsis thaliana seedlings challenged by tunicamycin (Tm), an antibiotic inhibiting Asn-linked glycosylation in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), causing an ER stress and eventually activating the UPR. Wild-type (WT), double mutant deficient for two main actors of the UPR (INOSITOL-REQUIRING ENZYME 1A [IRE1A] and INOSITOL-REQUIRING ENZYME 1B [IRE1B]), as well as doubles mutants complemented with either of the WT IRE1 alleles were used as genetic backgrounds in our experimental setup. This allows to distinguish among differentially-overaccumulated metabolites which ones are dependent on IRE1A and/or IRE1B. Also, shoots and roots were harvested separately to determine organ-specific metabolic responses to Tm.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Seedlings were grown in vitro under the same conditions as those described in Ducloy et al. (2024, doi: 10.1016/j.dib.2024.110243). Growth medium was supplemented with either DMSO (0.01 or 0.003% final concentration, control condition) or tunicamycin (final concentration: 30 or 100ng/mL, treatment conditions). Shoot and root samples of 14 day-old seedlings (starting couting from the end of the stratification period) were harvested separatly using a scalpel. Six independent biological replicates per modality were produced. One biological replicates correspond to root or shoot material harvested from approximately 30 seedlings. Each of the independent replicates were grown in different plates. Wild-type genetic background was Columbia-0 (Col-0). Mutant alleles were SALK_002316 and GABI_638B07 for the double ire1a ire1b mutant (dubbed ire1dm2). Mutants complemented with either IRE1A or IRE1B WT allele (Mishiba et al. 2019, doi: 10.26508/lsa.201900459) were also used in this study, and named ire1dm_comp_IRE1A and ire1dm_comp_IRE1B, respectively.</p>	<p>Apolar metabolite extraction from shoot and root materials was performed according to the single-extraction method described by Shiva et al. (2018, BMC Plant Biology 14, 14; https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-018-0282-y) with slight modifications. Briefly, shoot or root samples were directly warmed up for 15 min at 75°C in 1 mL of preheated isopropanol containing 0.01% BHT (w/v). Upon cooling, 3mL of a chloroform/methanol/water (30/41.5/3.5, v/v/v) mixture were added to each samples. Extraction was carried out overnight at room temperature. Extracts were then centrifuged at 700 x g for 5 min. Extracted materials (pellets) were dried at 50°C for 3 days and weighted to allow feature normalization. Apolar extracts (supernatants) were dried under gazeous nitrogen flux and dry residues were resuspended in 300µL of mix of acetonitrile/isopropanol(7:3) before injection. QC sample corresponding to shoot and root were pooled of each sample by organism part and the blank sample was mix of acetonitrile/isopropanol(7:3).</p>	<p>Untargeted lipidomic data were acquired using a UHPLC system (Ultimate 3000 Thermo) and an EC 100/2 NUCLEOSHELL Phenyl-Hexyl, (2,7 µm, 100x2 mm; Macherey-Nagel) was used for chromatographic separation. The mobile phases used for the chromatographic separation were: (A) H2O + 1% of 10mM ammonium formate in H2O + 0.1% formic acid; and (B) acetonitrile:isopropanol (7:3) + 1% of 10 mM ammonium formate in H2O + 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 400 µL/min, and the following gradient was used: 45% A for 1 min, followed by a linear gradient from 45% A to 30% A from 1 to 2 min, then a linear gradient from 30% A to 15% A from 2 to 7 min, a linear gradient from 15% A to 10% A from 7 to 15 min, a linear gradient from 10% A to 6% A from 15 to 19 min, a linear gradient from 6% A to 2% A from 19 to 26 min; then, 0% of A was held until 27 min, followed by a linear gradient from 0% A to 45% A from 27 to 35 min (35 min total run time).Injection volume was 5µL.</p>	<p>Data-dependent acquisition methods were used for mass spectrometer data in positive and negative ESI modes using a quadrupole time of flight mass spectrometer (Q-Tof Impact II Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany) and the following parameters: capillary voltage, 4.5 kV; nebulizer gas flow, 2.1 bar; dry gas flow, 6 L min−1; drying gas in the heated electrospray source temperature, 200°C. Samples were analysed at 8 Hz with a mass range of 100–1500 m/z. Stepping acquisition parameters were created to improve the fragmentation profile with a collision RF from 200 to 700 Vpp, a transfer time from 20 to 70 μsec, and collision energy from 20 to 40 eV. Each cycle included a MS fullscan and 5 MS/MS CID on the 5 main ions of the previous MS spectrum.</p>	<p>The .d data files (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany) were converted to .mzXML format using the MSConvert software (ProteoWizard package 3.0; Chambers et al., 2012). mzXML data processing, mass detection, chromatogram building, deconvolution, samples alignment and data export were performed using MZmine 2.53 software (http://mzmine.github.io/) for both positive and negative data files. The ADAP chromatogram builder (Myers et al., 2017) method was used with a minimum group size of scan 5, a group intensity threshold of 1000, a minimum highest intensity of 1000 and&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance of 10 ppm. Deconvolution was performed with the ADAP wavelets algorithm using the following setting: S/N threshold 10, peak duration range = 0.01–2 min RT wavelet range 0.01–0.2 min, MS2 scan were paired using a&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance range of 0.05 Da and RT tolerance of 0.5 min. Then, isotopic peak grouper algorithm was used with a&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance of 10 ppm and RT tolerance of 0.2 min. All the peaks were filtered using feature list row filter keeping only peaks with MS2 scan. The alignment of samples was performed using the join aligner with an&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance of 10 ppm, a weight for&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;and RT at 1, a retention time tolerance of 0.2 min. Metabolites accumulation was normalized according to the weight of biological material used for the extraction.</p>	<p>A first research in the library with Mzmine was done, with identification module and ‘custom database search’ to begin the annotation with our library, currently containing 1029 annotated features (RT and&nbsp;m/z) in positive mode and 450 in negative mode, with RT tolerance of 0.2 min and&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance of 0.005 Da.</p><p><br></p><p>Molecular networks were generated with MetGem software (Olivon et al., 2018; https://metgem.github.io) using the .mgf and .csv files obtained with MZmine2 analysis. The molecular network was optimized for the ESI+ and ESI− datasets, and different cosine similarity score thresholds were tested. ESI− and ESI+ molecular networks were generated using cosine score thresholds of 0.70 and 0.75, respectively.</p><p><br></p><p>Lipid metabolites annotation was performed in different consecutive steps. First, the ESI− and ESI+ lipidomic data used for molecular network analyses were searched against the available MS2 spectral libraries (Massbank NA, GNPS Public Spectral Library, NIST14 Tandem and NIH Natural Product), with absolute&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;tolerance of 0.02; 4 minimum matched peaks and minimal cosine score of 0.65. Second, in the different clusters of the molecular network, the result of the database search was validated using the different specific fragments and neutral loss for the different lipid class with their MS2 spectrum [Lipid Class Specific Fragments - Lipidomics-Standards-Initiative (LSI)]. If the database search result was valid, annotation of other features was performed by stepwise comparison from the valid lipid metabolite. Finally, for the cluster of molecular network that had no database search result, Sirius software (https://bio.informatik.uni-jena.de/software/sirius/) was used.</p>

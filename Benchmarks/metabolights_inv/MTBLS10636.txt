Study Title	Human gut bacteria increase PFNA excretion in vivo
Study Description	<p>Per- and polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) – the so-called ‘forever chemicals’ – are a major cause of environmental and health concern due to their toxicity and long-term persistence. Yet, no efficient mechanisms for their removal have been identified. Here we report the potential of human gut bacteria to increase fecal PFNA excretion.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>10 days after the second inoculation with Com20, high- or low-accumulating communities, mice were orally gavaged with PFNA (10 mg/kg or 0.1 mg/kg in 25 % DMSO) in a volume of 50 µl. Fresh fecal samples were collected before treatment, 3 h, 1 day and 2 days after treatment in sterile 1.5 ml Eppendorf-tubes and immediately frozen at -80 °C. On day 3 after treatment, mice were euthanized by CO2 and cervical dislocation, dissected and intestinal contents were taken from colon and the small intestine and collected in the same way.</p>	<p>Frozen fecal samples were weighed out into tubes with beads and 250 μl extraction buffer (methanol + 0.05 KOH + 15 μM caffeine (Assay1) or 0.01 mg/l M9PFNA (Assay 2)) was added. Tubes were then homogenised at 1500 rpm for 10 min followed by centrifugation at 14,000 rpm and 4 ºC for 5 min. 20 μl supernatant was added to 80 μl water + 0.1 % formic acid, vortexed, incubated at 4 ºC for 15 min and centrifuged at 14,000 rpm and 4 ºC for 5 min. The supernatant was transferred to LCMS vials with inserts. Samples for concentration calibration were processed in the same way.</p>	<p>Agilent 1290 Infinity II LC system</p><p><br></p><p>Column: ZORBAX RRHD Eclipse Plus column (C18, 2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Agilent 858700-902)</p><p>Guard column: ZOBRAX Eclipse Plus (C18, 2.1 × 5 mm, 1.8 μm; Agilent 821725-901)</p><p>Column compartment: 40 ºC.</p><p><br></p><p>Multisampler: 4 ºC</p><p>Injection volume: 2-10 μl (Assay 1: 2 µl; Assay 2: 10 µl; Assay 3: 5 µl)</p><p><br></p><p>Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min</p><p>Mobile phase A: water + 0.1 % formic acid</p><p>Mobile phase B: acetonitrile + 0.1 % formic acid</p><p><br></p><p>The 10 min gradient started with 5 % solvent B, which was increased to 90 % by 5 min and further increased to 100 % solvent B by 7 min, before returning to 5 % solvent B at 7.1 min and held until 10 min.</p>	<p>Agilent 6470 triple quadrupole (Agilent)</p><p><br></p><p>Dynamic MRM mode</p><p><br></p><p>Source parameters: </p><p>Gas temperature: 300 ºC</p><p>Gas flow: 10 L/min</p><p>Nebulizer: 50 psi</p><p>Sheath gas temperature: 300 ºC</p><p>Sheath gas flow: 11 L/min</p><p>VCap: 3500 V (positive) or 3000 V (negative)</p><p>Nozzle voltage: 2000 V (positive) or 500 V (negative)</p>	<p>The Agilent MassHunter Qualitative Analysis 10.0 software was used to qualify the selected xenobiotic standards. TIC, EIC and EIC-fragment graphs were extracted for each compound. The Agilent MassHunter QQQ Quantitative Analysis software (Version 10.1) was used to quantify the xenobiotic compounds in each sample. Calibration curves based on pure compound standards were used to estimate the concentrations of target compounds. </p><p>The ratio of compound response to internal standard response was used for concentration calculation. Data analysis was performed in RStudio Version 1.3.1093. Fold changes between medians were used to compare groups.</p><p>In addition, statistical comparison was performed using t-test (two-sided), and p-values were FDR corrected. An adjusted p-value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.</p>	<p>Pure standards were used for method development, compound identification and calibration.</p><p><br></p>

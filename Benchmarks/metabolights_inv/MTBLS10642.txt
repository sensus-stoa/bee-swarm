Study Identifier	MTBLS10642
Study Title	Ten Mouse Organs Proteome and Metabolome Atlas from Adult to Aging
Study Description	<p>The complexity of the aging process, encompassing numerous organs and systems, poses a significant challenge in identifying and targeting key factors that drive age-related decline. The multi-organ synchronous molecular intricacies underlying natural aging remain elusive. Here we presented a data resource for proteomics and metabolomics analyses on ten organs (brain, heart, intestine, kidney, liver, lung, muscle, skin, spleen and stomach) of mice spanning from youth to aging (4, 8, 12 and 20 months, n = 10). We detected and quantified 14,763 protein groups (PGs) and 4,779 metabolites across the ten organs. We found immunoregulation was deeply involved in multi-organ aging. Specifically, 18 immune-related proteins including Ighm showed significant expression changes in all ten organs. Besides, aging organs also expressed age-attributed proteins such as App, Plg and Saa1. Our study revealed that age-related metabolite changes are primarily concentrated in purine, pyrimidine and nicotinate-nicotinamide metabolism, and further constructed a differential proteins-metabolites network. These data highlight the dynamic molecular changes during organ aging, offering insights into geriatric health decline.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mice were deeply anesthetized using sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection, Sigma-Aldrich) then perfused with 20 mL of pre-cooled 1 x PBS via the myocardium and dissected. The brain, heart, lung, liver, kidney, spleen, stomach, intestine, muscle and skin were collected. The calf gastrocnemius muscle was extracted as a muscle sample and 1 cm^2 of skin tissue was removed from the neck as skin sample. The remaining organs were cut off intact and preserved. All samples were placed in liquid nitrogen immediately and stored at -80 °C until protein extraction.</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg of tissue was homogenized in water, followed by extraction with a methanol-acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) containing L-chlorophenylalanine (Sigma-Aldrich). The mixture was vortexed and incubated at -20°C for 2 hours. After centrifugation, the supernatant was concentrated to dryness and stored at -80°C.</p>	<p>Before the mass spectrometry assay, methanol-water buffer (3:7, v/v) was used for re-dissolution. All samples were loaded into the UPLC-ESI-Q-TOF-MSE system (Acquity UPLC I-class &amp; Vion IMS Qtof MS, Waters) fitted with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS C18 T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters) with the column temperature set to 45°C. The mobile phases comprised 0.1% formic acid in water (buffer A) and acetonitrile (buffer B). The flow rate was set to 0.4ml/min. Gradient: 0-3 min with buffer B ranging from 1% to 20%; 3-6 min with buffer B ranging from 20% to 100%; 6-9 min with buffer B maintained at 100%; 9-10 min with buffer B ranging from 100% to 1%; 10-13.5 min with buffer B maintained at 1%.</p>	<p>Both positive and negative electrospray ion modes were acquired for mass acquisition strategy. The capillary voltage was 2.0 kV for ESI positive and negative modes. Cone voltage: 40V; desolation temp: 450°C; desolation gas: 900 L/h and cone gas: 50 L/h; source temp: 115°C. Acquisition ranges from 50 to 1,000 m/z with the scan time at 0.2s. The collision energy was set at 6 eV for low and at 20-45 eV for high. Leucine-enkephalin was introduced as the lockmass generating a reference for positive ion mode and negative ion mode. A pooled mixture of all samples was used to assess instrument stability throughout the acquisition, and one QC sample was inserted every 10 samples during testing to monitor in-process stability.&nbsp;</p>	<p>All mass spectrometry data were converted using MSConvert.</p>	<p>All raw MS data processing utilized the Progenesis QI-v2.3 (Waters) software for peak detection, alignment and standardization. Peak annotation was performed by matching experimental m/z and fragmentation spectra information to HMDB databases (http://www.hmdb.ca/).</p>

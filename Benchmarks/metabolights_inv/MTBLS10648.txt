Study Title	Effects of solid food ingestion on the caecal metabolome of suckling rabbits
Study Description	<p>NMR metabolomics data of the caecum content from 25-day old rabbits exclusively suckling (milk group) or suckling and ingesting solid food (milk+solid group). The aim of this protocol was to use metabolomics to identify how the ingestion of solid food alters the concentration of metabolites in the caecum content in suckling rabbits. This protocol is part of the project METABOWEAN (Gut microbiota-derived metabolites: natural products to promote epithelial barrier maturation at the suckling-to-weaning transition) funded by the French National Research Agency (ANR-21-CE20-0048).</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The experiment was performed at the PECTOUL experimental facility (GenPhySE, INRAE, Toulouse, France). The handling of rabbits followed the recommendations outlined in the European Union's regulations for the protection of animals used in scientific research (2010/63/EU), and was consistent with French legislation (NOR: AGRG1238753A 2013). This protocol received approval from the local ethics committee “Comité d’éthique en expérimentation animale SCIENCE ET SANTE ANIMALES”, CEEA - 115 (SSA_2022_012). Multiparous dams (n = 4) were housed individually in wire cages (61 x 68 x 50 cm) equipped with a closed nest (39 × 27 × 50 cm). The litter size was limited to 10 pups per litter. From postnatal day (PND) 4, pups were placed in a new cage, separated from their mother. At PND12, litters were equalized to 6 pups in order to maximize milk ingestion. Pups from each litter were separated in 2 cages (3 pups/cage) to form two groups. In the first group (Milk), the pups were exclusively suckling. In the second group (Milk+Solid), the pups were suckling while having ad libitum access to food pellets (StabiGreen, Terrya). During all the experiment, the dam and the pups were placed once a day for 5-10 minutes in the nest for suckling before returning to their respective cages. Coprophagia was prevented by removing feces in the nest after suckling.&nbsp;One male pup per litter (n=4) and per group (Milk or Milk+Solid) was sacrificed after suckling at PND24 or PND25 by electronarcosis followed by exsanguination. The caecal content was collected and kept at -80°C until analysis.</p>	<p>Caecal contents (50 mg) were homogenized in 500 µL phosphate buffer (prepared in D2O, pH7, TSP 1 mM) in 2mL FastPrep tubes (Lysing D matrix) by using a FastPrep Instrument (MP biomedicals, Irvine, CA). After centrifugation (12&nbsp;000 g, 4°C, 10 min), supernatants were collected. The extraction step was repeated on the pellet. Supernatants of the two extraction steps were pooled and centrifuged (18&nbsp;000 g, 30 min, 4°C). The centrifugation step was repeated twice. </p>	<p>The resulting supernatant (600 µL) was transferred to a 5 mm NMR tube.</p><p>As described in the Extraction protocol, each sample is a supernatant resulting from homogenization and centrifugation steps of ceacal content (50mg) dissolved in 1ml of phosphate buffer prepared in deuterated water (0.2 M, pH 7.0), containing 1mM of TMP as internal standard.</p>	<p>All 1H NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker Avance DRX-600 spectrometer (Bruker SA, Wissembourg, France) operating at 600.13 MHz for 1H resonance frequency and at 300 K using the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) spin-echo pulse sequence.</p>	<p>The 1H signal was acquired by accumulating 128 transients over a spectral width of 20 ppm (note: chemical shift units kept ppm), collecting 64k data points. The interpulse delay of the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) sequence was set at 0.4 ms with n = 300 as defined in the following sequence: [90-(τ -180-τ )n acquisition]. A 2-s relaxation delay was applied.</p>	<p>The ASICS R package was used to perform spectra pre-processing (https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz248).&nbsp;Spectra (fid) were baseline corrected before binning (0.01 ppm bins, excluding the water region 4.5-5.1 ppm) and normalized to the total spectra area.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by overlaying sample spectra with the spectra of pure compounds (standards) acquired in the same buffer and with the same NMR spectrometer. The normalized intensity of a bucket corresponding to each metabolite was used for quantification.</p>

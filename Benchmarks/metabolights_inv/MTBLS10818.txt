Study Title	Therapeutic effects of traditional Chinese medicine compound Hua-Feng-Dan in a rat model of ischemic stroke involve renormalization of gut microbiota
Study Description	<p>Hua-Feng-Dan is a traditional Chinese medicine compound used to treat ischemic stroke, but little is known about its therapeutic mechanism. This study explored whether and how the mechanism involves readjustment of gut microbiota. Rats were subjected to middle cerebral artery occlusion as a model of ischemic stroke or to sham surgery, then treated or not with Hua-Feng-Dan. The different groups of animals were compared in terms of neurological score, cerebral infarct volume and brain histopathology to assess stroke severity. They were also compared in terms of indices of intestinal barrier permeability, inflammation and oxidative stress as well as composition of the gut microbiota and their metabolites. Hua-Feng-Dan significantly reduced cerebral infarct volume and improved neurological score and brain histopathology on the ischemic side of the brain. It partially reversed stroke-induced intestinal leakiness, inflammation, dyslipidemia and oxidative stress, as well as the stroke-induced increase in pathogenic gut microbiota (e.g. Escherichia-Shigella, Enterococcus, Clostridium_innocuum_group) and decrease in beneficial microbiota (e.g. Lachnospiraceae, unclassified__f__Lachnospiracea and Ruminococcus_torques_group). The treatment altered levels of 39 metabolites produced during metabolism mainly of amino acids, short-chain fatty acids and essential fatty acids. Levels of factors related to inflammation and intestinal barrier permeability correlated positively with relative abundance of Escherichia-Shigella and Clostridium_innocuum_group, and negatively with 4-(glutamylamino)butanoate, 2-hydroxy-3-methylbutyric acid, dihomo-alpha-linolenic acid, dihomolinoleic acid and 10-nitrolinoleic acid. Conversely, levels of 4-(glutamylamino)butanoate, 2-hydroxy-3-methylbutyric acid and 10-nitrolinoleic acid correlated positively with relative abundance of unclassified__f__Lachnospiracea. Our results suggest that Hua-Feng-Dan may mitigate ischemic stroke injury by renormalizing gut microbiota and restoring gut barrier function and gut metabolism, thereby helping to alleviate inflammatory and neurological damage.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male Sprague-Dawley rats free of specific pathogens were randomly assigned to sham-operated (Sham), model (MCAO) and Hua-Feng-Dan (HFD, 0.648 g/kg/day) groups. Rats were randomized to receive, by oral gavage, saline instead of drug treatment or Hua-Feng-Dan dissolved in saline. 10 days was the period of the treatment, with modeling conducted 1 h after the drug administration on the 7th day. At 72 h after modeling, rats were fasted without water. After blood collection from the abdominal aorta in anesthetized rats, the cecum contents were squeezed in 1.5 mL sterile EP tubes, quickly frozen in dry ice, and stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>sham: normal rats treated by oral gavage with saline instead of drug. 10 days was the period of the treatment, with modeling conducted 1 h after the drug administration on the 7th day.</p><p>model: MCAO rats treated by oral gavage with saline instead of drug. 10 days was the period of the treatment, with modeling conducted 1 h after the drug administration on the 7th day.</p><p>Hua-Feng-Dan: MCAO rats treated by oral gavage with Hua-Feng-Dan (HFD, 0.648 g/kg/day). 10 days was the period of the treatment, with modeling conducted 1 h after the drug administration on the 7th day.</p>	<p>The sample was accurately weighed at 50 ± 5 mg and placed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. A grinding bead with a diameter of 6 mm was added, followed by the addition of 400 µL of extraction solution [methanol/water, 4/1, v/v] containing 4 internal standards (L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL), etc). The sample was then ground for 6 min using a freezing tissue grinder (-10 °C, 50 Hz) and extracted by cryo-sonication for 30 min (5 °C, 40 KHz). Afterward, the sample was left at -20 °C for an additional 30 min before being centrifuged for another 15 min (13,000 x g, 4 °C). The resulting supernatant was transferred to an injection vial with an insert for analysis. Additionally, quality control samples were prepared by pipetting out 20 µL of supernatant from each sample and mixing them together.</p>	<p>The chromatographic column was ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, USA); the mobile phase A consisted of 95% water and 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), while the mobile phase B was composed of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol and 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid). The injection volume was set at 3 μL and the column temperature maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>Sheath gas flow rate: 50 Arb, auxiliary gas flow rate: 13 Arb, capillary temperature: 325 °C, full MS resolution: 60,000, MS/MS resolution: 7500, collision energy: negative mode 20/40/60, spray voltage: 3.5 kV (+) or -3.5 kV (-).</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS data underwent retention time correction, peak identification and peak alignment using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Subsequently, a data matrix containing information on retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity was obtained. The MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry information was compared with the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin database (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), among others. The MS mass error threshold was set to be less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on the secondary mass spectrometry matching score. The detected compounds were verified manually. The stability of the internal standard was assessed by checking whether the z-score values fell within 2 times the standard deviation. Finally, the data were analyzed in both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS data underwent retention time correction, peak identification and peak alignment using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA).

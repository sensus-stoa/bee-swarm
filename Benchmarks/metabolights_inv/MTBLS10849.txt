Study Title	Metabolomic Profiling and Trajectories During Gestation: Associations with Maternal and Child Health
Study Description	<p>The metabolic regulations during pregnancy are precisely programmed, even small deviations can bring devastating consequences. Here, we constructed a comprehensive view of the metabolome trajectories throughout pregnancy in 9,687 plasma samples from 3,339 pregnant women, including 2,348 naturally-conceived (NC) women and 991 conceived via assisted reproductive technology (ART). We profiled the broad-scale change in metabolome driven by pregnancy progression, which is generally consistent in NC and ART women with phosphatidylcholine and steroid hormone metabolism being the top regulated pathways. Overall, clinical characteristics inferred much more of the variations than lifestyle factors. For example, prednisone use in ART pregnancies exhibited long-term disturbance in maternal metabolome, particular in steroid metabolism. Moreover, we demonstrated the extensive associations of maternal metabolomic signatures with health outcomes in the mothers and children, including novel associations, which can be leveraged toward the precision medicine in prenatal care.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>In summary, the study included a total of 3,339 pregnant women who delivered singleton live infants. Of these, 2,348 were conceived naturally (NC group) and 991 were conceived using assisted reproductive technology (ART group). These participants were followed up throughout gestation and donated blood samples in early (NC, n=2,271; ART, n=980), middle (NC, n=2,328; ART, n=916), and late pregnancy (NC, n=2,292; ART, n=900). Notably, early-gestation samples were collected from naturally conceived women at gestational weeks 8-14 (extended from 6-14 weeks), and from ART conceived women at gestational weeks 4-6. Subsequent blood samples were collected in the same time windows for middle (gestational weeks 15-27) and late pregnancy (gestational weeks 28-40).&nbsp;</p>	<p> All samples were maintained at -80 °C until processed. To ensure quality control (QC), isotope internal standards were added prior to the initial extraction step. Protein precipitation was performed using methanol with vigorous shaking (Glen Mills GenoGrinder 2000) for two minutes, followed by centrifugation. This step removed proteins, dissociated small molecules bound to or trapped in the protein matrix, and recovered a wide range of metabolites. The resulting extract was divided into four fractions: two for analysis by two separate reverse phase (RP)/UPLC-MS/MS methods with positive ion mode electrospray ionization (ESI), one for analysis by RP/UPLC-MS/MS with negative ion mode ESI, and one for analysis by HILIC/UPLC-MS/MS with negative ion mode ESI. The samples were then evaporated to near dryness using a TurboVap® (Zymark). The sample extracts were stored overnight under nitrogen before final preparation for analysis.</p>	<p> All analyses were performed utilizing a Waters ACQUITY ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system coupled with a Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive high resolution/accurate mass spectrometry, interfaced with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI-II) source and Orbitrap mass analyzer operated at 35,000 mass resolution. The sample extracts were dried then reconstituted in solvents compatible to each of the four analytical methods. Each reconstitution solvent contained a series of standards at fixed concentrations to ensure consistency in injection and chromatography. One aliquot was analyzed using acidic positive ion conditions, optimized for hydrophilic compounds. The extract was gradient-eluted from a C18 column (Waters UPLC BEH C18-2.1x100 mm, 1.7 µm) using water and methanol, with 0.05% perfluoropentanoic acid (PFPA) and 0.1% formic acid (FA). Another aliquot was analyzed under acidic positive ion conditions but optimized for hydrophobic compounds. The extracts were gradient-eluted from the same previously mentioned C18 column using methanol, acetonitrile, water, 0.05% PFPA and 0.01% FA, at a higher organic content. A third aliquot was analyzed under basic negative ion optimized conditions using a dedicated C18 column. The basic extracts were gradient-eluted from the column using methanol and water with 6.5mM Ammonium Bicarbonate at pH 8. The fourth aliquot was analyzed via negative ionization following elution from a HILIC column (Waters UPLC BEH Amide 2.1x150 mm, 1.7 µm) using a gradient consisting of water and acetonitrile with 10mM ammonium formate at pH 10.8. The MS analysis alternated between MS and data-dependent MS2 scans using dynamic exclusion. The scan range varied slightly between methods but covered 70-1000 m/z.</p>	<p> All analyses were performed utilizing a Waters ACQUITY ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system coupled with a Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive high resolution/accurate mass spectrometry, interfaced with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI-II) source and Orbitrap mass analyzer operated at 35,000 mass resolution. Finally, 782 metabolites were included in the analysis. metabolomics data were log2-transformed, mean-centered and scaled to unit standard derivation (SD) prior to statistical analysis&nbsp;</p>	<p> Metabolic features were extracted and identified from raw data using Discovery HD4's hardware and software. Discovery HD4 maintains a library of more than 3,300 authenticated standards that contains the retention index (RI), mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and MS/MS spectral data. Compound identification was based on three criteria: RI within a narrow window of the proposed identification, m/z match to the library ± 10 ppm, and the similarity of forward and reverse scores of MS/MS spectra. The MS/MS spectral similarity scores were derived from comparing ions in the experimental spectrum with those in the authentic library spectrum. All of the three-dimensional data can be utilized to distinguish and differentiate compounds to remove false positive identification. All identified metabolites met the level one requirement by the Chemical Analysis Working Group (CAWG) of the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) (Sumner et al., 2007), except some asterisk labeled lipids whose MS/MS spectral were in silico matched. Finally, 869 metabolites spanning nine distinct biochemical species were identified for further analysis.</p>

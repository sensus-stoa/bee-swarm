Study Title	Biomarker Discovery in Oral Cancer (OSMF & OSCC) and its severity
Study Description	<p>Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is frequently the outcome of oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF), a common possibly premalignant disease. In our study, a cohort of 50 patients with OSCC and OSMF, along with 50 healthy controls, was analyzed to identify significant metabolic differences between the patient and control groups through multivariate statistical analysis using NMR-based metabolomics in saliva samples. The 2D scatter plot of PC1 versus PC2 scores clearly shows a distinction between the groups, with the principal component analysis (PCA) explaining 24.6% of the variance. Partial least-squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) demonstrated R2 and Q2 values of 0.94 and 0.90, respectively, indicating a robust model fit. A total of 20 distinct metabolites were identified, including 5 that were up-regulated and 5 down-regulated. Univariate ROC curve analysis identified 9 salivary metabolites with AUC values exceeding 0.70, including acetone, tryptophan, 5-aminopentanoic acid, betaine, aspartic acid, ethanol, acetoacetate, adipic acid and citrate. Notably, the distinct presence of three metabolites - acetone, tryptophan and 5-aminopentanoic acid - yielded AUC values of 0.98123, 0.95358 and 0.91506, respectively. The refined statistical model was subjected to metabolic pathway analysis, revealing interconnected pathways. We could also predict the disease's severity, specifically distinguishing between Stage I and Stage II OSCC. This differentiation was highlighted by the PCA score plot, which explained 28.6% of the variance. PLS-DA further confirmed these results. These insights pave the way for early diagnosis and predicting severity in oral cancer patients, enabling better management of the disease.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Collection of samples was done in the morning, from 9:00 to 11:00, to minimize the circadian variation effect on salivary composition. Before collection, participants were advised to abstain from all activities for at least 1 h, including eating, drinking, smoking, and brushing their teeth to avoid contamination and ensure the collection of unstimulated saliva. For each subject, 5 mL of full bland saliva was obtained. using the drooling method, where participants were instructed to allow their saliva to accumulate on the mouth's floor before being drooled into a sterile, chilled saliva container. Immediately after collection, saliva samples were transferred into sterile centrifuge tubes. The samples were centrifuged for 6 min at 4 °C at 3000 rpm to remove cells and debris. After being cautiously decanted into 2 mL aliquots, the supernatant was kept at -80 °C.</p>	<p>No extraction was carried out in this study.</p>	<p>Before performing NMR experiments, the samples were thawed, after which 250 μL of saliva was combined with an equal volume of saline buffer solution. This buffer was prepared using 100% D2O, 0.9% NaCl and a 50-mM sodium phosphate (Na3PO4) buffer, adjusted to a pH of 7.4. The resulting mixture was then vortexed thoroughly and transferred into clean Wilmad 5 mm NMR tubes. Each tube contained a Wilmad coaxial insert with TSP (the sodium salt of 3-trimethylsilyl-(2,2,3,3-d4)-propionic acid; CAS-No: 24493-21-8, Sigma-Aldrich) at a concentration of 0.05 mM, which served as an external standard for calibration.</p>	<p>All 1H-NMR spectra were collected on an Bruker AVANCE III 800 MHz spectrometer with a TCI (1H,&nbsp;13C,&nbsp;15N and&nbsp;2H lock) cryogenic probe. 1H-NMR spectra were acquired at 298 K using the first transient of the Varian tnnoesy-presaturation pulse sequence, which was chosen for its high degree of quantitative accuracy.</p>	<p>The specific NMR experiment performed was a 1D 1H experiment. A deuterium field frequency lock was used, and the standard concentration of the sample was 0.5 mM. The spectrometer operated at a frequency of 800 MHz, and a cryo inverse ATMA NMR probe was utilized. The solvent for the NMR experiment was D2O, and the sample was placed in a 5 mm x 7 in NMR tube. Shimming was achieved using the topshim method.</p><p><br></p><p>The pulse sequence employed was cpmgpr1d, and water suppression was performed using presaturation (presat). The size of the free induction decay (FID) was 92,160 points, with a receiver gain of 71.8 and an offset frequency of 3763.10 Hz. TSP was used as the chemical shift reference compound, and the temperature during the experiment was maintained at 298 K.</p><p><br></p><p>A total of 128 scans were performed, with 4 dummy scans preceding them. The acquisition time for each scan was 2.8753920 s, and the relaxation delay was set to 2 s. The spectral width was 16,025.641 Hz, and the FID resolution was 0.347779. A total of 32K data points were acquired, which were subsequently zero-filled to 64K real data points. The line broadening applied was 0.3, and Lorentzian apodization was used. Baseline correction was performed using a polynomial method, with TSP as the reference standard for chemical shifts.</p>	<p>The raw 1H-NMR spectra were manually processed and analyzed using Topspin 4.3.0. The integration values were then recorded in a spreadsheet for subsequent statistical analysis.</p>	<p>The complete set of salivary compounds identified, along with their concentrations, was meticulously compiled. Each compound was matched with its corresponding standardized HMDB identifiers for consistency. The dataset, derived from an extensive literature search, was manually annotated to include detailed information such as compound names, synonyms, descriptions and molecular structures.</p>

Study Title	Sex and subtype differences shape the blood metabolome profiles in Parkinson's disease.
Study Description	<p>Rationale: Sex and type of Parkinson's disease (PD) significantly influence onset age, clinical manifestations, treatment response, disease progression and life expectancy. However, the selective influence of idiopathic and familial forms of PD in disrupting blood metabolome profiles across genders remains unclear.</p><p>Methods: We conducted an untargeted 1H NMR-based metabolomics study in a large cohort of clinically and genetically well-characterized patients and controls (n=385), which included: i) 56 women and 65 men diagnosed with idiopathic PD; ii) 60 women and 64 men with a familial form of PD; iii) 80 men and 60 women serving as healthy control groups.</p><p>Results: Multivariate Analysis (MVA) did not reveal differences in serum metabolomes between idiopathic and familial PD patients in both sexes. Similarly, MVA did not show significant variation among blood metabolomes of patients carrying distinct pathogenic variants, including LRRK2, TMEM175, PRKN, PINK1, PARK7 and GBA1. MVA indicates remarkable metabolome differences between idiopathic and familial subtypes compared to healthy controls. Notably, 1H NMR-based metabolomics unveiled a striking gender effect in influencing metabolome dysregulation across idiopathic or familial patients compared to matched controls. Specifically, our results indicate that amino acids, glutathione biosynthesis, energy homeostasis pathways, phospholipids and ketone body metabolism alterations are critically associated with the sex and genotype of PD individuals. However, regardless of the specific disease type, MVA consolidates the evidence that more pronounced blood metabolome dysregulations occurred in males than women with the same form of PD.</p><p>Conclusion: We reveal that men and women exhibit distinct serum metabolome dysregulations across idiopathic and familial forms of PD compared to healthy controls, further indicating the key role of gender and genetic background in controlling varying pathophysiological features of the disease.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The Mediterranean Neuromed Institute (MNI) cohort of PD patients includes 804 independent and unrelated patients (501 males; 300 familial and 504 sporadic cases), belonging to the Parkinson’s disease Biobank of the IRCCS Neuromed and of the Institute of Genetics and Biophysics (CNR) (Carrillo et al 2025; Yahyavi et al. 2025). All the subjects were of European ancestry and were evaluated by qualified neurologists of the Parkinson Centre of the IRCCS INM Neuromed from June 2015 to December 2017, and from June 2021 to December 2023, with a thorough protocol comprising neurological examination and evaluation of non-motor domains. Information about family history, demographic characteristics, anamnesis, and pharmacological therapy was also collected (the treatment of the PD groups consisted for the most part of a combination of levodopa and dopamine agonists) (Gialluisi et al. 2021). The Movement Disorder Society revised version of the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale Part III (33 items, maximum score 132; hereafter called UPDRS)(Hentz et al., 2015) was used to assess clinical motor symptoms which include: language, facial expressions, tremor, rigidity, agility in movements, stability, gait and bradykinesia. Non-motor symptoms were assessed through an Italian validated version of Non Motor Symptoms Scale (NMS) for Parkinson Disease(Cova et al., 2017). This scale tests 9 items, including cardiovascular domain, sleep/fatigue, mood/cognition, perceptual problems/hallucinations, attention/memory, gastrointestinal, urinary, sexual function, and ability to taste or smell. This cohort was used to select the sub-cohort used in this study e was also analyzed without stratification in case-control association test to discover susceptibility or protective factors for PD.</p><p>NMR-PD cohort. 245 independent and unrelated PD patients (129 males; 86 familial and 159 sporadic cases) were selected from the entire MNI-PD cohort of 804 PD patients. This is the same cohort of patients that we used in our recent HPLC paper (Yahyavi et al. 2025). The mean age at diagnosis of the 245 selected patients was 66.5 years (SD 8). This cohort includes 121 idiopathic PD patients and 124 genetic PD patients, carrying at least one pathogenic mutation in the most frequently mutated genes LRRK2, GBA1, TMEM175 and PARK2/PINK1/PARK7 (see next paragraph “Mutation analysis of the study cohort” for details about the patient selection). This cohort was used for metabolomic analysis and for case-control association analysis to discover variants specific to sex and subtype associated with PD in genes encoding for serine and glycine metabolic enzymes. </p><p>MNI-PD subtype. This cohort includes 193 idiopathic PD patients (negative for mutations/variants in PD genes) and 283 genetic patients carrying at least one pathogenic mutation in the most frequently mutated genes LRRK2, GBA1, TMEM175 and PARK2/PINK1/PARK7 (see next paragraph “Mutation analysis of the study cohort” for details about the patient selection). This cohort was selected from the entire MNI-PD cohort by stratifying for sex and genetic background. This cohort was used as a validation cohort in the case-control association analysis. </p><p>MNI-HC cohort. 282 neurological healthy controls (HC) were recruited by the same group of neurologists, among the patients' wives/husbands, after having ascertained the lack of neurological pathologies and the absence of affected family members. WES data were available for the entire cohort. The MNI-HC cohort was used for association analysis.</p><p>NMR-HC cohort. 140 neurological controls (80 males; mean age 60.7 years; SD 11.3) were selected from the MNI-HC cohort. The NMR-HC cohort matched for sex and age with NMR-PD cohort and was negative for mutation/variant in PD genes (see next paragraph “Mutation analysis of the study cohort” for the selection criteria).&nbsp;</p>	<p>Serum samples were prepared in accordance with the NMR metabolomics sample quality preservation guidelines. {{Marino, 2022 271}Alwahsh, 2024 268}. No extraction was performed</p>	<p>To prepare NMR samples, 250 µL of phosphate buffer (0.075 M Na2HPO47H2O, 4% NaN3, and water) was mixed with 250 µL of blood serum and then placed into a 5 mm NMR tube. To calibrate and measure NMR signals, we used trimethylsilyl propionic acid and sodium salt (0.1% TSP in D2O) as an internal reference. NMR investigations were conducted using a Bruker DRX600 MHz spectrometer (Bruker, Karlsruhe, Germany) equipped with a 5 mm triple-resonance z-gradient TXI Probe. For spectrometer control and data analysis, we utilised TOPSPIN, version 3.2 (Bruker Biospin, Fllanden, Switzerland).</p>	<p>CPMG experiments were acquired using 600 MHz speectometer; conducted with a spectral width of 7 kHz and 32K data points. Water presaturation lasted for 5 s during the relaxation delay, and a spin-echo delay of 0.3 ms was utilised. A weighted Fourier transform was applied to the time-domain data with a line broadening of 0.5 Hz, followed by manual adjustments for phase and baseline to prepare for targeted profiling analysis.</p>	<p>The acquisition of the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) spectrum was essential because serum contains macromolecules, such as proteins, that may interfere with signals related to other metabolites. {Ghini, 2019 270} CPMG experiments were conducted with a spectral width of 7 kHz and 32K data points. Water presaturation lasted for 5 s during the relaxation delay, and a spin-echo delay of 0.3 ms was utilised. A weighted Fourier transform was applied to the time-domain data with a line broadening of 0.5 Hz, followed by manual adjustments for phase and baseline to prepare for targeted profiling analysis</p>	<p>The spectra were normalised taking into account the signal of the internal standard TSP at known concentration</p>	<p>The concentration matrices derived from NMR peak quantifications were examined using a univariate method that integrated a T-test with fold change analysis depicted in a robust volcano plot. A fold change threshold of 1 and a p-value threshold of less than 0.05 were established {Kumar, 2018 273} The matrices were normalised using sum and Pareto scaling prior to analysis. Partial least-squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was conducted on the normalised metabolomics data with MetaboAnalyst 6.0 (http://www.metaboanalyst.ca). {Pang, 2024 274} The performance of the PLS-DA model was evaluated through the Q2 coefficient (utilising a 10-fold internal cross-validation method) and the R2 coefficient, which indicates the variance that the model predicts and explains, respectively</p>	<p>Resonance assignments were performed using Chenomx software, and metabolite quantification was carried out using the automated Bayesil software</p>

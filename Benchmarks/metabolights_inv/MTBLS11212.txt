Study Identifier	MTBLS11212
Study Title	We characterize the anti-phage function of a type IV TA system, ShosTA toxin-antitoxin system provides phage defense via DNA damage and repair
Study Description	Widespread in bacteria and archaea, Toxin-antitoxin (TA) systems have been recently demonstrated to function in phage defense. Here we characterize the anti-phage function of a type IV TA system, ShosTA. Using structural and biochemical approaches, we show that ShosT couples phosphoribosyltransferase and pyrophosphatase activities to disrupt purine metabolism, resulting in DNA duplication, cell filamentation and ultimate cell death. ShosA binds DNA and likely recruits other proteins to facilitate DNA recombination to antagonize ShosT’s toxicity

Study Protocol Description	<p>Early-log cultures of BL21 (DE3) harboring pBAD-ShosT or empty vector were induced by 0.2% L-ara and cultured at 37 °C with shaking. Samples were collected 10 min before cell growth arrest (about 80 min after inducing). Altogether 5 replicates were made for each group.</p>	<p>Samples were collected 10 min before cell growth arrest (about 80 min after inducing). The metabolites were extracted from cells with 1 mL precooled methanol/acetonitrile/water (v/v, 2:2:1) under sonication for 1 h in ice baths, incubated at -20 °C for 1 h followed by centrifugation at 14,000 x g, 4 °C for 20 min. The supernatants were recovered and concentrated to dryness in vacuum.</p>	<p>For liquid chromatography (LC) separation, samples were analyzed using a ACQUITY UPLC&nbsp;HSS T3 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 μm) (Waters, USA). The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min and the mobile phase contained: A: 0.1% FA in water and B: 100% acetonitrile (ACN). The gradient was 0% buffer B for 2.0 min and was linearly increase to 48% in 4.0 min, and then up to 100% in 4.0 min and maintained for 2.0 min, and then decreased to 0% buffer B in 0.1 min, with 3.0 min re-equilibration period employed.</p>	<p>Each sample was detected by electrospray ionization (ESI) in positive (+) and negative (-) modes, respectively. The samples were separated by UPLC and analyzed by mass spectrometry using a QE Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) and ionized using a HESI source with the following ionization conditions: Spray Voltage: 3.8 kv (+) and 3.2 kv (-); Capillary Temperature: 320 °C (±); Sheath Gas: 30 (±); Aux Gas: 5 (±); Probe Heater Temp: 350 °C (±); S-Lens RF Level: 50. MS acquisition time: 15 min; parent ion scan range: 70-1050 m/z; primary MS resolution: 70,000 @ m/z 200; AGC target: 3e6; primary Maximum IT: 100 ms. The secondary MS analysis was acquired as follows: MS2 resolution: 17,500 @ m/z 200; AGC target: 1e5; secondary Maximum IT: 50 ms; MS2 Activation Type: HCD; Isolation window: 2 m/z,</p>	<p>The raw data were analyzed by MSDIAL software for peak alignment, retention time correction and peak area extraction. The structural identification of the metabolites was performed by precise mass number matching (mass tolerance &lt; 20 ppm) and secondary spectral matching (mass tolerance &lt; 0.02 Da), and the public databases such as HMDB, MassBank and the local metabolite standard library of BaySpectrum were searched.</p><p><br></p><p>For the extracted data, ion peaks with &gt;50% missing values were deleted from the group and were not involved in the subsequent statistical analysis; the positive and negative ion data were normalized by the total peak area, and the positive and negative ion peaks were integrated and identified by the R software, which was used to identify the patterns.</p>	<p>The raw data were analyzed by MSDIAL software for peak alignment, retention time correction and peak area extraction.

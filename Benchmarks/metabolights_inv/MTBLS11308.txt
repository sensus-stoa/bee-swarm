Study Identifier	MTBLS11308
Study Title	Host specific sphingomyelin is critical for replication of diverse RNA viruses
Study Description	Lipids and lipid metabolism play an important role in RNA virus replication, which typically occurs on host cell endomembrane structures in the cytoplasm through mechanisms that are not yet fully identified. We conducted genome-scale CRISPR screening and identified sphingomyelin synthase 1 (SMS1; encoded by SGMS1) as a critical host factor for infection by severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV). SGMS1 knockout reduced sphingomyelin (SM) (d18:1/16:1) levels, inhibiting SFTSV replication. A helix-turn-helix motif in SFTSV RdRp directly binds to SM(d18:1/16:1) in Golgi apparatus, which was also observed in SARS-CoV-2 and lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV), both showing inhibited replication in SGMS1-KO cells. SM metabolic disturbance is associated with disease severity of viral infections. We designed a novel SMS1 inhibitor that protects mice against lethal SFTSV infection and reduce SARS-CoV-2 replication and pathogenesis. These findings highlight the critical role of SMS1 and SM(d18:1/16:1) in RNA virus replication, suggesting a broad-spectrum antiviral strategy.

Study Protocol Description	<p> Huh7 (male) cells were obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC, Cat GDC0134), SGMS1-knockout (KO) Huh7 cells were generated by CRISPR genome editing system. The MinuteTM Golgi Apparatus Enrichment Kit (Invent Biotechnologies, Cat GO-037) was used for isolation of Golgi fractions. All samples were prepared as precipitates for freezing and preservation.</p>	<p>Lipids were quantitated in biological samples (whole cells and purified Golgi fraction) using internal standards including 15:0–18:1_(d7)PA (7 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1(d7)PE (5 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1(d7)PG (30 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1(d7)PI (10 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1(d7)_PS (5 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1 (d7) PC (160 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1 (d7) DG (10 μg/mL), 15:0–18:1 (d7)–15:0 TG (55 μg/mL), 18:1 (d7) CE (350 μg/mL), 18:1 (d7) LPC (5 μg/mL), 18:1 (d7) LPE (5 μg/mL), 18:1 (d7) MG (2 μg/mL), 18:1 (d9) SM (30 μg/mL), and Cholesterol (d7, 100 μg/mL) purchased from Sigma (Sigma-Aldrich). Samples were thawed in an ice bath, then 200 μL of isopropanol was added. The samples were ultrasonically disrupted for 3 seconds, kept at -20 °C for 10 min, vortexed for 10 min, maintained at 2-8 °C for 2 hours, and centrifuged at 18,000 × g for 10 min; supernatants were used for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>An ACQUITY UPLC BEH amide column (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) heated at 45 °C was utilized for chromatographic separation. The mobile phase A was acetonitrile(95:5 v/v) containing 5 mM ammonium acetate, while the mobile phase B was acetonitrile(50:50 v/v) containing 5 mM ammonium acetate. An injection volume of 1 µL was used. The gradient conditions were 0.1-20% B for 0-2 min, 20-80% B for 2-5 min, 80-0.1% B for 5 min, and 0.1% B for 5.1-8 min. The flow rate was set at 0.6 mL/min.</p>	<p>Lipids were detected by ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-triple quadrupole mass spectrometry (UPLC-TQMS) (Waters Corp., ACQUITY UPLC-Xevo TQ-S Milford). The capillary voltage was set at 1.9 kV negative and 2.8 kV positive, source temperature was kept at 120 °C, desolvation temperature was set to 500 °C, and desolvation gas flow was 1000 L/h. A mixture of all measured samples was used as a QC sample injected regularly throughout the analysis process.</p>	<p>Raw data generated by UPLC-TQMS was processed using MassLynx software (Waters Corp). Peak extraction, integration, and quantification were performed for each lipid. Samples were randomly tested according to group information to eliminate potential errors caused by the sequence of the analysis process.</p>	<p>Metabolic analysis was conducted using MetaboAnalyst 5.0 as previously described. Partial least-squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was performed for classification and prediction of data. Hierarchical clustering of all potential metabolites was performed after unit variance scaling for each metabolite. Pathway enrichment analyses were based on the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes database and the human metabolome database (KEGG) (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/).</p>

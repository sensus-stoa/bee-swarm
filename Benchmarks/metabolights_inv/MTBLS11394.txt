Study Identifier	MTBLS11394
Study Title	The efflux pump ABCC1/MRP1 constitutively restricts PROTAC sensitivity in cancer cells
Study Description	Proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTACs) are bifunctional molecules that induce selective protein degradation by linking an E3 ubiquitin ligase enzyme to a target protein. This approach allows scope for targeting “undruggable” proteins and several PROTACs have reached the stage of clinical candidates. However, the roles of cellular transmembrane transporters in PROTAC uptake and efflux remain underexplored. Here, we utilized transporter-focused genetic screens to identify the ATP binding cassette transporter ABCC1/MRP1 as a key PROTAC resistance factor. Unlike the previously identified inducible PROTAC exporter ABCB1/MDR1, ABCC1 is highly expressed among cancers of various origins and constitutively restricts PROTAC bioavailability. Moreover, in a genome-wide PROTAC resistance screen, we identified candidates involved in processes such as ubiquitination, mTOR signaling and apoptosis as genetic factors involved in PROTAC resistance. In summary, our findings reveal ABCC1 as a crucial constitutively active efflux pump limiting PROTAC efficacy in various cancer cells, offering insights for overcoming drug resistance.

Study Protocol Description	<p>HAP1 (sex: male origin but no Y chromosome in haploid state, RRID:CVCL_Y019) cells were cultivated in DMEM (D5796 Sigma) supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (10270–106, Lot 42F8381K, Gibco) and Penicillin-Streptomycin (Cat 15140–122, Gibco). </p><p><br></p><p>For measuring intracellular PROTAC concentrations by LC-MS, 6× 10^5 <strong>HAP1</strong> <strong>WT </strong>or <strong>ABCC1 KO (HAP1-ABCC1-KO-c5) </strong>cells per well were seeded in 12-well plates coated with poly-lysine. On the following day, PROTACs were added at the indicated concentrations in 500 µl volume. After two hours of drug treatment, cells were washed twice with culture medium, and cells were harvested or kept in culture in culture medium without drugs as indicated before washing one more time with medium.</p><p><br></p><p>To measure extracellular metabolite concentrations, 6× 10^5<strong> HAP1 WT </strong>or <strong>ABCC1 KO (HAP1-ABCC1-KO-c5) </strong>cells per well were seeded in 12-well plates coated with poly-lysine. On the following day, 25 µM MZ1 in 500 µl volume was added. After two hours of drug treatment, cells were washed twice with culture medium and kept in culture in 500 µl fresh media. 25 µl of medium was harvested at the indicated time points and centrifuged at 13.000 rpm for 5 minutes to remove detached cells.</p>	<p>To extract PROTACs, cells were washed once with 75 mM ammonium carbonate [pH 7.2] and lysed with 300 µl of a MeOH:ACN:H2O (2:2:1, v/v) solvent mixture. For targeted metabolomics samples, labeled 13C15N-Glu was added to each well as internal standard. Extracts were transferred to 1.5 ml sample tubes and frozen at -80°C, followed by two cycles of thawing and re-freezing. To precipitate proteins completely, the samples were then incubated for 1h at -20 °C, followed by 15 min centrifugation at 13,000 rpm at 4 °C. Precipitated protein was quantified by a BCA protein assay for normalization, supernatants were transferred to LC-MS assays tubes, dried, and resuspended in 65 µl HPLC grade H2O.</p><p><br></p><p>For media samples, 20 µl of the supernatant was mixed with 120 µl MeOH, 120 µl ACN and 40 µl H2O, and incubated for 1h at -20 °C followed by 15 min centrifugation at 13,000 rpm at 4 °C. Supernatants were transferred to LC-MS assays tubes, dried, and resuspended in 65 µl HPLC grade H2O.</p>	<p>Targeted compound measurements were performed on a Waters Xevo TQ system utilizing a Waters ACQUITY UPLC® HSS T3 1.8µm reversed-phase column maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phase A was water with 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The elution gradient was as follows: t = 0 min, 2% solvent B, flow rate 0.15 mL/min; t = 2 min, 2% solvent B, flow rate 0.15 mL/min; t = 6 min, 100% solvent B, flow rate 0.3 mL/min; t = 8 min, 100% solvent B, flow rate 0.3 mL/min; t = 8.1 min, 2% solvent B, flow rate 0.2 mL/min; t = 10 min, 2% solvent B, flow rate 0.15 mL/min. Column eluent was diverted to waste in the first 0.5 min and the last 3 min of the gradient.</p><p><br></p><p>Untargeted analyses were performed on a Thermo Fisher Scientific Vanquish HPLC system coupled to a Q-Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC® HSS T3 1.8µm reversed-phase column maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phase A was water with 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. Elution was performed with constant flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. In the first experiment (MZ1 washout, intracellular metabolites), the following gradient was used: t = 0 min, 0% solvent B; t = 1 min, 0% solvent B; t = 12 min, 100% solvent B; t = 15 min, 100% solvent B; t = 15.1 min, 0% solvent B; t = 18 min, 0% solvent B. In subsequent experiments (MZ1 washout, media extracts; MZ1 at varying concentrations, intracellular metabolites), the gradient was lengthened as follows: t = 0 min, 0% solvent B; t = 1 min, 0% solvent B; t = 16 min, 100% solvent B; t = 18 min, 100% solvent B; t = 18.1 min, 0% solvent B; t = 20 min, 0% solvent B.</p>	<p>For targeted compound measurements, the Waters Xevo TQ mass spectrometer was operated in positive electrospray ionization mode. Other settings were as follows: capillary voltage 3 kV, source temperature 150 °C, desolvation temperature 350 °C, desolvation gas flow 650 L/h, collision gas flow 0.15 mL/min. MRM transitions were determined for each target compound using the IntelliStart function of MassLynx software and were as follows: MZ1, 1004.4616 – 385.0532; ACBI1, 936.4299 – 466.2525; BI-3663, 918.3213 – 442.1501; VZ185, 995.551 – 309.1785; 13C15N-Glutamic acid, 154.1596 – 89.1233.</p><p><br></p><p>For untargeted analyses, the mass spectrometer was operated in positive electrospray ionization mode. In the first experiment (MZ1 washout, intracellular metabolites), the instrument was operated in full MS mode with a scan range of 250–1250 m/z, resolution 140,000 and AGC target 1e6. A selected number of samples were also run in combined full MS / AIF (All Ion Fragmentation) mode at resolution 70,000 and AGC target 3e6, with full MS scan range of 120–1250 m/z and AIF scan range 80–1200 m/z, fixed collision energy 35. Subsequent experiments (MZ1 washout, media extracts; MZ1 at varying concentrations, intracellular metabolites) were all performed in combined full MS / AIF mode with full MS scan range of 200–1500 m/z and AIF scan range 100–1200 m/z, fixed collision energy 35.</p>	<p>Waters RAW data folders (from targeted analyses) or Thermo raw files (from untargeted analyses) were mass centroided and converted to mzML files using Proteowizard MSConvert.</p>	<p>For targeted analyses, MRM transitions were determined for each target compound using the IntelliStart function of MassLynx software and were as follows: MZ1, 1004.4616 – 687.2059; ACBI1, 936.4299 – 466.2525; BI-3663, 918.3213 – 442.1501; VZ185, 995.551 – 309.1785; 13C15N-Glutamic acid, 154.1596 – 89.1233.

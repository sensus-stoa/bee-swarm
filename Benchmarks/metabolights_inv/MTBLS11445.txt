Study Title	MTBLS11445: Integrative omics reveals distinct metabolic and microbial signatures in broilers under gut health interventions
Study Description	Maintaining a well-balanced gut microbiota is crucial for ensuring optimal health and performance in poultry production. Common strategies supporting gut health include control methods for coccidiosis which is a common and economically important enteric disease in poultry. These interventions not only affect the gut microbial community but are also expected to influence metabolite levels, reflecting the dynamic relationship between gut microorganisms and metabolic processes. In this study, we compared the caecal content metabolite levels between birds given ionophores (T1) and vaccination against Eimeria species (T2), using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry based untargeted metabolomic analysis. All statistical methods were on performed on R v4.4.1. Data was normalised using the probabilistic quotient method and subjected through Sparse Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (sPLS-DA) and Data Integration Analysis for Biomarker discovery using Latent Components (DIABLO) analyses using the mixomics package. Pathway analysis was also performed using MetaboAnalystR package. Our analysis revealed that metabolites classified as prenol lipids as robust markers for T1, while indole derivatives were identified for T2. KEGG pathways involving amino acids (Tryptophan, Phenylalanine) were also enriched in both groups. We identified Bacillus fragilis to have the greatest impact on the metabolome, positively correlated to >240 metabolites, while UBA3818 spp. (Ruminococcaceae) was shown to have a significant negative relationship with 22 metabolites. Overall, the results indicated that differences in coccidiosis control elicit diverse metabolic events in broilers through its influences on nutrient breakdown, affecting the performance and health of broilers and may provide novel avenues for gut health interventions against Eimeria.

Study Protocol Description	<p>After industrial stunning at the factory, the entire caecal pouches from each chicken were taken out from the abdomen. The caecal samples were then immediately stored in ice and transferred to a -80 °C freezer. Under a laboratory hood, approximately 500 mg intestinal contents were extracted from the caecal pouches using sterile forceps and transferred into 2 mL tubes. All tubes were then subjected to quick freezing in liquid nitrogen for 15 min and stored in dry ice for shipping to the company.</p>	<p>First 25 mg of tissue for each sample was weighed and added into thickened centrifuge tubes; simultaneously, 2 magnetic beads were also added. Next 10 μL of the prepared internal standard 1 was added to each sample at the same time. Then 800 μL of precooled extraction reagent (methanol:acetonitrile:water, 2:2:1, v/v/v) was added, then the mixture was ground at 50 Hz for 5 min. The ground samples were placed at -20 °C for 2 h. The samples were centrifuged (25,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min), then 600 μL of each sample was transferred into new EP tubes and freeze-dried. Next 600 μL of 50% methanol was added to the dried samples, which were shaken until completely dissolved. The samples were centrifuged again (25,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min), and the supernatant was transferred into a new EP tube. Then 10 μL of each sample was taken and mixed to prepare QC samples. The prepared supernatant was then processed for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p><strong>Reverse phase </strong></p><p>Analysis was performed using a Waters UPLC I-Class Plus. Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (1.7 μm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm, Waters, USA), and the column temperature was maintained at 45 °C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid (A) and acetonitrile (B) in the positive mode, and in the negative mode, the mobile phase consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate (A) and acetonitrile (B). The gradient conditions were as follows: 0.0-1.0 min, 2% B; 1.0-9.0 min, 2-98% B; 9.0-12.0 min, 98% B; 12.0-12.1 min, 98-2% B; and 12.1-15.0 min, 2% B. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Hilic</strong></p><p>Analysis was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class PLUS System. Chromatographic separation was performed on BEH Amide column (1.7 μm 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters, USA). At positive ion mode with mobile phase A consisting 95% acetonitrile water + 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid and mobile phase B consisting 50% acetonitrile in water + 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid. At positive ion mode, with mobile phase A consisting 95% acetonitrile water + 10 mM ammonium formate (adjust pH=9 with ammonia water) and mobile phase B consisting 50% acetonitrile water + 10 mM ammonium formate (adjust pH=9 with ammonia water). The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C. The gradient conditions were as follows: 2% B over 0.0-0.5 min, 2-50% B over 0.5-12.0 min, 50-98% B over 12.0-14.0 min, held constant at 98% B over 14.0-16.0 min and washed with 2% B over 16.1-18.0 min. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>Using Q Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) perform primary and secondary mass spectrometry data acquisition. The full scan range was <strong>70-1050 m/z</strong>, with a resolution of 70000, and the automatic gain control (AGC) target for MS acquisitions was set to 3e6 with a maximum ion injection time of 100 ms. Top 3 precursors were selected for subsequent MSMS fragmentation with a maximum ion injection time of 50 ms and resolution of 17500, the AGC was 1e5. The stepped normalized collision energy was set to 20, 40 and 60 eV. ESI parameters were setting as: Sheath gas flow rate was 40, Aux gas flow rate was 10, positive-ion mode, Spray voltage was 3.80 kV, negative-ion mode Spray voltage was 3.20 kV, Capillary temperature was 320 °C, Aux gas heater temperature was 350 °C.</p>	<p>The resulting file from Compound Discoverer was input into MetaX for data preprocessing and further analysis. Data preprocessing included: (1) normalisation of the data to obtain relative peak areas by Probabilistic Quotient Normalization (PQN), (2) using Quality Control-based robust LOESS signal correction to correct batch effects, and (3) removing metabolites with a coefficient of variation larger than 30% in their relative peak areas in QC samples.</p>	<p>After importing the offline mass spectrometry data into Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) software and analyzing the data in combination with the BMDB (BGI Metabolome Database), mzCloud database and ChemSpider online database, a data matrix containing information such as metabolite peak area and identification results was obtained. The table was then further analyzed and processed. Software Information: Compound Discoverer Version: v.3.3. Parameters: Parent ion mass deviation: &lt; 5 ppm, Mass deviation of fragment ions: &lt; 10 ppm, Retention time deviation: &lt; 0.2 min.</p>

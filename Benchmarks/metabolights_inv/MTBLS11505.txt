Study Identifier	MTBLS11505
Study Title	Absence of MCJ/DnaJC15 promotes brown adipose tissue thermogenesis
Study Description	Obesity poses a global health challenge, demanding a deeper understanding of adipose tissue (AT) and its mitochondria. This study describes the role of the mitochondrial protein Methylation-controlled J protein (MCJ/DnaJC15) in orchestrating brown adipose tissue (BAT) thermogenesis. Here we show how MCJ expression decreases during obesity, as evident in human and mouse adipose tissue samples. MCJKO mice, even without UCP1, a fundamental thermogenic protein, exhibit elevated BAT thermogenesis. Electron microscopy unveils changes in mitochondrial morphology resembling BAT activation. Proteomic analysis confirms these findings and suggests involvement of the eIF2α mediated stress response. The pivotal role of eIF2α is scrutinized by in vivo CRISPR deletion of eIF2α in MCJKO mice, abrogating thermogenesis. These findings uncover the importance of MCJ as a regulator of BAT thermogenesis, presenting it as a promising target for obesity therapy.

Study Protocol Description	<p>To collect a brown adipose tissue (BAT) sample from a mouse, euthanize the mouse according to approved protocols, make a midline incision to expose the thoracic region, and carefully dissect out the brown adipose tissue located between the shoulder blades (interscapular region), ensuring minimal contamination with surrounding tissues, before placing the sample in a pre-labeled collection tube for further analysis. BAT were snap frozen using a&nbsp;liquid nitrogen-cooled Wollenburg clamp. Tissues then were transferred&nbsp;into 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes.</p>	<p>For mice tissue, the tissue powder was re-suspended in 800 µL pre-cooled HPLC-grade 60:40 Methanol:Water and then vortexed for 15 min at 4 °C. Next 500 µL of pre-cooled LC-MS grade chloroform was added to the lysate and again vortexed for 15 min at 4 °C. Samples were then centrifuged at 16,000 x g for 10 min at 4 °C, creating three layers: the top layer containing polar metabolites, the middle layer containing protein and the bottom layer containing non-polar metabolites. The top layer was transferred into a new Eppendorf tube, dried down in a Refrigerated CentriVap Benchtop Vacuum Concentrator connected to a CentriVap-105 Cold Trap, and stored at -80 °C until they were re-suspended for LC-MS analysis. The non-polar layer was discarded, and the remaining protein layer was saved for protein quantification.</p>	<p>A <strong>Vanquish Horizon UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific)</strong> was used to perform LC-MS experiments. Dried samples were re-suspended in enough HPLC-grade water to have a final concentration of 1 ug protein per mL water. 2 uL of the re-suspended polar metabolite samples were injected onto a SeQuant ZIC-pHILIC (5μm; 2.1 mm x 150 mm) analytical column equipped with a ZIC-pHILIC Guard (5 µm, 2.1 mm x 20mm; Merck Sequant). The column oven was held at 25 °C and the autosampler tray was held at 4 °C. Buffer A was comprised of 20 mM ammonium carbonate, 0.1% ammonium hydroxide. Buffer B was comprised of 100% acetonitrile. The chromatographic gradient was run at a flow rate of 0.150 mL/min as follows: 0-20 min: linear gradient from 80-20% B; 20-20.5 min: linear gradient from 20-80% B; 20.5- 28 min: hold at 80% B.</p>	<p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Q Exactive Plus</strong> quadrupole orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with an Ion Max source and a HESI II probe was used to perform LC-MS experiments. Prior to operation, the instrument underwent mass calibration for positive and negative ion mode using Calmix (Thermo Fisher Scientific) every 7 d. The mass spectrometer was set to full scan (70-1000 m/z), with the spray voltage set to 4.0 kV, heated capillary to 350 °C and the HESI probe at 30 °C. The sheath gas flow was 10 units, the auxiliary gas flow was 2 units and the sweep gas flow was 1 unit.&nbsp;The sheath gas flow was set at 10 units, auxiliary gas at 1 units and sweep gas flow at 1 unit. The resolution of scan was set to 70,000, AGC target to 1 x 10^6, and maximum injection time at 20 ms. An additional scan between 220-700 m/z was used to enhance nucleotide detection in the negative mode as well with the maximum injection time set to 80 ms. Data acquired by Thermo Fisher's Xcalibur software.</p>	<p>The raw files provided contain data from both positive and negative ion mode. TraceFinder 5.1 (Thermo Fisher) was used for data analysis. Peaks were integrated using a strict 5 ppm mass tolerance and attention to the retention times as determined by purified standards of the respective metabolites</p>	<p>Data analyzed by Thermo Fisher's Tracefinder software. To identify metabolites in brown adipose tissue (BAT) samples using Thermo Fisher’s TraceFinder software, first acquire raw data by running the sample through a Thermo Fisher mass spectrometer connected to the software. Next, apply a pre-configured metabolite identification method in TraceFinder, which processes the data to match detected peaks with known metabolites in the compound library, enabling precise identification and quantification of metabolites present in the BAT sample.</p>

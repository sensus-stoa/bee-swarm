Study Title	Multi-omics Analysis Reveals New Insights into Yak Lung Fibrosis Mechanisms Under High-Altitude Adaptation
Study Description	<p>The lung of yak is an important adaptive organ in the cold and low oxygen environment. Hypoxia can induce the pathological manifestations of pulmonary fibrosis in yak, but there was no study on hypoxia induced pulmonary fibrosis in yak. In this study full target metabolomics were used to conduct studies on normal and fibrotic lung tissues of yaks. The results showed that Palmitoyl-L-carnitine, Decanoyl-L-carnitine and O-Acetylcarnitine were significantly highly expressed in fibrotic lung, Racemethionine and L-Methionine S-oxide were significantly lower expressed in fibrotic lungs. This study revealed the molecular mechanism of anti-fibrosis of yak lung, and these findings provided a scientific basis for formulating effective prevention and control strategies, contributing to safeguarding yak health and the sustainable development of plateau animal husbandry, and providing strong support for animal health management and disease prevention and control in plateau areas.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The lung samples of yaks were collected in February 2024 from Qingyuan Slaughterhouse, located in the Hui Autonomous Prefecture of Linxia, Gansu Province, China. The samples were taken from 12 5-year-old yaks, which were euthanized through intravenous injection of pentobarbital sodium (200 mg/kg). Sequenced samples of transcriptomics, proteomics and non-target metabolomics were collected, labeled and quickly placed in liquid nitrogen and stored in a -80 °C refrigerator for RNA and protein extraction. After quality inspection to confirm their compliance with sample submission requirements, mass spectrometry experiments and bioinformatics analyses were conducted.</p>	<p>For non-targeted metabolomics, metabolite extraction involves isolating and purifying the biochemical compounds present in the sample. This process typically involves using organic solvents and extraction techniques to effectively recover a wide range of metabolites for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>In non-targeted metabolomics, the chromatographic conditions are carefully optimized to ensure the separation and detection of a broad spectrum of metabolites with high resolution and sensitivity. These conditions include selecting appropriate mobile phases, column temperatures, and flow rates to achieve optimal peak separation and metabolite identification.</p><p><br></p><p>The LC analysis was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Chromatography was carried out with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm) (Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The column maintained at 40 °C. The flow rate and injection volume were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively. For LC-ESI (+)-MS analysis, the mobile phases consisted of (B2) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (v/v) and (A2) 0.1% formic acid in water (v/v). Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0.0-1.0 min, 8% B2; 1.0-8.0 min, 8-98% B2; 8.0-10.0 min, 98% B2; 10.0-10.1 min, 98-8% B2; 10.1-12.0 min, 8% B2. For LC-ESI (-)-MS analysis, the analytes was carried out with (B3) acetonitrile and (A3) ammonium formate (5 mM). Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0.0-1.0 min, 8% B3; 1.0-8.0 min, 8-98% B3; 8.0-10.0 min, 98% B3; 10.0-10.1 min, 98-8% B3; 10.1-12.0 min, 8% B3.</p>	<p>For non-targeted metabolomics, specific mass spectrometry conditions include the use of high-resolution mass spectrometry to achieve accurate mass measurement of metabolites, which aids in their identification. Additionally, the application of appropriate fragmentation patterns and collision-induced dissociation (CID) energy settings facilitates the structural elucidation of metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p>Mass spectrometric detection of metabolites was performed on Q Exactive Focus (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with ESI ion source. Simultaneous MS1 and MS/MS (Full MS-ddMS2 mode, data-dependent MS/MS) acquisition was used. The parameters were as follows: sheath gas pressure, 40 arb; aux gas flow, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV and -2.50 kV for ESI(+) and ESI(-), respectively; capillary temperature, 325 °C; MS1 range, 100-1000 m/z; MS1 resolving power, 70000 FWHM; number of data dependant scans per cycle, 3; MS/MS resolving power, 17500 FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30 eV; dynamic exclusion time, automatic.</p>	<p>The MSConvert tool in Proteowizard package (v3.0.8789) converts the original mass spectrometry file to mzXML file format. The RXCMS software package was used for peak detection, peak filtering and peak alignment. The parameters were set as bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5,30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, method = 'centWave', and the substance quantitative list was obtained. The method of total peak area normalization is adopted to realize data correction and eliminate systematic errors.</p>	<p>In non-targeted metabolomics, identification involves matching the mass spectral data of detected metabolites to known databases, such as those from metabolite libraries, to assign potential identities to the peaks observed. This process also includes the use of statistical methods and multivariate analysis to distinguish between biological noise and true metabolites, enhancing the reliability of the identifications.</p>

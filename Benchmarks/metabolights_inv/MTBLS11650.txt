Study Title	A human metabolome study for femoral neck osteoporosis in Kyoto
Study Description	<p>This dataset was generated to elucidate the metabolic features of femoral neck osteoporosis, a major risk factor for fractures and bedbound states in the elderly. Using whole-blood metabolomics, we analyzed the metabolic profiles of elderly individuals with and without&nbsp;femoral neck osteoporosis.&nbsp;This repository aims to support the development of future preventative and diagnostic approaches for osteoporosis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>As previously reported 1) , whole blood was collected once at the laboratory of Kyoto University Hospital. All participants were asked to fast overnight for at least twelve hours until blood sampling.</p><p><br></p><p>1)Chaleckis, R. et al. Proc Natl Acad Sci 2016</p>	<p>Because some metabolites are easily degraded, the blood samples were rapidly quenched at -40°C in methanol for quick sample processing.</p>	<p>LC separation was performed via a ZIC-pHILIC column. The mobile phase was composed of ammonium carbonate buffer (10 mM, pH 9.3) and acetonitrile. Gradient elution from 80 to 20% acetonitrile was performed over 30 min at a flow rate of 100 μL/min.&nbsp;</p>	<p>LC-MS data were obtained using an Ultimate 3000 DGP-3600RS liquid chromatograph (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) combined with an LTQ Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).&nbsp;</p>	<p>LC-MS data processing and analysis were conducted using MZmine 2 (version 2.29) software (mzmine.github.io) for peak area measurement and metabolite identification.</p>	<p>A total of 129 unbiased metabolites were evaluated for each sample by comparing the retention times and m/z values of the peaks with those of standards. If no standard compound was available, the metabolites were identified by analysis of the MS/MS spectra.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS1168
Study Title	Supplementation of Bacillus sp. DU-106 reduces hypercholesterolemia and ameliorates gut dysbiosis in high-fat diet rats
Study Description	<p>Gut microbiota modulation by a probiotic is a novel therapy for hypercholesterolemia mitigation. This study initially investigated the potential hypocholesterolemic effect of Bacillus sp. DU-106 in hypercholesterolemic rats and explored its potential relation with gut microbiota. Sprague-Dawley rats received a high-fat diet, or a high-fat diet supplemented with 7.5 × 10<sup>9</sup> and 1.5 × 10<sup>10</sup>&nbsp;CFU/kg bw/day Bacillus sp. DU-106 (low-dose and high-dose groups). At the end of 9&nbsp;weeks, Bacillus sp. DU-106 treatment significantly decreased the body weight, liver index, and total cholesterol. 16S rRNA sequencing showed that Bacillus sp. DU-106 intervention significantly increased bacterial richness and particularly increased the genus abundance of Turicibacter, Acinetobacter, Brevundimonas, and Bacillus and significantly decreased the abundance of Ralstonia. Metabolomic data further indicated that the supplementation of Bacillus sp. DU-106 remarkably changed the gut metabolic profiles of hypercholesterolemic rats and, in particular, elevated the metabolites of indole-3-acetate, methylsuccinic acid, creatine, glutamic acid, threonine, lysine, ascorbic acid, and pyridoxamine. Spearman's correlation analysis showed the close relation between the different genera and metabolites. In conclusion, Bacillus sp. DU-106 supplement ameliorated high-fat diet-induced hypercholesterolemia and showed potential probiotic benefits for the intestine.</p><p><strong>KEY POINTS:</strong> • A novel potential probiotic Bacillus sp. DU-106 ameliorated hypercholesterolemia in rats. • Bacillus sp. DU-106 supplement regulated gut microbiome structure and richness. • Bacillus sp. DU-106 supplement changed metabolic profiles in high-fat diet rats. • Significant correlations were observed between differential genera and metabolites.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p class='ql-align-justify'>32 male Sprague–Dawley rats were purchased from the Model Animal Research Center of Southern Medical University (Guangzhou, China), housed in a standard 12 h light/12 h dark cycle, and maintained at a constant temperature of 24 ± 1 °C and humidity of 50% ± 5%. The method of hypocholesterolemic animal experiments was based on previous studies<strong>[1]-[3]</strong>. The rats were weight matched and randomly divided into the control diet (CD) and hypercholesterolemic model building groups after 1 week of acclimatization with a standard chow diet (purchased from The Medical Laboratory Animal Center in Guangdong, China). The standard chow diet was continuously fed to the rats in the CD group, whereas an HFD consisting of 15% lard, 20% sucrose, 10% casein, 1.2% cholesterol, 0.2% sodium cholate, and 53.6% standard chow diet was given to the rats in the hypercholesterolemic model building group. The HFD-fed rats met the hypercholesterolemic criteria from the result (see <strong>Table S4</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Subsequently, the hypercholesterolemic rats were matched in TC concentration and randomly divided into three groups (n = 8 in each group): (1) HFD group (HFD, administered intragastrically at 10 mL/kg bw/day for saline), (2) LD group (HFD, administered intragastrically at 7.5 x 109 CFU/kg bw/day for <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 supplement), and (3) HD group (HFD, administered intragastrically at 1.5 × 1010 CFU/kg bw/day for <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 supplement). The <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 used was live <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 powdered by lyophilization and purchased from Guangdong Institute of Microbiology (Guangzhou, China) with accession number GDMCC 60621. The CFU of powdered <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 was 1012 CFU/g and counted just before the administration of this study. Different amounts of the powdered <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 were dissolved in an equivalent volume of distilled water to prepare a solution with different CFU concentrations. All <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 intervention groups received an equivalent volume of 10 mL/kg bw/day solution. The rats were housed in plastic cages (4 rats/cage) with free access to food and water, and <em>Bacillus </em>sp. DU-106 treatment lasted for 7 weeks. Body weight and food intake were recorded weekly. At the end of the experiment, all rats were allowed to fast overnight and then killed with sodium pentothal. Colonic contents and blood samples were collected sterilely from each rat and immediately stored at −80 °C. The liver samples were immediately dissected and weighted. Liver index was calculated by liver weight/body weight (mg/g).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Collins KH, Paul HA, Hart DA, Reimer RA, Smith IC, Rios JL, Seerattan RA, Herzog W. PMID:29152632</p>	<p class='ql-align-justify'>Approximately 0.1&nbsp;g of freeze-dried colonic contents, 400&nbsp;μL of ice-cold methanol, and 0.1% formic acid mixture were added and homogenized on ice and vortex-mixed for 2&nbsp;min, and then incubated on ice for 5&nbsp;min. Subsequently, the samples were centrifuged at 15000&nbsp;rpm at 4&nbsp;°C for 5&nbsp;min, and the supernatant was added to liquid chromatograph-mass spectrometer (LC-MS) grade water until the final concentration containing 60% methanol. Finally, the samples were injected into the LC-MS/MS system analysis after passing through a 0.22-μm filter. QC samples are equal volume mixing samples of experimental samples to control the quality of the experiment. Blank samples are set up to remove background ions.</p>	<p class='ql-align-justify'>The samples were analyzed by using a vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The chromatographic separation was performed with a 1.9-μm C18 (100&nbsp;mm × 2.1&nbsp;mm) Hyperil Gold column. During the liquid chromatography, 3&nbsp;μL of the sample solution was injected onto the chromatographic separation column using a 16-min linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.2&nbsp;mL/min and maintained at 40&nbsp;°C. The mobile phases for the positive polarity mode comprised of 0.1% formic acid in water (vol:vol; eluent A) and methanol (vol:vol; eluent B). The mobile phases for the negative polarity mode comprised of 5&nbsp;mM ammonium acetate (pH&nbsp;9.0, vol:vol; eluent A) and methanol (vol:vol; eluent B; Sigma-Aldrich). The following gradient profile was used: 0–1.5&nbsp;min, 2% B; 1.5–12&nbsp;min, 100% B; 12–14&nbsp;min, 100% B; 14–14.1&nbsp;min, 2% B; and 14.1–16.0&nbsp;min, 2% B.&nbsp;</p>	<p class='ql-align-justify'>For mass spectrometry acquisition, data-dependent MS/MS scans were chosen and the instrument was set to acquire ions over the m/z range 100–1500. Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer conditions were set as follows: positive/negative polarity mode with spray voltage of 3.2&nbsp;kV, capillary temperature of 320&nbsp;°C, sheath gas flow rate of 35&nbsp;arb, and aux gas flow rate of 10&nbsp;arb.</p>	<p class='ql-align-justify'>The peak alignment, peak picking, and quantitation for each metabolite of raw UHPLC-MS/MS data were performed by Compound Discoverer 3.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The Compound Discoverer 3.0 conditions were set as follows: minimum intensity, 100,000; signal intensity tolerance, 30%; signal/noise ratio, 3; actual mass tolerance, 5&nbsp;ppm; and retention time tolerance, 0.2&nbsp;min. Subsequently, the peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity and generate normalized data. The normalized data was used to predict the molecular formula based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks, and fragment ions.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Peaks were then matched with the mzCloud <a href='https://www.mzcloud.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org</a> and ChemSpider <a href='http://www.chemspider.com' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.chemspider.com</a> database to receive accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results.&nbsp;</p>

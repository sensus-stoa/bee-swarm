Study Identifier	MTBLS11713
Study Title	Valvular lipidomic changes in progression of human calcific aortic valve stenosis
Study Description	This study investigates lipid dysregulation in calcific aortic valve stenosis (CAVS) by analyzing lipid profiles in valve tissues from 99 patients across different disease severities (mild, moderate, severe) using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS). The results reveal distinct lipid alterations that correspond to disease progression, with significant correlations between diacylglycerol (DG) and alkyl lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC(O)) levels and key markers of valve stiffness, such as peak aortic jet velocity and mean pressure gradient. Unique lipid profiles, marked by elevated cholesteryl esters and triacylglycerols, were identified in fast-progressing cases. Additionally, plasma lipids TG 52:4 and TG 54:4 emerged as promising non-invasive diagnostic markers, surpassing traditional markers in diagnostic performance. This study highlights the potential of specific lipidomic pathways as therapeutic targets in CAVS, given the current lack of effective pharmacological treatments.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Valve Tissue Cohort</strong></p><p>In total 99 aortic valve leaflets were obtained from patients having aortic valve replacement surgery at St. Boniface Hospital in Manitoba, Canada, during the years 2014 and 2015. In a subset of 17 patients, venous blood samples (EDTA-plasma) were also collected prior to surgery, before aesthetic infusion.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Plasma Cohort</strong></p><p>Venous blood samples were obtained from 58 patients diagnosed with severe aortic stenosis who underwent transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) at St. Boniface Hospital between 2015 and 2017. TAVR was offered to patients with a life expectancy of at least 1 year as well as those deemed 'inoperable.' Samples were taken before the procedure. Additionally, blood samples were collected from 49 healthy individuals with 2D echocardiography showing no evidence of aortic stenosis and confirmed on invasive haemodynamics during diagnostic coronary angiography. The plasma was stored at -80 °C after being separated by centrifugation (2500 x g, 5 min, 4 °C). The mean duration between collection and plasma isolation was around 20 min.</p>	<p>The entire explanted valve was cryo-milled into a fine powder and subsequently homogenised with the Bead Ruptor 24 (ParkinElmer Co.) in Tyrode’s buffer (pH 7.6) based on its weight. In total 10 µL of the tissue homogenate or blood plasma were added to a microcentrifuge tube (Eppendorf). Then, 200 µL of chloroform/methanol (2:1, v/v) and 30 µL of lipid internal standards (ISTD) in chloroform/methanol (1:1, v/v) combination were added. After 10 min of vortexing on a rotary mixer, the mixture was sonicated for 30 min at room temperature in a water bath. After letting the mixture settle for 20 min, it was spun at 20,000 x g for 20 min at room temperature. The lipid-rich top phase was moved to a fresh microcentrifuge tube and allowed to evaporate at room temperature while exposed to nitrogen gas. The lipids were subsequently reconstituted in 50 µL of water-saturated 1-butanol and subjected to sonication for 10 min. The lipid extract was mixed with 50 µL of 10 mM ammonium formate in methanol. It was then spun at 10,000 x g for 10 min at room temperature. For lipid analysis, 80 µL of the supernatant was finally aliquoted into a glass insert with Teflon cap.</p>	<p>Lipidomic analysis was carried out by liquid chromatography electrospray ionisation tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS). A Zorbax C18 column (1.8 μm, 50 x 2.1 mm; Agilent Technologies, Mississauga, ON) was used in positive ion mode in conjunction with a Prominence chromatographic system (Shimadzu Corporation, OR, USA). Instrument control and data processing were done with Analyst v1.6 and Multi-Quant v2.1 software (AB Sciex, MA, USA). The separation was achieved on a Zorbax C18, 1.8&nbsp;μm, 50 x 2.1&nbsp;mm column (Agilent Technologies, Mississauga, ON). The flow rate was set to 300&nbsp;μL/min using a linear gradient of mobile phase A and mobile phase B. Mobile phase A and B consisted of tetrahydrofuran, methanol and water in the ratio 20:20:60 (v/v/v) and 75:20:5 (v/v/v), respectively. Both A and B contained 10&nbsp;mM ammonium formate. Diacylglycerol (DG) and triacylglycerol (TG) species were separated in an isocratic fashion (100&nbsp;μL/min) by employing 85% mobile phase B over 8&nbsp;min. The analytical column and autosampler were maintained at 50 and 25 °C, respectively, during the analysis. The injection volume was 5&nbsp;μL.</p>	<p>Lipids eluted from the HPLC system were introduced into the AbSciex 4000 QTRAP triple quadrupole linear hybrid mass spectrometer (ESI positive), which operated in scheduled Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode. Our previous publication provides more details on MRM transitions and MS parameters for Diacylglycerol (DG) and triacylglycerol (TG) species.</p><p>DOI:&nbsp;10.1016/j.jacbts.2020.10.001</p>	<p>Lipid levels were initially normalized to the wet tissue weight. We used a pooled quality control (PQC)-based normalisation technique called systematic error removal using random forest (SERRF) to address batch-to-batch fluctuations. Following batch alignment, lipids were considered reproducible if their coefficient of variation among PQC samples was less than 20% and their maximum average was not present in the blank extracts. The lipid data were first sum-normalized and then log-transformed.</p>	<p>Detailed methodology, including the MRM transitions, internal standards used, mass spectral conditions and chromatographic gradient, were provided in our previous publication. This assay is for the species of Diacylglycerol (DG) and triacylglycerol (TG).</p><p>https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.103828</p>

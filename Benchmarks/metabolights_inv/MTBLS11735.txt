Study Identifier	MTBLS11735
Study Title	Clustering of chemical profiles of Centella asiatica cultivars, grown in greenhouses, allows grouping of metabolites with similar production trends
Study Description	"Centella asiatica (L.) Urban (also known as """"gotu kola"""") is a perennial plant, used in traditional medicine for promoting resilience to central nervous system (CNS) disorders. C. asiatica is a tropical medicinal herb from the Apiaceae family and is native to Southeast Asian countries. The chemical composition and contaminant profile of commercial C. asiatica is variable. The goal of this study was to guide the future cultivation of organically grown C. asiatica for obtaining optimized plant materials for pre-clinical studies and clinical trials. Optimized plant materials in this case are defined as producing similar amounts of biologically active components as previously studied material. In this study, C. asiatica cultivars were grown in Central Oregon and their phytochemical compositions were examined. Four different cultivars were grown in climate-controlled greenhouses over three different vegetative propagation periods. Aerial parts of the plant were collected at four different harvest times: 8, 10, 12, and 14 weeks from growth initiation. The phytochemical composition of each cultivar was analyzed by liquid chromatography high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS/MS). Global metabolomic profiles allowed cultivar-specific compositional differences to be distinguished and production trends of phytochemical constituents to be analyzed using multinomial Bayesian hierarchical clustering and Self-Organizing Maps. Production trends of known bioactive phytoconstituents are reported here and will inform cultivation and harvest strategies to obtain C. asiatica materials of desired composition for preclinical and clinical studies. The computational methods for analyzing cultivar-specific and time-course dependent metabolomic profiles can be applied to other medicinal plant cultivation efforts to optimize cultivation and harvest practices."

Study Protocol Description	Cuttings of four Centella asiatica cultivars (CA-9 Mountain Valley, CA-10 Hawaii, CA-11 White Cloud, CA-12 9EZ) from four commercial sources were grown in climate-controlled greenhouses at Oregon's Wild Harvest, Bend, OR, USA, in three vegetative propagation periods (groups 1-3, planted between March and September 2021). Aerial parts were harvested 8, 10, 12 and 14 weeks after planting, with three biological replicates (a, b, c) per harvest, then dried and stored at -20 C until analysis. CA-6, a commercial C. asiatica of mixed origin (Organic India batch UFU0070, via Oregon's Wild Harvest batch X090016), was used as reference material. Sample names follow group-CA-cultivar-week replicate-extracted mass (mg), e.g. 1-CA-9-8a-9.0. Voucher samples (BEN-CA-6, 9, 10, 11, 12) are kept at the BENFRA laboratories, Oregon Health & Science University. 2-CA-9-8b+c and 2-CA-9-8c+b record a pipetting mix-up between replicates b and c. 2-CA-11-12b was extracted twice (8.6 and 9.8 mg); one was excluded from the published analysis (143 samples), and both are deposited.	Dried plant material was ground to a fine powder in a blade grinder and suspended at 0.5 mg/mL and at 0.05 mg/mL in 70% aqueous methanol (v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid and 1 ug/mL digoxin-d3 internal standard. For each cultivar a pool was made from all harvest-week samples of the three propagation groups (CA-9-Pool, CA-10-Pool, CA-11-Pool, CA-12-Pool), and a full pool from the four cultivar pools plus CA-6, at both concentrations; pools were used for quality control, normalization and between-cultivar comparison. A calibration series of 12 authentic standards (caffeoylquinic acids and triterpenes: 3-, 4- and 5-CQA; 1,3-, 1,5-, 3,4-, 3,5- and 4,5-DiCQA; madecassoside, asiaticoside, madecassic acid, asiatic acid; Cayman Chemical) was prepared at 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000, 2500, 5000 and 10000 ng/mL in the same solvent with 1 ug/mL digoxin-d3. Solvent blanks are the extraction solvent.	Shimadzu Nexera UHPLC with an Inertsil Phenyl-3 column (GL Sciences) at 55 C, flow 0.8 mL/min, 3 uL injection. Solvent A water + 0.1% formic acid, solvent B methanol + 0.1% formic acid. Gradient: 0-0.1 min 2% B; 0.1-2.5 min 2-15% B; 2.5-3.0 min 15-25% B; 3.0-5.0 min 25-35% B; 5.0-6.0 min 35-100% B; 6.0-7.0 min 100% B; return to 2% B from 7.1-8.1 min.	AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600 with a Turbo V source, electrospray ionization in negative mode, untargeted data-dependent acquisition (DDA). Desolvation gas temperature 550 C. MS1 and MS/MS scan range m/z 70-1300. Samples were run in August 2022 in six batches (one folder per batch under FILES/RAW_FILES), separately for the 0.5 mg/mL (Point5) and 0.05 mg/mL (Point05) extracts, with pools, standards and blanks in each.	Raw .wiff/.wiff.scan files were converted to centroided, zlib-compressed mzML (FILES/DERIVED_FILES) with ProteoWizard msconvert (vendor peak picking). For the published analysis, a feature table of retention times and abundances was built from the 0.5 and 0.05 mg/mL data in Progenesis QI. Peak areas of the 12 markers, digoxin-d3 and 82 accurate-mass features were integrated in MultiQuant (Sciex), and normalized to the replicate pool samples by support vector regression (MetNormalizer). Multivariate analysis (PCA, SVD, ICA, UMAP) and time-course clustering (multinomial Bayesian hierarchical clustering, self-organizing maps) were done in R.	The 12 marker compounds were identified against authentic standards (retention time, accurate mass, MS/MS). A further 82 features were tentatively identified by accurate mass only, from compounds previously reported in C. asiatica (Alcazar Magana et al. 2020). A feature-based molecular network of the 0.05 mg/mL pool samples is public on GNPS (task 1786994c61db4463a93f7c6ddc113d21).

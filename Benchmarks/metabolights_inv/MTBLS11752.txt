Study Title	Deciphering the Role of N-Acetyltransferase 10 in Thalamic Hemorrhage Through Integrative Multi-Omics and Experimental Validation
Study Description	<p>Thalamic hemorrhage (TH) is a severe neurological condition, the molecular mechanisms of which are poorly understood, particularly in clinical settings. N-acetyltransferase 10 (NAT10), a regulator of RNA N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) modification, has been implicated in cell cycle regulation and identified as a potential therapeutic target. This study explored the effects of NAT10 inhibition on TH pathology using a multi-omics approach. A mouse model of TH was established via collagenase IV injection. NAT10 activity was detected by dot blot and inhibited using Remodelin. Comprehensive multi-omics analyses, including 16S ribosomal Deoxyribonucleic Acid (16S rDNA) sequencing, metabolomics, and transcriptomics, were used. Behavioral, histological, and molecular evaluations were conducted to evaluate the key genes.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>On day 21 post-intervention, mouse whole blood was collected via retro-orbital puncture. After standing for 2 hours, the blood was centrifuged at 3000 rpm and 4°C for 30 minutes, and the supernatant was collected for subsequent metabolomic sequencing. The time points were selected with reference to PMID: 34995755. The samples were extracted from 20 μL of sample using 120 μL of precooled 50% methanol buffer.</p>	<p>Briefly, 20μL sample was extracted with 120μL precooled 50% methanol, vortexed for 1min, and incubated at room temperature for 10min. The extraction mixture was then stored at -20 °C overnight. After centrifugation at 4000g for 20min, the supernatant was transferred to a new 96-well plate. Each sample was mixed with an equal amount of 10ul diluent to form a Quality&nbsp;Control (QC)&nbsp;sample. The samples were stored at -80 °C before Liquid Chromatograph Mass Spectrometer&nbsp;(LC-MS)&nbsp;analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separations were performed using an ultra performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system (SCIEX, UK). Samples were separated and analyzed with a ACQUITY UPLC T3 column (100mm*2.1mm, 1.8µm, Waters, UK) coupled to a Bruker TIMS-TOF (TripleTOF 5600+, Bruker, Bremen, Germany). Chromatographic separations were performed at 35 °C. The flow rate was 0.4ml/min and the mobile phase consisted of solvent A (water, 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (Acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid). Gradient elution conditions were set as follows: 0-0.5 min, 5% B; 0.5-7min, 5% to 100%B; 7-8min, 100%B; 8-8.1min, 100% to 5%B; 8.1-10min, 5%B. The injection volume for each sample was 4µl.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed by TripleTOF5600plus (SCIEX, UK). Each sample was collected once in positive ion mode and once in negative ion mode. The shielding pressure of the ion source was 30PSI(pounds per square inch), both gas 1 (auxiliary gas) and gas 2 (sheath gas) pressures were set to 60PSI, the source temperature was 650 degrees, and the voltage was positive 5000 volts in positive ion mode and -4500 volts in negative ion mode.The data collection mode was IDA (Information dependent acquisition) mode. During the acquisition process, the instrument accuracy was corrected at intervals of 20 samples. At the same time, QC samples were scanned every 10 samples. The quality gap between QC samples was used to correct for systematic errors in batch experiments.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS files were converted to .mzML with MSConvertGUI from the ProteoWizard software suite.</p>	<p>Peaks were classified and tentatively annotated with SIRIUS, ZODIAC, CSI:FingerID, and CANOPUS using ClassyFire and NPClassifier based on MS2 fragmentation patterns using SIRIUS software.&nbsp;</p>

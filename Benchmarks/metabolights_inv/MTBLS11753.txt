Study Identifier	MTBLS11753
Study Title	Trapped ion mobilty improves annotation accuracy in LC-HRMS screening applications for exposomics
Study Description	<p>The widespread use of a large number of chemicals results in a significant exposure of humans and the environment, posing potential risks to human health and ecosystems. To assess the exposure to chemical mixtures, screening applications covering a large number of analytes based on liquid chromatography coupled to high-resolution mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS) are required. False positives occur frequently in complex environmental and biological matrices due to the interference of ions with the same or a close m/z, stemming from isomeric and isobaric compounds, chemical noise, in-source fragmentation, or adduct formation. Besides that, interferences may also result in false negatives, especially when only trace levels are present. Due to an insufficient separation in the LC dimension, MS2 spectra may contain fragments from other analytes within the precursor isolation window, leading to decreased spectral matching values. In recent years, ion mobility separation (IM), and later on trapped ion mobility mass spectrometry (TIMS) was introduced as an additional separation dimension in HRMS. We developed a IM-target screening method and evaluated for a set of more than 700 chemicals of interest if the TIMS separation allowed for a separation of interfering ions as compared to the classical LC-HRMS method in different matrices. </p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The sample set contains a pooled urine sample (N=25), a commercially purchased sheep serum, a breastmilk sample, a 24-h composite sample a wastewater influent and a dust sample.</p><p><br></p>	<p><strong>Pooled urine:</strong> A pooled urine sample (N=25) was sampled among the laboratory participants and was prepared using a similar sample protocol as in a previous large-scale cohort studies. In brief, 500&nbsp;µL of pooled urine were diluted in 500&nbsp;µL of a phosphate buffer solution (pH 6.8) and loaded on HLB solid phase cardriges (30&nbsp;mg, 30&nbsp;µm particle seize, CHROMABOND® Machery Nagel, preconditioned with 500&nbsp;µL of MeOH and equilibrated with 500&nbsp;µL of H2O), washed with 200&nbsp;µL of H2O and extracted with 1&nbsp;mL of ACN:H2O (90:10 v/v). The eluate was evaporated under room temperature using 10&nbsp;µL of DMSO as a keeper and reconstituted in 100&nbsp;µL of H2O and 5&nbsp;µL of IS mixture in MeOH (10&nbsp;µg/mL).</p><p><strong>Sheep serum:</strong> Protein precipitation was performed as simple sample preparation step. In brief, 200&nbsp;µL of serum were combined with 800&nbsp;µL of ACN in a filtration vial (GVS Filter Technology, PFTE membrane, 0.2&nbsp;µm) and left for 1&nbsp;h at -20°C prior to filtration. The filtrate was evaporated under a gentle nitrogen stream (heated at 40°C) until near dryness and reconstituted with 100&nbsp;µL of H2O/ACN 90:10 (v/v) and 5&nbsp;µL of IS mixture (10&nbsp;µg/mL).</p><p><strong>Breastmilk sample:</strong> In brief, an aliquot of 400µL of breastmilk was added to 1.6mL of ACN, vortexed for 1&nbsp;min, and centrifugend for 10&nbsp;min at 3500&nbsp;rpm. The supernatant was loaded on Captiva EMR-Lipid cartridges (60&nbsp;mg, Agilent, preconditioned with 10&nbsp;mL of 80:20 ACN/H2O v/v) and eluted. The eluate was concentrated under a gentle nitrogen stream (heated at 35°C) until near dryness and reconstituted with 50&nbsp;µL of MeOH/H2O 70:30 (v/v) and 2&nbsp;µL of IS mixture (10&nbsp;µg/mL) in MeOH were added.</p><p><strong>Wastewater influent:</strong> A 24-h composite sample of a exemplary influent of a waste-water treatment plant was extracted based on a in-house method. In brief, after filtration through Whatmann GF/F microfiber filter (0.55&nbsp;µm, 135&nbsp;mm diameter), the 50&nbsp;µL of the filtrate was loaded on HRX cardriges (CHROMABOND®, 85&nbsp;µm, 200&nbsp;mg, Machery Nagel, preconditioned with 5&nbsp;mL of each, ethyl acetate and methanol, and 10&nbsp;mL of H2O) and washed with 4&nbsp;mL of H2O. The sample matrix was eluted using each 2&nbsp;mL of ethyl acetate and methanol. The eluent was evaporated under a gentle nitrogen stream until dryness and reconstituted in 500&nbsp;µL methanol. Before analysis, 100&nbsp;µL of the reconstituted sample were added to 96&nbsp;µL of LC-grade water and 5&nbsp;µL of IS mixture in MeOH (5&nbsp;µg/mL).</p><p><strong>Dust sample:</strong> In brief, 20&nbsp;mg of dust (&lt;100&nbsp;µm particle size) were two times extracted with 2.5&nbsp;mL of Hexane/Acetone (v/v) in a ultrasonic bath for 5&nbsp;min. The supernatant was evaporated under a gentle nitrogen stream (heated at 35°C) until near dryness and reconstituted in 140 µL of MeOH and 30&nbsp;µL of H2O and 50&nbsp;µL of IS mixture (1&nbsp;µg/mL).</p>	<p>The applied LC method was based on methods applied in previous Exposomics studies using a C18 column (2.1&nbsp;mm × 100&nbsp;mm, 1.7&nbsp;µm particle size, Waters Acquity BEH C18) at 50°C and a flow rate of 0.3&nbsp;mL/min. For positive mode, a gradient elution with water/methanol (both containing 0.1% of formic acid and 2&nbsp;mM ammonium formate) was used with 100:0 at 0&nbsp;min, 0:100 at 15&nbsp;min, 0:100 at 21&nbsp;min, 100:0 at 22&nbsp;min, and 100:0 at 30&nbsp;min (v/v). For negative mode analysis, a gradient elution of water and water/methanol (90:10 v/v), both containing 10&nbsp;mM ammonium bicarbonate was used with the same gradient program as in employed in positive mode. The injection volume was set to 5&nbsp;µL for all samples.</p>	<p>The Bruker timsTOF Pro 2 measurements were performed in positive and negative ionization using a VIP-HESI source. The TIMS cardrige tunnel pressure was set to 2.64&nbsp;mbar/0.768&nbsp;mbar (in/out) by the instrument. The inverse reduced mobility (1/K0) values were measured over a range of 0.45-1.45&nbsp;V*s/cm^2. The ion charge control (ICC) was activated, to reduce TIMS tunnel saturation and space charge effects, with a limit of 7.5e6. For TOF analyses, a scan range of 75-1250&nbsp;m/z was used. For calibration purposes, before each analysis, through a valve with a 20&nbsp;µL sample loop containing a 1:1 mixture of Agilent ESI-L Low Concentration Tune Mix and sodium-formate buffer solution (10&nbsp;mM) was injected. Data-dependent MS2 were acquired with Parallel Accumulation Serial Fragmentation (PASEF) with a cycle time of 0.53&nbsp;s for 2 ramps. The isolation window was set to 1&nbsp;m/z, employing a combined collision energy of 20 and 50&nbsp;eV. Precursor selection was performed with an active exclusion for 0.1&nbsp;min.</p>	<p>No data transformation step was needed for data analysis.</p>	<p>Manual evaluation and extraction of target compounds was performed in TasQ (Bruker, Bremen, Germany), version 2024b. The data processing steps included automatic mass and ion mobility recalibration, generation of extracted ion chromatograms (EICs), and extracted ion mobilograms (EIMs) and CCS(N2) calculation based on the measured 1/K0 values.</p>

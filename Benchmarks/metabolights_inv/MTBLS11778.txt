Study Identifier	MTBLS11778
Study Title	Intracellular iron overload rewires HSC metabolism by impairing mitochondrial fitness
Study Description	Iron is an essential element for several cellular processes and recent evidence highlighted its role in regulating the function of hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). When in excess it reduces quiescence and self-renewal, however, whether and how iron influences HSC metabolism is still unknown. Here, we show that intracellular iron overload (IO) impairs mitochondrial fitness and bioenergetics, inducing metabolic rewiring in HSCs. In three models of IO, HSCs accumulate mitochondria with elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS), low membrane potential and reduced oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS). IO HSCs proliferate despite low mitochondrial activity and OXPHOS and rely on glycolysis for energy production. Notably, restoration of mitochondrial function by targeting in vivo iron and mitochondrial ROS improved the quiescence and self-renewal of IO HSCs. Our results unravel the critical interplay between iron, ROS and mitochondria in HSCs, disclosing that iron acts as an extrinsic regulator of HSC metabolic programs.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Femurs, tibiae, pelvis, humeri and spine were isolated from female wt and th3 mice, cleaned from muscle tissue and placed in a pre-chilled mortar containing 5 mL of ice-cold MACS buffer. Bones were crushed using a pestle and cell clumps were broken by pipetting, then cell suspension was transferred to a 50 mL conical tube with 40 μm cell strainer, placed in an ice bucket. Bones were washed twice until fragments are completely white. Cell suspension was centrifuged for 5 min at 350 x g at 4 °C and Lin- cells were isolated by mouse Direct Lineage Cell Depletion Kit (Milteny Biotech, Cat 130-110-470). Cells were stained with anti-Lineage, cKit and Sca1 antibodies for 15 min at 4 °C, washed, resuspended in MACS buffer containing 7-AAD at a 1/200 dilution, and transferred to a 5 mL flow tube with cell strainer cap. Cells were immediately sorted as Lin- Sca1+ cKit+ (LSK) cells, centrifuged, washed with DPBS, resuspended in ice-cold Methanol and frozen at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Pelleted cells were lysed by adding 100 μL ice-cold 80% methanol in water and polar metabolites were extracted using a methanol:chloroform phase separation (1 mL methanol, 500 μL water and 1 mL chloroform). The samples were mixed on shaker at 4 °C for 15 min and centrifuged at 5000 x g 15 min at 4 °C. The aqueous phase was recovered and dried under nitrogen flow and resuspended in 50% acetonitrile in water, with a volume scaled to the extracted cell number (i.e., 28 μL was used for the lowest cell number samples and was scaled for each sample). Then 12 μL of each sample was transferred to a glass micro-insert and the rest was pooled to make the necessary pool sample for MS2 data acquisition.</p>	<p>A Vanquish chromatographic system (Thermo Electron North America, Madison, WI, USA) was used. Separation was performed on a ZIC-pHILIC peek-coated column (150 mm x 2.1 mm, 5 μm particles, maintained at 40 °C, Sigma-Aldrich). The flow rate was 0.15 mL/min, except for the first 30 s where it was ramped from 0.05 to 0.15mL/min. Mobile phases were 20 mM ammonium carbonate in water with 0.1% ammonium hydroxide for A and acetonitrile 97% in water for B. The gradient consisted of an isocratic step of 0.5 min at 93% B, then to 40% B in 18.5 min, then to 0% B in 9 min, followed by an isocratic step at 0% B for 5 min and back to 93% B in 3 min. The column was then re-equilibrated at 93% B for 9 min.</p>	<p>The liquid chromatographic system was coupled to a ID-X tribrid Mass spectrometer (Thermo Electron North America, Madison, WI, USA) fitted with an heated electrospray source (HESI). Samples were analyzed in MS1 only. The Pool samples were analyzed in MS2 in positive and negative mode separately, using the AquireX deepscan mode.</p><ul><li>For MS1 only data acquisition: Data was acquired in switching polarity mode, with a mass resolution of 120,000. The mass accuracy was within a 3 ppm range. The RF lens was set at 30% and data was acquired in the range 65-1000 m/z. Internal calibration was used.&nbsp;</li><li>For MS2 data acquisition in positive mode:</li></ul><p>Data was acquired in data dependent mode in positive mode only.</p><p>The MS1 scan used the same settings as the MS1 only acquisition.</p><p>First a solvent blank and an injection of the pool were run, acquiring only MS1 data. All peaks found in the blank were added to an exclusion list. All peaks found in the Pool sample were added to an inclusion list.</p><p>The pool sample was then reinjected and analyzed in MS2 mode, using the inclusion and exclusion lists to select the ions to fragment.</p><p>Data dependent MS2 was acquired with a 1.5 m/z isolation window, HCD stepped collision (15, 35, 55) and analyzing the resulting fragments in the orbitrap with a 30,000 resolution.</p><p>All peaks that were fragmented in the run were then added to the exclusion list and removed from the inclusion list. The sample was reinjected a total of 4 times to achieve as much MS2 coverage as possible.</p><ul><li>For MS2 data acquisition in negative mode: Data was acquired the same way as MS2 positive mode, but in negative mode.</li></ul>	<p>Data was analyzed in compound discoverer (For human AML PDX 5+3 and MLL-AF9 AML VenAza samples: version 3.3. For MLL-AF9 AML samples: version 2.0) (Thermofisher Scientific). For each sample, peaks were detected, de-adducted and polarity were combined. MS2 data from the pool samples were associated with the corresponding peaks. If a compound in samples was always less than 5 fold the average intensity in the blanks, it was removed from the results. Data was gap filled by reintegrating peaks or local noise for samples where a peak was not original detected. Intensities were median centered by sample.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>For all files, msConvert (ProteoWizard) was used to generate the mzML files, with peak picking selected to centroid the data.</p>	<p>Compounds were identified in Compound Discoverer (see data transformation).</p><p>All identifications were manually inspected. Basic stereochemistry was removed from most identification from the databases results.</p><p>Level 1: Identification was based first on an internal mzVault library with matching retention time created using authentic standards.</p><p>Level 2: Identification was based secondly on matches (based on MS trees) with the online mzCloud database.</p><p>Level 3: No good identification was obtained by library match, however some information was obtained from the MS2, or there was a retention time match with a standard without MS2 match. The name contains the description.</p><p>Level 4: For the other compounds, compound discoverer calculated the formulae based on the observed m/z within 3 ppm. Formulae were ranked based on ppm, distribution of the natural isotopes and fragments if available. The top formula was assigned then automatically based on a combined score.</p><p>Level 5: This was assigned to compounds where no formula could be calculated.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS11819
Study Title	Butyrate ameliorate doxorubicin-induced heart failure via microbiota/ metabolites-gut-Heart axis
Study Description	Doxorubicin(DOX) is widely used in cancer chemotherapy with a limitation of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity(DIC),resulting in heart failure(HF).HF accompanies by gut microbial dysbiosis.However,how gut microbiota regulate DIC-induced heart failure (DIHF)remains unclear.Here, we established a susceptibility model of DIHF,confirming that gut microbiota play a critical role in regulating DIHF. In the DIHF-high susceptibility group the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria was significantly reduced, fecal and serum butyrate level was lowered, and lipid metabolism was disordered,compared with control group. Sodium butyrate(NaB) reduces DOX-induced cardiomyocyte toxicity in vitro.Orally supplemental NaB increases the biodiversity of gut microbiota,optimizes the composition of gut microbiota, reduces serum total cholesterol level, thereby ameliorates HF in vivo. Mechanistically,supplemental NaB increases the concentration of serum and cardiac butyrate, enhances the barrier function of colonic and cardiac tissues, reduces gut microbiota translocation to heart and LPS level,results in significant differences in the microbiota composition in cardiac tissue, promotes the polarization of M1 macrophages to M2, increases the level of anti-inflammatory factors, and decreases the level of pro-inflammatory factors. NaB reduces ferroptosis of DOX-induced cardiomyocyte via GPX4/GSH pathway. Collectively,these findings demonstrate that Butyrate ameliorates DIHF via microbiota/metabolites-gut-heart axis,offering a potential prevention and therapeutic strategy for DIHF.

Study Protocol Description	<p>SD rat, DOX 3mg/kg weekly 5 times ,1% sodium butyrate in drinking wate , feces were collected from the DIHF model,stored in -80°C.</p>	<p>The collected samples were thawed on ice, and metabolite were extracted with 80% methanol </p><p>Buffer. Briefly, 50 mg of sample was extracted with 0.5 ml of precooled 80% methanol. The </p><p>extraction mixture was then stored in 30 min at -20°C. After centrifugation at 20,000 g for 15 min, </p><p>the supernatants were transferred into new tube to and vacuum dried. The samples were </p><p>redissolved with 100μL 80% methanol and stored at -80°C prior to the LC-MS analysis. In addition, </p><p>pooled QC samples were also prepared by combining 10 μL of each extraction mixture</p>	<p>All samples were acquired by the LC-MS system followed machine orders. Firstly, all</p><p>chromatographic separations were performed using an ACQUITY UPLC system (Waters, Milford,</p><p>MA, USA). An ACQUITY UPLC T3 column (100mm*2.1mm, 1.8µm, Waters, Milford, USA) was used</p><p>for the reversed phase separation. The column oven was maintained at 40°C. The flow rate was 0.3</p><p>ml/min and the mobile phase consisted of solvent A (water, 5mM ammonium acetate and 5mM</p><p>acetic acid) and solvent B (Acetonitrile). Gradient elution conditions were set as follows: 0-0.8</p><p>min, 2% B; 0.8-2.8 min, 2% to 70% B; 2.8-5.6 min, 70% to 90% B; 5.6-6.4 min, 90% to 100% B; </p><p>6.4-8.0 min, 100% B; 8.0-8.1 min, 100% to 2% B; 8.1-10 min, 2%B.</p>	<p>A high-resolution tandem mass spectrometer TripleTOF 6600 (SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA) was </p><p>used to detect metabolites eluted form the column. The Q-TOF was operated in both positive and </p><p>negative ion modes. The curtain gas was set 30 PSI, Ion source gas1 was set 60 PSI, Ion source </p><p>gas2 was set 60 PSI, and an interface heater temperature was 500 ℃.For positive ion mode, the </p><p>Ionspray voltage floating were set at 5000 V, respectively. For negative ion mode, the Ionspray </p><p>voltage floating were set at -4500V, respectively. The mass spectrometry data were acquired in </p><p>IDA mode. The TOF mass range was from 60 to 1200 Da. The survey scans were acquired in 150 </p><p>ms and as many as 12 product ion scans were collected if exceeding a threshold of 100 counts per </p><p>second (counts/s) and with a 1+ charge-state. Dynamic exclusion was set for 4 s.During the </p><p>acquisition, the mass accuracy was calibrated every 20 samples. Furthermore, in order to evaluate the stability of the LC-MS during the whole acquisition, a quality control sample (Pool of all </p><p>samples) was acquired after every 10 samples</p>	<p>The acquired MS data pretreatments including peak picking, peak grouping, retention time </p><p>correction, second peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts was performed using </p><p>XCMS software. LC−MS raw data files were converted into mzXML format and then processed by </p><p>the XCMS, CAMERA and metaX[2] toolbox implemented with the R software. Each ion was </p><p>identified by combining retention time (RT) and m/z data. Intensities of each peaks were recorded </p><p>and a three dimensional matrix containing arbitrarily assigned peak indices (retention time-m/z </p><p>pairs), sample names (observations) and ion intensity information (variables) was generated.</p>	<p>The online KEGG, HMDB database was used to annotate the metabolites by matching the exact </p><p>molecular mass data (m/z) of samples with those from database. If a mass difference between </p><p>observed and the database value was less than 10 ppm, the metabolite would be annotated and </p><p>the molecular formula of metabolites would further be identified and validated by the isotopic </p><p>distribution measurements. We also used a in-house fragment spectrum library of metabolites to </p><p>validate the metabolite identidification.</p>

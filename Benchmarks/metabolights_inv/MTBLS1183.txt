Study Identifier	MTBLS1183
Study Title	UPLC-QTOF-MS-Based Plasma Lipidomic Profiling Reveals Biomarkers for Inflammatory Bowel Disease Diagnosis
Study Description	Identification of new biomarkers may help in the early diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). In this study, ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography equipped with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-QTOF-MS) was used to analyze the untargeted lipidomics and compare plasma lipid profiles between IBD patients and control subjects. The principal component analysis and partial least-squares-discriminant analysis were carried out to distinguish IBD patients from control subjects. Using univariate and multivariate analysis, 55 significantly different metabolites from five lipid classes, fatty acyls (<i>n</i> = 19), glycerophospholipids (<i>n</i> = 5), prenol lipids (<i>n</i> = 10), sphingolipids (<i>n</i> = 2), and sterol lipids (<i>n</i> = 19) were identified. Forty-four of the 55 metabolites were analyzed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and area under curve (AUC) of >0.80. After validation in an independent cohort, IBD patients were differentiated from the control subjects by significantly altered plasma level of palmitic acid, 7alpha, 25-dihydroxycholesterol, 20-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic (HETE)-d6, (+/-)5,6-epoxy-eicosatrienoic acid (EpETrE), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), 9-heptadecylenic acid, lactucaxanthin, α-carotene, traumatic acid, and neoquassin with both sensitivity and specificity above 80%. Pathway analysis suggested that IBD dysregulation was related to the biosynthesis of primary bile acid, the metabolism of arachidonic acid, the metabolism of sphingolipid, fatty acid elongation, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. Our results suggest that the lipidomic profiling of patients plasma could be a potential method for IBD diagnosis.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Chemicals and Reagents</strong> </p><p>High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) grade methanol, 2-propanol, and acetonitrile were purchased from Fisher Scientific (Loughborough, U.K.). Methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE, HPLC grade) was obtained from Mallinckrodt Baker Inc. (Phillipsburg, NJ). Deionized water was obtained from a Milli-Q water purification system (Billerica, MA). Other chemicals and reagents were analytical grade with purity above 99.5%.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Participants</strong></p><p>Patients (&gt;18 years old) with <strong>IBD </strong>were recruited at the sixth affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University from July 2016 to April 2018. Through simple randomization, <strong>plasma </strong>samples from 150 adult individuals, including 99 <strong>IBD </strong>patients and 51 <strong>healthy controls</strong>, were used as the <strong>discovery </strong>sample set; and then, <strong>plasma </strong>samples from another 60 individuals, including 30 patients with <strong>IBD </strong>and 30 <strong>healthy subjects</strong>, were used for the validation. The selected patients with <strong>IBD </strong>were those diagnosed by clinical and histologic criteria<strong>[1]-[3]</strong>.Individuals who had known bleeding disorders, renal dysfunction, hepatic impairment, or known previous organ transplant were exclude. Demographic information, such as age, gender, and medication, were recorded. After overnight fasting, blood samples were collected and <strong>plasma</strong> was obtained after 10 min centrifugation at 5,700 g. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the sixth affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-sen University with approval number 2014ZSLYEC-038 and all subjects provided informed consent before they entered the study.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Lennard-Jones, J. E. Classification of inflammatory bowel disease. Scand. J. Gastroenterol., Suppl. 1989, 170, 2−6 discussion 16−19.</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Silverberg MS, Satsangi J, Ahmad T, Arnott ID, Bernstein CN, Brant SR, Caprilli R, Colombel JF, Gasche C, Geboes K, Jewell DP, Karban A, Loftus EV Jr, Peña AS, Riddell RH, Sachar DB, Schreiber S, Steinhart AH, Targan SR, Vermeire S, Warren BF. Toward an integrated clinical, molecular and serological classification of inflammatory bowel disease: report of a Working Party of the 2005 Montreal World Congress of Gastroenterology. Can J Gastroenterol. 2005 Sep;19 Suppl A:5A-36A. doi: 10.1155/2005/269076. PMID: 16151544</p><p><strong>[3] </strong>Mokrowiecka A, Kumor A, Jakubczyk E, Pietruczuk M, Malecka-Panas E. The application of Montreal classification in different clinical and serological IBD subtypes. Hepatogastroenterology. 2010 Jul-Aug;57(101):787-93. PMID: 21033230</p>	<p>For plasma lipid extraction, 50 µL plasma were added with 150 µL methanol. The components were mixed thoroughly by vortexing for 30 s. Next, 500 µL MTBE was added to each samples and then the samples were shaken for 1 h at room temperature. This was followed by adding 125 µL H2O to the solutions. The samples were vortexed for another 30 s and then centrifuged for 10 min at 12,000 rpm, 4 °C. Then the upper organic phase was collected. This step was repeated once, and the two fractions of upper organic phase were combined together. The aliquots were evaporated at room temperature under vacuum, and the residue was reconstituted with 80 μL isopropanol/methanol (1:1, v/v) before injection. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of each analyzed sample, and were run between samples.</p>	<p>The UPLC-MS analysis was performed on a Waters Acquity TM Ultra Performance LC system (Waters, USA) equipped with a Waters SYNAPT G2-S Q-TOF mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was carried out at 55°C on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 x 100mm, 1.8 um). A linear gradient elution of 10 mM ammonium formate in ACN/H2O (60/40) with 0.1% formic acid (A) and 10 mM ammonium formate in 2-propanol/ acetonitrile (90/10) with 0.1% formic acid (B) was used: from 0 to 2 min, 40.0 - 65.0% A, from 2.0 to 8.0 min, 65.0 - 85.0% A, from 8.0 to 10.0 min, 85.0 - 100.0% A, from 10.0 to 13.0 min, maintained 100.0% A and from 13.0 to 15.0 min, 100.0 - 40.0% A. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. The injection volume for each sample was 5 µL.</p>	The mass data were acquired in both positive and negative ion mode. The cone and capillary voltage were set at 60 V and 3 kV (positive mode) / 2 kV (negative mode), respectively. The desolvation gas was set at 700 L/h at a temperature of 400 °C; the source temperature was set at 120 °C. The MS E parameters were set as follows: 5 V collision energy and 50 – 1200 m/z for function 1, collision energy ramp of 20 – 40 V and 50 – 1200 m/z for function 2. The Lock-spray signal was acquired to calibrate the mass accuracy. The acquisition of data were operated by Waters MassLynx v4.1 software (Waters, Manchester, U.K.).	<p>The <strong>raw </strong>data of the mass spectrometer was imported into the commercial software <strong>Progenesis QI</strong> (<strong>version 2.2</strong>, Waters, Manchester, U.K.) for peak extraction. Information such as mass-to-charge ratio, retention time and ion area associated with the metabolite is obtained. QI's workflow consists of the following steps: peak alignment, peak extraction, and peak identification. Data preprocessing is done using <strong>metaX </strong>software, including the use of KNN methods for missing values to fill the extracted data, and to remove low-quality ions (remove the ions which was lost more than 50% in the sample in the QC sample, or the ions which was lost more than 80% in the actual sample).</p>	<p>The project used multivariate analysis of the VIP values of the first two principal components of the PLS-DA model, combined with univariate analysis of fold-change and q-value values to screen differentially expressed metabolites. Screening conditions: (1) VIP ≥ 1; (2) fold change ≥ 1.2 or ≤ 0.8333 ; (3) q-value &lt; 0.05, the three take the intersection to obtain the shared ions, which are the differential ions. The software used for differential ion identification was Progenesis QI (version 2.2) (Waters, UK), where metabolite identification was based on the database LipidMaps.</p>

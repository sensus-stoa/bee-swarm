Study Title	Butyrate ameliorate doxorubicin-induced heart failure via microbiota/ metabolites-gut-Heart axis
Study Description	Doxorubicin(DOX) is widely used in cancer chemotherapy with a limitation of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity(DIC),resulting in heart failure(HF).HF accompanies by gut microbial dysbiosis.However,how gut microbiota regulate DIC-induced heart failure (DIHF)remains unclear.Here, we established a susceptibility model of DIHF,confirming that gut microbiota play a critical role in regulating DIHF. In the DIHF-high susceptibility group the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria was significantly reduced, fecal and serum butyrate level was lowered, and lipid metabolism was disordered,compared with control group. Sodium butyrate(NaB) reduces DOX-induced cardiomyocyte toxicity in vitro.Orally supplemental NaB increases the biodiversity of gut microbiota,optimizes the composition of gut microbiota, reduces serum total cholesterol level, thereby ameliorates HF in vivo. Mechanistically,supplemental NaB increases the concentration of serum and cardiac butyrate, enhances the barrier function of colonic and cardiac tissues, reduces gut microbiota translocation to heart and LPS level,results in significant differences in the microbiota composition in cardiac tissue, promotes the polarization of M1 macrophages to M2, increases the level of anti-inflammatory factors, and decreases the level of pro-inflammatory factors. NaB reduces ferroptosis of DOX-induced cardiomyocyte via GPX4/GSH pathway. Collectively,these findings demonstrate that Butyrate ameliorates DIHF via microbiota/metabolites-gut-heart axis,offering a potential prevention and therapeutic strategy for DIHF.

Study Protocol Description	<p>DOX 3mg/kg,once weekly ,1% sodium butyrate in drinking water rat sermu </p>	<p>The collected samples were thawed on ice, and metabolite were extracted with 50% methanol </p><p>Buffer. Briefly, 20 μL of sample was extracted with 120 μL of precooled 50% methanol, vortexed </p><p>for 1 min, and incubated at room temperature for 10 min; the extraction mixture was then stored </p><p>overnight at -20°C. After centrifugation at 4,000 g for 20 min, the supernatants were transferred </p><p>into new 96-well plates. The samples were stored at -80°C prior to the LC-MS analysis. In </p><p>addition, pooled QC samples were also prepared by combining 10 μL of each extraction </p><p>mixture</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a SCIEX UPLC-Triple TOF 5600 system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5:47.5, v/v)(solvent B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was 40°C.</p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a quadrupole-time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Triple TOFTM5600+, Sciex, USA) equipped with a electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 550oC ; curtain gas (CUR) at 30 psi; both Ion Source Gas1 and Gas2 at 50 psi; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at -4000V in negative mode and 5000V in positive mode, respectively; declustering potential at 80V; collision energy(CE), 20-60 eV rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition was performed with the Information Dependent Acquisition (IDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 50-1000 m/z.</p>	<p>The acquired MS data pretreatments including peak picking, peak grouping, retention time </p><p>correction, second peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts was performed using </p><p>XCMS software. LC−MS raw data files were converted into mzXML format and then processed </p><p>by the XCMS, CAMERA and metaX[2] toolbox implemented with the R software. Each ion was </p><p>identified by combining retention time (RT) and m/z data. Intensities of each peaks were recorded </p><p>and a three dimensional matrix containing arbitrarily assigned peak indices (retention time-m/z </p><p>pairs), sample names (observations) and ion intensity information (variables) was generated. </p><p>The online KEGG, HMDB database was used to annotate the metabolites by matching the exact </p><p>molecular mass data (m/z) of samples with those from database. If a mass difference between </p><p>observed and the database value was less than 10 ppm, the metabolite would be annotated and the </p><p>molecular formula of metabolites would further be identified and validated by the isotopic </p><p>distribution measurements. We also used a in-house fragment spectrum library of metabolites to </p><p>validate the metabolite identidification</p>	<p>The intensity of peak data was further preprocessed by metaX[2]. Those features that were </p><p>detected in less than 50% of QC samples or 80% of biological samples were removed, the </p><p>remaining peaks with missing values were imputed with the k-nearest neighbor algorithm to </p><p>further improve the data quality. PCA was performed for outlier detection and batch effects </p><p>evaluation using the pre-processed dataset. Quality control-based robust LOESS signal correction </p><p>was fitted to the QC data with respect to the order of injection to minimize signal intensity drift </p><p>over time. In addition, the relative standard deviations of the metabolic features were calculated </p><p>across all QC samples, and those &gt; 30% were then removed</p>

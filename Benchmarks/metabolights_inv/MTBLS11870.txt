Study Identifier	MTBLS11870
Study Title	Thioridazine induce phospholipid accumulation and necroptosis in breast cancer cells
Study Description	An antipsychotic drug, Thioridazine has been shown to inhibit a broad spectrum of cancer cells. However, its molecular mechanism is still not well understood. In the present study, we have shown that thioridazine induces cytostatic effects in both parental and tamoxifen-resistant cancer cells through cell viability, cell count, caspase activation, cell cycle analysis and enhanced p21 levels. Further, using the pharmacometabolomics approach, we identified that thioridazine induces phospholipids accumulation in breast cancer cells. We proved that thioridazine induces only phospholipids accumulation and not neutral lipids in cells by using lipid-specific fluorescent quantification and image analysis. The accumulation of phospholipid induces necroptosis, which was assessed by propidium iodide uptake assay. Thioridazine activates RIP1 and RIP3 signalling and subsequent translocation of pore-forming MLKL to the plasma membrane to initiate necroptosis induction. The MLKL-induced membrane pores were confirmed using a scanning electron microscope cell surface visualization. Further, thioridazine co-treatment enhances the efficacy of tamoxifen in resistant breast cancer cells potentiating its usefulness for combinatorial treatment

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human breast cancer cell lines MCF-7, T47D and their tamoxifen-resistant breast cancer cell lines MCF-7R and T47D were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in DMEM and RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco) respectively and supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco) and 100 U/mL penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco). All cell lines were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 (CellXpert C170, Eppendorf, Germany).</p>	<p>The cultured cells were treated with thioridazine and incubated for 48 h. After incubation, the cells were trypsinized and mixed with 10 µL of an isotope-labeled internal standard (IS) mixture. To this, 750 µL of a chilled methanol-water solution (4:1 ratio) was added and the cells were lysed using a probe sonicator. Subsequently, 450 µL of chilled chloroform was added, and the mixture was vortexed for 3 min. Following this, 150 µL of chilled water was added, and the mixture was vortexed again for 2 min. A two-phase liquid-liquid extraction method was employed to separate metabolites and lipids. The methanol-water mixture was used to extract polar metabolites, while chloroform was utilized to extract lipids from the cells. The resulting solution was incubated at -20 °C for 30 min. After incubation, the samples were centrifuged at 5000 RPM and 4 °C for 10 min to separate the methanol and chloroform layers. Both layers were collected into the same centrifuge tube and vacuum-dried at 30 °C. The dried residue was reconstituted in 500 µL of a methanol-water solution (1:1 ratio). Amicon filters, preconditioned with 500 µL of methanol-water solution (1:1 ratio), were used for sample cleanup. The reconstituted sample was added to the filter, centrifuged at 10,000 rpm and 4 °C for 45 min, and the filtrate was discarded. The final filtration was vacuum-dried and reconstituted in 100 µL of methanol-water solution (1:1 ratio) before injection into the mass spectrometry system</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II HPLC coupled with an Agilent 6495C triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The system was equipped with a Jet Stream Technology Electrospray Ionization (AJS-ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ionization modes. Metabolite separation was achieved using a Shodex Asahipak NH2P-40 2D column (150 mm x 2.0 mm, 4 µm) maintained at 35 °C. The mobile phase comprised solvent A (95% water, 5% acetonitrile, 20 mM ammonium carbonate, pH 9.75) and solvent B (100% acetonitrile). The following gradient program was employed: 95% B, 0.0-3.5 min; 85% B, 3.6-8.0 min; 75% B, 8.1-13.0 min; 0% B, 13.5-35.0 min; and 95% B, 36.0-46.0 min. The total run time was 46 min, with a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min and an injection volume of 5 µL. Samples were maintained at 4 °C during analysis. MS/MS detection of metabolites was performed using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II HPLC coupled with an Agilent 6495C triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The system was equipped with a Jet Stream Technology Electrospray Ionization (AJS-ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ionization modes. Source parameters were set as follows: gas temperature at 250 °C, gas flow at 14 L/min, sheath gas temperature at 250 °C, sheath gas flow at 11 L/min and nebulizer pressure at 20 psi. The capillary voltage was set to ±3000 V and the nozzle voltage was ±1500 V.</p>	<p>Instrument control and data acquisition were managed using Agilent MassHunter Workstation software, while data analysis was performed using Agilent MassHunter Quantitative Analysis software. Each chromatographic peak was identified and confirmed through manual assessment, and peak areas were integrated.</p>	<p>A multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) method was used to annotate the metabolites. Detailed methodology, including internal standards used, mass spectral conditions and chromatographic gradient, were provided in our previous publication.</p><p>Reference: 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.2c00401</p>

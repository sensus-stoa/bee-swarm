Study Protocol Description	<p>All plant and soil samples used in the present study were collected from Dengliu Village, Xianhu Town, Wuming District, Nanning City, and Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (108.09° N, 23.35° E, altitude 700 m). The soil of each groups was the rhizosphere soil of banana plants, that is, the rhizosphere soil samples were obtained by shaken off from the roots to remove impurities and through a 50-mesh sieve. Using sterile tweezers, the plant residue in the soil was extracted and placed into two sections of sterilized bags. Mixed samples are treated in the same way as soil samples from the rhizosphere, which were kept at -80 °C for DNA extraction and microbiome sequencing.</p>	<p> Samples are frozen and stored at −20°C/-80°C, and mailed in dry ice. Appropriate liquid samples were taken, placed in the corresponding numbered 50mL centrifuge tube, frozen overnight in a -80℃ refrigerator, and vacuum freeze-dried. After freeze-drying, 70% methanol containing internal standard extraction solution is added according to the concentration ratio of 30 times, for example, 9mL sample is freeze-dried and 300μL extraction agent is added, 6mL sample is freeze-dried and 200μL extraction agent is added. Vortex 15 min, ice water bath ultrasound (KQ5200E) 10min. At 12000 r/min, centrifuge (5424R, Eppendorf) at 4℃ for 3min. The supernatant was removed, filtered with a microporous filter membrane (0.22 μm pore size), and stored in the sample vial for LC-MS/MS detection.</p>	<p> The Liu et al., (2023) analytical conditions were adhered to for the HPLC column (Agilent SB-C18), which measures 1.8 µm by 2.1 mm by 100 mm (32). The solvent system consists of ultrapure water and acetonitrile, both of which add 0.04% acetic acid. The gradient program includes five stages: 95:5 V/V (H2O/acetonitrile) at 0.00 min, 5:95 V/V at 11.0 min, 5:95 V/V at 12.0 min, 95:5 V/V at 12.1 min, and 95:5 V/V at 15.0 min. The flow rate is 0.40 mL/min, the temperature is 40°C, and the injection volume is 2 μl. An alternative connection between the effluent and the ESI-triple quadrupole-linear ion trap (Q TRAP)-MS was made.</p>	<p> A triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spectrometer (Q TRAP), an API 6500 Q TRAP LC-MS/MS system with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, was used to produce LIT and triple quadrupole (QQQ) scans. It is managed by the Analyst 1.6 program and is capable of operating in both positive and negative ion modes (AB SCIEX). The ESI source operating parameters were as follows: high collision gas (CAD), ion source gas I (GSI), gas II (GSII), and curtain gas (CUR) set at 55, 60, and 25.0 psi, respectively; ion source, turbo spray; source temperature 500 °C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (positive ion mode)/-4500 V (negative ion mode). Using 10 and 100 μmol/L polypropylene glycol solutions in QQQ and LIT modes, respectively, the device was adjusted and calibrated. Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) trials with collision gas (nitrogen) set to 5 psi yielded QQQ scans. The DP and CE of each MRM ion pair were finished through further tuning of DP and CE. Based on the metabolites eluted during each interval, a specific set of MRM ion pairs was examined.</p>	<p> Analyst 1.6.1 software and SPSS 20.0 were used to examine all metabolite data from banana samples. The means SD were calculated from these data. One-way ANOVA was used, followed by Tukey's significant difference test, and p-values of 0.05 were considered significant. Multiple supervision methods were used to develop an orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) model. The parameter variable effect on projection (VIP) was used to assess the relative importance of each metabolite in the OPLS-DA model. R software (www.r-project.org) was used to do a hierarchical clustering analysis of the metabolites between each sample. The screening criteria for differential metabolites were | log2(fold change) | ≥ 1 and VIP ≥ 1.</p>	<p>Based on the local metabolic database, the metabolites of the samples were qualitatively and quantitatively analyzed by mass spectrometry. The multi-peak diagram of metabolite detection in the multiple reaction monitoring mode MRM in the figure shows the substances that can be detected in the sample, and each chromatographic peak of different colors represents a detected metabolite. The characteristic ions of each substance are screened out by the triple quadrupole, and the signal intensity (CPS) of the characteristic ions is obtained in the detector. The mass spectrometry file of the sample is opened with MultiQuant software to integrate and correct the chromatographic peaks. The peak area (Area) of each chromatographic peak represents the relative content of the corresponding substance. Finally, all chromatographic peak area integral data are exported and saved.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS11963
Study Title	RIOK1 Phase Separation Shapes Stress Adaptation and Cell Fate in Hepatocellular Carcinoma
Study Description	<p>Cancer cells can always be unexpected rescued from desperate environment by mounting adaptive responses to resolve the arising cellular stress. The dynamic stress granules (SGs) assembly and metabolic reprogramming play crucial roles in maintaining cellular growth and homeostasis. However, the mechanisms underlying their interaction remain elusive. Here we screened RIOK1 is highly expressed in liver cancer tissues and is transactivated by NRF2 under stressful conditions. Mechanistically, the oncogenic phenotype observed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells is associated with the capacity of RIOK1 to undergo liquid-liquid phase separation. The non-enzymatic regulation of IGF2BP1 tyrosine phosphorylation by RIOK1 contributes to incorporating RIOK1/IGF2BP1/G3BP1 into stress granules which concurrently sequester PTEN mRNA. The decrease in PTEN translation concomitantly activates the pentose phosphate pathway to resolve stress and confer cytoprotection. Additionally, chidamide downregulates RIOK1 expression and enhances the efficacy of TKIs, presenting a promising therapeutic strategy for HCC treatment.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The experiment involves 5 RIOK1 knockout and 5 control HCCLM3 liver cancer cell line samples for metabolomics analysis. Cells are treated and after collection, cells are processed with 80% methanol, flash frozen, followed by sonication, centrifugation and freeze-drying. The lyophilized samples are reconstituted in 10% methanol and analyzed by LC-MS/MS.</p>	<p>Add 800 μL of pre-cooled 80% methanol aqueous solution to each sample. Vortex thoroughly and sonicate in an ice bath for 20 min. Incubate the mixture at -20 °C overnight, followed by centrifugation at 16,000 x g (4 °C) for 20 min and collect the supernatant</p>	<p>Samples were maintained at 4 °C in the autosampler throughout analysis. Separation was performed using a Shimadzu Nexera X2 LC-30AD UHPLC system with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 50 mm; Waters) column. Parameters included: injection volume 5 μL, column temperature 4 °C, flow rate 0.2 mL/min, mobile phase A (0.1% formic acid in water), and mobile phase B (acetonitrile).&nbsp;Gradient program for negative ion mode: 0.0-2.0 min (0% B), 2.0-2.5 min (0-20% B, linear), 2.5-3.5 min (20-40% B, linear), 3.5-4.5 min (40-50% B, linear), 4.5-10.0 min (50-100% B, linear), 10.0-15.4 min (100% B, isocratic), 15.4-15.5 min (100-0% B, linear), 15.5-18.0 min (0% B, isocratic). Gradient program for positive ion mode: 0.0-2.5 min (0% B), 2.5-9.0 min (0-30% B, linear), 9.0-10.0 min (30-100% B, linear), 10.0-15.4 min (100% B, isocratic), 15.4-15.5 min (100-0% B, linear), 15.5-18.0 min (0% B, isocratic).&nbsp;QC samples were interspersed in the sample queue to monitor system stability and data reliability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was conducted on a QTRAP 5500 instrument with ESI source parameters optimized for each polarity (positive: ISVF +5500 V; negative: ISVF -4500 V), using MRM mode for targeted ion detection. HESI source parameters for positive ion mode: Source temperature 550 °C, Gas1 (nebulizer) 40 psi, Gas2 (heater) 50 psi, curtain gas (CUR) 35 psi, ion spray voltage (ISVF) +5500 V.&nbsp;Negative ion mode parameters: Source temperature 550 °C, Gas1 40 psi, Gas2 50 psi, CUR 35 psi, ISVF -4500 V. In this study, metabolite identification was performed exclusively in<strong> negative ion mode (NEG)</strong>, as the focus was on the detection and quantification of metabolites specifically detectable in this mode.</p>	<p>Chromatographic peak areas and retention times were extracted using MultiQuant software. Metabolites were identified by matching retention times, Q1/Q3 ion pairs, and secondary MS spectra against authentic standards.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and data processing were performed as follows: Chromatographic peak areas and retention times were extracted using&nbsp;MultiQuant software, followed by metabolite annotation through alignment with authentic chemical standards, retention time matching and verification of&nbsp;Q1/Q3 ion pairs&nbsp;and&nbsp;secondary MS/MS spectra. Data processing included peak integration, normalization [if applicable] and quality control using interspersed QC samples to ensure system stability and reproducibility</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Upon admission, initial serum samples from stroke patients were collected. All samples were stored at -80°C until further analysis.</p>	<p>The samples (100 μL) were placed in EP tubes and resuspended in prechilled 80% methanol with thorough vortexing. The samples were then incubated on ice for 5 minutes and centrifuged at 15,000×g at 4°C for 20 minutes. The supernatant was diluted with LC-MS grade water to a final methanol concentration of 53%. The diluted samples were transferred to fresh Eppendorf tubes and centrifuged again at 15,000×g at 4°C for 20 minutes. Finally, the supernatant was injected into the LC-MS/MS system for analysis.</p>	<p>UHPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher, Germany). Samples were injected onto a Hypersil Gold column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm) with a 17-minute linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The eluents for the positive ion mode were eluent A (0.1% FA in water) and eluent B (methanol). The eluents for the negative ion mode were eluent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9.0) and eluent B (methanol). The solvent gradient was programmed as follows: 2% B, 1.5 min; 2-85% B, 3 min; 85-100% B, 10 min; 100-2% B, 10.1 min; 2% B, 12 min.</p>	<p>MS analyses were performed using an Orbitrap Q Exactive™ HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Germany). The instrument was operated in both positive and negative ionization modes with a spray voltage of 3.5 kV, a capillary temperature of 320°C, a sheath gas flow rate of 35 psi, an auxiliary gas flow rate of 10 L/min, an S-lens RF level of 60, and an auxiliary gas heater temperature of 350°C.</p>	<p>The raw data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 (CD3.1, Thermo Fisher) for peak alignment, peak picking, and metabolite quantification. The main parameters were set as follows: retention time tolerance, 0.2 minutes; mass accuracy tolerance, 5 ppm; signal intensity threshold, 30%; signal-to-noise ratio, 3. Subsequently, peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity. The normalized data were then used to predict molecular formulas based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks, and fragment ions.</p>	<p>The peaks were matched against the mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/), mzVault, and MassList databases to obtain accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 3.4.3), Python (version 2.7.6), and CentOS (release 6.6). For data that were not normally distributed, standardization was performed to generate relative peak areas. Compounds with coefficients of variation (CVs) for relative peak areas in QC samples exceeding 30% were excluded. The final metabolite identification and relative quantification results were thus determined.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS11993
Study Title	Achyranthes bidentata alleviates hypertension with regulating gut microbiota
Study Description	<p>Background: Hypertension remains a significant global health challenge, with limited treatment efficacy in many patients. Emerging evidence suggests that gut microbiota plays a crucial role in blood pressure regulation. This study investigates the anti-hypertensive effects of Achyranthes bidentata (Ab) extract and its interaction with the gut microbiota, particularly focusing on the enrichment of Dubosiella sp. and its impact on arachidonic acid metabolism.</p><p>Results: Here, we identified a novel microbiota-driven anti-hypertensive mechanism mediated by the Ab extract and Dubosiella sp. Ab significantly ameliorated hypertension and kidney damage by enriching Dubosiella in hypertensive mice. Metabolomics analysis revealed that Ab enhances lipid metabolic pathways, particularly arachidonic acid metabolism. In vitro, Ab significantly promoted the biofilm formation of D. newyorkensis DSM103457. Further gavage experiments confirmed that D. newyorkensis DSM103457 alleviates hypertension, cardiac and kidney injury. Combined microbial and metabolic profiling via 16S rRNA sequencing, metagenomics and metabolomics demonstrated that D. newyorkensis DSM103457 modulates gut microbiota, arachidonic acid metabolism, restoring prostaglandins and leukotrienes essential for cardiovascular homeostasis. Notably, arachidonic acid significantly promotes D. newyorkensis DSM103457 growth, enabling rapid generation and transformation of HETEs and HODEs in vitro.</p><p>Conclusions: This study identified Ab-Dubosiella-arachidonic acid axis as a novel anti-hypertensive pathway, offering promise for the development of integrated herbal prebiotics and microbial probiotics for targeted hypertension therapy.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Six mice were maintained on a normal diet and served as the normal control group (<strong>N</strong>). Twelve mice were induced with hypertension by administering <strong>0.5 mg/mL L-NAME</strong> (Meilunbio, Dalian, China) in drinking water for two weeks, followed by a one-week washout period (4% NaCl; Xietong Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). These mice were then fed a <strong>high-salt diet (4% NaCl) </strong>for three weeks to establish a hypertension model. Dried roots of <em>Achyranthes bidentata </em>(Ab, 0.4 kg) were extracted three times with 2 L distilled water at 100 °C for 20 min each. The concentrated extract was freeze-dried using an ALPHA 1–4 LDplus freeze-dryer (Martin Christ, Germany) and mixed with a 4% NaCl diet to prepare a final concentration of <strong>8% Ab extract</strong>. Hypertensive mice were divided into 2 groups: the hypertensive model group (<strong>M</strong>) received the high-salt diet, while the <strong>Ab-treated group (A) </strong>received the high-salt diet supplemented with Ab extract for 5 weeks. Fecal samples from the colon were collected at weeks 13 and were stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Briefly, 50 mg solid sample was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead was added. Next 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol:water, 4:1, v:v) containing 0.02 mg/mL of internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine) was used for metabolite extraction. Samples were ground by the Wonbio-96c (Shanghai wanbo biotechnology co., LTD) frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10 °C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5 °C, 40 kHz). The samples were left at -20 °C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4 °C, 13,000 x g), and the supernatant was transferred to the injection vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon UHPLC System equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid inacetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5:47.5, v/v)(solvent B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was 40 °C.</p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive Mass Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 400 °C; sheath gas flow rate at 40 arb; Aux gas flow rate at 10 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at -2800 V in negative mode and 3500 V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy, 20-40-60 V rolling for MS/MS. Full MS resolution was 70000 and MS/MS resolution was 17500. Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC-MS raw data was performed by Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) software, and a 3D data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this 3D matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, deredundant and peak pooled.</p>	<p>The metabolites were identified by searching database, and the main databases were the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca), Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu) and Majorbio Database. The data were analyzed through the free online platform of majorbio cloud platform (cloud.majorbio.com). Metabolic features detected at least 80% in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, minimum metabolite values were imputed for specific samples in which the metabolite levels fell below the lower limit of quantitation, and each Metabolic features were normalized by sum. To reduce the errors caused by sample preparation and instrument instability, the response intensity of the sample mass spectrum peaks was normalized by the sum normalization method, and then the normalized data matrix was obtained. Meanwhile, variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% of QC samples were removed, and log10 processing was performed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p>

Study Title	Untargeted Lipidomics Profiling Provides Novel Insights into Pediatric Patients with Sepsis: An Exploratory Study
Study Description	<p><strong>Background: </strong>The plasma lipidome has emerged as an important indicator for assessing host metabolic and immune status in sepsis. While previous studies have largely examined specific lipid class changes in adults sepsis, comprehensive investigations into plasma lipidomic alterations in pediatric sepsis are limited. This study aimed to characterize the plasma lipidome in pediatric sepsis using a metabolomics-based exploratory approach, providing insights into pathophysiological mechanisms and potential biomarkers.</p><p><strong>Methods:</strong> A retrospective study was conducted on pediatric patients with sepsis admitted to the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU). Untargeted lipidomics analysis using ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with Orbitrap mass spectrometry (UPLC-Orbitrap) was performed to compare metabolomic profiles between non-infected control patients and sepsis patients.</p><p><strong>Results:</strong> Compared to controls, plasma lipid levels in sepsis patients decreased by 33.3%, increased by 20.2%, and remained unchanged in 46.5% of cases. Several lipid molecules were identified to be associated with disease severity and inflammatory markers. In the recovery and deterioration subgroups, 186 differential lipid molecules were identified, with triglycerides (TG) being the most abundant class. Notably, 15 lipid molecules overlapped between those associated with disease severity and those linked to clinical outcomes. Fatty acid (FA) levels were significantly elevated in the sepsis group compared to controls, with arachidonic acid (FA(20:4)) showing the most significant increase (P &lt; 0.001).</p><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Alterations in plasma lipid profiles among children with sepsis reflect disease severity, systemic inflammatory responses, and clinical outcomes. These findings underscore the prognostic potential of lipidomics and its value in understanding sepsis pathophysiology.</p><p>Key words&nbsp;Pediatric sepsis, Plasma lipidome, Untargeted lipidomics, Triglycerides, Fatty acid&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Blood samples (2 mL) were collected from all participants in EDTA anticoagulant tubes to obtain plasma. The samples were subjected to a two-step centrifugation process: an initial centrifugation at 400 g for 10 min at 4C to remove cells, followed by a second centrifugation at 3, 000 g for 10 min at 4C to obtain platelet-poor plasma. Supernatants were aliquoted into 200 L portions, placed on dry ice, and stored at -80C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Take an appropriate amount of the sample and add 200 ?L of water, vortex the mixture. Then add 800 ?L of MTBE, vortex again, followed by the addition of 240 ?L of pre-chilled methanol and vortex mix. Sonicate the mixture in a low-temperature water bath for 20 minutes, then let it stand at room temperature for 30 minutes. Centrifuge at 14,000 g, 10C for 15 minutes. Collect the upper organic phase and dry it under nitrogen. For mass spectrometry analysis, reconstitute the sample with 200 ?L of 90% isopropanol/acetonitrile solution, vortex thoroughly, and take 90 ?L of the reconstituted solution. Centrifuge again at 14,000 g, 10C for 15 minutes, and collect the supernatant for analysis.</p>	<p>The samples were separated using the UHPLC Nexera LC-30A ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system. A C18 column was used with a column temperature of 45C and a flow rate of 300 ?L/min. The mobile phase composition was as follows: A: Acetonitrile-water solution (acetonitrile:water = 6:4, v/v) + 0.1% formic acid + 0.1 mM ammonium formate, B: Acetonitrile-isopropanol solution (acetonitrile:isopropanol = 1:9, v/v) + 0.1% formic acid + 0.1 mM ammonium formate. The gradient elution program was as follows: 02 min, B maintained at 30%; 225 min, B increased linearly from 30% to 100%; 2535 min, B maintained at 30%. Throughout the analysis, the samples were kept in the automatic sampler at 10C. To minimize the impact of instrument signal fluctuations, the samples were analyzed in a randomized sequence for continuous analysis.</p>	<p>The samples were analyzed using both positive and negative ion modes via electrospray ionization (ESI). After separation by UHPLC, mass spectrometry was performed using the Q Exactive series mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). The ESI source conditions were as follows:</p><p>Heater Temp 300 C, Sheath Gas Flow rate 45 arb, Aux Gas Flow Rate15 arb, Sweep Gas Flow Rate 1arb, spray voltage 3.0KV, Capillary Temp 350 C, S-Lens RF</p><p>Level 50%, MS1 scan ranges: 200-1800?</p><p>The mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of lipid molecules and lipid fragments was acquired using the following method: after each full scan (MS1), 10 fragment spectra (MS2 scan, HCD) were collected. The resolution for MS1 at m/z 200 was set to 70,000, while the resolution for MS2 at m/z 200 was set to 17,500.</p>	<p>Raw data were acquired using a Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) coupled with a UHPLC Nexera LC-30A system (SHIMADZU). The following steps were performed for data processing and transformation:</p><p>Chromatographic Separation</p><p>Chromatographic separation was achieved using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC CSH C18 column (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm).</p><p>The mobile phases consisted of acetonitrile and isopropanol (Thermo Fisher), with methanol (Thermo Fisher) used for sample preparation.</p><p>Centrifugation</p><p>Samples were centrifuged at 4°C using an Eppendorf 5430R refrigerated centrifuge to remove debris prior to injection.</p><p>Mass Spectrometry Acquisition</p><p>Data were collected in positive and negative ion modes using the Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer.</p><p>Data Conversion</p><p>Raw files (.RAW) generated by the Q-Exactive Plus were converted to the mzML format using ProteoWizard software (version X.X).</p><p>Peak Detection and Alignment</p><p>Peaks were detected and aligned using XCMS (version X.X) with the following parameters:</p><p>Peak width: 10–60 seconds</p><p>Mass tolerance: 10 ppm</p><p>Normalization</p><p>Data were normalized to the total ion count (TIC) to correct for variations in sample injection volume.</p><p>Imputation</p><p>Missing values (features with less than 50% presence across samples) were removed, and remaining missing values were imputed using k-nearest neighbors (kNN) imputation.</p><p>Transformation</p><p>Log2 transformation was applied to the normalized data to stabilize variance and improve downstream statistical analysis.</p><p>Quality Control</p><p>Internal standards were used to ensure data quality, and batch effects were corrected using the ComBat algorithm in MetaboAnalyst.</p>	<p><br></p><p>Metabolite identification was performed using MetaboAnalyst (version X.X) with the following steps:</p><p>Data Preprocessing:</p><p>Raw data were processed by peak detection, alignment, and normalization (TIC normalization, Log2 transformation) to correct for variations.</p><p>Metabolite Identification:</p><p>Metabolites were annotated by matching m/z values and retention times against databases such as HMDB, KEGG, and LIPID MAPS.</p><p>Confidence levels were assigned based on MSI guidelines (Level 1-4).</p><p>MS/MS Spectral Matching:</p><p>For unannotated metabolites, MS/MS fragmentation patterns were compared to libraries like mzCloud and MassBank.</p><p>Pathway Analysis:</p><p>Identified metabolites were mapped to biological pathways using MetaboAnalyst's Pathway Analysis module.</p><p>Statistical Analysis:</p><p>ANOVA, t-tests, and fold change analysis were performed to identify significantly altered metabolites.</p>

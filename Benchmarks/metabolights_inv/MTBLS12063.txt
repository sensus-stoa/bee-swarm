Study Identifier	MTBLS12063
Study Title	Microbiome-produced nicotinic acid controls colon regional identity and injury susceptibility
Study Description	<p>Regional structure of the intestinal epithelium is critical for its function and is associated with the risk of specific gut diseases. However, how regionalization is established and how it influences disease susceptibility remains poorly understood. Here, we investigated the role of the gut microbiome – which comprises all microorganisms residing in the gut lumen and is itself regionalized – in regulating regionalization of the colon. We found that the proximo-distal identity of colonocytes is disrupted in mice lacking a microbiome and that the proximal colonic microbiome produces high levels of nicotinic acid, which induces Pparα expression to establish proximal colonocyte identity. Furthermore, we report that microbiome-driven proximal identity confers protection against tissue injury in the mouse. Finally, we determined that colon regionalization and the loss of proximal identity during disease are also present in human colon samples.ate the study abstract/description</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Control (SPF) or antibiotic-treated mice were euthanized, and the entire colon was collected and measured. The luminal content was discarded, and the mucus layer was scraped using a sterile razor blade. The scraped mucus was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C. Proximal content was collected from the first centimeter of the colon, the mid-content from the middle 1 cm of the colon, and the distal content from the final centimeter of the colon.</p>	<p>Frozen dissected tissue (4-16 mg) was homogenized using Precellys 24-bead homogenizer pre-cooled with dry ice at 6400rpm, 3 x 20s with 30s of rest time. 1mL of acetonitrile:methanol:water (2:2:1) was then added to each sample and incubated on ice for 30 min. The extraction buffer additionally contained internal standard 2-amino-3-bromo-5-methylbenzoic acid (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No. 631531). Precipitates and other cellular debris were pelleted by centrifugation at 21,000 rpm for 10 min. Supernatants were transferred to separate Eppendorf tubes and dried under vacuum. Samples were kept in -80°C until the day of data acquisition. 100 μL of water were added to each sample and then vortexed to resuspend dried small molecules. Samples were centrifugated at 21,000 rpm for 10 min to pellet any particles that did not go into solution. The samples were then transferred to UPLC-MS vials for data acquisition</p>	<p>A 5 μL aliquot of each sample was injected onto a Shimadzu 30-AD UPLC system. Small molecules were chromatographically separated on a Kinetex F5 2.6 μm, 2.1 × 150 mm LC column (Phenomenex, Cat. No. 00F-4723-AN). The mobile phases consisted of [A] water + 0.1% formic acid and [B] acetonitrile + 0.1% formic acid. At a constant flow rate of 200 μL/min, the gradient program was:</p><p>0 min, 0.2% B -&gt; 2 min, 0.2% B -&gt; 5 min, 2% B -&gt; 11 min, 25% B -&gt; 13 min, 98% B -&gt; 16 min, 98% B -&gt; 16.1 min, 0.2% B -&gt; 20 min, 0.2% B.</p>	<p>The UPLC was coupled in series to a SCIEX 6600+ QTOF operated in data-dependent acquisition mode. Data were acquired separately in positive and negative ionization modes using the following source parameters: CUR = 35, GS1 = 60, GS2 = 80, ISVF = 5500 (pos) / −4500 (neg), TEM = 400. MS1 scans were collected from 50–1000 m/z, and the 20 most intense ions per cycle were selected for MS2 fragmentation, with a total cycle time of 0.651 s.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using MS-DIAL (Riken, version 4.9.221218) for peak picking, alignment, and metabolite annotation. Metabolite features were filtered according to: intensity height &gt; 3000 cps, mass accuracy &lt; 10 ppm, cohort frequency &gt; 50%, and MS2 library match score &gt; 70%. Data were normalized to the average area of the internal standard across all samples.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using the publicly available curated high-resolution MS2 spectral library (MS-DIAL version 17). All annotated metabolites were manually reviewed for quality control.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12070
Study Title	The molecular basis for acetylhistidine synthesis by HisAT/NAT16
Study Description	<p>Acetylhistidine has been detected in human blood, but its origin and function are not known. It is formed when the acetyl group of acetyl-CoA is transferred to the α-amino group of histidine. Here we identify the intracellular NAT16 as the human histidine acetyltransferase (HisAT) responsible for histidine acetylation in vitro and in vivo. A NAT16 variant (p.Phe63Ser) present in &gt;5% of the population was previously found to correlate with reduced plasma levels of acetylhistidine and increased risk of kidney disease. Our biochemical analysis of HisAT/NAT16 Phe63Ser shows reduced affinity for Histidine supporting a model where this variant has less acetylhistidine catalysis leading to lower blood level of acetylhistidine. We find that HisAT adopts a double-GNAT (Gcn5-related N-Acetyltransferase) fold where the N-terminal domain binds acetyl-CoA and with unique active site conformation allowing the binding of histidine in between the two domains. We detected similar structures from across living organisms and found that the HisAT structure is conserved in several archaeal and bacterial species. In sum, NAT16 is the human histidine acetyltransferase utilizing a rare double GNAT structure to steer plasma acetylhistidine levels with potential impact for kidney function.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cells (homo sapiens, HAP1) were grown in Iscove´s Modified Dulbecco´s Medium (IMDM, biowest, L0191-500), 10 % Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS, Sigma-Aldrich, F7524), 1 % Penicillin-Streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich, P0781) at 37 °C and 5 % CO2.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>Cells (<em>homo sapiens</em>, HAP1) were seeded inside a 10 cm dish at 2.5 x e ^6 cells, and harvested 24 h after seeding. From each cell line, 5 plates were collected in total representing replicates. Harvesting was performed for 2 dishes at the same time, by washing cells twice in ice-cold PBS, followed by scraping into suspension with further ice-cold PBS (1 ml). Cells were collected by centrifugation (300 x g, 8 min, 4 °C) before removal of the supernatant, weighing the pellet (average weight was 24 mg) and storing the samples at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction was performed with chloroform and methanol (300 μl, 4:1 vol/vol), and stable isotope-labelled internal standards were added. The samples were homogenized by vortex mixing. Insoluble material was pelleted by centrifugation at 3,000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C (Fisher Scientific Mega Star 600R42309168), and 2 equal aliquots of supernatant were transferred to new tubes. Extracts were dried under a gentle nitrogen flow and reconstituted in the same volume using 2 separate solvent mixtures for polar and semi-polar metabolite analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>For polar metabolite analysis (HILIC), an equal mixture of mobile phase eluent A and B was applied. For semi-polar metabolite analysis (reverse phase) a mixture of 10% mobile phase B in mobile phase A was applied.</p><p><br></p><p>Stable isotope-labelled internal standards were added to all samples. Reconstituted extracts were filtered through a Spin-X centrifuge tube filter (22 μm, Corning Costar) using centrifugation at 15,000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C.</p>	<p>Polar and semi-polar metabolite profiling was performed by <strong>MS-Omics</strong> (part of cmbio, Vedbæk, Denmark) in 2 assays.</p><p><br></p><p>The semi-polar analysis via reverse phase UHPLC was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using an adapted version of the protocol previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, UHPLC separation on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 (2.1 x 150 mm, 1.8 μm) column employed a gradient elution over 15 min with a flow rate of 300 μl/min. The gradient program began with 0.0% eluent B, held isocratically for 2.0 min, followed by a ramp to 35% B over the next 10.0 min. It's increased to 90.0% B for 1 min, before being held isocratically for 1.0 min at 90% B, followed by a reduction to 0% B between 14.0 and 15.0 min. A composition of 0% B is maintained to the end, leading to a total cycle time of 18.0 min. The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C with an injection volume of 50 µl, using a 50:50 methanol:Milli-Q water (H2O) injection solvent. Eluent A consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate in water with 0.1% formic acid, and eluent B consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate in methanol with 0.1% formic acid. </p><p><br></p><p>Chromatographic separation of polar metabolites was achieved using a hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) method optimized for the analysis of polar metabolites using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p><p><br></p><p>HPLC separation on an InfinityLab Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z (150 x 2.1, 2.7 μm) column employed a gradient elution over 24 min with a flow rate of 250 µl/min. The gradient program began with 90.0% eluent B, held isocratically for 2.0 min, followed by a ramp to 60% B over the next 10.0 min. 60.0% B is held for 3 min, before increasing back to 90% B between 15.0 and 16.0 min. A composition of 90% B is maintained to the end, leading to a total cycle time of 24.0 min. The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C with an injection volume of 5 µl, using a 50:50 acetonitrile (ACN):Milli-Q water (H2O) injection solvent. Eluent B consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate in ACN, pH 10, and eluent A consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate in 40.0% ACN, pH 10 with 0.1% medronic acid.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Doneanu CE, Chen W, Mazzeo JR. UPLC-MS Monitoring of Water-Soluble Vitamin Bs in Cell Culture Media in Minutes, 2011, Waters Application Note720004042. <a href='https://www.waters.com/content/dam/waters/en/app-notes/2011/720004042/720004042-en.pdf' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.waters.com/content/dam/waters/en/app-notes/2011/720004042/720004042-en.pdf</a></p>	<p>Polar and Semi-polar metabolite profiling was performed by <strong>MS-Omics</strong> (part of cmbio, Vedbæk, Denmark) in two separate assays.</p><p><br></p><p>For polar metabolite analysis, the Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer operated with the following parameters: full MS scan, a resolution of 60,000, AGC target of 1 x e^6, maximum injection time of 200 ms, and a scan range of 60 to 900 m/z. Data-dependent MS/MS (dd-MS2) parameters included a TopN of 10, resolution of 30,000, AGC target of 2 x e^5, maximum injection time of 55 ms, isolation window of 1.0 m/z, and stepped collision energy (NCE) of 20, 40, 60. Dynamic exclusion was set to 9.0 s to prevent repeated fragmentation of the same ion, with an intensity threshold of 1.5 x e^4 for ion selection, and an apex trigger set to 2 to 9 s. Isotope exclusion was enabled to focus on the most abundant isotopes, and lock masses were used to ensure calibration stability throughout the runs. An electrospray ionization interface was used as ionization source. Analysis was performed in positive and negative ionization mode.</p><p><br></p><p>For semi-polar metabolite analysis, the Orbitrap Exploris 240 MS (Thermo Scientific operated with following parameters: full MS scan, a resolution of 60,000, AGC target Standard, maximum injection time mode auto, and a scan range of 65 to 975 m/z. Data-dependent MS/MS (dd-MS2) parameters included a TopN of 5, resolution of 30,000, AGC target of Standard, Auto maximum injection time, isolation window of 0.4 m/z, and stepped collision energy (NCE) of 20, 40, 60. Dynamic exclusion was set to 2.5 s to prevent repeated fragmentation of the same ion, with an intensity threshold of 1.0 x e^3 for ion selection, and an apex trigger with a trigger window of 30%. Isotope exclusion was enabled to focus on the most abundant isotopes, and lock masses were used to ensure calibration stability throughout the runs. An electrospray ionization interface was used as ionization source. Analysis was performed in positive and negative ionization mode in switching (alternating) polarity.</p>	<p>Metabolomics processing was performed untargeted using <strong>Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Scientific)</strong> and <strong>Skyline 22.2 (MacCoss Lab Software) </strong>for peak picking and feature grouping.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>	<p>A <strong>MS-Omics</strong> in-house annotation and curation pipeline written in <strong>MatLab (2022b, MathWorks)</strong> was applied.</p><p><br></p><p>Identification of compounds was performed at 4 levels:</p><p><strong>Level 1: </strong>identification by retention times (compared against in-house authentic standards, with an accepted deviation of 0.2 min), accurate mass (with an accepted deviation of 3 ppm), and MS/MS spectra (with a match factor above 60);</p><p><strong>Level 2a:</strong> identification by retention times (compared against in-house authentic standards, with an accepted deviation of 0.2 min), accurate mass (with an accepted deviation of 3 ppm);</p><p><strong>Level 2b:</strong> identification by accurate mass (with an accepted deviation of 3 ppm), and MS/MS spectra from in-house authentic standards and mzCloud (ThermoScientific) at a match factor &gt;60;</p><p><strong>Level 3:</strong> identification by accurate mass alone (with an accepted deviation of 3ppm), based on the <strong>Human Metabolome database (v 5.0)</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Polar and semi-polar profiling results were merged in annotation <strong>levels 1</strong> and <strong>2a</strong> using compound annotations for matching.</p><p><br></p><p>Duplicate detections based on similar retention time, accurate mass and signal correlation were removed manually. </p><p><br></p><p>In the case of known isotopes with indistinguishable retention time and fragmentation behaviour, co-eluting peaks were manually integrated and reported as compound groups (e.g. hexoses or sugar alcohols).</p>

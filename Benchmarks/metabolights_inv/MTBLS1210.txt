Study Identifier	MTBLS1210
Study Title	What CHO is made of: Variations in the biomass composition of Chinese hamster ovary cell lines
Study Description	<p><strong>Background:</strong> Cell line specific, genome-scale metabolic models enable rigorous and systematic in silico investigation of cellular metabolism. Such models have recently become available for Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) too. However, a key ingredient, namely an experimentally validated biomass function that summarizes the cellular composition, was so far missing in all genome-scale metabolic models of CHO cell lines. Here, we close this gap by providing extensive experimental data on the biomass composition of 13 parental and producer CHO cell lines under various conditions.</p><p><strong>Results:</strong> We report protein, lipid, DNA, RNA and carbohydrate content, cell dry mass, and protein and lipid composition. Furthermore, we present meticulous data on exchange rates between cells and environment and provide detailed experimental protocols on how to determine all of the above. The biomass composition is converted into cell line and condition specific biomass functions for usage in cell line specific, genome-scale metabolic models of CHO. Finally, flux-balance analysis (FBA) is used to demonstrate consistency between in silico predictions and experimental analysis.</p><p><strong>Conclusions:</strong> Our study reveals a strong variability of the total protein content and cell dry mass across cell lines. Yet, the relative amino acid composition is independent of the cell lines and conditions and thus needs not be explicitly measured for each new cell line. In contrast, the lipid composition is strongly influenced by the growth media and needs to be determined for each case. These cell line specific variations in the biomass composition have a small impact on growth rate predictions with FBA, as inaccuracies in the predictions are typically dominated by inaccuracies in the exchange rate spectra. Cell specific biomass variations become only important if the experimental errors in the exchange rate spectra drop below twenty percent.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Links:</strong></p><p>All data and code for data processing are available at <a href='http://dx.doi.org/10.17632/g3wz7spyc9.1' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>Data Mendeley</a>.</p><p>Cell line specific metabolic models were deposited in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biomodels/(Chelliah et al, 2014)' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>BioModels</a>, identifiers are listed in the study sample table.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cultivation and sampling of CHO cells:</strong></p><p>After thawing, cells were maintained in 125 mL non-baffled Erlenmeyer shake flasks (Corning, NY, USA) at a working volume of 30 mL and passaged every 2-4 days at a seeding density of 1.6 x 10^5 viable cells/mL, to ensure they were always in exponential phase. Cultures were regularly checked for Mycoplasma contamination with MycoAlert-Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza, Basel, Switzerland). The cell concentration, viability and cell diameter were measured with Vi-CELL XR (Beckman Coulter, CA, USA). The device was calibrated with ViaCheck-Concentration Control (Bangs Laboratories, Inc., IN, USA). The cell volume was calculated from the the cell diameter, assuming the cells have a spherical shape. All experiments were performed at passage 3-4 after thawing. To determine the duration of the exponential phase, batch cultures were run in triplicates at a working volume 100 mL in 250 mL flasks and three samples were taken per day in 6 h intervals for cell counting. Subsequently, batch cultures were run in 500 mL non-baffled Erlenmeyer shake flasks at a working volume of 200 mL. Three replicates were inoculated at one time point and another three 6 h later. During the exponential phase, samples were taken 3x per day in 6 h intervals for cell counting and extracellular metabolites, which covered 4 time points per day because of the time shift (except for batches K1par-8mMCD and K1par-0mMCD, which were done only in triplicates). Another three replicates were run for quantification of dry mass and biomass components. Cultures were monitored by cell counting and the biomass samples were taken at one time point in the mid-exponential phase. Afterwards, these cultures were finished because of the big volume reduction. 10^7 cells were taken for lipid quantification and approximately 1 mg for protein amino acid analysis (exact cell dry weights for each sample are specified in the Sample sheet). Cells were centrifuged at 200 x g for 8 min, washed with PBS and centrifuged again. The samples for amino acid analysis were stored as pellets, the samples for lipidomics were resuspended in 1 mL MeOH. All samples were stored at -80 °C until further analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Preparation of blanks for lipidomics (DI-MS + LC-MS):</strong></p><p>Two types of blank samples were prepared for the lipidomics measurements:</p><p>Blank: cultivation medium without cells which underwent the lipid extraction process as described in the Extraction protocol.</p><p>Solventblank: Disolving agent (isopropanol/ methanol/ chloroform 4:2:1 (v/v/v) containing 7.5 mM ammonium formate).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Lipid analysis</strong></p><p>Biological replicates (extraction replicates of the same Fermentation Batch) and technical replicates (multiple measurement of the bioligical replicates) are indicated in the Replicate column in the Assay table.</p>	<p><strong>Lipid extraction for DI-MS and LC-MS (SIMPLEX protocol):</strong></p><p>Cell pellets (10^7 cells) resuspended in 1 mL MeOH were stored at -80 °C in 2&nbsp;mL microcentrifuge tubes until further processing. The thawed cells were lysed in a waterbath sonicator at 4 °C (Example settings for Bioruptor NGS (Diagenode): 30 s ON, 30 s OFF x 30 cycles at high intensity) and placed on ice. SPLASH LIPIDOMIX Mass Spec Standard, 10 μL and 5 μL Cardiolipin Mix I were added as internal standards and the samples were transferred into 5 mL microcentrifuge tubes. 3375 μL ice-cold MTBE was added and incubated at 4 °C for 1 h under agitation. After 846 μL of 0.1% (w/w) ammonium formate solution were added the mixture was centrifuged for 5 min at 3000 x g for complete phase separation. The upper phase (1 mL) was transferred into the muffled glass vials and dried under nitrogen flow. The dried lipids were stored at 4 °C until measurement.</p>	<p><strong>DI-MS for lipid analysis:</strong></p><p>For the direct infusion analysis, a robotic nanoflow ion source TriVersa NanoMate (Advion BioSciences, Ithaca NY, USA) was coupled to a high field Q Exactive H quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany). The dried samples were diluted in 500 µL isopropanol:methanol:chloroform 4:2:1&nbsp;(v/v/v) containing 7.5 mM ammonium formate and 30&nbsp;µL were placed in a 96 twin.tec well plate (Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany). NanoESI chips with spraying nozzles of 5&nbsp;µm nominal internal diameter were used and the whole NanoMate was controlled by the Chipsoft 8.3.1 software (both Advion BioSciences). Following settings were applied: ionization voltage 1.25&nbsp;kV(+)/&nbsp;-1.25&nbsp;kV; backpressure 0.9&nbsp;psi; capillary temperature 250&nbsp;°C, S-Lens RF level 60. Each sample was measured for 17 min and polarity switching was triggered after 8&nbsp;min (1 min afterwards for equilibration) via contact closure signal by the mass spectrometer. For each polarity, only MS1 spectra were acquired at the beginning for 30 s before 200 data independent acquisition (DIA) scans alternated with a MS1 scan for quantification. In MS1, the resolution was set to 240,000, the automatic gain control (AGC) target to 1e6 and the maximum injection time (IT) to 150&nbsp;ms. For the DIA scans, a resolution of 60000 was applied and the AGC target and the max IT was set to 2e^5 and 130&nbsp;ms, respectively. Normalized collision energy (NCE) of 25 was used in positive mode and 28 in negative mode. The scan range was set to 200-1600 m/z in both modes.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted GC-EI-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>After hydrolysis protein amino acid quantification was performed on a GC-EI-MS/MS platform (7890B GC with 7010B triple&nbsp;quadrupole GC-MS, Agilent) in three separate batches each calibrated independently. The samples were measured in batches following the assignment to the hydrolysis batches (Batch number (hydrolysis)). Isotopically labeled ”Cell Free” Amino Acid Mix&nbsp;(U-13C, 97-99% &amp; U-15N, 97-99%, Cambridge Isotope Laboratories), which was pretreated prior to addition to the samples before hydrolysis, was used as internal standard. Data evaluation was based on external calibration and internal standardization (AA standards of certified purity and stability were used). EI was carried out at 70 eV and 230<em> </em>°C. Selective MS/MS transitions were used for both the target analyte and the isotopically labeled internal standard compounds. More detailed information on GC-EI-MS/MS analysis and the preparation of the internal standard is published previously<strong>[1]</strong>. Arginine (quantitative evaluation in GC as ornithine), glutamate and histidine were measured and quantified both using LC-MS/MS and GC-MS/MS.</p><p>Results in the MAF file are given as nmol/mg CDW.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted LC-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>After hydrolysis quantification of arginine, glutamate and histidine were performed on an LC-MS/MS platform (Thermo Scientific CTC PAL autosampler, Accela 1250 pump, TSQ Vantage MS/MS) in three separate batches each calibrated independently. The samples were measured in batches following the assignment to the hydrolysis batches (Batch number (hydrolysis)) employing the following MS settings for the heated electrospray source: capillary temperature 350 °C, spray voltage 3300 V, sheath gas pressure 60 psig, auxiliary gas pressure 15 psig, vaporizer temperature 350 °C, scan polarity positive, selective MS/MS transitions for both target analytes and isotopically labeled internal standard compounds.</p><p>Results in the MAF file are given as nmol/mg CDW.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Nagrath D, editor. Metabolic Flux Analysis in Eukaryotic Cells; Robust analytical methods for the accurate quantification of the total biomass composition of mammalian cells. Methods in Molecular Biology; 2020. p. 119–60.</p>	<p><strong>DI-MS for lipid analysis and RP-LC-MS for lipid analysis:</strong></p><p>The raw files were converted with MSConvert into mzML, following the description at the LipidXplorer Wiki (https://lifs.isas.de/lipidxplorer.html). The open-source software LipidXplorer was used for identification and will be described under metabolite identification. Data processing after metabolite identification was performed in R and the scripts are available in Mendeley Data at http://dx.doi.org/10.17632/g3wz7spyc9.1 in the folders Figure 4 and biomass_equations. The data in the Assay table are presented in nmol/10^7 cells.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted GC-EI-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>The raw files were converted with MSConvert into mzML. The data in the Assay table are presented in nmol/mg of cell dry weight (the cell dry weights per sample are provided in the Sample table.) Further data processing steps were performed in R and the scripts are available in Mendeley Data at http://dx.doi.org/10.17632/g3wz7spyc9.1 in the folders Figure 3 and biomass_equations.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted LC-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>The raw files were converted with MSConvert into mzML. Data processing was performed in R and the scripts are available in Mendeley Data at http://dx.doi.org/10.17632/g3wz7spyc9.1 in the folders Figure 4 and biomass_equations. The data in the Assay table are presented in nmol/10^7 cells.</p>	<p><strong>DI-MS for lipid analysis:</strong></p><p>Data evaluation for identification and quantification was performed with LipidXplorer software. The spectra were imported into a Master Scan database using the following settings: mass tolerance 5 ppm, min occupation of 0, intensity threshold 10000&nbsp;(MS1)/&nbsp;5000&nbsp;(MS2), resolution 260000&nbsp;(MS1)/&nbsp;65000&nbsp;(MS2), resolution gradient -102&nbsp;(MS1)/&nbsp;-60&nbsp;(MS2). The MFQL files used for identification can be found in the supplementary. Limits of detection (LODs) were calculated according to EURACHEM, The Fitness for Purpose of Analytical Methods, 2nd edition (2014) by repetitive injections of a low concentrated internal standard. Filtering and quantification was performed with R. The R scripts can be found in Mendeley data at http://dx.doi.org/10.17632/g3wz7spyc9.1 in the folders 'Figure 4' and 'biomass_equations'.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>RP-LC-MS for lipid analysis:</strong></p><p>For mass spectrometry a high field Thermo Scientific&nbsp;Q Exactive HF quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer&nbsp;equipped with an electrospray source was used. The following parameters were used: sheath gas 35, auxiliary gas 5, spray&nbsp;voltage 2.8 kV in negative and 3.5 kV in positive mode, capillary temperature 220 °C, S-Lens RF level 30 and auxiliary gas&nbsp;heater 300 °C. Spectral data were acquired in profile mode. The full MS runs in positive mode were acquired in the range of&nbsp;200-2000 m/z at a resolution of 120,000, an AGC target at 1e^6 and a maximum IT of 200 ms. Skyline 4.2 was used for the&nbsp;quantification.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted identification and quantification of protein amino acids via GC-MS/MS and LC-MS/MS:</strong></p><p>Identification was based on selective MS/MS transition and retention time in comparison to standards run in the same batch.</p><p>The following list shows, which hydrolysis method and instrument for the quantification was chosen for the results reported. Results from LC-MS/MS and GC-MS/MS did not differ significantly. The <strong><em>results reported in the MAF file are given as nmol/mgCDW</em></strong></p><p><br></p><ul><li>alanine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>arginine: HCL / LC-MS/MS</li><li>aspartate: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>cysteine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>glutamate: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>glycine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>histidine: Batch 1: HCL / GC-MS/MS, Batch 2 and 3: HCL / LC-MS/MS</li><li>isoleucine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>leucine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>lysine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>methionine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>phenylalanine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>proline: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li><li>serine: MSA / GC-MS/MS</li><li>threonine: MSA / GC-MS/MS</li><li>tyrosine: MSA / GC-MS/MS</li><li>valine: HCL / GC-MS/MS</li></ul>	<p><strong>RP-LC-MS for lipid analysis:</strong></p><p>As cholesterol (ST) and cardiolipin (CL) were out of the measurement range of shotgun MS, they were additionally quantified by Reversed-phase (RP) chromatography high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS). The samples were prepared with SIMPLEX protocol (see Extraction) and dissolved in IPA:MeOH:CHCl3 4:2:1 (v/v/v) containing 7.5 mM ammonium formate. Acquity HSS T3 (2.1 mm x 150 mm, 1.8 µm, Waters) with a VanGuard Pre-column (2.1 mm x 5 mm,100 Å, 1.8 µm) was used for the chromatography with column temperature of 40 °C and flow rate 250 µL/min. The temperature of the autosampler was 10 °C. Before each injection, the injector needle was washed with 75% IPA, 25% H2O, 0.1% formic acid for 5 s. The injection volume of the samples was 2 µL. Solvent A was ACN:H2O (3:2, v/v) and solvent B IPA:ACN (9:1, v/v), both with 0.1% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium formate. The following gradient was used: 0-2 min&nbsp;30% B, 2-17 min ramp to 75% B, 15-17 ramp to 100% B, 17- 22 100% B, 22 min fast switch to 30% B and equilibrated at the&nbsp;starting conditions for 5 min (22.0-27.0 30% B).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted GC-EI-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>The protein amino acids were quantified by gas chromatography - electron ionization - tandem mass spectrometry (GC-EI-MS/MS) in three separate batches (B1, B2, B3). The assignment to the GC-EI-MS/MS batches followed the assignment to the hydrolysis batches (Batch number (hydrolysis)). The hydrolysates were dried, silylated and separated using a 7890B GC (Agilent) equipped with a&nbsp;5% diphenyl 95% dimethyl polysiloxane GC-column (30 m,&nbsp;0.25 mm ID, 0.25 mm film thickness, Phenomenex ZB5 MS) and a non-polar guard column (3 m x 0.25 mm ID, Phenomenex). The Programmed Temperature Vaporizer (PTV, Gerstel) was equipped with a baffled Siltek liner (Gerstel) and was operated in the split mode (split ratio 1:30). PTV temperature program: 70 °C (0.1 min), 12 °C/s to 260 °C (hold for 1 min), 12 °C/s to 300 °C (hold for 5 min). GC temperature program:160 °C (1 min), 20 °C/min to 250 °C, 5 °C/min to 270 °C, 30 °C/min to 310 °C ( 3.5 min). Injection volume: 1 µL. Transferline temperature: 280 °C. He column flow:1.2 ml/min in constant flow mode (precolumn flow 1.1 ml/min). More detailed information on GC-EI-MS/MS analysis is published previously<strong>[1]</strong>. Results for arginine (quantitative evaluation in GC as ornithine), glutamate and histidine were measured and quantified both using LC-MS/MS and GC-MS/MS.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted LC-MS/MS for protein amino acid analysis:</strong></p><p>After hydrolysis quantification of arginine, glutamate and histidine were performed also on an LC-MS/MS platform (Thermo Scientific CTC PAL autosampler, Accela 1250 pump, TSQ Vantage MS/MS) in three separate batches (B1, B2, B3). The assignment to the GC-EI-MS/MS batches followed the assignment to the hydrolysis batches (Batch number (hydrolysis). An XBridge Amide analytical column (150 x 2.1 mm, 3.5 µm particle size, Waters, Milford, USA) was used for a hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) gradient separation employing a column temperature of 40 °C and an injection volume of 5 µL. Solvent A was acetonitrile, solvent B was 10 mmol/l ammonium formate with a mobile phase gradient of 10-70% (v/v) B applied between 1-12 min at a flow rate of 350 µL/min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Széliová et al. Metabolic Flux Analysis in Eukaryotic Cells; Robust analytical methods for the accurate quantification of the total biomass composition of mammalian cells. Nagrath D, editor. Methods in Molecular Biology; 2020. p. 119–60.</p>	<p><strong>Hydrolysis</strong></p><p>For subsequent protein amino acid analysis by GC-EI-MS/MS and LC-MS/MS cell pellets of all samples were hydrolyzed both with HCl combined with prior oxidation and with methanesulfonic acid (MSA). The CDW of the cell pellets used for hydrolysis is given in the Samples table (Cell dry weight (hydrolysis)). The samples were hydrolyzed in three Batches: B1, B2, B3. The number of the Batch in which samples were hydrolyzed and finally also analyzed is also given in the Samples tables (Batch number (hydrolysis)). The hydrolysate which was used for quantification of a specific amino acid (HCl or MSA) is stated in the protocol Metabolite Identification. During hydrolysis, glutamine is converted to glutamate and asparagine to aspartate, therefore these pairs are measured as a sum. Tryptophan is degraded in both hydrolysis methods and was hence not quantified. A detailed description of the hydrolysis methods is published previously<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Derivatization</strong></p><p>For GC-MS/MS analysis the protein amino acids were just in time - online derivatized with N-(tert-butyldimethylsilyl)-N-methyltrifluoroacetamide with 1% tert-butyldimethylchlorosilane on an automated sample preparation system (MPS2, Gerstel). A detailed description of derivatization is published previously<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Nagrath D, editor.

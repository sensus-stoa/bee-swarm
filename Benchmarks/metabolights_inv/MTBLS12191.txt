Study Identifier	MTBLS12191
Study Title	FOXK1 and FOXK2 Promote Cardiomyocyte Proliferation and Heart Regeneration
Study Description	Promoting the proliferation of endogenous cardiomyocytes represents a promising strategy for treating cardiac injuries. Identifying key factors that regulate cardiomyocyte proliferation can advance the development of novel therapies for heart regeneration. Here we identify that FOXK1 and FOXK2 act as master regulators of cardiomyocyte proliferation and metabolism. The expression of FOXK1 and FOXK2 decreased with postnatal heart development. Cardiomyocyte-specific knockout of Foxk1 or Foxk2 inhibited neonatal heart regeneration after myocardial infarction (MI) injury. Conversely, AAV9-mediated cardiomyocyte-specific overexpression of FOXK1 or FOXK2 prolonged the postnatal proliferative window of cardiomyocytes and enhanced cardiac repair in adult mice by promoting endogenous cardiomyocyte proliferation after MI. Mechanistically, FOXK1 and FOXK2 induce Ccnb1 and Cdk1 transcription and cardiomyocyte cell cycle progression, respectively. Ccnb1 knockdown hindered FOXK1 overexpression-induced cardiomyocyte proliferation, and the same effect was observed when Cdk1 was knocked down in FOXK2 overexpressing cardiomyocytes. Additionally, we further revealed that FOXK1 and FOXK2 induced a metabolic shift toward glycolysis by promoting HIF1α expression in cardiomyocytes, which favors cardiomyocyte proliferation. Our findings identify FOXK1 and FOXK2 as critical triggers of cardiomyocyte proliferation and define these two transcription factors as novel therapeutic targets for myocardial infarction.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Briefly, 2-month-old WT mice were injected with AAV9-GFP, AAV9-Foxk1 and AAV9-Foxk2 via tail vein. After 14 days, the heart was quickly excised and intubated to Langendorff perfusion apparatus maintained at 37 °C using a temperature-controlled bath. The isolated hearts were perfused retrogradely with Krebs-Henseleit (KH) buffer containing U-13C-glucose (8 mM) for 60 min. Subsequently, the heart was rapidly frozen and pulverized in liquid nitrogen.</p>	<p>The heart samples were sonicated and extracted with 80% methanol solution. The mixtures were centrifuged at 18,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C, then the supernatant was concentrated by centrifugation, 100 μL of 80% methanol was added for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>For LC-MS analysis, the samples were analyzed by ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) system (ACQUITY UPLC-Xevo TQ-S, Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). For HPLC, column: UPLC HILIC (4.6-mm column, Amide XBridge, Waters), column temp: 40 °C, sample manager temp: 10 °C, mobile phases: A = 5% acetonitrile in water (with 20 mM ammonium hydroxide and ammonium acetate); and B = acetonitrile, gradient conditions: 0.0-3.5 min (85-32% B), 3.5-12.0 min (32-2% B), 12.0-16.5 min (2% B), 16.5-17.0 min (2-85% B), 17.0-25.5 min (85% B), flow rate: 0.40 mL/min, and injection vol.: 5.0 μL.</p>	<p>For LC-MS analysis, the samples were analyzed by ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) system (ACQUITY UPLC-Xevo TQ-S, Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). For mass spectrometer, capillary 2.5 (ESI-) kV, source temp. 150 °C, desolvation temp. 550 °C and desolvation gas flow 1000 L/h.</p>	<p>The raw data files generated by UPLC-MS/MS were processed using the MassLynx software (v4.1, Waters, Milford, MA, USA) to perform peak integration, calibration and quantitation for each metabolite. The free and open-source R language (version 4.1.1) was used for subsequent statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by standard identification and integration using MassLynx software. Mass spectrometry-based quantitative metabolomics refers to the determination of the concentration of a substance in an unknown sample by comparing the unknown to a set of standard samples. Mass spectrum-based metabolomics refers to the determination of metabolites by comparing an unknown sample to a standard set of samples.</p>

Study Title	Engineered Oncolytic VSV Enhances Tumor Killing in HBV-Positive HCC
Study Description	Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection being a major contributor to its development. Despite this, effective treatment for HBV-associated HCC remains a significant challenge. In this study, we combined the strengths of vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) and therapeutic vaccines to propose a novel strategy for treating HBV-related liver cancer. We engineered an oncolytic virus that delivers hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and the IL-15 cytokine directly to tumor cells, thereby enhancing immune activation and promoting tumor cell destruction. As the tumor cells die, they release additional virus particles, spreading HBsAg and IL-15 expression to neighboring tumor cells. This approach demonstrated promising efficacy in multiple HBV-positive HCC mouse models.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Collection of serum samples from mice with no high salt in the sample and no descaling agents.</p>	<p>1. Remove the sample stored at -80 °C, thaw at room temperature, remove 100 μL of sample, add 20 μL of internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine, 0.3 mg/mL, configured in methanol) and vortex for 10 s. </p><p>2. Add 300 μL of methanol:acetonitrile, a protein precipitant (2:1, v:v), and vortex for 1 min.</p><p>3. Extract the sample by sonication with an ice water bath for 10 min, and leave for 30 min at -20 °C.</p><p>4. Centrifuge for 10 min (13,000 rpm, 4 °C), then volatilise 300 μL of supernatant in an LC-MS injection vial.</p><p>4. centrifuge for 10 min (13000 rpm, 4 °C), take 300 μL of supernatant into the LC-MS injection vial to be evaporated;</p><p>5. redissolve with 300 μL of methanol:water (1:4, v:v), add 10 μL of the internal standard (Lyso PC17:0, 0.1 mg/mL, methanol), vortex for 30 s, and then ultrasonic for 3 min.</p><p>6. leave at -20 °C for 2 h;</p><p>7. centrifuge for 10 min (13,000 rpm, 4 °C), and 150 μL of supernatant was loaded into an LC-MS injection vial with a foot-lined tube for analysis</p>	<p>LC-MS is the most commonly used technique in metabolomics. It uses a liquid chromatography column to separate metabolites of different chemical properties, and then detects and quantifies these metabolites by mass spectrometry. The advantages of LC-MS are mainly in the simplicity of sample preparation and pre-treatment, good experimental reproducibility, high resolution, and a wide range of separations and analyses, while the disadvantages are the insufficiently developed database and the relative difficulty of characterisation.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry is another commonly used metabolomics technique that generates spectral data based on the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) and the relative abundance of metabolites. In mass spectrometry, a biological sample first needs to be ionised and then analysed by a mass spectrometer. Ionised compounds of different molecules will produce different peak patterns, thus defining the fingerprint of the metabolite.</p>	<p>Statistical analysis is the core process of metabolomics data processing, through different analytical methods and tools can be detected in different groups of samples metabolic profile analysis, and then use the relevant statistical software (XploreMET\SIMCA, etc.) or the R language to carry out univariate statistical analysis or multivariate statistical analysis to find and visualise the difference metabolite information so that researchers can more intuitively capture the key difference metabolite information and carry out their visualisation. Univariate statistical analysis or multivariate statistical analysis using relevant statistical software (XploreMET\SIMCA, etc.) or R language can be used to search for differential metabolites and visualise them, so that researchers can capture key differential metabolite information more intuitively and carry out in-depth studies on them.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>Non-targeted metabolomics is achieved by comparing the retention time (RT), primary mass spectrometry (MS1) and secondary mass spectrometry (MS2) of the metabolites detected in the actual sample with the three information in the database.</p>

STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS12198
Study Title	Targeting the Ceramidase ACER3 Attenuates Cholestasis in Mice by Mitigating Bile Acid Overload via Unsaturated Ceramide-mediated LXRβ Signaling Transduction
Study Description	<p>In this work, we show that ACER3 expression is upregulated in cholestatic livers and positively correlates with the severity of CLI in patients. Targeting ACER3 increases CER(d18:1/18:1) in the cholestatic liver, which binds to LXRβ and activates its signaling transduction to enhance BA sulfation and restore lipogenesis. These mechanisms mitigate BA overload in hepatocytes and attenuate CLI. Our findings highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting ACER3 and CER(d18:1/18:1) to attenuate CLI.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>BDL was conducted to induce CLI in 8-week-old mice, while Sham operation was used as control. The procedures of BDL required the midventral laparotomy and the isolation of the common bile duct above the duodenum. Following this, the common bile duct was ligated at two sites, thereby generating a model of obstructive cholestasis 1. Mice were sacrificed 7 days after the BDL operation. The tissues and serum were collected and stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>1.Tag, C. G. et al. Bile duct ligation in mice: induction of inflammatory liver injury and fibrosis&nbsp;by obstructive cholestasis. J. Vis. Exp., (2015).</p>	<p>Lipids were extracted from approximately 30 mg of frozen tissues using a modified version of the Bligh and Dyer's method.&nbsp;Briefly, tissues were homogenized in 900 µL of chloroform:methanol:MilliQ H2O (3:6:1) (v/v/v).&nbsp;The homogenate was then incubated at 1500 rpm for 30 min at 4 °C.&nbsp;At the end of the incubation, 350 µL of deionized water and 300 µL of chloroform were added to induce phase separation.&nbsp;The samples were then centrifuged and the lower organic phase containing lipids was extracted into a clean tube.&nbsp;Lipid extraction was repeated once by adding 500 µL of chloroform to the remaining aqueous phase, and the lipid extracts were pooled into a single tube and dried in the SpeedVac under OH mode.&nbsp;Samples were stored at -80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>The lipid extracts were analyzed by a Thermo Fisher Scientific Vanquish UHPLC. In total 5 μL of lipid extract was injected, and chromatographic separation was carried out at 40 °C on an Acquity UPLC columns C30 (150 x 2.1 mm, 3.0 μm). The mobile phase A: 10 mM HCOONH4, 0.1% formic acid, ACN:H2O = 60:40, mobile phase B: 10 mM HCOONH4, 0.1% formic acid, isopropanol:ACN = 90:10.</p>	<p>Both positive and negative ion modes were used. Mass scan was performed as the following settings: Orbitrap Resolution: 60,000; Scan Range: 200-2000 m/z; Radiofrequency Lens: 60%; Gain Control Target: 2.0e5; Maximum Injection Time: 100 ms; Polarity: Positive.</p>	<p>The identification of lipid molecular species was conducted by Lipid Search software (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).</p>	<p>The identification of lipid molecular species was conducted by Lipid Search software (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).</p>

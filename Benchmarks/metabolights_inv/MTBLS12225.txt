Study Title	Moxibustion Inhibits Inflammation in Monosodium Urate Crystal-induced Gouty Arthritis Model Rats through Metabolomic Regulation
Study Description	<p>Background: Moxibustion is a form of therapy that to warm the acupoints located skin by using dried mugwort leaves. It is widely used to treat gouty arthritis (GA). However, the mechanism of moxibustion on improving GA has not been fully revealed. In this study, we explore the mechanism of moxibustion on GA via metabolomics combined with traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) theory.</p><p>Methods: Three days before model&amp;nbsp;induction, the rats&amp;nbsp;of moxibustion&amp;nbsp;groups were treated with moxibustion in&amp;nbsp;the&amp;nbsp;ST36 and SP6, and then, a GA rat model induced by monosodium urate&amp;nbsp;(MSU) was established. &amp;nbsp;Biological samples, including joint synovial tissue and serum&amp;nbsp;samples,&amp;nbsp;were collected and measured&amp;nbsp;by histopathological staining, molecular biology assays and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)-based metabolomics.</p><p>Results: We found that moxibustion could reduce the ankle edema induced by MSU crystals, decrease the expression of related proinflammatory genes, decrease the levels of serum IL-18 and IL-1β, and restore the metabolism of glycerol phospholipids, niacin and nicotinamide in GA model rats.</p><p>Conclusion: Moxibustion can regulate the metabolism of GA model rats widely to inhibit inflammation. Our research deepens our understanding of the complex mechanisms of moxibustion and promotes the application of moxibustion in the clinical practice.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male SD rats weighing approximately 180 ± 20 g were purchased from GemPharmatech Company Limited (Guangdong, China). The animal study protocol was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Peking University Shenzhen Graduate School (Permit Number: AP0044001). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the regulations of the “International Council for Laboratory Animal Science”.</p><p><br></p><p>The rats were randomly divided into 4 groups (6 rats per group): the control (Con) group (the group that did not receive any specific intervention in the experimen), acute gouty arthritis model (AGA) group, moxibustion-treated (Mox) group and colchicine-treated (Col) Group. All animals were maintained under controlled environmental conditions&nbsp;(21 ± 1 °C, relative humidity of 65 ± 5%, and 12 h light/dark cycle) with free access to water and food.&nbsp;After 3&nbsp;days of adaptation, all rats except for the control rats were subjected to&nbsp;AGA modeling via&nbsp;injection&nbsp;to&nbsp;the right ankle&nbsp;with 0.3 mL 5% monosodium urate&nbsp;(MSU) [20]. The control rats were administered the same amount of saline during the experimental period.</p>	<p>Moxibustion treatments were carried out 3 days before MSU injection. The bilateral Sanyinjiao acupoint (SP 6, located 1 cm straight above the tip of the medial malleolus of the hind limbs) and Zusanli acupoint (ST 36, located 3 mm below the fibular head and lateral to the anterior tubercle of the tibia) were selected as the body stimulation sites for the rats and were shaved for exposure [21]. Prior to treatment, the heads of the rats were completely covered with a breathable and opaque headgear to help the rats remain calm during treatment.&nbsp;Moxibustion&nbsp;stimulation at&nbsp;a corresponding temperature of 42 °C at&nbsp;the ST36 and SP6 acupoints&nbsp;was applied with a small animal moxibustion strip for 15 min&nbsp;once a day&nbsp;for 6 consecutive days.&nbsp;During&nbsp;the process of moxibustion intervention, a digital temperature recorder was used to detect the temperature of the moxibustion site during, before and after moxibustion, and the target temperature was obtained by adjusting the distance between the moxibustion strips and the acupoint. Rats in the Con group&nbsp;and the AGA group were subjected to the same procedure but were not treated with moxibustion.</p><p>Colchicine&nbsp;(0.1 mg/kg) was orally administered once a day for 6 consecutive days 3 days&nbsp;before MSU injection as a positive&nbsp;control [18].</p><p>All rats were anesthetized with 2.5% tribromoethanol (0.3 mL/100 g body weight) after treatment.&nbsp;4-6 mL serum&nbsp;samples&nbsp;were collected from the abdominal aorta, and the rats were sacrificed. The ankles of the rats were surgically removed. In total 3 ankles from each group were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The&nbsp;synovium of the other ankles was&nbsp;surgically removed,&nbsp;placed in&nbsp;a 1.5 mL sterilized centrifuge tube, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and then transferred to a -80 °C ultralow&nbsp;temperature freezer&nbsp;for storage.</p><p>Prior to LC-MS&nbsp;analysis, 100 μL of serum samples was added to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube with 400 μL of a solution (acetonitrile:methanol, 1:1, v:v) containing 0.02 mg/mL internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine) to extract metabolites. The samples were mixed by vortexing&nbsp;for 30 s and low-temperature sonicated for 30 min&nbsp;(5 °C,&nbsp;40 KHz). The samples were placed at -20 °C&nbsp;for 30 min&nbsp;to precipitate the proteins.&nbsp;Then,&nbsp;the samples were centrifuged for 15 min&nbsp;(4 °C, 13,000 x g).&nbsp;The supernatant was removed,&nbsp;and the sample was blown dry under nitrogen. The sample was then resolubilized with 100 µL of solution (acetonitrile:water, 1:1) and extracted by low-temperature ultrasonication for 5 min&nbsp;(5 °C, 40 kHz), followed by centrifugation at 13,000 x g and 4 °C for 10 min. The supernatant was transferred to sample vials for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p><p>As a part of the system conditioning and quality control process, a pooled quality control&nbsp;sample (QC) was prepared by mixing equal volumes of all samples. The QC samples were&nbsp;disposed and tested in the same manner as the analytic samples. It helped to represent the whole&nbsp;sample set, which would be injected at regular intervals (every 6 samples) in order to monitor&nbsp;the stability of the analysis.</p>	<p>LC-MS analysis of the samples was conducted on a Thermo UHPLC-Exploris 240&nbsp;system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5:47.5, v/v) (solvent B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min,&nbsp;and the column temperature was 40 °C. The injection volume was 3&nbsp;μL.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometric data were collected using a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. The optimal conditions were as follows: aux gas heating temperature, 350 °C; capillary temperature, 320 °C; sheath gas flow rate,&nbsp;60 psi; aux&nbsp;gas flow rate,&nbsp;20 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) voltage, -3500 V&nbsp;in negative mode and 3400 V&nbsp;in positive mode; and normalized&nbsp;collision energy, 20-40-60 eV rolling for MS/MS. The full MS resolution was 60000, and the MS/MS resolution was 15000. Data acquisition was performed in Data Dependent Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS data were converted into a common format by Progenesis QI software (Waters, Milford, USA) through baseline filtering, peak identification, peak integration, retention time correction and peak alignment. Then, the data matrix containing sample names, m/z values, retention times and peak intensities was exported for further analyses. Moreover, the metabolites were identified by searching databases, and the main databases used were the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) (http://www.hmdb.ca), Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu) and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China).</p>	<p>Differentially abundant metabolites among the two groups were mapped to their biochemical pathways through metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis based on the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (http://www. genome.jp/kegg). These metabolites can be classified according to the pathways they are involved in or the functions they perform. The Python package “scipy.stats” (https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy) was used to perform enrichment analyses and obtain the most relevant biological pathways for the experimental treatments.</p>

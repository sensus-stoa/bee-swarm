Study Title	CarS2-mediated cysteine catabolism drives brown fat development and thermogenesis through persulfidating EBF2
Study Description	Targeting transcriptional regulatory complexes is a promising strategy to activate thermogenic fat and treat obesity. In this study, we focused on identifying critical and specific metabolic pathways enriched during brown adipocyte differentiation. By analyzing metabolomics and RNA sequencing results from precursor and mature brown and white adipocytes, we revealed cysteine catabolism pathway is a unique metabolic process that is highly increased in brown adipocyte differentiation. Then, we demonstrated cysteine persulfidation synthase CarS2 is directly induced by EBF2, mediates cysteine catabolism and triggers brown fat protein persulfidation both in vitro and in vivo. Loss of CarS2 in thermogenic fat blocks brown fat formation in both male and female mice, and CarS2 depletion in mice reduces thermogenesis and energy expenditure. More importantly, CarS2 generates cysteine persulfide, and its derivative H2S cell-autonomously induces brown adipogenesis and enhances uncoupled respiration by persulfidating EBF2. Mechanistically, persulfidated EBF2 facilitates its interaction with PPARγ or BRG1, enhancing the recruitment of the EBF2-PPARγ complex to the browning gene promoter, thus driving brown fat development and boosting thermogenic activity. Furthermore, treatment with CarS2 coenzyme PLP or H2S donor elevates brown adipocyte function and ameliorates obesity progression in mice under HFD feeding, indicating a novel metabolite-based strategy for obesity treatment.

Study Protocol Description	<p>3T3-L1 cell was collected at day 0 and day 8 during differentiation. Cells were scraped in PBS, and the cell pellet were acquired by centrifuge at 1000 rpm, 5 min, 4 °C. The cell pellets were quickly frozen by liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C until metabolite detection.</p>	<p>The sample was thawed on ice, then mixed with 500 µL of 80% methanol/water (precooled at -20 °C) and vortexed for 2 min under the condition of 2500 rpm. The sample was frozenin liquid nitrogen for 5 min, removed on ice for 5 min, after that, the sample was vortexed for 2 min. The previous step was repeated for 3x times. The sample was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. Take 300 μL of supernatant into a new centrifuge tube and place the supernatant in -20 °C refrigerator for 30 min. Then the supernatant was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. After centrifugation, transfer 200 μL of supernatant through Protein Precipitation Plate for further LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>The sample extracts were analyzed using an LC-ESI-MS/MS system. The analytical conditions were as follows: Reverse phase assay: column, Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 (100 mm x 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm); solvent system, water with 0.05% formic acid (A), acetonitrile with 0.05% formic acid (B). The gradient was started at 5% B (0.0 min), increased to 95% B (8.0-9.5 min), finally ramped back to 5% B (9.6-12.0 min); flow rate, 0.35 mL/min; temperature, 40 °C; injection volume, 2 μL. HILIC assay: column, ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 μm); solvent system, water with 10 mM Ammonium acetate and 0.3% Ammonium hydroxide (A), 90% acetonitrile/water (v/v) (B); The gradient was started at 95% B (0.0-1.2 min), decreased to 70% B (8.0 min), 50% B (9.0-11.0 min), 53 finally ramped back to 95% B (11.1-15.0 min); flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; temperature, 40 °C; injection volume, 2 μL.</p>	<p>AB 6500+ QTRAP LC-MS/MS System, equipped with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, operating in both positive and negative ion modes and controlled by Analyst 1.6 software (AB Sciex). The ESI source operation parameters were as follows: ion source, turbo spray; source temperature 550 °C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (Positive), -4500 V (Negative); curtain gas (CUR) was set at 35 psi; DP and CE for individual MRM transitions was done with further DP and CE optimization. A specific set of MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the plant hormones eluted within this period.</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS data underwent retention time correction, peak identification and peak alignment using MultiQuant 3.0.3. Subsequently, a data matrix containing information on retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity was obtained. The MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry information was compared with the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) (http://www.hmdb.ca) and KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp), among others. The MS mass error threshold was set to be less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on the secondary mass spectrometry matching score. The detected compounds were verified manually. The stability of the internal standard was assessed by checking whether the z-score values fell within 2x times the standard deviation. Finally, the data were analyzed in both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>All those metobolites were detected by MetWare (http://www.metware.cn) based on the AB Sciex QTRAP 6500 LC-MS/MS platform.</p>

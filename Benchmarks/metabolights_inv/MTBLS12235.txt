Study Title	Long-term application of biogas slurry altered the microbial community structure and metabolites in rhizosphere soil of poplar plantations
Study Description	<p>We aim to investigate the changes in the structure and function of rhizosphere microorganisms when different concentrations of biogas slurry are applied to the rhizosphere of poplar trees. By measuring the variations and types of rhizosphere metabolites, we can determine the microbial changes and their impact on the rhizosphere microenvironment of poplar trees.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>After long-term application of biogas slurry in the poplar plantation, we meticulously collected rhizosphere soil samples from around the poplar trees. These samples, which are expected to contain a diverse range of microbial communities and organic compounds influenced by the biogas slurry treatment, were carefully placed into sterile containers and immediately stored in a -80 °C freezer. This low-temperature storage ensures the preservation of metabolites and other substances, allowing us to conduct comprehensive and accurate analysis in subsequent research on soil health and ecosystem functioning.</p>	<p>1) The samples were taken out from the -80 °C freezer and thawed on ice. A 300 mg soil sample was weighed and mixed with 1 mL of a solvent mixture of methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1). The mixture was vortexed for 30 s to ensure thorough mixing, followed by sonication for 10 min; 2) The mixture was then centrifuged at 13,000 rpm at 4 °C for 15 min; 3) The supernatant (700 μL) was transferred to a freeze-dryer and dried until all solvents were evaporated; 4) The dried sample was reconstituted with 100 μL of acetonitrile:water (1:1) solution, vortexed for 30 s and sonicated for 10 min; 5) The reconstituted sample was centrifuged again at 13,000 rpm at 4 °C for 15 min; 6) A volume of 50 μL of the supernatant was transferred to an autosampler vial for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide 1.7 μm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Mobile phase: Phase A is ultrapure water (containing 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonia), Phase B is acetonitrile; Flow rate 0.5 mL/min, column temperature 40 °C, injection volume 2 μL.</p>	<p>Analysis were performed using an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600+ mass spectrometer. Electrospray ionization source temperature (TEM): 650 °C; Mass spectrometry voltage (ISVF): 5500 V (positive ions), -4500 V (negative ions); De-clustering voltage (DP): 60 V; Ion source gases: Gas 1 (Gas1) at 60 psi, Gas 2 (Gas2) at 60 psi and curtain gas (CUR) at 30 psi; Collision-induced dissociation parameters are set to high.</p>	<p>In this project, mass spectrometry data were statistically analyzed using the commercial software MasterView (SCIEX, USA) and a self-developed metabolomics analysis platform. Metabolite identification was based on a self-constructed database.</p>	<p>In non-targeted metabolomics experiments, QC (quality control) samples are typically interspersed among the test samples to assess the stability of the experiment. Based on the characteristics of QC and test samples, our platform has established multiple quality control points, which mainly include: QC sample TIC (total ion current) overlay plots, principal component analysis (PCA) and the CV (coefficient of variation) distribution of quality control features, among other indicators. These are used to evaluate the stability of the experiment and to determine whether the test samples are abnormal, ensuring the accuracy and stability of the test results.</p>

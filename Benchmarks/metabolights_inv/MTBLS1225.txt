Study Identifier	MTBLS1225
Study Title	Sacha inchi oil alleviates gut microbiota dysbiosis and improves hepatic lipid dysmetabolism in high-fat diet-fed rats
Study Description	Dietary ω-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are beneficial for humans against the development of hyperlipidaemia, but the underlying mechanisms are still poorly understood. Here, we demonstrated that oral consumption of sacha inchi oil, which is rich in α-linolenic acid, alleviated dyslipidemia, hepatic steatosis and inflammatory infiltration in high-fat diet (HFD)-fed rats. Sacha inchi oil administration reversed gut microbiota dysbiosis and altered the gut microbiota metabolome and in particular prevented bile acid dysmetabolism caused by a HFD. Sacha inchi oil intake ameliorated hepatic lipid dysmetabolism in HFD-fed rats, via potentiating the biosynthesis and reuptake of bile acids, reducing the de novo lipogenesis, promoting fatty acid beta-oxidation, and alleviating the dysregulation of glycerolipid, glycerophospholipid, and sphingolipid metabolisms. The results showed that dietary sacha inchi oil can alleviate gut microbiota dysbiosis and reduce lipid dysmetabolism in HFD rats, and provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms by which plant-derived ω-3 PUFAs prevent the development of hyperlipidaemia.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Animals and experimental design </strong></p><p>8-w-old male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (body weight ∼180–220 g) were obtained from the Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center (Foshan, China). All rats were housed 1 per cage in the Animal Center of Guangzhou Quality Supervision and Testing Institute (Guangzhou, China), under a 12-h light/dark cycle at 22 ± 2 °C. After a 1-w acclimation period, the rats were randomly allocated to 7 groups (n = 10) by feeding the following <strong>diets</strong>: (1) <strong>normal chow diet </strong>with 10 kcal% fat (<strong>CD </strong>group; research diets LF10B, Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center, Foshan, China; ESI Table S1†), (2) <strong>HFD</strong>-feeding with 45 kcal% fat (<strong>HFD </strong>group; Research diets HF45, Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center, Foshan, China; ESI Table S1†), (3) <strong>HFD</strong>-fed rats orally gavaged with<strong> low dose sacha inchi oil </strong>(0.5 mL/kg/d) (<strong>SLD </strong>treated group); (4) <strong>HFD</strong>-fed rats orally gavaged with <strong>middle dose sacha inchi oil </strong>(1.0 mL/kg/d) (<strong>SMD </strong>treated group); (5) <strong>HFD</strong>-fed rats orally gavaged with <strong>high dose sacha inchi oil</strong> (1.5 mL/kg/d) (<strong>SHD </strong>treated group); (6) <strong>HFD</strong>-fed rats orally gavaged with <strong>fish oil</strong> (1.0 mL/kg/d) (<strong>FD </strong>treated group); (7) <strong>HFD</strong>-fed rats orally gavaged with <strong>simvastatin </strong>(10 mg/kg/d) (<strong>SCD </strong>treated group). The <strong>fish oil</strong> and cold-pressed <strong>sacha inchi</strong> <strong>oil </strong>were kindly provided by BASF Health and Nutrition A/S (Omevital 3322 TG, Ludwigshafen, Germany) and Pure U-Multitude Biological Resources Co. Ltd (Puer, China), respectively. The composition of <strong>sacha inchi oil </strong>was identified in our previously reported study<strong>[1]</strong>; thereinto,<strong> sacha inchi oil </strong>mainly consists of 45.62% ± 0.38% α-linolenic acid, 8.39% ± 0.28% oleic acid, 38.11% ± 0.20% linoleic acid, and 63.89 ± 0.06 mg per 100 g tocopherol (ESI<strong> Table S2</strong> in the paper associated with this study). The animal treatment lasted for 8 w, during which both water and diet were consumed ad libitum, and body weight and food intake were recorded daily for each rat.</p><p><br></p><p>At the end of experiments, overnight fasted animals were anaesthetized by sodium pentothal injection (75 mg/kg). Blood were collected from the abdominal aorta and centrifuged at 1.500 g for 15 min to obtain serum samples. <strong>Stool </strong>samples were collected sterilely from the small intestine. The liver tissues were quickly removed, weighed, and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for further analyses. Partial liver and epididymal white adipose tissues (eWAT) were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for histological studies. The Animal and Ethics Review Committee of Guangzhou Quality Supervision and Testing Institute approved all the animal experimental protocols (ID: SYXK 2014-0137).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Li P, Deng J, Xiao N, Cai X, Wu Q, Lu Z, Yang Y, Du B. Identification of polyunsaturated triacylglycerols and CC location isomers in sacha inchi oil by photochemical reaction mass spectrometry combined with nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Food Chem. 2020 Mar 1;307:125568. doi: 10.1016/j.foodchem.2019.125568. Epub 2019 Oct 14.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p>	<p>Approximately 0.1 g of freeze-dried colonic contents, 500 μL of ice-cold prechilled methanol, and 0.1% formic acid mixture were added and homogenized on ice and vortex-mixed for 2 min. After centrifugation at 15,000 rpm at 4 °C for 5 min, the supernatant was added to LC–MS-grade water until the final concentration containing 60% methanol. The samples were injected into the LC–MS/MS system analysis after passing through a 0.22 μm filter.</p>	<p>LC–MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher). Samples were injected onto a Hyperil Gold column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm) using a 16 min linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min.&nbsp;The eluents for the positive polarity mode were eluent A (0.1% FA in Water) and eluent B (Methanol). The eluents for the negative polarity mode were eluent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9.0) and eluent B (Methanol). The solvent gradient was set as follows: 2% B, 1.5 min;&nbsp;2-100% B, 12.0 min; 100% B, 14.0 min；100-2% B, 14.1 min；2% B, 16 min.</p>	<p>Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with spray voltage of 3.2 kV, capillary temperature of 320°C, sheath gas flow rate of 35 arb and aux gas flow rate of 10 arb. </p>	<p>The raw data files generated by UHPLC–MS/MS were processed using <strong>Compound Discoverer 3.0 </strong>(CD 3.0, Thermo Fisher) to perform peak alignment, peak picking, and quantitation for each metabolite. The main parameters were set as follows: retention time tolerance, 0.2 min; actual mass tolerance, 5ppm; signal intensity tolerance, 30%; signal/noise ratio, 3; and minimum intensity ,100000. After that, peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity. The normalized data was used to predict the molecular formula based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks and fragment ions.</p>	<p>Peaks were then matched with the <strong>mzCloud </strong>(<a href='https://www.mzcloud.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org/</a>) and <strong>ChemSpider </strong>(<a href='http://www.chemspider.com/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.chemspider.com/</a>) database to receive accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results.</p>

Study Title	Osh2 mediates Candida resistance to miltefosine by regulating zymosterol transport
Study Description	Miltefosine has been identified for its antifungal properties, yet the mechanisms underlying its action and the development of resistance remain unclear. In this study, we induced drug resistance in various Candida species through concentration gradient exposure, successfully obtaining a miltefosine-resistant strain of Candida glabrata. Whole-genome sequencing revealed a premature stop codon mutation in the OSH2 gene within the resistant strain. To further investigate the function of the Osh2, we deleted it in Candida albicans, observing a significant increase in miltefosine resistance in the knockout strain. RNA-seq and lipidomics analyses indicated that Osh2 influences the ergosterol biosynthetic pathway by regulating zymosterol transport, thereby mediating miltefosine resistance. Additionally, exogenous supplementation of zymosterol significantly enhanced the resistance of Candida to miltefosine. Further studies revealed that deletion of the ERG11 gene, a rate-limiting enzyme in the zymosterol synthesis pathway, rendered the strain highly sensitive to miltefosine, while deletion of the ERG6 gene, which catalyzes the conversion of zymosterol to fecosterol, led to miltefosine resistance. This study is the first to elucidate that the Osh2 gene mediates miltefosine resistance in Candida by regulating zymosterol transport and affecting the ergosterol biosynthetic pathway, providing new theoretical insights into the mechanism of action of miltefosine and strategies to overcome fungal resistance.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Candida albicans&nbsp;SN152 (wild-type) and its isogenic&nbsp;osh2Δ/Δ&nbsp;mutant were cultured in YPD broth at 30 °C with agitation (200 rpm) to mid-log phase. Cells were treated with 2 μg/mL miltefosine or PBS (vehicle control) for 1 h, generating 4 experimental groups: (1) SN152 + miltefosine, (2) SN152 + PBS, (3)&nbsp;osh2Δ/Δ&nbsp;+ miltefosine, (4)&nbsp;osh2Δ/Δ&nbsp;+ PBS, each with 3 biological replicates (total n=12). Following treatment, cells were harvested by centrifugation, washed twice with ice-cold PBS, and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen prior to storage at -80 °C for subsequent omics analyses.</p>	<p>Lipid extraction was performed using a methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE)-based protocol. Briefly, frozen samples were spiked with a lipid internal standard mixture (e.g., Avanti SPLASH LIPIDOMIX) and homogenized with 200 μL ultrapure water and 240 μL methanol. After vortexing for 30 s, 800 μL MTBE was added to induce phase separation, followed by ultrasonication in an ice-water bath and static incubation at room temperature (30 min). Phase separation was achieved by centrifugation (14,000 x g, 15 min, 10 °C), and the upper organic phase containing lipids was collected. The lipid extracts were dried under a nitrogen stream and stored at -80 °C until LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using reversed-phase liquid chromatography on a CSH C18 column (1.7 μm particle size, 2.1 x 100 mm; Waters) maintained at 45 °C. Lipid extracts were reconstituted in 200 μL of 90% isopropanol/acetonitrile (v/v), centrifuged (14,000 x g, 15 min, 10 °C), and 3 μL of supernatant was injected. Mobile phases consisted of (A) acetonitrile/water (6:4, v/v) and (B) acetonitrile/isopropanol (1:9, v/v), both supplemented with 0.1% formic acid and 0.1 mM ammonium formate. The gradient elution program was as follows: 30% B (0.0-2.0 min), 30-100% B (2.0-25.0 min, linear), 100% B (25.0-28.0 min), and re-equilibration at 5% B (28.0-38.0 min), with a constant flow rate of 300 μL/min.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes using a Q Exactive Plus hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The ESI parameters were optimized as follows: ion source temperature, 300 °C; capillary temperature, 350 °C; ion spray voltage, 3.0 kV; and S-lens RF level, 50%. Full-scan spectra were collected over a mass range of&nbsp;200-1800 m/z with a resolving power of 70,000 (at&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;200) in profile mode. Instrument calibration was performed weekly using a Pierce LTQ ESI Positive and Negative Ion Calibration Solution (Thermo Scientific) to ensure mass accuracy &lt;2 ppm.</p>	<p>In this project, mass spectrometry data were statistically analyzed using the commercial software LipidSearch (developed by Thermo Fisher Scientific), which includes a built-in database containing more than 30 lipid classes and over 1,500,000 fragment ions. Lipid identification was performed based on this database.</p>	<p>Lipid species were identified using&nbsp;LipidSearch software (version 4.2, Thermo Scientific)&nbsp;with a curated database encompassing &gt;30 lipid classes (e.g., phospholipids, sphingolipids, sterols) and &gt;1.5 million annotated MS/MS spectra. Identification criteria included: 5 ppm mass accuracy thresholds for both precursor and fragment ions, retention time alignment (±0.3 min) and HCD-specific fragmentation patterns.</p>

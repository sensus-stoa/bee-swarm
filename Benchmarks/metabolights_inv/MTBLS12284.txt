Study Identifier	MTBLS12284
Study Title	Nanoinducers-mediated Mitochondria-Selective Degradation Enhance T Cell Immunotherapy Against Multiple Cancers
Study Description	<p>Cancer immunotherapy utilizing cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) has demonstrated significant promise in clinical applications, but cancer immunosuppressive mechanisms hamper further progress in T cell immunotherapy. Here we show a correlation between cancer cell mitochondria content and their resistance to immunotherapy. Observing that cancer cells with higher mitochondrial content show increased resistance to CD8+ T cells, we developed mitochondrial nanoinducers designed to selectively target and degrade mitochondria within autophagosomes. The direct degradation of mitochondria not only enhances the recognition and activation of CD8+ T cells but also increases the susceptibility of cancer cells to CD8+ T cell-mediated cytotoxicity. We demonstrated the feasibility and efficacy of this strategy in multiple in vitro and in vivo tumor therapeutic models. This nanoinducer, designed to manipulate cellular mitochondrial degradation, holds promise as a versatile tool for enhancing adoptive T-cell therapy, CAR T-cell therapy and tumor vaccine-based immunotherapy.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>C57BL/6 mice were subcutaneously injected with 1.0 x 10^6 B16-OVA tumor cells. When the tumor size reached approximately 500 mm^3, the mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 3 per group): i) Control group (intravenous injection of PBS only); ii) mitoNIPs group (intravenous injection of 50 mg/kg mitoNIPs). After 48 h, the mice were euthanized, and the tumors were excised and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen.</p>	<p>The main processing flow is as follows: First, weigh 100 mg of evenly mixed sample and put it into 2 mL centrifuge tube. Second, add 1 mL of 70% methanol and 3 mm steel balls, shake and crush them with automatic sample rapid grinder (jxfstprp-48, 70Hz) for 3 min, and after cooling, Cryogenic sonication (40 KHZ) for 10 min. Third, centrifuge at 12,000 rmp for 10 min at 4 °C, and dilute the supernatant by 2-100 times. Last, on-machine inspection with 0.22 μm PTFE filter head.</p>	<p>Chromatographic analysis was conducted using an Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography system (Thermo Vanquish UHPLC, Thermo Fisher Scientific) fitted with a C18 column (Zorbax Eclipse C18, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm). The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C and the flow rate was consistently set to 0.3 mL/min throughout the experiment. The mobile phase was composed of solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water) and solvent B (pure acetonitrile), with an injection volume of 2 μL and an autosampler temperature of 4 °C. The gradient elution program was structured as follows: the mobile phase began at 5% B and 95% A for the first 2 min, followed by a linear increase to 30% B and 70% A from 2 to 6 min. This composition was held constant from 6 to 7 min. Subsequently, a linear gradient adjusted the mobile phase to 78% B and 22% A between 7 and 12 min, with this ratio maintained from 12 to 14 min. The composition then shifted linearly to 95% B and 5% A from 14 to 17 min and remained stable until 20 min. Finally, the system returned to 5% B and 95% A via a linear gradient from 20 to 21 min and was equilibrated at these initial conditions from 21 to 25 min prior to the next injection.</p>	<p>High-resolution mass spectrometry was conducted using a Q-Exactive HF instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The analysis was performed in both positive and negative ion modes, employing identical operational parameters for each: a heater temperature of 325 °C, a sheath gas flow rate of 45 arbitrary units (arb), an auxiliary gas flow rate of 15 arb, a sweep gas flow rate of 1 arb, an electrospray voltage of 3.5 kV, a capillary temperature of 330 °C and an S-Lens RF level of 55%. For data acquisition, a primary full scan was executed over a mass-to-charge ratio range from 100 to 1500 m/z, coupled with a data-dependent secondary mass spectrometry scan (dd-MS²) targeting the top 5 ions (TopN = 5). The resolution was configured at 120,000 for the primary mass spectrometry scan and 60,000 for the secondary mass spectrometry scan, with higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) employed as the collision mode.</p>	<p>The raw data obtained from mass spectrometry analysis undergoes a series of processing steps, including retention time correction, peak identification, peak extraction, peak integration and peak alignment. These procedures are performed to construct a data matrix related to metabolites. This matrix serves as the foundation for subsequent metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>For metabolite identification, multiple databases can be employed as valuable tools. Specifically, the Thermo mzCloud online database and the Thermo mzVault local database provide robust resources for this purpose. Additionally, the ChemSpider database, along with other similar platforms, can be utilized to enhance the accuracy and reliability of the identification process.</p>

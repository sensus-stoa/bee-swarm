Study Identifier	MTBLS12303
Study Title	Metabolomic determinants of carotid atherosclerosis plaque stability
Study Description	Our objective is to identify the metabolites associated with stability of carotid atheromatous plaques compared to vulnerable plaques, through a metabolomic approach. Eighty consecutive patients with carotid artery stenosis (CAS) who underwent carotid endarterectomy (CEA) according to current guidelines with metabolomic analysis of the removed plaque were included between may 2021 and august 2022, in the vascular surgery department of Marseille University Hospital. Symptomatic patients were qualified by stroke physicians if they experienced an ischemic stroke (IS) or transient ischemic attack (TIA) in the territory of the culprit artery less than a month before the surgery, with an otherwise negative exhaustive etiological assessment. Patients who did not report any medical history of IS/TIA in the last 6 months before surgery were considered asymptomatic. Patients with history of IS/TIA between one and six months before surgery were excluded (n=8). The metabolomes of 72 remaining carotid atherosclerotic plaques were compared according to their symptomatic status. The Institutional Review Board of Assistance Publique-Hôpitaux de Marseille approved this study (No. CSE24-4).

Study Protocol Description	<p>Carotid plaques were collected by vascular surgeon during CEA procedure. Eversion endarterectomy was performed for asymptomatic CAS, and longitudinal arteriotomy using a shunt, followed by patch closure for symptomatic CAS. The removed surgical plaques were immediately frozen at -20 °C then transferred to -80 °C until metabolomic analyses in a single batch. At the time of thawing, after a brief ice-cold saline solution (NaCl 0.9%) rinsing. Then, an area of interest, corresponding to the region most at risk of causing ischemic event, according to macroscopic appearance of the plaque (highest degree of stenosis, ulceration or intraplaque hemorrhage) was cut for metabolomic analysis. Each sample was incubated for 20 min at 4 °C in lysis buffer (7 M urea, 2 M thiourea, 4% dodecyl maltoside and 50 mmol/L dithiothreitol (Thermo Scientific)) to denature the fibrin network. Chemical solubilization was completed by manual mechanical grinding using a homogenizing pestle. After centrifugation of samples (13,000 x g, 15 min, 4 °C), the hydrophilic supernatants were collected and stored at -80 °C until metabolomic analysis. A protein assay with the Bradford colorimetric technique (Thermo Scientific) was carried out to analyze each solubilized sample to standardize the quantity of material used for metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>The metabolome of each carotid plaque was established by liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS) from a volume of hydrophilic supernatant equivalent to 200 μg of proteins. The extraction of metabolites was carried out in solvent by mixing the supernatant into 5 volumes of methanol.&nbsp;After overnight incubation at -20 °C, samples were centrifuged (13,000 x g, 15 min at +4 °C) and supernatants were dried using a nitrogen evaporator.</p><p><br></p><p>Samples were randomly assigned to the injection (5 µL) and quality control samples (QC, a pool of all samples of a biological matrix) were injected several times at the beginning of each batch for column equilibration and interspersed every set of 5 samples throughout the experimental sequence to evaluate the data quality.&nbsp;The extraction blank of the solvent mixture was analyzed at the beginning of the experimental batch sequence.</p>	<p>Dry samples were resuspended into 130 μL of a 90:10 Mili-Q water/Acetonitrile (hypergrade for LC-MS, LiChrosolv, Supelco) mixture and with 0,1% formic acid (Optima LC/MSTM, Fisher chemical) before the centrifugation (11,000 rpm, 15 min at +4 °C). Liquid chromatographic separations were performed with the DIONEX UltiMate 3000 HPLC system coupled to two different columns to enrich metabolome coverage.</p><p><br></p><p>For reversed phase chromatography, a Hypersil GOLD C18 100 x 2.1 mm, 1.9 µm (Thermo Fisher Scientific, France) was used with a mobile phase consisting of 0.1% formic acid in Mili-Q water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B) at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min (40 °C) over 16 min. The gradient started at 0% (B) for 1 min, decreased to 100% (B) over 10 min, was maintained at 100% B for 2 min, and then returned to 0% (B) in 1 min and equilibrated the column at 0% (B) for 2 min.</p><p><br></p><p>For hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC), a SeQuant ZIC-HILIC Peek Coated (150 x 2.1 mm, 5 µm) (Merck Millipore, MA, USA) column&nbsp;was used with a mobile phase consisting of Mili-Q water plus 16 mM ammonium formate (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B) at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min (40 °C) over 27 min. The gradient started at 97% (B) for 2 min, decreased to 70% (B) over 8 min and to 10% (B) in 5 min, then was maintained at 10% (B) for 2 min, was returned to 97% (B) in 1 min and held at 97% (B) until the end of the gradient for column equilibration.</p><p><br></p><p>Samples were randomly assigned to the injection (5 µL) and quality control samples (QC, a pool of all samples of a biological matrix) were injected several times at the beginning of each batch for column equilibration and interspersed every set of 5 samples throughout the experimental sequence to evaluate the data quality.&nbsp;The extraction blank of the solvent mixture was analyzed at the beginning of the experimental batch sequence.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on an ESI-Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, France). For <strong>reversed phase chromatography</strong>, full scan-ddMS2 spectrometric acquisition was applied using positive and negative ionization mode in the 80-1000 m/z range with a mass resolving power of 35,000 FWHM. For hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (<strong>HILIC</strong>), full scan spectrometric acquisition was applied using alternating ionization mode in the 80-1000 m/z range with a mass resolving power of 35,000 FWHM. In addition, full scan-ddMS2 spectrometric acquisition in positive and negative ionization mode was performed on pooled samples for annotation.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Each metabolite was semi-quantified by its ionic intensity measured by the peak height in each sample.&nbsp;Logarithmic transformation (base 10) and data scaling (1-2) were applied to metabolomic data.</p>	<p>Posttreatment of raw MS data was performed with MZmine version 4.1.0, to provide a list of metabolites annotated found in more than 90% of samples. High-confidence annotations (levels 1 &amp; 2) were based on MS, MS isotopes/adducts presence and by matching either MS2 data to fragmentation spectra from public libraries (MassBank, GNPS, HMDB, Bermingham, MoNA) and/or matching retention times to those of internal library standards acquired under the same LC-MS conditions. Each metabolite was semi-quantified by its ionic intensity measured by the peak height in each sample.</p>

Study Title	Multi-omics analysis of thermogenic lipolysis in brown adipocytes
Study Description	<p>The integration of omics technologies into molecular biological research has transformed it into a field where a system can be investigated as a whole by combining information from multiple biochemical layers. This extension of traditional molecular biology, where various data sources are considered independently, offers novel insights into complex molecular interactions. Here, we present a multi-omics analysis of murine brown adipocytes undergoing thermogenic lipolysis, which is part of the non-shivering response to cold. Through combining information from multiple types of biomolecules, we describe what is important for each biochemical layer, and the coordinated molecular responses across different layers at the system level, at different time points. Furthermore, a classification of data integration approaches for multi-omics data analysis is presented, and their applications in our study are compared, providing insights into the focus and effectiveness of these methods.</p><p><br></p><p>For Proteomics data, see - PRIDE: <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pride/archive/projects/PXD061570' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>PXD061570</a></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Immortalized murine brown preadipocytes were grown in 6-well plates with DMEM media (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) at 37 °C and 5 % CO2 in a humidified incubator.</p><p><br></p><p>For differentiation, cells were grown until confluence (day 0) and subsequently treated with differentiation media. On day 0, fresh growth media supplemented with an induction cocktail of 20 nM insulin (Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA), 1 nM triiodothyronine (Sigma-Aldrich), 1 µM dexamethasone (Sigma-Aldrich), 0.125 mM indomethacin (Sigma-Aldrich) and 0.5 mM isobutylmethylxanthine (Sigma-Aldrich). After 2 days of cultivation (day 2), the media was changed to growth media supplemented with 20 nM insulin and 1 nM triiodothyronine. The media was changed every 2 days (day 4 and day 6). All experiments were performed on day 7. Cells were stimulated with 1 µM L-(−)-norepinephrine (+)-bitartrate salt monohydrate (Sigma-Aldrich) and samples were taken at the specified sampling time points. Samples were taken for metabolomics, transcriptomics and proteomics prior to induction of lipolysis (0 h) and 4 h and 24 h after stimulation with norepinephrine. Additional samples were taken for metabolomics analysis at 30 min, 1 h, 2 h and 8 h after stimulation.</p>	<p>For metabolomics, the cells needed to be quenched and the metabolites extracted. A working solution of ice-cold 90 % methanol with internal standards (0.441 mg/L valine D8, 22.06 mg/L glutamic acid 13C5 15N, 22.06 mg/L palmitic acid D31, 2.21 mg/L 3-hydroxybutyric acid 13C4, 0.22 mg/L succinic acid D4, 1.25 mg/L decanoic acid D19, 0.378788 mg/L phenylalanine 13C9 15N, 1.25 mg/L vitamin B5 13C5 15N, 1.25 mg/L oleic acid D17) was used. Labeled standards were acquired from Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc. (Tewksbury, MA, USA). Cells were taken from the incubator and kept on ice for the following steps. The media was transferred to reaction tubes and each well was washed with 1mL PBS (prepared in-house). After removing the PBS, 500 µL of ice-cold 90 % methanol working solution was added. Cells were scraped off the well bottoms using cell scrapers. The cells were collected in the methanol solution and transferred to reaction tubes.</p><p><br></p><p>For metabolite extraction, the cell suspensions were snap frozen in liquid nitrogen, thawed at 4 °C and vortexed at least three times. After incubation at 4 °C for one hour for protein precipitation, the suspensions were centrifuged at 16,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min and the cell debris pellet was separated from the methanolic extract supernatants, which were stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Metabolomics data was acquired using four separate methods: <strong>GC-MS</strong> (Assay 1), reverse phase LC-MS (<strong>RP-LC-MS) </strong>(Assay 4 &amp; 6), <strong>HILIC-LC-MS</strong> (Assay 2 &amp; 3) and derivatized sugar phosphates measured using Triple Quadrupole (<strong>QQQ</strong>) LC-MS (Assay 5). For the reverse phase- and HILIC analysis, 200 µL of samples were dried and reconstituted in 50 µL (methanol:water, 9:1 v:v) before use.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Dried extracts of 50 µL of sample for GC-MS-TOF analysis were silylated by derivatized with 12.5 μL methoxamine (MOX, 2% in pyridine, Thermo Fisher Scientific), incubated at 45 °C for 60 min and combined with 12.5 μL N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide with 1% trimethylchlorosilane (BSTFA with 1 % TMCS, Thermo Fisher Scientific) by a Gerstel MSP multisampler (Mülheim an der Ruhr, Germany), and again incubated at 45 °C for 60 min. Prior to injection, 50 μL of 10 mg/L−1 4,4′-dibromooctafluorobiphenyl in hexane were added as an injection standard. The measurement was performed with a Leco Pegasus BT GC/TOFMS instrument (Leco Corp., Saint Joseph, Michigan) using a Restek Rxi 5 ms column (pn:13423–6850) with 1.2 mL·min−1 of helium flow and an inlet temperature of 270 °C. The chamber was held at 40 °C prior to injection, then kept for 1 min before being accelerated at 20 °C/min to 340 °C, where it remained stable for 3 min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>RP-LC-MS</strong></p><p>First, the reverse phase analysis was performed using an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column, 100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm by 100 mm (Waters, Milford, MA) in an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system Agilent 1290 Infinity II (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Water (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 0.1% formic acid (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for solvent A and a 3:1 (vol/vol) mix of acetonitrile and 2-propanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 0.1% formic acid. The column and autosampler temperatures were maintained at 40 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The following gradient was applied: 0 min 3 % B, from 0 min to 9 min to 100 % B, 9 min to 14 min 100 %, 14 min to 14.5 min to 3 % B, 14.5 min to 17 min 3 % B, with a flow of 0.4 mL/min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC-LC-MS</strong></p><p>For HILIC, the same setup as for reverse phase with the following changes was used. The same samples as for reverse phase were measured with an Atlantis Premier BEH Z-HILIC 1.7 µm VanGuard Fit 2.1 by 100 mm column and the following gradient. Solvent A: water with 10 mM NH4HCO3. Solvent B: acetonitrile with 10 mM NH4HCO3. Gradient: 0 min 95 % B, 0 min to 2 min 95 % B, 2 min to 12 min to 55 % B, 12 min to 14 min 55 % B, 14 min to 14.5 min to 95 % B, 14.5 min to 20 min 95 % B, at a flow of 0.2 mL/min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>QQQ</strong></p><p>Sugar phosphates were derivatized following a method described in Rende et al. [1] In brief, 50 µL samples were dried together with 50 ng of Fructose 6-phosphate 13C as an added standard before being oxomylated followed by propionylation. The dried extracts were reconstituted in 20 µL MOX (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and incubated at 60 °C for 1 h. 6 µL 1-Methylimidazole (Sigma Aldrich) were added as a catalyst, together with 12 µL of the reactant propionic anhydride before incubating for 30 min at 37 °C. Samples were dried for 30 min and reconstituted in water with 0.1 % formic acid. The samples were analyzed with an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column, 100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm by 100 mm (Waters, Milford, MA) on a Premier Acquity UPLC (Waters) connected to a Xevo TQ-XS triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Waters) equipped with an electrospray ionization source. Samples were randomized prior to data acquisition. The following separation was applied. Solvent A: water with 0.1 % formic acid. Solvent B: acetonitrile with 0.1 % formic acid. Data was acquired from 0 min to 14 min. The following gradient was applied: 0 min 0.1 % B, from 0 min to 0.54 min 0.1 % B, from 0.54 min to 3.5 min increase to 30 % B, from 3.5 min to 6 min increase to 35 % B, from 6 min to 8 min 35 % B, from 8 min to 10 min increase to 70 % B, from 10 min to 12 min increase to 99 % B, from 12 min to 14 min 99 % B, from 14 min to 14.5 min 0.1 %, from 14.5 min to 17.5 min 0.1 % B with a flow of 0.5 mL/min.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[<strong>1</strong>] Rende, U., Niittylä, T. &amp; Moritz, T. Two-step derivatization for determination of sugar phosphates in plants by combined reversed phase chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry.&nbsp;Plant Methods&nbsp;15, 127 (2019). <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-019-0514-9' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-019-0514-9</a></p>	<p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Mass fragmentation occurred at 70 eV with an electron ionization source. Mass spectra were acquired by the detector in scan mode from 50 to 750 Da at 10 Hz.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>RP-LC-MS</strong></p><p>With a Bruker timsTOF Pro instrument equipped with a trapped-ion mobility spectrometer (TIMS) coupled to a hybrid quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (TOF-MS) (Bruker, Bremen, Germany), in both positive and negative electrospray ionization modes. Water (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 0.1% formic acid (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for solvent A and a 3:1 (vol/vol) mix of acetonitrile and propanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 0.1% formic acid. The column and autosampler temperatures were maintained at 40 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The following gradient was applied: 0 min 3 % B, from 0 min to 9 min to 100 % B, 9 min to 14 min 100 %, 14 min to 14.5 min to 3 % B, 14.5 min to 17 min 3 % B, with a flow of 0.4 mL/min. The VIP-ion source used 10 liters/min of drying gas at a temperature of 220°C, set at a 3500-V capillary voltage and 300 kPa nebulizer pressure. A m/z range of 50 to 1000 over 17 min was detected at at 2 Hz.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC-LC-MS</strong></p><p>The VIP-ion source used 10 liters/min of drying gas at a temperature of 220°C, set at a 3600-V capillary voltage and 220 kPa nebulizer pressure. A m/z range of 50 to 1000 over 20 min was detected at at 3 Hz.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>QQQ</strong></p><p>With a Xevo TQ-XS triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Waters) equipped with an electrospray ionization source. The samples were measured in negative mode with a capillary voltage of 0.5 kV and a cone voltage 40 V. Desolvation temperature was 400 °C with a gas flow of 800 L/h and cone gas flow of 150 L/h. The MRM settings were the same as previously described [<strong>1</strong>].</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[<strong>1</strong>] Rende, U., Niittylä, T. &amp; Moritz, T. Two-step derivatization for determination of sugar phosphates in plants by combined reversed phase chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry.&nbsp;Plant Methods&nbsp;15, 127 (2019). <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-019-0514-9' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-019-0514-9</a></p>	<p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Raw output files were converted into centroid mode and exported as netCDF files before being extracted using a targeted in-house library with software from the Swedish Metabolome Center.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>RP-LC-MS</strong></p><p>Data was processed using <strong>MetaboScape</strong> version 5.0 (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany). Sodium formate was used as an internal calibrant.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC-LC-MS</strong></p><p>The HILIC data was processed using <strong>MzMine </strong>3.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>QQQ</strong></p><p>The data was processed with the Waters TargetLynx software.</p>	<p>An in-house metabolite library with documented m/zs and retention times was used for each method to annotate metabolites.</p>

Study Title	Histidine improves Hashimoto thyroiditis by inhibiting the production of Neutrophil Txtracellular Traps
Study Description	<p>Objective: Metabolite abnormalities are potentially implicated in pathogenesis of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis (HT). An in-depth study of the relationship between HT and small molecule metabolites, as well as its pathogenesis, can help enhance the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of HT patients.</p><p>Methods: We used metabolomics to analyze changes in serum metabolite levels in 20 HT patients and 20 healthy controls (HC) and the most significant differentially expressed metabolite was analyzed. Western blot and qPCR were used to measure the expression of histidine decarboxylase (HDC) and histamine receptor 1 (H1R). Increasing concentrations of histidine were used to treat neutrophils and observe the effect on neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) synthesis. Histidine treated neutrophils were co-culture with thyroid follicular cells to study the protective effects of histidine on HT thyroid follicular cells and related mechanisms.</p><p>Results: A total of 48 differentially expressed metabolites were found. Histidine exhibited reduced expression levels in HT patients, displaying the most significant discrepancy (p&lt;0.001). ROS and NETs were increased and HDC, H1R, and histamine were upregulated in neutrophils upon stimulated. These effects were corrected by addition of histidine, in a dose dependent manner. In addition, in co-culture experiments, histidine enhanced expression of SOD and suppressed production of inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α and NF-κB pathway in thyroid follicular cells, thereby inhibiting inflammation and oxidative stress.</p><p>Conclusion: Histidine inhibits NETs synthesis and NF-κB signaling. Thus, histidine play an anti-inflammatory and antioxidant role by decreasing thyroid follicular cell inflammation in HT.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>1. Draw 3-4 mL of venous blood and inject it into a serum-specific separation tube (or a clean centrifuge tube).</p><p>2. At room temperature (20-25 °C), let it stand for about 30 min (no more than 2 h) until the blood is completely coagulated. The time for blood coagulation and serum separation is related to the ambient temperature. It is recommended that the best standing temperature be 25 °C. If the laboratory temperature is low, the standing time should be appropriately extended to ensure that the serum is fully separated and that all samples for this project are taken at the same time.</p><p>3. After the serum is fully separated, centrifuge at 3000 rpm for 5 min and take the supernatant.</p><p>4. Centrifuge again at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C for 10 min and take the supernatant.</p><p>5. Carefully draw about 1.5 mL of the upper clear liquid into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube (ensuring that the serum volume is ≥ 500 uL), press the lid of the centrifuge tube tightly, check the fit, and place it in the sample box.</p><p>6. Store the cryopreservation box in a -80 °C freezer (or liquid nitrogen).</p><p>7. Send with sufficient dry ice.</p>	<p>Samples were thawed on ice-bath to diminish sample degradation. Next 20 μL of plasma was added to a 96-well plate. Then the plate was transferred to the Eppendorf epMotion Workstation (Eppendorf Inc., Humburg, Germany). Next 120 μL ice cold methanol with partial internal standards was automatically added to each sample and vortexed vigorously for 5 min. The plate was centrifuged at 4000 x g for 30 min. Then the plate was returned back to the workstation. Then 30 μL of supernatant was transferred to a clean 96-well plate, and 20 μL of freshly prepared derivative reagents was added to each well. The plate was sealed and the derivatization was carried out at 30 °C for 60 min. After derivatization, 330 μL of ice-cold 50% methanol solution was added to dilute the sample. Then the plate was stored at -20 °C for 20 min and followed by 4000 x g centrifugation at 4 °C for 30 min.</p>	<p>Column: ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 1.7 µM VanGuard pre-column (2.1 x 5 mm) and ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 1.7 µM analytical column (2.1 x 100 mm);</p><p>Column Temp: 40 °C; Sample Manager Temp: 10 °C; Mobile Phases: A = water with 0.1% formic acid; B = acetonitrile / IPA (70:30); Flow Rate: 0.40 mL/min; Injection Vol: 5.0 µL.</p>	<p>A ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) system (ACQUITY UPLC-Xevo TQ-S, Waters Corp.,&nbsp;Milford, MA, USA) was used to quantitate all targeted metabolite s in this project. The optimized instrument settings are briefly described below. The instrument performance optimization and routine maintenance were performed every week.</p><p>Capillary: 1.5 (ESI+) / 2.0 Kv (ESI-); Source Temp : 150 °C; Desolvation Temp: 550 °C; Desolvation Gas Flow: 1000 L/h.</p>	<p>QuanMET software (v2.0, Metabo-Profile, Shanghai, China) was used to process the original data files generated by the UPLC-MS/MS, and peak integration, calibration and quantification of each metabolite were carried out. QuanME is currently hosted on a Dell PowerEdge R730 server using the Linux Ubuntu 16.10 operating system. The R studio package is used for statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry-based quantitative metabolomics refers to the determination of the concentration of a substance in an unknown sample by comparing the unknown to a set of standard samples of known concentration (i.e., calibration curve). The calibration curve is a plot of how the analytical signal changes with the concentration of the analyte (the substance to be measured). For most analyses a plot of instrument response vs concentration will show a linear relationship. This yields a model described by the equation y = ax + b, where y is the instrument response e.g., peak height or area, a represents the slope/sensitivity and b is a constant that describes the background. The analyte concentration (x) of unknown samples may be calculated from this equation.</p>

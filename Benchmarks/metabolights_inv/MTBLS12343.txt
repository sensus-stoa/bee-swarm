Study Identifier	MTBLS12343
Study Title	Non-targeted metabolomics data from solid fermented tobacco leaves derived from Enterobacter hormaechei
Study Description	<p>Enterobacter hormaechei-mediated solid-state fermentation (SSF) metabolomics in tobacco leaves remains underexplored. Using gas chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry (GC-HRMS), we generated a time-resolved metabolomic dataset revealing 88 dynamically regulated metabolites, including amino acids, carbohydrates and organic acids. This resource elucidates phase-specific biochemical shifts during SSF and advances mechanistic understanding of microbial-driven tobacco modification, supporting bioprocessing optimization.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Culture preparation</p><p>Primary seed cultures were initiated by inoculating -80 °C glycerol stocks into LB medium (1:1000 v/v) with incubation at 37 °C (200 rpm, 8 h). Secondary cultures were prepared by transferring 2 mL primary culture to fresh LB (1% v/v inoculum) under identical conditions for 16 h. Cells were harvested by centrifugation (8000 x g, 4 °C, 10 min), washed twice with sterile water, and adjusted to 0.01 g/mL wet weight.</p><p><br></p><p>Solid-state fermentation of filler leaves</p><p>E. hormaechei&nbsp;suspensions (1% v/w, based on filler leaf dry mass) were aseptically sprayed onto tobacco leaves (30% moisture content). Control groups received equivalent sterile water. Treated leaves were sealed in bags and incubated at 30 °C / 70% humidity for 0, 6, 18, 24 and 72 h.</p>	<p>Samples (25 mg ± 10%) were weighed into 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes and mixed with 0.5 mL of pre-chilled (-20 °C) acetonitrile:isopropanol:water&nbsp;(3:3:2,&nbsp;v/v/v) and 3-4 zirconium beads (2 mm). Homogenization was performed using a high-throughput tissue grinder (30 Hz, 20 s pulses; 10 s intervals; 8 cycles), followed by ice-water bath sonication (5 min). An additional 0.5 mL of chilled solvent was added, and samples were sonicated again (5 min) (Fiehn &amp; Kind, 2007). After centrifugation (12,000 x g, 2 min), 500 μL of supernatant was transferred to new tubes, vacuum-concentrated to dryness (8-10 h), and derivatized with 80 μL methoxyamine hydrochloride (MEOX, 20 mg/mL; 60 °C, 60 min). BSTFA-TMCS (99:1, 100 μL) was added, vortexed (30 s), and incubated (70 °C, 90 min) (Zhao et al., 2023). Post-centrifugation (14,000 x g, 3 min), 90-100 μL of supernatant was transferred to GC vials and analyzed by GC-TOF within 24 h.</p>	<p>Gas chromatographic and Mass Spectrometry Conditions. Separation was achieved using a DB-5MS capillary column (30 m x 250 μm, 0.25 μm film; Agilent J&amp;W Scientific) with helium carrier gas (1 mL/min constant flow). Samples (1 μL) were injected in split mode (1:10 ratio) at 280 °C. The temperature program initiated at 50 °C (0.5 min), ramped to 320 °C at 15 °C/min, and held for 9 min. Transfer line and ion source temperatures were maintained at 320 and 230 °C, respectively.</p>	<p>Full-scan mass spectra were acquired at 10 spectra/s with a&nbsp;range of 67-1000 m/z. Electron energy was set to -70 eV, with a 3-min solvent delay (Fiehn et al., 2008).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was conducted using a GC-MS system coupled with a custom spectral library (Nuomi Metabolomics Biotechnology Co., Ltd., v3.1.5). Quantification and raw data preprocessing were performed with MS-DIAL v4.90 and XCMS v3.7.2 (Gao et al., 2023). The processed data matrix was imported into the SIMCA software (v14.1, Sartorius Stedim Data Analytics AB, Umea, Sweden) that normalized and standardized the data matrix and performed orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), using the P-value ≤ 0.05 and VIP (Variable Importance for the Projection) ≥ 1 for screening differential metabolites (Papadimitropoulos, Vasilopoulou, Maga-Nteve, &amp; Klapa, 2018).</p>	<p>Finally, the MetaboAnalyst 5.0 and KEGG databases were used to analyze the metabolic pathways of the identified differential metabolites (Li et al., 2020). Data visualization was generated using Origin Pro 2023b (Origin Lab) and Python matplotlib v3.7.1(Weng et al., 2024).</p>

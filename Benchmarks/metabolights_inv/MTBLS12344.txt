Study Identifier	MTBLS12344
Study Title	Alzheimer’s disease risk gene Wwox protects against amyloid pathology through metabolic alterations in a Drosophila model
Study Description	<p>Genome wide association studies have identified multiple loci that mediate the risk of developing late-onset Alzheimer’s Disease (LOAD). Exploring how the genes associated with these loci convey risk of disease is necessary for a complete understanding of LOAD pathology and design of novel therapeutic interventions. The gene WW-domain containing oxidoreductase (WWOX) has been identified in recent LOAD risk meta-analyses, yet its function in the brain poorly understood. Using Drosophila, we discovered that knockdown of the highly conserved Wwox gene impacts longevity and sleep, having roles in both neuronal and glial subtypes. In an Amyloid Beta 42 (Aβ42) transgenic model of AD, neuronal knockdown of Wwox significantly decreased both lifespan and locomotion whilst elevating soluble Aβ42.</p><p>Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses revealed that these effects were accompanied by elevated lactate dehydrogenase (Ldh) mRNA and lactate levels, downstream of an increase in the unfolded protein response protein Atf4. Furthermore, knockdown of Ldh rescued locomotion deficits in Wwox knockdown Aβ42 flies, implicating lactate metabolism in the mechanism of interaction. Strikingly, we found that upregulation of Wwox in the Aβ42 model significantly reduced amyloid load, improved longevity and locomotion, without altering lactate levels. Multi-omics analysis revealed Wwox upregulation partially reversed Aβ42-induced transcriptional changes in the brain and reduced levels of the amino acid L-methionine and enzymes within the cysteine and methionine metabolic pathway. These findings support a role for reduced WWOX levels in the genetic risk of developing LOAD via pyruvate metabolism and point towards WWOX activation as a protective therapeutic strategy.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples were n=5 replicates of pooled heads (10 heads per rep) of day 14 female <em>Drosophila melanogaster</em>. Using the GAL4-UAS system the effect of neuronal <em>Wwox</em> knockdown in the presence of amyloid and the effect of neuronal <em>Wwox</em> overexpression in the presence of amyloid were investigated. The following geotypes were present:</p><p><br></p><p>Knockdown experiment</p><ul><li>RNAi control + LacZ expression control</li><li><em>Wwox</em> knockdown + LacZ expression control</li><li>RNAi control + AB42</li><li><em>Wwox</em> knockdown +AB42</li></ul><p><br></p><p>Overexpression experiment</p><ul><li>Control gRNA + LacZ expression control</li><li><em>Wwox</em> gRNA + LacZ expression control</li><li>Control gRNA + AB42</li><li><em>Wwox</em> gRNA +AB42</li></ul><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Once heads were dissected and pooled they were homogenised in 1:3:1 chloroform:methanol:water and agitated at 4 °C for an hour. Pooled samples and matrix blanks were used as controls.</p>	<p>Hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography was performed on a Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC system, using a ZIC-pHILIC column maintained at 25 °C (Merck Sequant).</p><p>Samples were eluted with a linear gradient (20 mM ammonium carbonate in water, A and acetonitrile, B) over 26 min at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min:</p><ul><li>0 min: %A 20, %B 80</li><li>15 min: %A 80, %B 20</li><li>15 min: %A 95, %B 5</li><li>17 min: %A 95, %B 5</li><li>17 min: %A 20, %B 80</li><li>26 min: %A 20, %B 80</li></ul><p><br></p><p>Samples were kept at 5 °C then 10 μL injected.</p>	<p>Mass Spec was performed with a Thermo Orbitrap QExactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in polarity switching mode with the following settings:</p><p><br></p><ul><li>Resolution 70,000</li><li>AGC 1e6</li><li>m/z range 70-1050</li><li>Sheath gas 40</li><li>Auxiliary gas 5</li><li>Sweep gas 1</li><li>Probe temperature 150 °C</li><li>Capillary temperature 320 °C</li></ul><p><br></p><p>For positive mode ionisation: source voltage +3.8 kV, S-Lens RF Level 30.00, S-Lens Voltage 25.00 (V), Skimmer Voltage 15.00 (V), Inject Flatopole Offset 8.00 (V), Bent Flatapole DC 6.00 (V). For negative mode ionisation: source voltage-3.8 kV.</p><p><br></p><p>The calibration mass range was extended to cover small metabolites by inclusion of low-mass calibrants with the standard Thermo calmix masses (below m/z 138), butylamine (C4H11N1) for positive ion electrospray ionisation (PIESI) mode (m/z 74.096426) and COF3 for negative ion electrospray ionisation (NIESI) mode (m/z 84.9906726). To enhance calibration stability, lock-mass correction was also applied to each analytical run:</p><p><br></p><ul><li>Positive Mode Lock mass 1 (m/z): 144.9822</li><li>Negative Mode Lock mass 1 (m/z): 100.9856</li></ul>	<p>The .raw files were converted to positive and negative ionisation mode mzXML files. These files were then analysed using IDEOM (Creek, Jankevics, Burgess, Breitling, &amp; Barrett, 2012), mzMatchISO and PiMP (Gloaguen et al., 2017) with the FrAnK in-house (Glasgow Polyomics) fragmentation data analysis software.</p>	<p>Standards from the Metabolomics Standards Initiative were used and mapped using PiMP software.</p>

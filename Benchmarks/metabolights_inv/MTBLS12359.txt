Study Title	Lipid Droplet Inhibitors for Disrupting Lipid Accumulation in Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD)
Study Description	Excessive lipid droplet accumulation in hepatocytes drives the progression of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), often leading to inflammation and fibrosis. As obesity and metabolic syndrome rise, MASLD has become a global concern, spurring research into effective treatments. Here, we present the design of a Lipid droplet inhibitor (LDI), incorporating ultra-large mesoporous silica nanostructures along with PKCα C1A and Candida Rugosa lipase, aimed at directly degrading lipid droplets. Through its dual-functional design, this nanostructure captures diacylglycerol using PKCα C1A while hydrolyzing triacylglycerol into smaller molecular fragments via the lipase. Notably, the amphiphilic biomolecules in LDI facilitate the formation of a Pickering emulsion, ensuring stable localization at the lipid-water interface for efficient interaction with lipid droplets. LDI reduced lipid droplet formation and growth in HepG2 cells. In a high-fat diet-induced MASLD model, it alleviated liver pathology and, lowered injury scores by up to 84%. Furthermore, lipidomic analysis confirmed that LDI effectively modulated the hepatic lipid profile, suggesting its potential as a nanoplatform for counteracting lipid droplet accumulation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The C57BL/6 mice (6 weeks old) were randomly divided into 3 groups. After a week acclimation period, a high-fat diet (HFD) was provided for 16 weeks. Starting from the 12th week of the diet, the negative control group received pSiO2-BSA, while the LDI-treated group received LDI (Nanoparticle in HEPES 10 nM buffer, pH 7.4) via tail vein injection. The dosage was set at 800 µg / 200 µL per mouse, administered twice weekly for 4 weeks. At the end of the 16 weeks, all mice were sacrificed, liver samples were collected, lyophilized and stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>2 mg liver powder mixed with 50 µL water to prepare homogenate. Homogenate separated for protein quantification and lipidomic analysis. Lipid extraction was done by adding 600 µL of ice-cold MeOH with 1 mM BHT:CHCl3 (1:2, v/v). The Folch method was used for sample preparation.</p>	<p>Instrument: Vanquish LC (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Column: ACE Excel 2 superC18 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 μm, Advanced Chromatography Technology, Aberdeen, Scotland). Column Temperature: 45 °C. Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min. Injection Volume: 5 µL. Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid and 2 mM ammonium acetate in 60% acetonitrile. Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid and 2 mM ammonium acetate in 90:10, isopropanol:acetonitrile.</p>	<p>Instrument: Q Exactive Orbitrap (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). MS mode: Positive &amp; Negative ion mode. Scan Range: 70-1050 m/z. Resolution: 70,000 (for Full scan), 17,500 (For MS2 scan). Capillary temperature: 320 °C. Spray Voltage: 3.5 kv (positive mode) / 3.0 kv (negative mode).</p>	<p>Compound Discoverer 3.3 was used to extract and analyze the raw data following LC-MS/MS analysis. Raw files were imported into the compound discoverer software and processed, followed by the selection of spectra, alignment of peak retention times, prediction, and matching of lipid composition and fragmentation patterns. Ultimately, the ions detected in the raw sample files were extracted and saved as an Excel file.</p>	<p>Compound discoverer consisted of lipids various databases like, mzCloud, mzVault and ChemSpider, and Inhouse spectral library of 2500 compounds. So, to identify lipids the precursor m/z, chemical formula and fragment/daughter ions from the raw data were compared with established spectral libraries.</p>

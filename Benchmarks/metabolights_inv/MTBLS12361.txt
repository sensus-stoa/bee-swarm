Study Identifier	MTBLS12361
Study Title	Co-Housed Mice Reveal the Tumor-Promoting Potential of Akkermansia muciniphila
Study Description	Specific pathogen-free laboratory mice are widely used in biomedical research; however, their neonatal-like physiological state often limits their ability to model adult human conditions. Here, we induced adult-like physiological characteristics in laboratory mice by co-housing them with naturally raised mice obtained from pet stores. Co-housing resulted in significant changes in the gut microbiota composition and a marked suppression of tumor growth in multiple tissues. We discovered reduction in Akkermansia muciniphila was a contributing factor to the observed tumor suppression. Furthermore, decreased serum indole metabolite levels were linked to both Akkermansia muciniphila and the tumor-suppressive effects. These findings suggest that Akkermansia muciniphila may, under certain situations, be tumor-promoting and highlight the value of using adult-like mouse models in uncovering critical environmental factors in cancer and other health conditions.

Study Protocol Description	<p>After C57BL/6 mice were co-housed together with pet mice for four weeks, one group was intragastrically administered with PBS, and the other group was intragastrically administered with Akkermansia muciniphila (10^10 colony-forming units, CFU). The intragastric administration was carried out for 2 wk, 3 times a wk.&nbsp;Serum was collected&nbsp;to compare differences in serum metabolite levels and immediately transferred to a -80 °C freezer.</p>	<p>Serum samples were thawed on ice and mixed with an extraction solution (ACN:Methanol, 1:4, v/v) containing internal standards, then subjected to LC-MS analysis. Data acquisition was performed in information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode using Analyst TF 1.7.1 Software (Sciex, Concord, ON, Canada). The original data files acquired by LC-MS were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard software. Peak extraction, peak alignment, and retention time correction were performed using the XCMS program. The 'SVR' method was used for peak area correction. Peaks with a detection rate lower than 50% in each group of samples were discarded.</p>	<p>Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm. Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid/water; Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid/acetonitrile. Column temperature: 40 °C; Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min; Sample size: 4 μL</p>	<p>Spray Voltage: 3500 (ESI+) / 3200 (ESI-), Sheath gas (Arb): 30 (ESI+) / 30 (ESI-), Aux gas (Arb): 5 (ESI+) / 5 (ESI-), lon transfer tube temperature: 320 (ESI+) / 320 (ESI-), Vaporizer temperature: 300 (ESI+) / 300 (ESI-), Scan Range (MS1): 75-1000 (ESI+) / 75-1000 (ESI-), Resolution: 35000 (ESI+) / 35000 (ESI-), AGC target: 1 x 106 (ESI+) / 1 x 106 (ESI-), RF lens (%): 50 (ESI+) / 50 (ESI-), Scan Range (MS2): 75-1000 (ESI+) / 75-1000 (ESI-).</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard, and peak extraction, alignment and retention time correction were performed using the XCMS program. Peaks with a missing rate &gt;50% across sample groups were filtered, and missing values were filled using KNN imputation. Peak areas were then corrected using the SVR method.</p>	<p>The corrected and filtered peaks were subjected to metabolite identification by searching against the laboratory's in-house database, integrated public databases, predictive databases and the metDNA method. Finally, compounds with an identification composite score &gt;0.5 and a QC sample CV value &lt;0.3 were extracted. These were then merged across positive and negative ionization modes (retaining the entry with the highest identification confidence level and the smallest CV value) to obtain the final metabolite list.</p>

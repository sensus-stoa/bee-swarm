Study Identifier	MTBLS12365
Study Title	Gut microbiome and metabolome dynamics as predictors of clinical outcomes in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation
Study Description	Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) induces significant alterations in the gut microbiome and metabolome, which influence transplant-related complications and surviv-al. This study longitudinally analyzed gut microbiota and metabolites at pre- (T1), peri- (T2), and post-transplant (T3) stages to identify key determinants of clinical outcomes. Lower microbiome diversity at T1 and T3 correlated with graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), progressive disease (PD), and reduced overall survival (OS). Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), particularly acetate, declined over time, with reductions at T2-T1 linked to GVHD, diarrhea, PD, and lower OS. Specific perspective metabolites (SPMs) such as elevated uric acid at T2 were associated with GVHD, while reduced 1-phenylethylamine correlated with diarrhea. Patients with enriched beneficial taxa (e.g., Lachnospiraceae, Ruminococcaceae) exhibited improved OS. These findings highlight the gut microbiome-metabolome axis as a predictive marker for HSCT outcomes. Identifying microbial and metabolic signatures may provide novel diagnostic and therapeutic targets for mitigating HSCT-related complications, enhancing patient prognosis.

Study Protocol Description	<p>This prospective observational study was conducted at the Hematology and Transplantation Unit of Ulsan University Hospital, Korea, between March 2022 and May 2023. Stool samples were serially collected from 60 patients undergoing autologous or allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) with various conditioning regimens. Sampling was performed at three time points: before the conditioning regimen, on the day of transplantation or the following day, and on day 14 post-transplant.</p><p>Two participants were excluded due to insufficient sample volume, resulting in a final analysis of 174 stool samples from 58 patients. The Hematopoietic-Cell Transplantation Comorbidity Index (HCT-CI) was used to assess the mortality risk following HSCT.</p><p>Study approval was granted by the Ulsan University Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval No: 2022-01-020, Date: 21.03.2022), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg of dried stool sample was transferred into a 2 mL microcentrifuge tube containing 1 mL of 50% methanol, and ceramic beads were added to facilitate extraction. For extraction, the samples were vortexed for 10 min and then sonicated for 10 min at room temperature. Then, the samples were centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and only the supernatant was filtered through a 0.2-μm polytetrafluoroethylene syringe filter. Supernatants of all filtered samples were completely dried using a speed vacuum concentrator (Labogene, Daejeon, Republic of Korea).</p>	<p>Gas chromatography was performed using an Agilent 7890 GC system (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA) equipped with a PAL-3 auto-sampler (CTC Analytics AG, Zwingen, Switzerland). The separation was achieved using an Rtx-5MS column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 µm; Restek Corp., Bellefonte, PA, USA), with helium as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. A 1 µL aliquot of the derivatized sample was injected into the GC system using a split ratio of 10:1. The oven temperature was programmed as follows: it was initially maintained at 75°C for 2 min, then increased at a rate of 15°C per min up to 300°C, and finally held at 300°C for 3 min. The temperatures of both the front inlet and the transfer line were set to 250°C.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a Pegasus BT TOF MS system (LECO Corp., St. Joseph, MI, USA). The instrument operated with electron ionization (EI) at -70 eV. Full-scan analysis was conducted over a range of 45-600 m/z. The mass spectrometer utilized a Time-of-Flight (TOF) system, ensuring high-resolution detection of metabolites.</p>	<p>The raw data were collected and processed using LECO Chroma TOF software (version 5.40), and the resulting files were converted into NetCDF format (.cdf) using the same software. Peak alignment and retention time adjustments were performed using the Metalign software suite (http://www.metalign.nl). For statistical analysis, principal component analysis (PCA), partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and orthogonal PLS-DA (OPLS-DA) were performed using SIMCA P+ software (version 16.0, Umetrics, Umea, Sweden). Additionally, a heatmap visualization was generated to display the relative concentrations of discriminant metabolites, with fold changes normalized against the mean values for each metabolite.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using ChromaTOF software (LECO Corp.) with spectral matching against NIST, Wiley9, TargetSearch, and LECO default libraries (mainlib, replib). In addition, identifications were confirmed using an in-house library and authentic standards analyzed under the same chromatographic conditions.</p>	<p>The dried samples were derivatized for GC-TOF-MS analysis by adding 40 μL of methoxyamine hydrochloride in pyridine (20 mg/mL) at 30 °C for 120 min, followed by the adding 40 μL of N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide (MSTFA) at 37 °C for 60 min.</p>

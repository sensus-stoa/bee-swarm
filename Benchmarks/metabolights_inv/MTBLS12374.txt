STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS12374
Study Title	Endophytic mycobiont provides growth benefits via a Phenylpropanoid-Auxin axis in host plants
Study Description	<p>Beneficial association with symbiotic fungi helps improve the growth and fitness in most land plants, and shows great potential as biofertilizers in precision agriculture. Here, we demonstrated that a root fungal endophyte, <em>Tinctoporellus</em> species isolate AR8, enabled yield improvement in Brassicaceae leafy green Choy Sum (<em>Brassica rapa var. parachinensis</em>). Mechanistically, AR8 colonized the root endosphere during plant interaction and channeled the metabolic flux to phenylpropanoids and requisite secondary metabolites to promote plant growth. AR8 enabled biosynthesis of auxin improved root growth and provided an intrinsic source for long-distance signal that enhanced shoot biomass. Chemical complementation with <em>p</em>-coumaric acid restored the auxin signaling and enhanced growth in AR8-inoculated <em>pal1</em> mutant plants, thus implicating such phenylpropanoid-auxin nexus as a pivotal regulator of symbiotic plant growth. Comparative metabolomics established hydroxycinnamic acid and <em>p</em>-coumaric acid as major plant growth-promoting hubs that bridge phenylpropanoid pathway and auxin signaling in the AR8 symbiosis model.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Conidial suspension (1 x 10^6 spores) of mycobiome was inoculated into the rhizosphere region of (4-day-old) Choy Sum. Seedlings were grown in the soil, and conidia were directly inoculated to the roots and surrounding soil. Distilled water was used as a mock control with the same inoculation method. Plants were harvested at 21 days post-inoculation (dpi).</p>	<p>Briefly, freeze-dried Choy Sum powder (50 mg) was extracted thrice by 1 mL of precooled 80% methanol (v/v) and internal standard FMOC-glycine (25 μg/mL) under sonication in an ice bath for 15 min and then centrifuged for 10 min at 14,000 rpm at 5 °C. The extraction was repeated thricely, and the supernatant was kept at -80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed in an Agilent 1290 Infinity II. The separation of compounds was achieved on a Kinetex C18 100 Å (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Phenomenex). The autosampler (Agilent 1290 Infinity II Multisampler) and column were maintained at 4 and 35 °C, respectively. For analysis, plant samples were injected with 5 μL. Separation was performed at a constant flow rate of 0.3 mL/min in a gradient of solvent A (water acidified with 0.1% formic acid) and B (acetonitrile acidified with 0.1% formic acid). The gradient program was 2-20% solvent B (0.0-5.0 min), 20-30% solvent B (5.0-12.0 min); 30-100% solvent B (12.0-12.1 min); 100% solvent B (12.1-15.0 min); re-equilibration from 100-2% solvent B (15.0-15.1 min); and reconditioned with 2% solvent B (15.1-19.0 min).</p>	<p>An Agilent 6495 Series Triple Quadrupole LC-MS system was performed in negative ion mode with the following source parameters: ion source: end plate; capillary voltage: 3000 V; nebulizer: 40 psi; drying gas (N2): 12 L/min; dry temperature: 280 °C; scan mass range: 50-800 m/z.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were acquired in the MRM mode by MassHunter Qualitative Analysis software 10.0 (Agilent).</p>	<p>The target compounds were obtained from the comparison to authentic standards (MSI level 1) on the basis of their retention time and characteristic mass to charge ratio (m/z).</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12389
Study Title	Integrated multi-omics analysis of liver metabolic dysregulation in ACE2 knockout mice
Study Description	This study systematically investigated the impact of ACE2 knockout (ACE2KO) on hepatic metabolic homeostasis and its molecular mechanisms, using integrated transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic profiling. ACE2 deficiency exacerbates hepatic lipid accumulation, as evidenced by elevated total cholesterol and triglyceride levels, while disrupting the renin-angiotensin system equilibrium via increased angiotensin II (Ang II) and reduced Ang-(1-7). Histopathological analysis revealed hepatocyte edema, vacuolar degeneration, and inflammatory infiltration in the ACE2KO mice. Multi-omics integration uncovered systemic metabolic dysregulation: transcriptomics identified 1,004 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including lipid metabolism regulators (Scd1, Fabp1) and circadian rhythm modulators (Arntl, Cry1), 191 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) associated with interferon signaling activation (Oas1a, Rsad2) and lipid synthesis suppression (Scd1, Fasn), and metabolomics highlighted 193 differential metabolites (DEMs) indicative of bile acid dysregulation, glutathione redox imbalance, and amino acid metabolism anomalies. Cross-omics analysis indicated that the PPAR signaling pathway is a central regulatory hub driving lipid metabolic reprogramming and oxidative stress amplification. Furthermore, ACE2 deletion disrupted cytochrome P450-mediated detoxification and glutathione metabolism, thereby establishing a self-reinforcing oxidative injury cycle. These findings comprehensively delineate ACE2’s multifaceted role in hepatic metabolic homeostasis and provide mechanistic insights into and therapeutic targets for ACE2-associated liver diseases.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Animal Model: ACE2 knockout (ACE2KO) mice (C57BL/6J background) and wild-type (WT) controls.</p><p>Procedure:</p><p>Mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (80 mg/kg, intraperitoneal).</p><p>Liver tissues were excised under hypothermic dissection (ice-cooled platform), perfused with PBS (pH 7.4) to remove residual erythrocytes.</p><p>Tissues were either fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for histopathology or snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for downstream analyses.</p>	<p>Twenty-five milligrams of liver tissue were homogenized in 500 μL of a methanol-acetonitrile-water mixture (2:2:1, v/v/v) using a TissueLyzer II at a frequency of 35 Hz for 4 minutes. Following homogenization, the samples were subjected to three cycles of sonication in an ice-water bath, each lasting 5 minutes. After sonication, the samples were incubated at -40°C for 60 minutes. Subsequently, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C to collect the supernatant. The resulting supernatants were stored at -80°C until analysis by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS).</p>	<p>The liquid chromatography analysis was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC I-Class Plus system (Waters). The separation was carried out on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The flow rate was maintained at 0.35 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–2 min, 95% A and 5% B; 2–4 min, linear gradient to 70% A and 30% B; 4–8 min, linear gradient to 50% A and 50% B; 8–10 min, linear gradient to 20% A and 80% B; 10–14 min, linear gradient to 0% A and 100% B; 14–15 min, maintained at 0% A and 100% B; 15.1–16 min, returned to initial conditions (95% A and 5% B). The column temperature was maintained at 45°C, and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in dual polarity mode (±). The scan parameters were set as follows: the mass scan range was 100–1200 m/z in both positive and negative modes. The resolution was 120,000 for MS1 and 7,500 for MS2 in both modes. The spray voltage was +3.6 kV in positive mode and -3.2 kV in negative mode. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas flows were set to 30 and 25 arbitrary units, respectively, and the capillary temperature was maintained at 350°C in both modes. The normalized collision energy (NCE) was set to 28% for MS2 acquisitions.</p>	<p>Raw data processing involved peak alignment, normalization, and integration using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Nonlinear Dynamics). The parameters were set as follows: precursor ion tolerance of 5 ppm for the self-built library and 10 ppm for METLIN; product ion tolerance of 10 ppm for the self-built library and 20 ppm for METLIN. Data normalization was performed using total ion current (TIC) normalization. For statistical analysis, orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) modeling was conducted using SIMCA v16 to discriminate metabolites, with a variable importance in projection (VIP) threshold of &gt; 1.0 and a significance level of p &lt; 0.05.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using the following databases: the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), LipidMaps (v2.3), METLIN, and EMDB2.0, which provided retention time, MS/MS fragment information, and isotopic distribution data. Metabolites were matched based on retention time alignment, exact mass (MS1) and MS/MS fragmentation patterns, and isotopic distribution matching. A total of 193 differential metabolites (DEMs) were identified, with criteria including VIP &gt; 1.0 and p &lt; 0.05. These metabolites were classified into 13 categories, including amino acids, bile acids, and carbohydrates.</p>

Study Title	Plasma non-targeted metabolome of high- and low-body-weight broilers
Study Description	<p>In large-scale poultry farming, broilers of the same breed share a common genetic background and identical management conditions, yet substantial individual variation in body weight persists. In this study, we identified distinct plasma proteomic profiles between high and low weight broilers, with low weight broilers exhibiting a heightened systemic inflammatory response. The gut microbiome differences between groups were minimal, and fecal microbiota transplantation from high or low weight broilers did not significantly affect body weight. However, lung microbiome analysis revealed that high weight broilers had a significantly higher abundance of Lactobacillus, associated with altered plasma tryptophan metabolism. Aerosolized<em> Lactobacillus rhamnosus</em>, <em>Lactobacillus reuteri</em>, and their combination were administered to modulate the lung microbiota. This intervention significantly enhanced production performance and alleviated LPS-induced systemic inflammation. Aerosolization of Lactobacillus strains elevated metabolites such as L-tryptophan (Trp), indoleacrylic acid (IAA), and indole-3-carboxaldehyde (I3C) in plasma and lung tissues. Mechanistic explorations elucidate that IAA and I3C activated the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) pathway, promoting nuclear translocation and upregulating SOCS3 expression in macrophages. This inhibited the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway, mitigating LPS-induced inflammation. Our findings provide insights into the interaction between poultry production and lung microbiota, establishing Lactobacillus aerosolization as a promising strategy to combat systemic inflammation and improve poultry health and performance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Twenty high-weight broilers (&gt;3.1 kg, n=10) and twenty low-weight broilers (&lt;2.7 kg, n=10), aged 42 days, were obtained from the broiler breeding facility of Shaanxi Haobang Food Co., Ltd. Blood was collected from the wing veins of broilers, transferred into anticoagulant tubes, and centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes at a low temperature to separate plasma for untargeted metabolomic sequencing.</p>	<p>Plasma samples (100 L) were mixed with 400 L of extraction solution (acetonitrile:methanol = 1:1) containing 0.02 mg/mL of internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine). The mixture was subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 minutes (5C, 40 kHz), followed by incubation at -20C for 30 minutes. After centrifugation for 15 minutes, the supernatant was removed, dried under nitrogen, and re-dissolved in 100 L of re-solution (acetonitrile:water = 1:1). A second round of low-temperature ultrasonic extraction was performed for 5 minutes (5C, 40 kHz), followed by centrifugation for 10 minutes. The final supernatant was transferred to an injection vial for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Analyses were performed by Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China) using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System and an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) column. The mobile phases were: solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water: acetonitrile, 95:5, v/v) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile: isopropanol: water, 47.5:47.5:5, v/v). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C </p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF-X Mass Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. The settings were as follows: source temperature 400 °C; sheath gas flow rate 40 arb; auxiliary gas flow rate 10 arb; ion spray voltage floating (ISVF) −2800 V (negative mode) and 3500 V (positive mode); normalized collision energy (NCE): 20-40-60 V; full MS resolution: 70,000; MS/MS resolution: 17,500. Data acquisition was carried out in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode, with a scan range of 70–1050 m/z.</p>	<p>The LC-MS raw data were processed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, resulting in a data matrix of retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. The MS and MS/MS data were matched against the HMDB, Metlin, and the self-built library of Majorbio to identify metabolites. The raw data were uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform (cloud.majorbio.com) for preprocessing.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Differential metabolites were identified based on the variable importance in projection (VIP) values from the OPLS-DA model and Students t-test (P&lt;0.05). Metabolites with VIP&gt;1 and P&lt;0.05 were considered significantly differential. Pathway analysis of differential metabolites was performed using the KEGG database, and pathway enrichment was carried out using Fishers exact test via the Python package scipy.stats.</p>

Study Title	TEX44 CPT1B Axis Regulates Mitochondrial Sheath Assembly and Fatty Acid Oxidation in Sperm
Study Description	<p>Mitochondrial fatty acid β-oxidation (FAO) is essential for energy production and cellular homeostasis, yet its specific role in sperm function has remained unclear. Through whole-exome sequencing of 673800 patients with asthenozoospermia, we identified biallelic TEX44 variants in six individuals, all of whom exhibited defective mitochondrial sheath assembly and impaired sperm motility. Using Tex44 knockout mice, we demonstrated that TEX44 interacts with carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1B (CPT1B) to form a mitochondrial glue, anchoring adjacent mitochondria and facilitating the assembly of the sperm-specific mitochondrial sheath. In vitro, we show that purified TEX44 protein can TEX44 modulates CPT1B enzymatic activity, limiting the conversion of long-chain fatty acids such as palmitic acid and myristic acid into acyl-carnitines, thereby reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production. Loss of TEX44 disrupts this regulatory mechanism, leading to unregulated FAO, excessive ROS generation, and severe oxidative damage to sperm DNA and flagellar structure. Additionally, germ cell-specific Cpt1b knockout mice exhibited phenotypes similar to TEX44 deficiency, including mitochondrial sheath defects and reduced sperm motility. These findings reveal a sperm-specific mechanism by which TEX44 regulates CPT1B activity to balance FAO and ROS generation, providing critical insights into energy metabolism, mitochondrial integrity, and male infertility.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mouse spermatozoa were collected from the cauda epididymis of 8-week-old male mice by dissection. The epididymal tissues were incubated at 37°C for 5 minutes in modified Human Tubal Fluid (HTF) medium (FUJIFILM Irvine Scientific, 90126) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, 16000-044) to allow spermatozoa to swim out.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted using 800 μL of cold methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v), which also served to precipitate proteins. The mixture was transferred to a new centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 14,000 g for 5 minutes to collect the supernatant. The supernatant was dried under vacuum centrifugation, and the dried extracts were re-dissolved in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were separated using a Vanquish LC ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher) equipped with a HILIC column. The column temperature was set at 25°C, the flow rate was 0.5 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The mobile phase consisted of A: water containing 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonium hydroxide, and B: acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0 to 0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5 to 7 min, B decreased linearly from 95% to 65%; 7 to 8 min, B decreased linearly from 65% to 40%; 8 to 9 min, B was maintained at 40%; 9 to 9.1 min, B increased linearly from 40% to 95%; and 9.1 to 12 min, B was maintained at 95%. During the analysis, samples were kept at 4°C in the automatic injector. To minimize instrument signal fluctuations, samples were analyzed in a random order. A QC sample was inserted into the sample queue to monitor system stability and assess the reliability of the experimental data.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on an Orbitrap Exploris™ 480 mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher) coupled with the Vanquish UHPLC system. Both positive and negative ion modes were employed using electrospray ionization (ESI). The ESI source conditions were as follows: Ion Source Gas1 (Gas1) set to 50, Ion Source Gas2 (Gas2) set to 2, source temperature set to 350°C, and IonSpray Voltage Floating (ISVF) set to 3500 V in positive mode and 2800 V in negative mode. The TOF MS scan was performed over the m/z range of 70–1200 Da with a resolution of 60,000 and scan accumulation time of 100 ms. MS/MS data were acquired using data-dependent acquisition (DDA) with a resolution of 60,000, scan accumulation time of 100 ms, and a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s. The scan range for MS/MS was 70–1200 Da.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>The raw MS data were converted to mzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert before being imported into the freely available XCMS software. For peak picking, the following parameters were used: centWave m/z = 10 ppm, peakwidth = c(10, 60), and prefilter = c(10, 100). For peak grouping, bw = 5, mzwid = 0.025, and minfrac = 0.5 were applied. The extracted ion features were filtered to retain only those with more than 50% nonzero measurements in at least one group. Missing values were imputed using the K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) algorithm, and extreme outliers were removed. The total peak area was normalized to ensure comparability across samples.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using CAMERA (Collection of Algorithms of MEtabolite pRofile Annotation) for isotopes and adducts. Compound identification was based on matching the accurate m/z value (within &lt;10 ppm) and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against an in-house spectral library constructed using authentic chemical standards.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12459
Study Title	Single-cell Transcriptomics and Time-series Metabolite Profiling Reveal the Spatiotemporal Regulation of Flavonoid Biosynthesis Genes and Phytohormone Homeostasis by PAP1 in Arabidopsis
Study Description	Understanding the spatiotemporal regulation of specialized metabolism in plants is critical for advancing both basic plant biology and biotechnological applications. In this study, we utilized single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and time-series metabolite profiling to investigate the regulation of flavonoid biosynthesis and phytohormone homeostasis in Arabidopsis thaliana by PAP1 (PRODUCTION OF ANTHOCYANIN PIGMENT 1). By comparing single-cell transcriptomes of the pap1-D mutant and wild-type plants, we constructed a cell-type-specific atlas of gene expression and high-resolution dynamics of metabolites across developmental stages. Our findings reveal that PAP1 overexpression induces distinct spatiotemporal regulation of phenylpropanoid pathway genes in different cell types and widespread upregulation of glycosylation processes. Metabolomic profiling validated these transcriptional patterns and showed significant changes of metabolites in phenylalanine metabolic processes as pap1-D leaf mature. Additionally, PAP1 overexpression leads to significant changes in phytohormone levels, particularly jasmonate and salicylate, indicating complex crosstalk between flavonoid biosynthesis and hormone homeostasis. This integrated multi-omics approach provides unprecedented insights into the cell-specific regulatory networks controlling specialized metabolism and establishes a valuable framework for optimizing metabolic engineering strategies to enhance the production of bioactive plant compounds.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The Arabidopsis (Arabidopsis thaliana) plants Col-0 (Columbia-0) and pap1-D (Production of Anthocyanin Pigment 1-Dominant) were used in this study. Arabidopsis plants were grown in a growth room at 20-22 °C with 16 h of light and 8 h of dark (long days). In order to avoid the influence of mutual shading among plant populations, the planting gap of plants was controlled so that adult leaves can fully capture light. The sampling time points and types of leaves were stated in the result text. Fresh leaf tissue was collected and stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Fresh leaf tissue was collected and stored at -80 °C. Sample is then lyophilized with a -50 °C freeze-dryer (FreeZone, LABCONCO). Next 10 mg of freeze-dried leaf tissue was homogenized with 1 mL of methanol with metal beads at -20 °C using a homogenizer (Servicebio). Then the sample was centrifuged at 15,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>The separation was performed with Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Scientistic) and BEH C18 column (1.7 µm particle size, 2.1 x 100mm, Waters). The mobile included: 0.1% formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B. The separation was performed under gradient elution as follow: 0.0-1.0 min 2% B, 1.0-19.0 min 2-100% B, 19.0-21.0 min 100% B, 21.0-21.1 min 100-2% B, 21.1-25.0 min 2% B. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. The column temperature was set to 40 °C.</p>	<p>The LC-MS separation was performed on the Vanquish UHPLC system with Orbitrap Exploris 120 MS (Thermo Scientific) (LC-ESI-Orbitrap).&nbsp;The detailed conditions of ESI and MS were as follow. The ESI was at 3500 V for positive mode and -3000 V for negative mode; Sheath gas 45 Arb, Aux Gas 10 Arb, Sweep gas 1 Arb; The Vaporizer temperature was at 320 °C; The ion transfer tube 350 °C. The MS was in full scan with a range of 90-900 m/z with 50% RN lens, 60000 resolution at 200 m/z, followed by DDMS2 (DDA) for MS2 with collision energy 15-35%; intensity filter 1e5; Data dependent properties with 4 MS2 scans.</p>	<p>The LCMS data (.raw) was processed with Progenesis QI software for peak alignment, picking and identification.</p>	<p>Metabolites were agliented with our in-house library, HMDB 5.0, ChEBI), METLIN MS/MS library and LipidBlast with 10 ppm mass tolerance for the precursor and the fragment ion matching using Progenesis MetaScope.</p>

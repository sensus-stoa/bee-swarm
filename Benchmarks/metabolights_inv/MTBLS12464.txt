Study Identifier	MTBLS12464
Study Title	Phyllosphere microbes mediate variety-specific accumulation of di-n-butyl phthalate in choysum (Brassica parachinensis)
Study Description	<p>Plant-microbe interactions in the phyllosphere have been demonstrated to facilitate plant growth and enhance resistance to diverse stresses; nevertheless, their role in the bioaccumulation of contaminants by plants is rarely considered. Here, by comparing differences in the phyllosphere morphology, bacterial community assembly and metabolites between two choysum (Brassica parachinensis) varieties differing in di-n-butyl phthalate (DBP) accumulation, we revealed the pivotal roles of key phyllosphere exudate (fumarate) and recruited specific microbes in determining the variety-specific DBP accumulation in choysum. The low-DBP accumulation variety (LAV) enhanced the recruitment of phyllosphere microbes capable of degrading DBP by increasing fumarate secretion, relative to the high-DBP accumulation variety (HAV), thereby facilitating the colonization of DBP-degrading bacteria via the induction of biofilm formation and ultimately resulting in lower DBP accumulation in leaves. These findings offer novel insights into the LAV formation from the phyllosphere microbial perspective and highlight the role of phyllosphere microbes in mediating pollutant accumulation within crops, which is instrumental in minimizing pollutant accumulation through regulating the phyllosphere microbial community.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Seeds of the two varieties (LAV and HAV) were surface-sterilized with 75% ethanol solution for 30 s, followed by immersion in 2% sodium hypochlorite solution for 10 min. After germinating in a sterile vermiculite for 14 days, the seeds were then transplanted into polyethylene pots with each containing ~6 kg of treated soil. There were 3 treatments for DBP and community-disrupting agent as shown above, i.e., CK, DBP and DBP+community-disrupting agent (Ciprifloxacin). Altogether 6 replicates were conducted for each treatment. Plants were randomly arranged in a glass-enclosed greenhouse, maintaining natural day-night condition and temperature ranging from 25 to 32 °C, accompanied by a relative humidity of 60%. Replenishment of deionized water was conducted every alternate day to maintain appropriate soil moisture content. After 45 days of growth,&nbsp;rhizosphere soil tightly attached to the roots (&lt;2 mm away from the roots) was collected. The soil samples were homogenized, freeze-dried, and then grounded to pass 2 mm sieves for the extraction and determination of root for metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>1. Weigh 50 mg of the sample and add 1000 μL of the extraction solution containing the internal standard (the volume ratio of methanol, acetonitrile and water is 2:2:1, with an internal standard concentration of 20 mg/L), then vortex for 30 s.</p><p>2. Add steel beads and process with a 45 Hz grinding instrument for 10 min, followed by ultrasonic treatment for 10 min (in an ice water bath).</p><p>3. Let it stand at -20 °C for 1 h.</p><p>4. Centrifuge the sample at 4 °C and 12,000 rpm for 15 min.</p><p>5. Carefully take 500 μL of the supernatant and transfer it to an EP tube.</p><p>6. Dry the extract in a vacuum concentrator.</p><p>7. Add 160 μL of the extraction solution (the volume ratio of acetonitrile and water is 1:1) to the dried metabolites for re-dissolution.</p><p>8. Vortex for 30 s and ultrasonic treat for 10 min in an ice water bath.</p><p>9. Centrifuge the sample at 4 °C and 12,000 rpm for 15 min.</p><p>10. Carefully take 120 μL of the supernatant and transfer it to an injection vial. Take 10 μL from each sample and mix them to form a QC sample for machine detection.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separations were performed on an Acquity UPLC system (Waters) equipped with a ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) coupled to a ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 VanGuard Pre-column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 5 mm; Waters) applying the following binary gradient at a flow rate of 400 µL/min: 0.0-0.25 min, isocratic 98% A (water/formic acid, 99.9/0.1, v/v), 2% B (acetonitrile/formic acid, 99.9/0.1, v/v); 0.25-10.0 min, linear from 2 to 98% B; 10.0-13.0 min, 2% A and 98% B; 13.0-13.1 min, linear from 98 to 2% B; 13.1-15.0 min, 98% A and 2% B. The injection volume was 1.0 µL (full loop injection) by Waters ACQUITY I-Class Sample Manager.</p>	<p>Scan Polarity: positive and negative</p><p>Scan range: 50-1200 m/z</p><p>Instrument: Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high-resolution mass spectrometer</p><p>Ion Source: electrospray ionization, capillary voltage: 2500 V (positive ion mode) or -2000 V (negative ion mode); cone voltage: 30 V; ion source temperature: 100 °C; desolvation gas temperature: 500 °C; reverse gas flow rate: 50 L/h; desolvation gas flow rate: 800 L/h; mass-to-charge ratio acquisition range: 50-1200 m/z.</p><p>Mass Analyzer: MassLynx v4.2 and Progenesis QI software</p>	<p>The raw data collected using MassLynx v4.2 underwent data processing operations such as peak extraction and alignment through Progenesis QI software. The identification was conducted based on the online METLIN database within Progenesis QI software and the self-constructed library of Biomarker, along with the recognition of theoretical fragments. The mass number deviation of the parent ions was within 100 ppm, and that of the fragment ions was within 50 ppm.</p>	<p>1. The raw data collected using MassLynx v4.2 is processed by Progenesis QI software for peak extraction, peak alignment and other data processing operations, based on the Progenesis QI software online METLIN database and Biomark’s self-built library for identification, and at the same time, theoretical fragment identification and mass deviation All are within 100 ppm.</p><p>2. After normalizing the original peak area information with the total peak area, the follow-up analysis was performed. Principal component analysis and Spearman correlation analysis were used to judge the repeatability of the samples within group and the quanlity control samples. The identified compounds are searched for classification and pathway information in KEGG, HMDB and lipidmaps databases. According to the grouping information, calculate and compare the difference multiples, T test was used to calculate the difference significance p-value of each compound. The R language package ropls was used to perform OPLS-DA modeling, and 200 times permutation tests was performed to verify the reliability of the model. The VIP value of the model was calculated using multiple cross-validation. The method of combining the difference multiple, the P value and the VIP value of the OPLS-DA model was adopted to screen the differential metabolites. The screening criteria are FC &gt; 1, P value &lt; 0.05 and VIP &gt; 1. The difference metabolites of KEGG pathway enrichment significance were calculated using hypergeometric distribution test.</p>

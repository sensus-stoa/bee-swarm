Study Title	Multi-omics analysis of Arabidopsis leaves upon infection by necrotrophic pathogen Botrytis cinerea
Study Description	<p>Botrytis cinerea is a necrotrophic pathogen that induces host cell death, causing substantial economic losses in agriculture. In this study, we show that functional depletion of INOSITOL AUXOTROPHY 80 (INO80), a nuclear factor involved in local chromatin remodeling, reduces the susceptibility of model plant Arabidopsis to this necrotrophic pathogen. Using spatial transcriptomics, a cutting-edge technology enabling simultaneous acquisition of transcriptomes from distinct regions within the same tissue, we provide an unprecedented spatial profiling of the transcriptional response in Arabidopsis leaves following B. cinerea infection. Sequential changes in numerous key genes associated with plant defense pathways were observed across different regions of infected leaves. Additionally, by comparing the phosphoproteome and metabolome of wild-type (WT) and ino80 mutant leaves after B. cinerea infection, we reveal the role of INO80 in regulating multiple hormone pathways and metabolic processes, underscoring the critical importance of chromatin machinery in modulating plant responses to biotic stresses. Collectively, our findings establish a framework for describing, exploring and elucidating spatial information alongside plant multi-molecular responses upon encountering pathogenic challenges.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The Botrytis cinerea strain B05.10 was cultured on potato dextrose agar at 28 for 7-10 days, and subsequently transferred to potato dextrose broth and cultured for another 2-3 days. The spores were diluted by sterile water as a spore suspension with a final concentration of 1 x 105 spores per milliliter (equivalent to 10 spores in the middle 25 squares of a hemocytometer). And 5-6 weeks-aged Arabidopsis plants were selected for spore inoculation. The spore suspension was inoculate.</p>	<p><strong>1.Metabolite Extraction:</strong></p><p>Briefly, 100 mg solid sample was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead was added. 800 L of extraction solution (methanol:water, 4:1, v:v, containing four internal standards ( 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine, etc) were used for metabolite extraction. Samples were ground by the Wonbio-96c (Shanghai wanbo biotechnology co., LTD) frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10 °C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extractionfor 30 min (5 °C, 40 kHz). The samples were left at -20 °C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4 °C, 13,000 x g), and the supernatant was transferred to the injection vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p><p><strong>2.Quality control sample:</strong></p><p>As a part of the system conditioning and quality control process, a pooled quality control sample (QC) was prepared by mixing equal volumes of all samples. The QC samples were disposed and tested in the same manner as the analytic samples. It helped to represent the whole sample set, which would be injected at regular intervals (every 5-15 samples) in order to monitor.</p>	<p>1. The <strong>LC-MS/MS analysis</strong> of sample was conducted on a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive systemequipped with an ACQUITY BEH C18 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.7 m; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China).</p><p>2. The <strong>mobile phases</strong> consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (2:98, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (solvent B).</p><p>3. The <strong>gradient conditions</strong> are as follows: 0.0-0.5 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 2%; 0.5-7.5 min, mobile phase B was increased from 2% to 35%; 7.5-13.0 min, mobile phase B was increased from 35% to 95%; 13.0-14.4 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 95%; 14.4-14.5 min, mobile phase B was decreased from 95% to 2%; 14.5-16.0 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 2%.</p><p>4. The <strong>column temperature</strong> was 40 °C.</p><p>5. The <strong>flow rate</strong> was 0.40 mL/min.</p><p>6. The <strong>injection volume</strong> was&nbsp;3 L.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis was performed using a Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system (Thermo Scientific) coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The instrument operated in both positive and negative ion modes. The optimal conditions were set as followed:</p><p>(1) source temperature at 400 °C;</p><p>(2) sheath gas flowrate at 40 arb;</p><p>(3) Aux gas flow rate at 10 arb;</p><p>(4) ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at -2800 V in negative mode and 3500 V in positive mode, respectively;</p><p>(5) Normalized collision energy , 20-40-60 V rollingfor MS/MS.</p><p>(6) Full MS resolution was 70000, and MS/MS resolution was 17500.</p><p>(7) Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent Acquisition (DDA) mode.</p><p>(8) The detection was carried out over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC-MS raw data was performed by <strong>Progenesis QI</strong> (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) software, and a 3D data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this 3D matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, <strong>deredundant and peak pooled</strong>.</p><p>The data were analyzed through the <strong>free online platform of majorbio choud platform</strong> (cloud.majorbio.com). Metabolic features detected at least 80% in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, <strong>minimum metabolite values were imputed</strong> for specific samples in which the metabolite levels fell below the lower limit of quantitation, and <strong>each metabolic features were normalized by sum</strong>. To reduce the errors caused by sample preparation and instrument instability, the response intensity of the sample mass spectrum peaks was normalized by the sumnormalization method, and then the normalized data matrix was obtained. Meanwhile, variables with<strong> relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30%</strong> of QC samples were removed, and <strong>log10 processing </strong>was performed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p><strong>1.Transformation of Raw Data:</strong></p><p>The LC-MS raw data were preprocessed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation). A 3D data matrix (sample, retention time, ion intensity) in CSV format was exported. Internal standard peaks, noise, column bleed and derivatization-related artifacts were removed. Data were filtered to retain metabolic features detected in at least 80% of samples, missing values were imputed with minimum values, and sum normalization was applied. Features with RSD &gt; 30% in QC samples were excluded, and the data were log10-transformed for downstream analysis. All subsequent processing was performed via the Majorbio Cloud Platform.</p><p><strong>2.Metabolite Identification:</strong></p><p>Metabolite features were identified by matching MS/MS data to a self-built plant-specific metabolite database (MJDBPM) within Progenesis QI. Perform variance analysis on the matrix file after data preprocessing. The Rpackage ropls (version 1.6.2) was used to perform principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal least partial squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), and 7-cycle interactive validation evaluating the stability of the model. The metabolites with VIP &gt; 1, p &lt; 0.05 were determined as significantly different metabolites based on the Variable importance in the projeciton (VIP) obtained by the OPLS-DA model and the p-value generated by students t test.</p><p><strong>3.Metabolite Annotation:</strong></p><p>Differential metabolites among two groups were mapped into their biochemical pathways through metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis based on KEGG database (http://www. genome.jp/kegg). These metabolites could be classified according to the pathways they involvedor the functions they performed. Enrichment analysis was used to analyze a group of metabolites in a function node whether appears or not. The principle was that the annotation analysis of a single metabolite develops into an annotation analysis of a group of metabolites. Python packages scipy.stats (https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy) was used to perform enrichment analysis to obtain the most relevant biological pathways for experimental treatments.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12471
Study Title	Combined analysis of transcriptome and metabolome reveals the accumulation of related metabolites and gene regulation networks during faba bean seed development
Study Description	<p>Background: Faba bean seeds are nutrient-dense, serving as a vital plant-based protein source for food and vegetables. However, the metabolic accumulation and transcriptional regulatory networks during seed development remain poorly understood.</p><p>Results: Metabolomic analysis of two developmental stages in the vegetable faba bean TongCanXian No.7 (TCX) and the grain faba bean QiDou No.2 (QD) identified 868 metabolites. Key metabolic pathways such as flavonoid biosynthesis, flavone and flavonol biosynthesis, ABC transporters, and amino acid metabolism were highlighted. Transcriptomic analysis revealed 37,457 genes with 9,862 differentially expressed genes (3,550 up-regulated and 4,655 down-regulated between the 45 days after anthesis (DAA) and 30 DAA phases). These genes were notably enriched in pathways including starch and sucrose metabolism, flavonoid biosynthesis, and glutathione metabolism. Specifically, within the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway, 19 differentially accumulated metabolites and 34 differentially expressed genes were identified.</p><p>Conclusion: This study delivers a robust theoretical foundation for elucidating the molecular and metabolic development in faba bean seeds, thereby advancing our understanding of seed biology and its potential applications in improving nutritional quality.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The vegetable faba bean cultivar TCX and the grain faba bean cultivar QD were grown in Xueyao village, Rugao City, Nantong City, Jiangsu Province, China in 2021. Tag the flowers of two cultivars when they opened completely. Fresh pods collected at 30 and 45&nbsp;days after anthesis were processed as follows: Pods were manually dehulled, and fresh seeds were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for subsequent analysis. Fresh seeds pooled from multiple plants constituted one biological replicate. Altogether 3 biological replicates were performed for physiological parameter measurements, while transcriptome and metabolome analyses were conducted with 4 and 6 biological replicates, respectively.</p>	<p>Briefly, weigh 50 mg of sample into a 2 mL microtube. Add 400 µL of extraction solvent (methanol:water, 4:1, v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine). Process as follows, grinding: 6 min at -10 °C, 50 Hz (Freezer/Mill system), ultrasonic extraction: 30 min at 5 °C, 40 kHz (Branson 5800 bath), incubation: 30 min at -20 °C, centrifugation: 15 min at 13,000 x g, 4 °C (Beckman Coulter Allegra X-15R), supernatant transfer: collect 200 µL clarified supernatant for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>The instrument platform for LC-MS analysis is UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system of Thermo Fisher Scientific. The chromatographic separation and analytical conditions were as follows: A sample injection volume of 2 μL was separated using an HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm particle size) prior to introduction into the mass spectrometry system. The column was maintained at a constant temperature of 40 °C, while all test samples were stored at 4 °C throughout the analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry parameters were configured as follows: ion source heater temperature at 425 °C, capillary temperature at 325 °C, sheath gas flow rate 50 arb, auxiliary gas flow rate 13 arb; ion spray voltage set to -3500 V in negative ion mode and 3500 V in positive ion mode; and MS/MS analysis employing three-step gradient collision energies (20-40-60 V). Full-scan mass spectrometry resolution was set at 60,000, while the MS/MS resolution was 7,500. Data acquisition was conducted via Data Dependent Acquisition mode, covering a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>LC-MS raw data preprocessing was conducted using Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was achieved through database matching against HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca), Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu) and the Majorbio Database (Ren et al., 2022). Subsequent data analysis was performed on the Majorbio Cloud Platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com). Multivariate statistical analyses, including Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA), were implemented using the R package ropls (v1.6.2). DAMs were identified using stringent thresholds: fold change (FC) ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5, P &lt; 0.05 and variable importance in projection (VIP) ≥ 1. Functional annotation of identified differential metabolites was performed through pathway mapping using the KEGG database (http://www.genome.jp/kegg). Pathway significance analysis was conducted via Fisher's exact test using the scipy. stats module (https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy) from Python's scientific computing ecosystem.</p>

Study Title	Integrated analysis of therapeutic efficacy and resistance mechanisms of lytic phages targeting ST218 K57 CR-hvKp
Study Description	Clinical infection caused by carbapenem-resistant hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (CR-hvKP) is gradually increasing and spreading across the world, and phage therapy is a viable application as an alternative to antibiotics. However, additional clinical application is still restricted by the phage resistance. In order to further explore the mechanism of phage resistance, particularly the difference between in vivo and in vitro. Here, we used a mouse intra-abdominal infection (IAI) model to evaluate the antibacterial properties of two lytic phages and further isolated and characterized phage-resistant mutants. Finally, we determined through genomic and transcriptomic analysis that most of the mutation sites in the resistant mutants were located in the capsular polysaccharides gene cluster. However, RM01 and RM12 developed phage resistance by downregulating capsular polysaccharide (CPS) and its transcriptional regulators without any mutations in the CPS gene. In summary, these findings provided further evidence in phage therapy, particularly in addressing the issue of CR-hvKP infections.

Study Protocol Description	<p>1) Measure the OD value of the bacteria solution grown to a certain degree, and adjust the OD value of each sample to the same with the culture medium to ensure the uniform number of sampled bacteria.</p><p>2) Take one 15m centrifuge tube, weigh and record the weight of the centrifuge tube (only one needs to be recorded here as it was leveled in step 1).</p><p>3) Add 5 mL of the same amount of bacterial solution to the above centrifuge tube, discard the medium after centrifugation, then wash it 2-3 times with saline or PBS, wash off the residual medium, centrifuge to the supernatant, retain the bacterial precipitate, completely remove the liquid with a gun, weigh again and record the weight B, use B-A to get the wet weight of the bacteria, see if it meets the sample delivery requirements, and if not enough, continue to take the bacterial solution and centrifuge until it meets the sample delivery requirements. Record the volume of bacterial solution required to meet the sample delivery requirements. </p><p>4) For all remaining bacterial samples, take the volume recorded above, centrifuge to remove the medium, wash 2-3 times in saline or PBS, and thoroughly remove the supernatant with a lancet.</p><p>5) The pellet, together with the centrifuge tube, was frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored in a -80 °C refrigerator.</p>	<p>1. Pipette 100 μL of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube; 2. Add 400 μL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) and vortex for 30 s; 3. Freeze in a-20℃ refrigerator for 30 min; 4. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, take 400 μL of the supernatant and concentrate to dryness under vacuum; 5. Add 150 μL 50%methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine) to dissolve and vortex for 30 seconds; 6. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22 μm filter membrane, and add the filtrate to the test bottle; 7. Take 10-20 μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample to evaluate instrument stability and data reliability</p>	<p>Chromatographic methods An ACQUITY UPLCHSST3column (100Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min, a column temperature of 40°C, an autosampler temperature of 8°C, and an injection volume of 2 μL. Positive and negative mode mobile phase: mobile phase A is 0.1% formic acid water, mobile phase B is acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid)</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source, spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325℃, auxiliary gas temperature 300℃, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT 100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s, secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto. All formal samples and QC sample were loaded into the equipment according to the above-mentioned chromatography and mass spectrometry methods. Before the formal injection, QC sample were injected 2-4 times to balance the system. During the injection process, one QC sample was injected for every 5-10 samples for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p>Metabolite library search The .raw format data was imported into the commercial software Compound Discoverer™ 3.3 (version 3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithm of the software, peak extraction, alignment, correction and other operations were performed. The unique peak quality rating calculation and filter greatly reduced the interference of background peaks and low-quality peaks. The peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the missing values of the undetected peaks were filled based on the software Fill Gaps algorithm, and the Sum total peak area was normalized. The identification of metabolites was based on self-built library PSNGM Database, mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/) and NIST_2020_MSMS spectral library. The MS1mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>	<p>Expression abundance analysis Expression abundance density plots and violin plots for metabolite abundances were generated using ggplot2 (version 3.4.1). Metabolite abundance values were subjected to clustering analysis using the Pheatmap package (version 1.0.12) in R, concurrently performing bidirectional clustering of both samples and metabolites. A heatmap was drawn to visualize the abundance profiles of metabolic products in the samples.</p>

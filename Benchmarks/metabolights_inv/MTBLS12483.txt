Study Title	Tryptophan metabolic disorder in gut-brain axis induces anxiety-like behavior via neuronal pyroptosis in mice exposed to radiofrequency radiation
Study Description	<p>Radiofrequency radiation (RFR) with specific parameters could alter brain function, yet the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. The gut-brain axis is reported to be important in regulating brain function. Therefore, this study used gut microbiota intervention, regulation of tryptophan metabolite and other methods to explore the effect of the gut-brain axis in brain function changes caused by RFR. It was found that 4.9 GHz (a commonly used frequency of 5G) RFR exposure 35 d induced anxiety-like behaviors in mice, accompanied by gut microbiota dysbiosis, tryptophan metabolites imbalance and neuronal pyroptosis. Intervention of intestinal flora through probiotics alleviated RFR-induced anxiety-like behaviors, improved tryptophan metabolism in the brain and reduced neuronal pyroptosis in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC). Furthermore, elevating the levels of tryptophan metabolism 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT, a key molecule for regulating anxiety) in brain through the administration of the selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) paroxetine ameliorated RFR-induced anxiety-like behaviors and NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated neuronal pyroptosis in the mPFC. This study reveals that RFR can induce gut microbiota dysbiosis, tryptophan metabolism imbalance of gut-brain axis, subsequent neuronal damage in the mPFC and ultimately anxiety-like behaviors in mice. These findings provide novel insights into the mechanism of RFR-induced brain function disorders.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mice were randomly allocated into Sham group and C group. The awake mice in C group were placed&nbsp;into a porous plexiglass box (8 x 4 x 3 cm) and were exposed to&nbsp;5G RFR. The RFR device&nbsp;consist of a signal generator, an amplifier and a radiating antenna. Mice were exposed under the specific conditions&nbsp;with a frequency of 4.9 GHz and a power density of 50 W/m2&nbsp;for 35 d (1 h/d). The whole exposure process was performed in the specific room composed of absorbers to shield the influence of other electromagnetic radiation. The awake mice in Sham group were&nbsp;exposed to same treatment procedure as previously described while RFR device&nbsp;was not&nbsp;turned on. The weight of mice was measured regularly. The rectal temperature of mice before and after exposure was measured with a temperature meter. In the last week of RFR exposure, we collected feces and brains of mice to analyze metabolite content.</p>	<p>The standards of tryptophan and its metabolites were accurately weighed, and individual stock solutions were prepared using 50% methanol. Appropriate volumes of each stock solution were combined to prepare a mixed standard, which was then diluted with ultrapure water to generate a series of mixed standard solutions at varying concentrations. For sample preparation, mouse brain tissue samples were accurately weighed, and the internal standard working solution (4000 ng/mL) was added. The extraction solvent (methanol:water, 4:1) was added to the samples. The mixture was homogenized by grinding for 6 min at -10 °C and 50 Hz, followed by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min at 5 °C and 40 kHz. The samples were allowed to stand at -20 °C for 30 min and then centrifuged at 13,000 RCF for 15 min at 4 °C. A 150 µL sample&nbsp;of the supernatant was collected and dried under nitrogen. The residue was redissolved in 1% acetonitrile water (containing 0.1% formic acid), vortexed for 30 s, and subjected to low-temperature ultrasonication for 15 min at 5 °C and 40 kHz. The solution was centrifuged again at 13,000 RCF for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a Nexera Series LC-40 system coupled with a QTRAP 6500+ mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China). Briefly, samples were separated by an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1 x 150 mm, 1.8 μm) thermostated at 40 °C. Separation of the metabolites was achieved at 1 mL/min flow rate with a mobile phase as a gradient consisted of water, containing 0.1% formic acid (solvent A) and 100% acetonitrile in water containing 0.1% formic acid (solvent B). The total chromatographic separation was 18 min. The conditions of the elution gradient were as follows: 0.0-2.5 min, 1-11% B; 2.5-5.5 min, maintain 11% B; 5.5-6.5 min, 11-28% B; 6.5-7.5 min, maintain 28% B; 7.5-12.5 min, 28-50% B; 12.5-13.5 min, 50-95% B; 13.5-15.5 min, maintain 95% B; 15.5-15.6 min, 95-1% B; 15.6-18 min, maintain 1% B. During the eriod of analysis, all these samples were stored at 4 °C.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometric data was collected using a UHPLC coupled to a QTRAP 6500+ mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, USA) equipped with a electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode. The source and gas settings were as follows: source temperature at 550 °C; curtain gas (CUR) at 35 psi; CAD gas pressure Medium; both Ion Source Gas1 and Gas2 at 50 psi; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at 5500 V in positive mode.</p>	<p>Default parameters were used in AB Sciex quantitative software OS for automatic identification and integration of each ion fragment, and manual inspection was assisted. Linear regression standard curve was drawn with the mass spectrum peak area of the analyte as ordinate and the concentration of the analyte as abscissa to calculate sample concentration: The mass spectrum peak area of the sample analyte was substituted into the linear equation to calculate the concentration result.</p>	<p>To evaluate the stability of the analytical system during the run-on process, a quality control sample (Quality Control, QC) will be prepared during the experiment. The QC samples were made by mixing all test samples and are treated in the same way as formal samples. During instrument testing, a QC sample was inserted every 5-15 samples. The repeatability of quality control (QC) samples reflected the stability of the instrument in the whole analysis process. At the same time, it could also be used to find variables with large variation in the analysis system to ensure the reliability of the results.</p>

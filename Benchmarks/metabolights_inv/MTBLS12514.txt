Study Title	Untargeted Serum Metabolomics Reveals Differential Signatures in Gallstone-Associated and Gallstone-Free Gallbladder Cancer
Study Description	<p>Gallbladder cancer (GBC) is an aggressive malignancy often associated with gallstones (GBCGS), a condition distinct from gallstone disease (GSD). Both GBC and GBCGS are rare, with unclear pathogenesis and no established biomarker-based solutions. This pilot study aimed to identify distinct metabolic signatures in GBC and GBCGS for early diagnosis and stratification of high-risk GSD patients. A comparative untargeted serum metabolomic profiling was performed across three patient groups: GBC (n1=9), GBCGS (n2=11), and GSD (n3=10). A total of 35,385 mass-features in both positive and negative ionization modes with MS/MS characteristics were detected and annotated into 736 biochemicals.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 30 patients diagnosed with GBC (n1=9), GBCGS (n2=11) and GSD (n3=10) were enrolled. Benign GSD patients were included as the control group, while GBC and GBCGS as the malignant gallbladder variants.&nbsp;Patient blood samples were collected, incubated for 30 min at room temperature and centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 5 min to separate out the serum. The collected serum was then stored at -80 °C for further analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction and profiling was performed as previously described[1]. Serum metabolites were extracted by the protein precipitation method. Next 100 µL of serum samples were vortexed with 300 µL of chilled methanol, following centrifugation at 15,000 x g, in 4 °C for 15 min. The supernatant containing the extracted metabolites was carefully collected, lyophilized and resuspended in 100 µL solution of water:methanol (80:20, v/v), and analyzed with a UHPLC-HRMS system.</p><p><br></p><p>For quality assurance, pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared in triplicates by taking small aliquots from study samples and were injected at three points: start of the run to set the column conditions, between samples, and at the end of analysis to monitor intra-study reproducibility and avoid systemic errors. Additionally, a mixture of 10 different amino acids was injected at the beginning and end of the batch to ensure system stability.</p><p><br></p><p>[1] Dunn, W. B.; Broadhurst, D.; Begley, P.; Zelena, E.; Francis-McIntyre, S.; Anderson, N.; Brown, M.; Knowles, J. D.; Halsall, A.; Haselden, J. N.; Nicholls, A. W.; Wilson, I. D.; Kell, D. B.; Goodacre, R. Procedures for Large-Scale Metabolic Profiling of Serum and Plasma Using Gas Chromatography and Liquid Chromatography Coupled to Mass Spectrometry. Nat. Protoc. 2011, 6 (7), 1060–1083. https://doi.org/10.1038/nprot.2011.335.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed with a Dionex Ultimate 3000 UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), equipped with a Phenomenex Kinetex C18 column (150 x 2.1 mm, 2.6 µm particle size). The column was maintained at 40 °C with an injection volume of 10 µL and flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (Solvent A) and methanol (solvent B), with elution gradient maintained as: 5% B from 0.0 to 1.0 min, 50% B at 5.0 min, 95% B from 10.0 min to 22.0 min, and 5% B from 24.0 to 25.0 min.</p>	<p>Profiling was performed in both positive and negative ionization modes with a Q-Exactive high resolution mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, USA) equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) probe. A full scan over an m/z range of 80-1200 at a resolution of 70,000 was conducted, followed by MS/MS analysis in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Product ions were generated in the LTQ trap using stepped/normalized collision energy of 20, 40 and 60 eV. The HESI source parameters were set as follows: spray voltage: 3.5 KV; sheath gas flow rate: 30; capillary temperature: 275 °C; and auxiliary gas heater temp: 350 °C.</p>	<p>Raw spectra obtained were analyzed with an established&nbsp;protocol[1]. The steps included conversion of raw data into analysis base file (*.abf) format using ProteoWizard software and processing with MS-Dial with the default parameters.</p><p><br></p><p>[1] Tsugawa, H.; Nakabayashi, R.; Mori, T.; Yamada, Y.; Takahashi, M.; Rai, A.; Sugiyama, R.; Yamamoto, H.; Nakaya, T.; Yamazaki, M.; Kooke, R.; Bac-Molenaar, J. A.; Oztolan-Erol, N.; Keurentjes, J. J. B.; Arita, M.; Saito, K. A Cheminformatics Approach to Characterize Metabolomes in Stable-Isotope-Labeled Organisms. Nat. Methods. 2019, 16 (4), 295–298. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0358-2.</p>	<p>The metabolites were identified with MS-finder (Version 3.6) and manual curation using public databases such as human metabolome database (HMDB), Serum metabolome database, PubChem, Chemical Entities of Biological Interest (ChEBI), Mass Bank of North America (MONA) and Lipid-maps.</p>

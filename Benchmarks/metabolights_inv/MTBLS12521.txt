Study Identifier	MTBLS12521
Study Title	Metabolomic Microbiome Modulations Induced by ZIF8@ZIF67 Supplementation in the Midgut of Bombyx mori
Study Description	Bombyx mori is an economically crucial lepidopteran insect, and its health is closely related to the gut microbiota and metabolic status. Our previous research confirmed that metal organic frameworks nanocomposite ZIF8@ZIF67 supplementation can significantly enhance silkworm survival rates upon bacterial challenges without adversely affecting its growth or cocoon weight. To elucidate the mechanism, this study integrated non targeted metabolomics and 16S rRNA sequencing to dissect ZIF8@ZIF67 driven midgut modulations. The results showed that ZIF8@ZIF67 supplementation led to significant metabolic shifts in the midgut of B. mori, with 96 metabolites up-regulated and 54 down-regulated in the negative ion model and 117 up regulated and 43 down regulated in the positive ion model. Key pathways included glutathione metabolism, purine/pyrimidine metabolism and histidine metabolism, which are involved in antioxidant defense, nucleotide and protein synthesis for growth and immunity regulation. Furthermore, the gut microbiota composition of B. mori was significantly altered after ZIF8@ZIF67 supplementation, with a marked increase in the relative abundance of Mammaliicoccus and Enterococcus. Lastly, the correlation between metabolites and microbiomes was analyzed, including Enterococcus and 1_methylhistidine (r=0.8895, P=0.0001), Akkermansia and N_acetyl_d_galactosamine (r=0.8674, P=0.0003). These findings demonstrated that ZIF8@ZIF67 could optimize silkworm health by orchestrating metabolite-microbe interactions to enhance nutrient assimilation and oxidative stress resilience, while simultaneously activating pathways essential for growth regulation and immune function.

Study Protocol Description	<p>ZIF8@ZIF67 was fed to the larvae for 3 consecutive days.&nbsp;midgut tissues from 5 randomly selected B. mori from each replicate (C and T) were collected, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and preserved at -80 °C</p>	<p>Take 100 mg sample, add 1 mL cold 90% methanol. The lysate was homogenized by MP homogenizer (24 x 2, 6.0 m/s, 60 s, twice). The homogenate was sonicated at low temperature (30 min/once, twice), The mixture was centrifuged for 20 min (14,000 x g, 4 °C). The supernatant was dried in a vacuum centrifuge. Quality control samples (QC) are prepared by mixing equal volumes of the samples to be tested.</p>	<p>Samples were separated using a Vanquish LC Ultra-High Performance Liquid Chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a HILIC column. The column temperature was maintained at 25 °C, the flow rate was set to 0.5 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The mobile phase consisted of A: water + 25 mM ammonium acetate + 25 mM ammonia and B: acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0.0-0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5-7.0 min, B linearly decreased from 95% to 65%; 7.0-8.0 min, B further decreased from 65% to 40%; 8.0-9.0 min, B was held at 40%; 9.0-9.1 min, B increased from 40% to 95%; 9.1-12.0 min, B was maintained at 95%. Throughout the analysis, samples were stored in a 4 °C autosampler. To mitigate the effects of instrument signal fluctuations, samples were analyzed continuously in a random order. Quality control (QC) samples were interspersed within the sample queue to monitor and evaluate system stability and data reliability.</p>	<p>Following separation via the Vanquish LC Ultra-High Performance Liquid Chromatography (UHPLC) system, samples were subjected to mass spectrometry analysis using an Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer (Thermo). Detection was performed in both positive and negative ion modes using electrospray ionization (ESI). The ESI source and mass spectrometry parameters were set as follows: Nebulizing gas/auxiliary heating gas 1 (Gas1): 50; auxiliary heating gas 2 (Gas2): 2; ion source temperature: 350 °C; spray voltage (ISVF): 3500 V (positive ion mode) and 2800 V (negative ion mode). The primary mass-to-charge (m/z) detection range was 70-1200 Da, with a resolution of 60,000 and a scan accumulation time of 100 ms. For secondary analysis, a segmented acquisition method was employed with an m/z range of 70-1200 Da, a resolution of 60,000, a scan accumulation time of 100 ms and a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s.</p>	<p>The raw MS data were converted to mzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert (v3.0.6428) before importing into freely available XCMS software (online 3.7.1). For peak picking, the following parameters were used: centWave, m/z = 10 ppm, peakwidth = c(10,60), prefilter = c(10,100). For peak grouping, bw = 5, mzwid = 0.025, minfrac = 0.5 were used. CAMERA (Collection of Algorithms of MEtabolite pRofile Annotation) was sued for annotation of isotopes and adducts. In the extracted ion features, only the variables having more than 50% of the nonzero measurement values in at least one group were kept. Compound identification of metabolites was performed by comparing of accuracy m/z value (&lt;10 ppm), and MS/MS spectra with an in-house&nbsp;database&nbsp;established&nbsp;with&nbsp;available&nbsp;authentic standards.The missing data were filled by KNN (K-Nearest Neighbor) method, and features with RSD greater than 50% are filtered out.</p>	<p>Both positive and negative ion modes were used for metabolite detection to enhance coverage, with each mode analyzed separately. Quality control was maintained through QC samples, which were analyzed using PCA via the R language gmodels package to assess result reliability.</p><p>Cluster analysis was conducted using hierarchical clustering and heatmap visualization after z-score normalization, with correlation analysis to quantify sample similarity. Multidimensional statistical analysis included Principal component analysis (PCA) for preliminary visualization and Partial Least-Squares discriminant analysis and Orthogonal partial Least-Square discriminant analysis for group discrimination, validated by cross-validation and permutation tests.</p><p>Differential metabolites were identified using variable importance in projection (VIP) score from the (O)PLS model, with a threshold of 1, and univariate analysis using the T-test, with a p-value threshold of 0.05. Correlation heatmaps were generated with the R corrplot package, highlighting significant correlations (p ≤ 0.05). Cluster heatmaps and z-score analyses were conducted to show metabolite accumulation differences and relative content. ROC curve analysis using the R pROC package evaluated the predictive power of metabolites, identifying those with the most significant AUC as the most predictive.</p><p>Pathway analysis was conducted to annotate and enrich differentially expressed metabolites. Metabolites were mapped to Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) metabolic pathways for annotation and enrichment analysis, with significantly enriched pathways identified through False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction</p>

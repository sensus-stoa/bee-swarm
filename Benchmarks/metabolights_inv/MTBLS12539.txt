Study Title	Intestinal Lachnospiraceae bacterium-derived Propionate Inhibits the Progression of Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma
Study Description	Gut microbiota has been reported to be associated with the development of various diseases, however, its interaction with clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) remains unknown. To investigate the potential relationship between gut microbiota alteration and ccRCC development, we analyze feces from healthy volunteers and ccRCC patients. We realize that ccRCC patients have a lower abundance of Lachnospiraceae bacterium (L. bacterium). Further experiments reveale that L. bacterium and its metabolites propionate exerte the antitumor effects. Mechanistically, L. bacterium-derived propionate inhibit tumor cell proliferation and migration by down-regulating the expression of HOXD10 and its downstream IFITM1 and then activating JAK1-STAT1/2 pathway. Furthermore, we designe a biofilm coated-L. bacterium as a potential probiotic to improve oral delivery and therapeutic efficacy. Finally, the expanded validation cohort confirme that measuring and targeting L. bacterium and its associated pathways will provide valuable insights into clinical management and improve the prognosis of patients with ccRCC.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Three types of samples were analyzed in this study:</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Human serum</strong></p><p>Human serum samples were collected from 10 patients diagnosed with clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) and 10 age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers (HV). Peripheral blood was drawn and processed by centrifugation to isolate serum. All samples were collected under institutional ethical approval and stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Mouse serum</strong></p><p>Mouse serum samples were obtained from 12 tumor-bearing mice subjected to oral gavage with Lachnospiraceae bacterium or vehicle. Blood was collected via cardiac puncture at the endpoint of the study and processed to isolate serum, which was then stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bacterial culture supernatants</strong></p><p>Bacterial culture supernatants were prepared by culturing Lachnospiraceae bacterium and parallel control in blank PYG medium. After 48 h incubation under anaerobic conditions, cultures were centrifuged to collect supernatants, which were filtered (0.22 µm) and stored at -80 °C prior to analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>All serum and supernatant samples were thawed on ice and randomized before metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were prepared by protein precipitation using appropriate organic solvents. Quality control (QC) samples were generated by pooling equal volumes from each sample and were injected at the beginning and after every 10 samples throughout the analytical run to monitor instrument performance and data reproducibility. No internal standards were used in this targeted analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm; Waters, UK). The column oven was maintained at 50 °C. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (water with 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid). Gradient elution was applied: 0.0-2.0 min, 100% A; 2.0-11.0 min, 0-100% B; 11.0-13.0 min, 100% B; 13.0-15.0 min, 100-0% B. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min and the injection volume was 10 µL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was conducted using a Xevo G2 XS QTOF instrument (Waters, UK) in both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes. Capillary and sampling cone voltages were set at 3.0 kV and 40 V for positive mode, and 2.0 kV and 40 V for negative mode. Data were acquired in centroid MSE mode with a mass range of 50-1200 Da and scan time of 0.2 s. For MS/MS, collision energy was ramped from 20-40 eV. A lock mass (LE) signal was acquired every 3 s for mass calibration. Samples were analyzed in a randomized order within a single batch.</p>	<p>Data preprocessing was performed using Progenesis QI software (version 2.2). Features were aligned, normalized using support vector regression (SVR), and filtered. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on auto-scaled intensities (mean=0, SD=1) using the R package “mixOmics”. Statistical significance was determined using FDR-adjusted p-values (Benjamini-Hochberg method).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based on accurate mass and MS/MS spectra. Identification was performed by spectral matching against public metabolomics databases and published literature. Both reverse-phase positive (RP+) and negative (RP-) ion modes were used to enhance metabolite coverage and confidence in annotation.</p>	<p>Human serum samples were collected from 10 patients diagnosed with clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) and 10 age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers (HV). Peripheral blood was drawn and processed by centrifugation to isolate serum. All samples were collected under institutional ethical approval and stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>Mouse serum samples were obtained from 12 tumor-bearing mice subjected to oral gavage with Lachnospiraceae bacterium or vehicle. Blood was collected via cardiac puncture at the endpoint of the study and processed to isolate serum, which was then stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Bacterial culture supernatants were prepared by culturing Lachnospiraceae bacterium and parallel control in blank PYG medium. After 48 h incubation under anaerobic conditions, cultures were centrifuged to collect supernatants, which were filtered (0.22 µm) and stored at -80 °C prior to analysis.</p><p>All serum and supernatant samples were thawed on ice and randomized before metabolomic analysis.</p>

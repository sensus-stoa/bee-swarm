Study Title	Chemerin sustains the growth of spongiotrophoblast and sinusoidal trophoblast giant cells through fatty acid oxidation
Study Description	<p>Background: Placental metabolic abnormalities are linked to pregnancy complications such as preeclampsia, gestational diabetes mellitus and fetal growth restriction. However, little is known about how the metabolic processes regulate placental development and trophoblast differentiation. The adipokine chemerin has elevated serum levels in pregnant women and regulates placental lipid metabolism, potentially playing a role in both placental development and trophoblast differentiation.</p><p>Results: In this study, we observed the increased chemerin expression on the serum and placenta from the pregnant mice. Chemerin is highly expressed in the extraembryonic primary parietal trophoblast giant cells and the ectoplacental cone (EPC) trophoblast cells. Excessive chemerin treatment in mice results in the increased placental lipid accumulation, promotes the expansion of glycogen trophoblast cell (GlyT) and syncytiotrophoblast, and restricts the growth of spongiotrophoblast (SpT) and sinusoidal trophoblast giant cell (S-TGC). Chemerin deficiency led to increased expression of placental fatty acid oxidation enzymes and disrupted the proliferation of SpT and S-TGC in the labyrinth. Furthermore, we utilized the fatty acid oxidation inhibitor etomoxir, demonstrated that blocking fatty acid oxidation hinders the proliferation of SpT and S-TGC in the labyrinth.</p><p>Conclusions: Our study demonstrated that chemerin-related lipid metabolism is crucial for EPC trophoblast differentiation during placental development, providing evidence that chemerin determines the growth of SpT and S-TGC through fatty acid oxidation. These findings also imply a possible pathological mechanism for pregnancy complications associated with chemerin.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The wild-type group were included placentas from C57BL/6 mice (5 mice) at pregnancy day 18.5. The treated group were included the placentas from chemerin knockout mice (5 mice) at pregnancy day 18.5.</p>	<p>Briefly, 40 μg of&nbsp;tissue sample add 200 uL of water MP homogenate, vortex for 60 s, add 800 uL of methanol acetonitrile solution (1:1, v/v), add 10 uL (10 mM/L SUCCINIC ACID-D6 internal standard), vortex for 60 s, low-temperature ultrasound for 30 min, twice, place at -20 °C for 1 h to precipitate protein, centrifuge at 14,000 rpm at 4 °C for 20 min, take the supernatant and freeze dry it, store the sample at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system were used to separate samples. The sample is placed in a 4 °C automatic sampler with a column temperature of 35 °C. The mobile phase A is a 50 mM ammonium acetate aqueous solution + 1.2% ammonium hydroxide, and the mobile phase B is a 1% acetylacetone acetonitrile solution. The flow rate is 300 μL/min and the injection volume is 2 μL. The relevant liquid phase gradient is as follows: 0.0-1.0 min, 70% for solution B, 1.0-10.0 min, linear change from 70% to 60% for solution B; 10.0-12.0 min, liquid B changes linearly from 60% to 30%; 12.0-15.0 min, B solution maintained at 30%, 15.0-15.5 min, B solution linearly changed from 30% to 70%, 15.1-22.0 min, B solution maintained at 70%. Set a QC sample for each interval of a certain number of experimental samples in the sample queue to detect and evaluate the stability and repeatability of the system; The standard mixture of target substances in the sample queue is used for correcting chromatographic retention time.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a 5500 QTRAP mass spectrometer (SCIEX) in negative ion mode. The QTRAP ESI source conditions are as follows: 5500 QTRAP ESI source conditions are as follows: source temperature: 450 °C; ion Source Gas1: 45; Ion Source Gas2: 45; Curtain gas: 30; ionSapary Voltage Floating: -4500 V; Using MRM mode to detect the ion pairs to be tested.</p>	<p>The raw MS data (wiff / wiff.scan) were converted to mzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert</p>	<p>Use Multiquant 3.0.2 software to extract chromatographic peak area and retention time. Use standard samples of the target substance to correct retention time and perform metabolite identification.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12560
Study Title	Metabolomic landscape of fetal organs development during late gestation in mice
Study Description	Metabolism is the critical basis for mammalian physiological functions. The systematic metabolic characteristics of major organ development during late gestation to adapt to postnatal environmental changes are still absent. Here, we detected metabolic patterns of the mouse fetal organs, including the heart, stomach, liver, brain, and placenta, from embryonic days (E)15.5 to 19.5 using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry combined with RNA sequencing and proteomics data. Our metabolic and multi-omics data showed that organs exhibited their unique metabolic characteristics during late gestation, and significant metabolic pattern transitions, especially the enhancement of digesting the fatty acids and proteins, occurred at the E16.5 to E18.5 stage. Additionally, we found the abundance of carnosine and histidine in the placenta may serve as a way to test their levels in the newborn brain in vitro. Our dataset provides a comprehensive metabolic landscape of mammalian organ development in late gestation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Anatomically dissected pregnant mice at E15.5, E16.5, E18.5, and E19.5, cut out the uterus with microscopic scissors, rinsed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS), the brain, heart, liver, and stomach were removed intact and collected.</p>	<p>Transferred the thawed samples of each tissue to a tissue grinder , and added&nbsp;100 μL cooled water. After thorough grinding, added&nbsp;400 μL&nbsp;of&nbsp;chloroform-methanol (1:1) mixed solution, carefully ground the mixture,&nbsp;and transferred to a new tube. Then vortexed&nbsp;the tube for 30 seconds to mix it thoroughly. All samples were dried with nitrogen.&nbsp;50 μL water-methanol (1:1) mixed solution was&nbsp;added&nbsp;to&nbsp;each dried sample, and vortexed&nbsp;and mixed&nbsp;for 20 seconds, and centrifuged&nbsp;at&nbsp;13000 g for 15 min at 4 ℃. The solution after high-speed centrifugation was&nbsp;transferred to an injection bottle containing an inner liner for testing.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Instrument information: Ultimate 3000 (UPLC, Thermo Fisher)-timsTOF. Chromatographic column: Waters BEH C18 (Dim. (mm): 100 * 2.1), injection volume: 3 μL, flow rate: 0.4 mL/min, column temperature: 40 ℃. The mobile phase consists of 0.05% formic acid water (A) and 0.05% acetonitrile (B).</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry parameters are: ion source type: ESI ion, polarity: positive/negative, capillary tube: 4500 volts (+)/3500 volts (-), nebulizer: 2.0 Bar, dry gas: 8.0 L/min, drying temperature: 200 ℃, calibration solution: sodium formate, quality scanning range: 20-1050, scanning mode: automatic MS/MS.</p>	<p>The raw MS data were first converted to MzXML files using RawConverter (Scripps Research Institute)&nbsp;.</p>	<p>Converted data subjected&nbsp;to peak picking (mz diff = 20 ppm) and grouping (bw = 5, mz window = 0.025, minfrac = 0.2) with MSDIL (version 3.0) software (version 3.14.1) based on the endogenous metabolite database in HMDB. The assigned&nbsp;metabolites were further annotated by matching the isotopic abundance ratio&nbsp;(12C/13C approximately 1.003)&nbsp;and MS/MS spectra&nbsp;information.</p>

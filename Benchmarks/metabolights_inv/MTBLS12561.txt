Study Title	Single-Cell and Spatial Multi-Omics Reveal Mechanical Stress Driving Heterogeneous Xylem Development in Populus [Spatial metabolomics_LCM]
Study Description	Xylem, the predominant tissue for structural support, forms tension wood with G-layer-rich fibers under mechanical stress. Despite being recognized over a century ago, three key biological questions remained unclear: (1) Are fibers in normal and tension wood distinct cells due to morphological differences? (2) Do tension wood fibers arise from different lineages? (3) What are the key genes regulating tension wood formation? We conducted single-cell RNA-seq on normal, tension and opposite xylem. Fibers in normal and tension wood belong to the same cell type and lineage. Differential developmental speed and cell-type ratio in tension and opposite xylem were further validated by spatial transcriptomics and metabolomics. Phosphoproteomics uncovered mechanical sensing mechanisms conserved between stems and roots across angiosperms. We identified a group of genes involved in the cell fate transition in tension wood. The knowledge on the heterogeneity of cell development offers insights for optimizing biomass production and bioenergy yield.

Study Protocol Description	<p>For spatial sample collection, laser capture microdissection (LCM) technology was applied to collect fusiform xylem samples at different differentiation stages on debarked stem cross-sections. Three regions were defined based on their distance from the outer edge: Region 1 (0 to 50 μm), Region 2 (100 to 150 μm), and Region 3 (200 to 250μm). In each region, three layers of libriform fibers were collected using LCM (Leica, LMD7000). Each area consisted of 1000 to 1200 μm2 of libriform fibers. Four area were collected for each region. Additionally, three biological replicates were performed for each sample. To avoid contamination, libriform fibers adjacent to vessel elements or ray parenchyma cells were not collected. After laser dissection, the libriform fibers were collected into 500-μl tube caps and visualized under microscope, which was then added 16 μl of 50% methanol, frozen with liquid nitrogen and stored at -80℃. For the blank control, 1000 μm2 of PET membrane was collected with five technical replicates. Before LC-MS/MS analysis, the collected libriform fibers were transferred to snap neck sample vials.</p>	<p>No extraction procedures were performed in this study.</p>	<p>All LCM samples were analyzed by our optimal single cell metabolomic analytical platform using a multinozzle emitter (Newomics) for electrospray ionization. The instrument consists of a Dionex 3000 UPLC system coupled with a Q Exactive Plus Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The mobile buffer of 50% acetonitrile/0.1% formic acid in ultra-pure water at a flow rate of 700 nl/min for a 5-min data collection time.</p>	<p>The Q Exactive Plus Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was operated in the data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode, in which a full scan (from m/z 100-1500 with the peak resolution of 70,000 at m/z 200) was followed by higher-energy collisional dissociation fragmentation using the Top 10 intensity ions with normalized collision energy of 15 and 25 with the resolution of 17,500 at m/z 200. The automatic gain control (as AGC) value of MS was set as 1e5 with the max injection time as 100 ms. The isolation window was set as 3.0 while the dynamic exclusion duration as 20 s. All samples were analyzed in both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>We performed MS data processing to obtain all available compound information from the datasets of different differentiation stages (Region 1, 2 and 3), including peak picking, peak centroiding and data binning for collecting all m/z-intensity features. The MS raw data was first converted into mzML format followed by peak picking and peak centroiding using MSConvert.</p>	<p>All mass spectra information from 5-min data collection time were then averaged to perform data binning. To reduce peak variability of features from different mass spectra, data binning was performed with a bin width set at 0.0003 based on mass accuracy of our LC-MS/MS system. To distinguish signals/features from random background noise, only features that satisfy both conditions were retained: (1) their maximum intensity is at least 10 times higher than the background across all regions from all samples, and (2) their average intensity in each region is greater than their corresponding standard deviations.</p>

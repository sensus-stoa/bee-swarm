Study Title	Targeting lung microbiota with Lactobacillus aerosolization: a novel strategy to alleviate inflammation and enhance productivity in broilers
Study Description	<p>Background&nbsp;In large-scale poultry farming, broilers of the same breed, sharing a common genetic background and identical management conditions, nevertheless exhibit considerable individual variation in body weight. Multitudinous stressors can profoundly influence the host microbiota, thereby modulating overall health and growth. This study employed multi-omics approaches to elucidate the underlying factors contributing to the disparities between high- and low-weight broilers and to evaluate the effects of&nbsp;Lactobacillus&nbsp;aerosolization on growth performance and systemic inflammation in these animals.</p><p>Results&nbsp;This study identified distinct plasma proteomic profiles between high- and low-weight broilers, with the latter exhibiting a systemic inflammatory response. Gut microbiota differences were minimal, and fecal microbiota transplantation did not alter broiler body weight. However, lung microbiota analysis revealed a higher&nbsp;relative&nbsp;abundance of&nbsp;Lactobacillus&nbsp;in high-weight broilers, linked to altered plasma tryptophan metabolism. Aerosolized&nbsp;Lactobacillus reuteri&nbsp;C501,&nbsp;Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus&nbsp;UA260, and their combination enhanced growth and mitigated LPS-induced inflammation. This intervention increased L-Tryptophan (L-Trp), indoleacrylic acid (IAA), and indole-3-carboxaldehyde (I3C) levels in plasma and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). Mechanistic studies showed that IAA and I3C activated the AHR pathway, promoting SOCS3 expression in macrophages, inhibiting JAK2/STAT3 signaling, and alleviating LPS-induced inflammation.</p><p><strong>Conclusions</strong>&nbsp;Aerosolization of&nbsp;Lactobacillus&nbsp;enhances broiler performance and alleviates LPS-induced stress responses. This effect is mediated through the modulation of tryptophan metabolism, which interacts with the AHR/SOCS3 axis to inhibit JAK2/STAT3 signaling, thereby reducing both pulmonary and systemic inflammation. Our findings provide insights into the interaction between poultry production and lung microbiota, establishing&nbsp;Lactobacillus&nbsp;aerosolization as a promising strategy to combat systemic inflammation and improve poultry health and performance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All birds were weighed after fasting for 12 h. Blood samples were collected from the wing vein using blood collection tubes containing anticoagulants and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 5 min at 4&nbsp;°C to separate plasma. The animals were then euthanized by cervical dislocation. The trachea was cut open using sterile scissors, and 1×&nbsp;phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, Servicebio, China) was injected into the lungs through a 5 mL syringe inserted into the tracheal lumen, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected by three rounds of aspiration and reinjection.</p>	<p>Samples were extracted using methanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a 1:5 ratio, followed by cryo-sonication (40 kHz, 30 min) and incubation at -20 °C for 30 minutes. The resulting supernatant was evaporated under a gentle stream of nitrogen and subsequently reconstituted in methanol. The solution was then filtered through a 0.22 μm nylon membrane.</p>	<p>All analyses were conducted using a QTRAP®&nbsp;5500+ triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX), coupled to an ExionLC™&nbsp;ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a Phenomenex C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7μm particle size, 100 Å pore diameter). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (eluent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (eluent B), delivered at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min using a gradient elution program. The total run time was 10 minutes, with all target analytes eluting within the first 8 minutes. The autosampler temperature was maintained at 15 °C, and the column temperature was set to 35 °C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed in positive electrospray ionization (ESI+) mode, employing a timed multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) approach optimized for each analyte.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Standard solution preparation and detection procedures were based on the method described by van Zundert et al&nbsp;[2].For calibration, standard curves were constructed using L-tryptophan (Sigma-Aldrich) at concentrations of 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000, and 10,000μmol/L; indole acrylic acid (Sigma-Aldrich) at 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, and 1000μmol/L; and indole-3-carboxaldehyde (Sigma-Aldrich) at 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2.5, 5, 10, and 50μmol/L.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The quantification of metabolite concentrations and the generation of standard curves are presented in Table S4 and Fig. S7. External calibration was employed for the quantification of metabolites in the samples (Table S5-10). Statistical analysis and graphs were performed using SCIEX OS 3.4.5 (SCIEX, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, USA), with data expressed as mean ± S.E.M. from at least three independent samples. Statistical significance is indicated by *P&lt;0.05, **P&lt;0.01, ***P&lt;0.001, P&lt;0.05,&nbsp; P&lt;0.01 and&nbsp; P&lt;0.001.</p>

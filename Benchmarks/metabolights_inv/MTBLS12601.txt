Study Identifier	MTBLS12601
Study Title	Single-Cell and Spatial Multi-Omics Reveal Mechanical Stress Driving Heterogeneous Xylem Development in Populus [Spatial metabolomics_MALDI imaging]
Study Description	Xylem, the predominant tissue for structural support, forms tension wood with G-layer-rich fibers under mechanical stress. Despite being recognized over a century ago, three key biological questions remained unclear: (1) Are fibers in normal and tension wood distinct cells due to morphological differences? (2) Do tension wood fibers arise from different lineages? (3) What are the key genes regulating tension wood formation? We conducted single-cell RNA-seq on normal, tension and opposite xylem. Fibers in normal and tension wood belong to the same cell type and lineage. Differential developmental speed and cell-type ratio in tension and opposite xylem were further validated by spatial transcriptomics and metabolomics. Phosphoproteomics uncovered mechanical sensing mechanisms conserved between stems and roots across angiosperms. We identified a group of genes involved in the cell fate transition in tension wood. The knowledge on the heterogeneity of cell development offers insights for optimizing biomass production and bioenergy yield.

Study Protocol Description	<p>For tissue sectioning and sample preparation, the debarked stems of normal and tension wood were frozen with liquid nitrogen, and then the 16-μm thick cross-sections of debarked stems were generated using a cryostat (Leica, CM1900). The tissue slices were mounted onto 3M electrical tape (E17385) and glass conducting slides for spatial metabolomics analysis using MALDI-TOF MS (matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry). To ensure that the tissue slices were firmly appressed to the tape and slide, stamp tweezers were used to further flatten them.</p>	<p>The study is not applicable to this protocol</p>	<p>To supply the matrix for metabolite ionization, the tissue slices were coated with 5 layers of 10 mg/mL CHCA (α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid) using a Suncollect nebulizer (SunChrom, Friedrichsdorf, DE) at a flow rate from 15 to 30 μL/min, with a line distance of 1 mm and a Z-height of 30 mm. After matrix coating, the samples were dried for 10 min under a low vacuum system followed by MALDI-TOF MS analyses.</p><p>Optimal analyte signal was obtained with a MALDI matrix composed of 10 mg/mL CHCA dissolved in 70% MeOH with 0.1% TFA. All standards were suspended in 50% MeOH to 5 µg/mL of final concentration and mix with matrix CHCA (1:1) for MALDI-TOF MS analyses. All MS experiments were conducted using an UltrafleXtreme mass spectrometer (Manufacturer: Bruker, Bremen, DE) equipped with a Smartbeam II 1 kHz Nd:YAG laser, frequency-tripled to a 355 nm wavelength and with an approximate laser spot size of 15 μm in diameter.</p><p>After MALDI imaging analysis, images were acquired with a spatial resolution of ~15 μm per laser spot, collecting data from a 40 x 40 μm area with 2000 laser shots per pixel to provide optimal analyte ion signals. The laser spot movement distance was set to 40 μm. The samples were analyzed in positive ion mode.</p>	<p>The study is not applicable to this protocol</p>	<p>Mass spectra were processed using FlexAnalysis Version 3.4 (Bruker, Bremen, DE), and images were generated using Bruker FlexImaging Version 4.0 software. The data were normalized to the total ion current.</p>	<p>Abscisic acid (ABA), (±)-jasmonic acid-isoleucine (JA-Ile), 5-hydroxyferulic acid (5H-Aci), 5-hydroxyconiferaldehyde (5H-Ald), coniferaldehyde (G-Ald), ferulic acid (G-Aci), caffeoyl shikimic acid (C-SHK) and caffeic acid (C-Aci) were applied as standard compounds for metabolite identification.</p>

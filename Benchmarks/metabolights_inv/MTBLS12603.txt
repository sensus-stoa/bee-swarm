Study Title	Impact of oral Chlamydia Vaccination on Host Gut Microbiome and Metabolite Composition
Study Description	<p>Chlamydia trachomatis, an intracellular pathogen, is recognized as the most common sexually transmitted bacterial infection among women worldwide. Chlamydia&nbsp;infections can lead to undesirable clinical outcomes, including pelvic inflammatory disease and infertility. Recently, the gut has been identified as a niche for Chlamydia colonization; however, despite the biological impact on the host remaining under investigation, oral inoculation of Chlamydia as a whole-organism vaccine has been reported as a promising strategy for preventing genital Chlamydia infections. Few studies have evaluated the impact of oral Chlamydia vaccination on the gut microbiome and metabolite changes. In this study, we assessed time-series alterations in the gut microbiome and metabolites following oral Chlamydia&nbsp;inoculation, and we analyzed the composition and correlation between serum immune parameters and the sequencing profiles in the host. We identified 129 microbial changes and 186 significantly different metabolites in the gut across various vaccination approaches during the 30-day immunization process. Additionally, we discussed potential biomarkers of effective immunization based on correlation analysis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from female C57BL/6J mice on different days after intragastric inoculation with or without&nbsp;C. muridarum. Prior to the intragastric inoculation, fecal samples were collected from all mice to establish a baseline for each mouse and were defined as day 0 samples. The fecal samples were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until DNA extraction.</p>	<p>1.Accurately&nbsp;weigh&nbsp;an&nbsp;appropriate&nbsp;amount&nbsp;of&nbsp;fecal&nbsp;sample&nbsp;into&nbsp;a&nbsp;2&nbsp;mL&nbsp;centrifuge&nbsp;tube.&nbsp;Add&nbsp;600&nbsp;μL&nbsp;of&nbsp;methanol&nbsp;containing&nbsp;2-amino-3-(2-chlorophenyl)propionic&nbsp;acid&nbsp;(4&nbsp;ppm)&nbsp;as&nbsp;an&nbsp;internal&nbsp;standard,&nbsp;and&nbsp;vortex&nbsp;for&nbsp;30&nbsp;s.</p><p>2.Add&nbsp;stainless&nbsp;steel&nbsp;beads&nbsp;and&nbsp;homogenize&nbsp;the&nbsp;mixture&nbsp;in&nbsp;a&nbsp;tissue&nbsp;grinder&nbsp;at&nbsp;50&nbsp;Hz&nbsp;for&nbsp;120&nbsp;s.</p><p>3.Sonicate&nbsp;the&nbsp;sample&nbsp;at&nbsp;room&nbsp;temperature&nbsp;for&nbsp;10&nbsp;min.</p><p>4.Centrifuge&nbsp;at&nbsp;12,000&nbsp;rpm&nbsp;for&nbsp;10&nbsp;min&nbsp;at&nbsp;4&nbsp;°C.&nbsp;Filter&nbsp;the&nbsp;supernatant&nbsp;through&nbsp;a&nbsp;0.22&nbsp;μm&nbsp;membrane,&nbsp;and&nbsp;transfer&nbsp;the&nbsp;filtrate&nbsp;into&nbsp;an&nbsp;LC-MS&nbsp;vial&nbsp;for&nbsp;analysis.</p>	<p>The LC analysis was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Chromatography was carried out with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm) (Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The column maintained at 40 °C. The flow rate and injection volume were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively. For LC-ESI (+)-MS analysis, the mobile phases&nbsp;consisted of (B2) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (v/v) and (A2) 0.1% formic acid in water (v/v).&nbsp;Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0.0-1.0 min, 8% B2; 1.0-8.0 min, 8-98% B2; 8.0-10.0 min, 98% B2; 10.0-10.1 min, 98-8% B2; 10.1-12.0 min, 8% B2. For LC-ESI (-)-MS analysis, the analytes was carried out with (B3) acetonitrile and (A3) ammonium formate (5 mM).&nbsp;Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0.0-1.0 min, 8% B3; 1.0-8.0 min, 8-98% B3; 8.0-10.0 min, 98% B3; 10.0-10.1 min, 98-8% B3; 10.1-12.0 min, 8% B3.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection of metabolites was performed on Orbitrap Exploris 120 (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA) with ESI ion source. Simultaneous MS1 and MS/MS (Full MS-ddMS2 mode, data-dependent MS/MS) acquisition was used. The parameters were as follows: sheath gaspressure, 40 arb; aux gas flow, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV and -2.50 kV for ESI(+) and ESI(-), respectively; capillary temperature, 325 °C; MS1 range, m/z 100-1000; MS1 resolving power, 60000 FWHM; number of data dependant scans per cycle, 4; MS/MS resolving power, 15000&nbsp;FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30%; dynamic exclusion time, automatic.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry files were converted to mzXML format using the MSConvert tool within the ProteoWizard software package (v3.0.8789). Peak detection, peak filtering, and peak alignment were performed using the R package XCMS with the following parameters: bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, method = 'centWave'. A quantitative feature table was subsequently generated.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>Principal component analysis and Spearman correlation analysis were used to judge the repeatability of the samples within group and the quanlity control samples. The identified compounds are searched for classification and pathway information in KEGG, HMDB and lipidmaps databases. According to the grouping information, calculate and compare the difference multiples, T test was used to calculate the difference significance pvalue of each compound. The method of combining the difference multiple, the P value and the VIP value of the OPLS-DA model was adopted to screen the differential metabolites.</p>

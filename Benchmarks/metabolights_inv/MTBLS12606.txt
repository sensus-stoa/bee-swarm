Study Title	Protein lactylation of citrate synthase promotes the AKI–CKD transition by activating the NLRP3 inflammasome.
Study Description	<p>Interstitial renal inflammation contributes to the transition from acute kidney injury (AKI) to chronic kidney disease (CKD). Recently, protein lactylation modification has emerged as a novel mechanism for mediating chronic organ damage. We investigated lactylated protein profiles and the role of protein lactylation during AKI progression. Severe and moderate AKI mouse models were constructed by bilateral renal ischemia for 35 and 25 min, respectively. Lactylation enhancers and inhibitors were used to verify the effect of protein lactylation. Lactylated proteomics was used to detect lactylated protein changes in kidneys, and the lactylated proteins related to kidney injury were screened for verification. We observed significantly higher lactate and protein lactylation levels in the severe AKI model than in the moderate AKI model 1-28 days post-injury. Inhibition of protein lactylation protects against renal interstitial fibrosis. In vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that protein lactylation activated Nod-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasomes, promoting the AKI-CKD transition. Comprehensive lactylome profiling of severe AKI models revealed a role for lactylated proteins in metabolic pathways, primarily the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, where the rate-limiting enzyme, citrate synthase (CS), exhibited significantly elevated lactylation levels 3-7 days post-AKI induction; K370 was the most significant lysine residue. In vitro, following hypoxia/reoxygenation, the modified/lactylated K370T group significantly decreased CS activity and mitochondrial function. Furthermore, CS-K370 lactylation activated the NLRP3 inflammasomes. Thus, lactylation of CS promotes the AKI-CKD transition through NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Inhibition of CS lactylation shows therapeutic potential for preventing this transition.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Wild-type mouse renal tubular epithelial cell line and mRTEC cell line CS-K370T point mutation cells.</p><p>(1) Suspension cell samples: Collect suspension cells by centrifugation and quickly wash 2-3 times with pre-chilled PBS, 4 °C, 1000 x g:</p><p>(2) Centrifuge at low speed for 1 min, discard the supernatant, collect cells (50 μL of cell pellet) in a 2 mL imported centrifuge tube, freeze liquid nitrogen for 15 min, store at -80 °C, and transport with sufficient dry ice;</p><p>(3) Adherent cell samples: remove the medium of cultured cells, wash them with pre-chilled PBS 2-3 times, discard the supernatant, and scrape all cells in the culture vessel using cell scraping</p><p>Clean, count the cells (10'), transfer to 2 mL inlet centrifuge tubes (cryopreservation tubes), flash freeze liquid nitrogen for 15 min, store at -80 °C and transport on dry ice in sufficient quantities.</p>	<p>Add glass beads to an appropriate amount of sample, accurately add 500 μL of methanol and vortex to mix. Immerse the centrifuge tube containing the sample in liquid nitrogen for rapid freezing for 5 min, take out the centrifuge tube and freeze it at room temperature. Install it into the grinder and oscillate at 55 Hz for 1 min, repeating twice. Transfer to a thermostatic metal shaker and shake at 1500 rpm, 4 °C for 10 min. After shaking, centrifuge at 12,000 rpm, 4 °C for 10 min. Take 100 μL supernatant into the centrifugal tube, add appropriate amount of mixed internal standard, vortex fully, blow dry the solution in the fume hood with nitrogen and use 50% acetonitrile/aqueous solution to re-dissolve as the alkaline machine reagent. Take 100 μL supernatant into the centrifugal tube, add appropriate amount of mixed internal standard, vortex fully, blow dry the solution in the fume hood with nitrogen, and use 0.1% formic acid to 40% methanol/aqueous solution to redissolve as acid drawing reagent. After full vortex, centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min. Take the supernatant to the vial and test it on the machine.</p>	<p>Chromatographic conditions 1. The ExionLC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (AB Sciex, USA) was operated on an Agilent Eclipse XDB-C18 chromatographic column (2.1 x 150 mm, 5 μm, Agilent, USA) with an injection volume of 5 μL and a column temperature of 40 °C, and the mobile phases were A - 6.25 mM ammonium acetate containing 0.2% acetic acid in water, and B - 0.2% acetic acid in methanol. The gradient elution conditions were 0.0-2.0 min, 20% B; 2.0-6.0 min, 20-70% B; 6.0-7.0 min, 70-20% B; 7.0-9.0 min, 20% B. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. </p><p>Chromatographic conditions 2.The ExionLC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (AB Sciex, USA) was operated using a Luna 3 μm NH2 column (2 x 150 mm, 3 μm, Phenomenex.) with an injection volume of 5 μL and a column temperature of 40 °C, and the mobile phases were A - 15 mM ammonium bicarbonate in aqueous solution containing 4% ammonia and B - acetonitrile. The gradient elution conditions were 0.0-2.0 min, 40-5% B; 2.0-6.0 min, 5% B; 6.0-9.0 min, 5-10% B; 9.0-12.0 min, 10-5% B; 12.0-15.0 min, 5% B; 15.0-17.0 min, 5-40% B; 17.0-20.0 min, 40% B. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min.</p>	<p>The MS analysis was performed using an AB Sciex Triple Quadrupole 6500 plus mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, USA) in the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The electrospray ionization (ESI) parameters in negative mode were as follows: ion source voltage, -4000 V; ion source temperature, 600 °C; curtain gas, 30 psi; gas 1, 60 psi and gas 2, 60 psi.</p>	<p>Raw data conversion MS Convert software (Proteowizard, v3.0.8789) was applied to convert “.wiff” format raw data to “.mzML” format, which would be used for downstream analysis. Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis of Metabolites The software SCIEX OS was used to collect the raw data and qualitatively and quantitatively analyze the results. QC RSD calculation To assess the technical precision of each experiment, the relative standard deviation of peak areas was calculated for every compound detected in the QC sample (RSD = 100 * standard deviation / average of peak areas) with ideal RSD &lt; 15%. Quantification calculation The calibration curves were obtained as plots of the peak area ratio of the target compounds to an internal standard versus the target compound concentration. The peak area ratio of target compounds to an internal standard was put into the formula to calculate the concentration in samples. In calculation, all metabolites concentrations lesser than 0 were reported as not detected (ND). Statistical analysis Only metabolites present in &gt; 50% of the samples were kept further analysis. The T test or Mann-Whitney-Wilcoxo test was used to calculate P.value. When the comparison group was more than three or more groups, the one-way test of variance or K-H test was used to calculate P.value. Pathway analysis Target metabolites were subjected to pathway analysis. The identified metabolites in metabolomics were then mapped to the KEGG pathway for biological interpretation of higher level systemic functions. The metabolites and corresponding pathways were visualized using KEGG Mapper tool</p>	<p>KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) is a database for systematic analysis of gene functions and genomic information[8]. Each database contains a wealth of useful information: genomic information is stored in the GENES database, including complete and partial sequenced genome sequences; more advanced functional information is stored in the PATHWAY database, including illustrated cellular biochemical processes such as metabolism, membrane transport, signal transduction and cell cycle, as well as information on conserved sub-pathways; another KEGG database is LIGAND, which contains information on chemical substances, enzyme molecules, enzyme reactions, etc.</p><p><br></p><p>KEGG provides an excellent integrated metabolic pathway (Pathway) query, including the metabolism of carbohydrates, nucleosides, amino acids and the biodegradation of organic substances. It not only provides all possible metabolic pathways but also comprehensively annotates the enzymes catalyzing each step of the reaction, including amino acid sequences and links to the PDB library. KEGG is a powerful tool for metabolic analysis and metabolic network research in organisms, helping researchers study genes and their expression information as an integrated network.</p>

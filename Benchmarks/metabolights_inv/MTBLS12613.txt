Study Title	Rhizosheath-associated Massilia contributes to maize growth heterosis
Study Description	<p>Heterosis, or hybrid vigor, refers to the phenomenon where F1 hybrids outperform their parental inbred lines. Despite more than a century of intensive research across various species, the genetic basis and environmental regulation of heterosis, particularly belowground, remain poorly understood. Recent studies have suggested that the soil microbiome may influence the expression of heterosis. However, it remains unclear whether and to what extent the manifestation of heterosis shapes the associated microbiome under abiotic stress, and how these microbiomes, in turn, influence the heterotic phenotype in response to stress.&nbsp;In this study, we explored the potential role of rhizosheath formation in maize growth heterosis under nitrogen deprivation. Soil sterilization and inoculation experiments with bacteria of the genus Massilia demonstrated that several functional root traits contribute to the manifestation of rhizosheath-associated heterosis. Untargeted metabolomics profiling and validation with flavone-deficient mutants indicated that Massilia promote flavone secretion, which is linked to rhizosheath formation. In addition, the integration of rhizosheath transplantation experiments with soil metagenomic sequencing data confirmed that rhizosheath size determined plant growth performance under stress conditions. Taken together, these results suggest that the rhizosheath formation, which integrates root development, root exudates, and their associated microbiome, can be considered as an adaptive and heritable root trait with potential applications in modern crop breeding to combat global climate change.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Root exudates from the rhizosheath were collected under different soil microbiome conditions, including the presence or absence of soil microbiome and ASV37 Massilia inoculation, in nitrogen-poor soil. The collection method was adapted from a previous publication35. First, the maize roots were carefully excavated from the soil, with large aggregates removed. The roots were washed under running tap water for 1 min, followed by three washes with deionized distilled water. Afterward, the whole root system was transferred to a 500 mL plastic beaker containing 200 mL of CaSO4 solution (0.01 mmol L-1) and submerged at 20 C for 4 h. This procedure was performed to collect the root exudates from the rhizosheath. The solution was then filtered through filter paper (43-48 um pore size; Filter-Lab) and further filtered using a 0.22-um membrane filter (Sartorius Stedim Biotech GmbH, Goettingen, Germany) to remove any particulate matter. The filtrate was flash-frozen using liquid nitrogen and subsequently lyophilized using a CHRIST ALPHA1-4/2-4 LSC basic freeze dryer (Germany) for metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>50 μg of samples were weighed into 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes respectively and soaked with 800 μL of precooling extraction solution (methanol : H2O = 7:3, v/v) and 20 μL Internal Standard 1 (IS1). Homogenization was conducted with weaving grinder at 50 Hz for 10 minutes and then water bath ultrasonication at 4 °C for 30 minutes. Following the standing still at -20 °C for 1 hour, the extracts were centrifuged at the speed of 14000 rpm at 4 °C for 15 minutes. 600 μL of the supernatant was iltered with 0.22 μm membrane and 20 μL of filtered solution from each sample composited the mixed QC sample to evaluate the repeatability and stability of LC/MS analysis. Filtered samples and mixed QC samples were transferred to the 1.5 mL sample vials for instrument running.</p>	<p>The separation was performed using a&nbsp;Waters 2777C UPLC&nbsp;coupled with a&nbsp;Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). A&nbsp;Hypersil GOLD aQ Dim column (1.9 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm, Thermo Fisher Scientific)&nbsp;was used with a mobile phase of&nbsp;0.1% formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The gradient elution was as follows: 5% B (0–2 min), 5–95% B (2–22 min), 95% B (22–27 min), followed by a wash (27.1–30 min). The flow rate was&nbsp;0.3 mL/min, injection volume&nbsp;5 µL, and column temperature&nbsp;40 °C.</p>	<p>The&nbsp;Q Exactive HF&nbsp;operated in&nbsp;ESI positive/negative mode&nbsp;with scan ranges of&nbsp;125–1500 m/z (positive) and 100–1500 m/z (negative)&nbsp;at&nbsp;120,000 resolution. MS/MS used the&nbsp;top 3 precursors&nbsp;with&nbsp;stepped NCE (20, 40, 60 eV)&nbsp;and&nbsp;30,000 resolution. ESI settings included a&nbsp;spray voltage of ±3.8 kV (positive)/−3.2 kV (negative), sheath gas&nbsp;40 AU, aux gas&nbsp;10 AU, capillary temperature&nbsp;320 °C, and aux heater&nbsp;350 °C.</p>	<p>The raw data were preprocessed using metaX software for metabolite identification and quantification. The identified metabolites were classified, and functional annotation was carried out by comparing against the KEGG database to obtain KEGG IDs, categories and associated pathways. Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA) was conducted to identify the differences between groups and Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) was used to build a relationship model between metabolite expression and sample categories. The Variable Importance for Projection (VIP) score was calculated to measure the contribution of each metabolite to the differentiation of sample categories. Using the KEGG database, we performed metabolic pathway enrichment analysis to identify which metabolic pathways were significantly altered due to the different treatments. Pathways with a p-value &lt; 0.05 were considered significantly enriched. To visualize these results, we used the top 10 metabolic pathways with the smallest p-values and presented them in a bubble chart, reflecting the expression levels of the differential pathways.</p>	<p>After importing the off-line data of mass spectrometry into Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) software and analyzing the mass spectrometry data in combination with BMDB (BGI metabolome database), mzCloud database and ChemSpider online database, a data matrix containing information such as metabolite peak area and identification results will be obtained. After that, the table will be further analyzed and processed. Software info: Compound Discoverer Version: v.3.3. Parameter: Parent ion mass deviation: &lt; 5 ppm. Mass deviation of fragment ions: &lt; 10 ppm. Retention time deviation: &lt; 0.2 min. Official Website: https://mycompounddiscoverer.com/</p>

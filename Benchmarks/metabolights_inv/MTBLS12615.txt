Study Title	Giardiavirus infection mediates Enolase to the upregulation of Glycolysis in Giardia
Study Description	<p>Giardia duodenalis is a prevalent intestinal parasite that cause giardiasis, a condition characterized by diarrhea and frequently linked to malnutrition and growth impairments in children. The presence of Giardiavirus (GLV) can lead to reduced pathological damage in mice infected with Giardia and different pro-inflammatory responses in host cells stimulated by Giardia. As is well known, viruses as “non-cellular organisms” that control host proteins, metabolic resources to complete replication and transmission. However, the understanding of the impact of the Giardiavirus on the G. duodenalis itself is currently limited. Here, we found that GLV infection can reduce the mRNA and expression levels of Giardia genes, but lead to upregulation of translation efficiency in Giardia genes. Additionally, GLV infection causes Giardia's energy metabolic reprogramming, 21 significantly different energy metabolites were identified, and the most significant pathway by KEGG enrichment was Glycolysis/ Gluconeogenesis. After the viral load of GLV stabilizes, glycolysis is significantly upregulated with increasing GLV infection generations. The glycolytic enzyme enolase in Giardia is closely associated with the viral load of GLV. and it was found that knocking down the expression level of enolase also led to a decrease in the viral load of GLV. Overall, these results highlight that GLV infection can regulate the protein and energy metabolism of G. duodenalis for its own survival, and discover the potential of GLV as a novel intervention strategy for the development of drugs and vaccines for the prevention and treatment of giardiasis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>WB+GLV, WB-GLV and Giardia+GLV trophozoites samples placed in dry ice were sent to the METWARE company (Wuhan, China) for metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Weighing: Accurately weigh approximately 50 mg (± 2.5 mg) of each sample into a 2 mL microcentrifuge tube, and record the exact weight.</p><p>Extraction: Immediately add 500 μL of pre-chilled (-20 °C) 70% methanol/water extraction solution to each weighed sample, followed by vortex mixing for 3 min.</p><p>First Centrifugation: Centrifuge the mixture at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, then carefully transfer 300 μL of the supernatant to a new 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube.</p><p>Precipitation &amp; Second Centrifugation: Allow the supernatant to stand at -20 °C for 30 min, then centrifuge again at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C.</p><p>Protein Removal &amp; Storage: Pass 200 μL of the clarified supernatant through a protein precipitation plate. The final supernatant (ready for analysis) should be stored at -20 °C until LC-MS/MS or other instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed using a Waters ACQUITY H-Class UPLC system coupled with a SCIEX QTRAP® 6500+ mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 μm, 100×2.1 mm) maintained at 40°C with a 0.4 mL/min flow rate. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 10 mM ammonium acetate/0.3% ammonia in water and (B) 90% acetonitrile/water. A gradient elution was employed: 5% A (0-1.2 min), increased to 30% A (8 min) and 50% A (9-11 min), then returned to 5% A (11.1-15 min). The injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry analysis was conducted using electrospray ionization (ESI) with the ion source temperature set at 550°C. The ion spray voltage was maintained at 5500 V in positive ion mode and -4500 V in negative ion mode, with a curtain gas pressure of 35 psi. On the QTRAP® 6500+ system, all ion transitions were monitored using optimized declustering potentials (DP) and collision energies (CE) for each target analyte.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were processed using MultiQuant™ 3.0.3 software (SCIEX). Chromatographic peaks of target analytes were identified by comparing their retention times and peak profiles with reference standards, followed by integration and calibration to ensure accurate qualitative and quantitative analysis.</p>	<p>Quantitative analysis was performed using the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode on a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. In this mode, the first quadrupole selectively filters precursor ions (parent ions) of target compounds, effectively eliminating interference from ions with different mass-to-charge ratios. These precursor ions are then fragmented in the collision cell, generating product ions that are further filtered by the third quadrupole to select specific characteristic fragment ions. This two-stage mass filtering significantly enhances analytical specificity, improves quantitative accuracy, and ensures better reproducibility. Following data acquisition, chromatographic peaks of all target analytes were integrated and quantified against established calibration curves.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Twenty 3-day-old piglets that tested negative for both PEDV and PRRS antigens/antibodies were randomly divided into four groups: mock, DMSO, LR, and HDCA groups. The treatment groups received oral administration for five consecutive days prior to PEDV challenge. Intestinal contents were collected from piglets showing severe clinical symptoms for metabolomic sequencing. For piglets that either remained asymptomatic or displayed mild symptoms, jejunal contents were collected following euthanasia on day 7 post-challenge for subsequent metabolomic analysis.Samples were immediately stored in liquid nitrogen for later use.</p>	<p>Add 50 μL of internal standard working solution (200 ng/mL) and 350 μL of extraction solution (methanol), vortex and mix for 30 s, sonicate at low temperature for 30 min (5°C, 40 KHz), than store at -20°C for 30 min, centrifuge at 4°C for 15 min at 13000 rcf, remove the supernatant and blow dry with nitrogen, add 100 μL of 50% acetonitrile, vortex and mix for 30 s, sonicate at low temperature for 10 min (5°C, 40 KHz), centrifuge at 4°C for 15 min at 13000 rcf, remove the supernatant for LC-MS/MS. The supernatant was purged with nitrogen, added 100 μL of 50% acetonitrile, vortexed and mixed for 30 s, sonicated at low temperature for 10 min (5℃, 40 KHz), centrifuged at 4℃ for 15 min at 13000 rcf, and the supernatant was taken for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a ExionLC AD system coupled with a QTRAP® 6500+ mass spectrometer (Sciex, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Briefly, samples were separated by an Waters BEH C18(150*2.1 mm,1.7 μm) thermostated at 50℃. The mobile phases consisted of water solution ( with 0.025% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate, solvent A) and acetonitrile:methanol (9:1, v/v) solution (solvent B), was delivered at a flow rate was set to 0.4 mL/min. The solvent gradient changed according to the following conditions:from 0 to 0.5 min, 5% B to 10% B; from 0.5 to 1.0min, 10% B to 20% B; from 1.0 to 2.0 min, 20% B to 28% B ;from 2.0 to 8.0 min, hold at 28% B; from 8.0 to11.0 min, 28% B to33% B ; from 11.0 to 20.0 min, 33% B to 45% B; from 20.0 to 23.0 min, 45% B to50% B;from 23.0 to 29.0 min,50% B to 80% B; from 29.0 to 29.01 min, 80% B to 100% B; from 29.01 to 31.0 min, hold at 100%B;from 31.0 to 31.01 min,100% B to 5% B; from 31.01 to 33.0 min, hold at 5% B for equilibrating the systems. During the period of analysis, all samples were stored at 4 ℃.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometric data was collected using a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ Mass Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in negative ion mode. The optimal parameters were set as followed: Curtain Gas(CUR):35,Collision Gas(CAD):Medium,IonSpray Voltage(IS):-4500,Temperature(TEM):550,Ion Source Gas1(GS1):50,Ion Source Gas2(GS2):50.</p>	<p>The LC-MS raw data were imported into Sciex software OS. All ion fragments were automatically identificated and integrated by using default parameters, besides, all intergration was checked manually. The metabolite concerntration of sample was calculated according to linear regression standard curve.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on an ExionLC AD system coupled with a SCIEX QTRAP® 6500+ mass spectrometer (Sciex, USA) in negative ESI mode. Chromatographic separation used a Waters BEH C18 column (150 × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm) with a gradient of solvent A (0.025% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate in water) and solvent B (acetonitrile:methanol, 9:1 v/v). Key MS parameters included: curtain gas (35), collision gas (medium), ion spray voltage (−4500 V), temperature (550°C), and gas flows (GS1/GS2: 50).</p><p>For metabolite identification, MS/MS</p><p>spectra were acquired under these optimized conditions, though the specific</p><p>data acquisition mode (e.g., DDA, MRM) and software tools (e.g., SCIEX OS,</p><p>MS-DIAL) should be explicitly stated. Annotation likely relies on matching</p><p>fragmentation patterns and retention times against reference databases (e.g.,</p><p>HMDB, NIST), but the database names, versions, and scoring thresholds must be detailed</p><p>for reproducibility. Additional metadata should include mass accuracy (&lt;5</p><p>ppm for MS1), quality control measures (e.g., internal standards), and</p><p>post-processing workflows (e.g., peak alignment, noise filtering).</p>

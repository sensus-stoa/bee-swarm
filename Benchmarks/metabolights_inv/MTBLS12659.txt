Study Title	Regulation impact of Duolang sheep-sourced Ligilactobacillus salivarius on rumen microbiome homeostasis and host metabolism in Hu sheep
Study Description	<p>Background: Hu sheep, renowned for their prolificacy and economic value, have been introduced to Xinjiang for intensive farming operations. However, their suboptimal adaptation to local harsh environments limits growth efficiency and health management. In contrast, Duolang sheep, an indigenous Xinjiang breed, exhibit exceptional environmental resilience, largely attributed to their unique rumen microbiome enriched with fiber-degrading bacteria, which enhance roughage utilization and stress tolerance. To address Hu sheep’s adaptive challenges, this study applies Ligilactobacillus salivarius KS1018, a probiotic strain isolated from Duolang sheep, to Hu sheep, investigating its role in improving rumen microbial homeostasis, metabolic adaptation, and physiological resilience under Xinjiang’s environmental conditions.</p><p>Results: Thirty-two male Hu lambs were allocated into four groups: Control (C) and three treatment groups receiving daily doses of 0.5×109 CFU/d (low-dose group, LLS, 1.0×109 CFU/d (medium-dose group, MLS), or 1.5×109 CFU/d (high-dose group, HLS) for 56 days. Although growth performance did not reveal significant enhancement, supplementation with the high dose of L. salivarius KS1018 (HLS group) significantly reduced ruminal NH3-N (p &lt; 0.05) and increased total VFAs (p = 0.043), alongside elevated serum triglycerides (p = 0.021) and β-hydroxybutyrate (p &lt; 0.05), indicating enhanced nitrogen utilization and ketogenesis. Rumen metabolomics revealed dose-dependent metabolic shifts: group HLS prioritized oxidative phosphorylation and pyrimidine metabolism (p &lt; 0.001), while group MLS upregulated branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis (p &lt; 0.01). Procrustes analysis confirmed strong microbiome-metabolome coordination (M2 = 0.56, p = 0.001), linking Lachnospira and Negativicutes enrichment to VFA production and redox homeostasis. Group HLS exhibited a decline in ruminal pH, suggesting a potential risk of subacute acidosis at higher doses.</p><p>Conclusions: L. salivarius KS1018 optimizes rumen metabolism through microbial-host crosstalk, improving nitrogen efficiency and stress resilience in Hu sheep. The strain promoted ketogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation pathways, which alleviated oxidative stress and enhanced immune function under environmental challenges. Notably, the arid-adaptive genetic traits likely contribute to stabilizing rumen microbial networks, mimicking the superior roughage utilization capacity of its native Duolang sheep host. Our findings highlight the potential of L. salivarius KS1018 as a metabolic modulator in ruminant nutrition, redefining its role beyond traditional growth-centric applications.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>30 mL was collected from each lamb via orogastric intubation before morning feeding. The first 15 mL was discarded to minimize saliva contamination strained rumen fluid&nbsp;was preserved at –80°C for microbial and metabolomic studies</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted by mixing 100 µL of rumen fluid with 400 µL of ice-cold acetonitrile:methanol (1:1, v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, sonicated at 40 kHz and 5°C for 30 min, and then centrifuged. After protein precipitation at –20°C for 30 min and nitrogen drying, the residues were reconstituted in acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v), sonicated at 5°C for 5 min, and centrifuged before LC-MS analysis. A pooled quality control (QC) sample, consisting of equal volumes from all experimental samples, was prepared and analyzed at five-sample intervals to monitor system stability and reproducibility. Additionally, a solvent blank (acetonitrile:water, 1:1, v/v) was included as a process control.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed using a SCIEX UPLC-Triple TOF 6600 system (Majorbio Bio-Pharm, China) equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile, 95:5) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol:water, 47.5:47.5:5). The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–8 min, 0–100% B in both positive and negative ion modes. The flow rate was set to 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C. The injection volume was not explicitly stated but was likely optimized based on instrument specifications.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry analysis was conducted using the SCIEX Triple TOF 6600 system (Majorbio Bio-Pharm, China) with electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative ion modes (±5000 V). The mass range (m/z) was set to 50–1000. Key MS parameters included:1.Source temperature: 550°C; 2.Curtain gas pressure: 30 psi; 3.Collision energy: 20–60 eV (ramped); 4.Scan mode: Information-Dependent Acquisition (IDA).</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (Waters, USA) for baseline correction, peak alignment, and retention time calibration. Data matrices were filtered to retain features detected in 80% of samples, normalized via sum normalization, and subjected to QC-based exclusion (RSD &gt; 30%). Multivariate statistical analyses, including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), were performed using the R package 'ropls' (v1.6.2).</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using the following databases:1.Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Metlin, Majorbio Database (MJDB), Differential metabolites were identified based on a VIP score &gt;1 and p &lt; 0.05 (Student’s t-test). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using the Majorbio Cloud Platform (cloud.majorbio.com), with statistical validation performed via Python’s 'scipy.stats' and cross-referenced against the KEGG database.</p>

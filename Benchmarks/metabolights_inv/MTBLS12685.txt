Study Title	NGT induced mitochondrial dysfunction, led to apoptosis, and changed metabolites associated with oxidized lipids in sperm of Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas).
Study Description	<p>The risk of norgestrel (NGT) exposure in the aquatic environment has been identified, but the toxic effects on invertebrate germ cells are unknown. Therefore, the toxic effects of NGT on oyster sperm were investigated by combining transcriptome and metabolome analysis with histology. Transcriptome sequencing showed that differential genes were significantly enriched in regulation of cellular processes, protein digestion and absorption signaling pathways, and focal adhesion. We found that NGT exposure caused significant decreasing of mitochondrial ATP binding cassette and calcium uptake protein 1, and triggered apoptosis in sperm. Meanwhile, metabolome data showed that most of the differential metabolites were phosphatidylic acid, ammonium phosphatidylacetate and phosphatidylcholine, all of which were one of the main components of cell membrane, indicating that NGT exposure significantly affected the integrity and function of sperm cell membrane. In addition, NGT exposure induced significant changes in oxidized lipid metabolites, which may be related to apoptosis. In conclusion, this study suggests that NGT may induce mitochondrial dysfunction, lead to apoptosis, and significantly change the cell membranes and metabolites associated with oxidized lipids. This study is helpful to reveal the toxic effect of water environment NGT on in vitro fertilization invertebrate germ cells, and provide theoretical basis for formulating relevant environmental protection measures.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>After cleaning the oysters thoroughly, specialized knives were used to open and mince the mature gonads. The sex was determined by examining subsamples of the gonads under a microscope. The sperm concentration was measured using a Computer-Assisted Sperm Analysis (CASA) system and a hemocytometer, and sperm with higher activity were selected for subsequent experiments.</p><p>In the experiment, concentrations of 0, 10 ng/L, and 1000 ng/L were set to simulate the levels of NGT pollution in the aquatic environment. The experiment was divided into a control group (0/C), a low-concentration group (L/10 ng/L), and a high-concentration group (H/1000 ng/L). The sperm stress test was conducted in 24 glass beakers, with 50 μL of&nbsp;Crassostrea gigas&nbsp;sperm added to each beaker containing seawater prepared with the corresponding NGT concentration. The final sperm concentration in the beakers was approximately 1×10&lt;sup&gt;6&lt;/sup&gt;&nbsp;cells/mL.</p><p>The experiment used sperm from eight male oysters with high motility, with sperm from each male oyster added to three glass beakers, totaling eight replicates. The stress duration was set to 30 minutes. After the stress period, 50 mL was taken from each beaker and centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 10 minutes. The supernatant was discarded, and the white pellet at the bottom was resuspended in 1×PBS buffer. The centrifugation was repeated once, and after removing the supernatant, the samples were frozen and stored for further analysis.</p>	<p>The sample stored at -80 °C refrigerator was thawed on ice. The thawed sample was homogenized by a grinder (30 HZ) for 20 s. A 400 μL solution (Methanol : Water = 4:1, V/V) containing internal standard was added into 40 mg of the sample and vortexed for 3 min. The sample was placed in liquid nitrogen for 5 min and on the dry ice for 5 min, and then thawed on ice and vortexed for 2 min. This freeze-thaw circle was repeated three times in total. The sample was centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 10 min (4 °C). A 300 μL of supernatant was collected and placed in -20 °C for 30 min. The sample was then centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 3 min (4 °C). A 200 μL aliquots of supernatant were transferred for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>All samples were for two LC/MS methods. One aliquot was analyzed using positive ion conditions and was eluted from T3 column (Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm) using 0.1 % formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1 % formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B in the following gradient: 5 to 20 % in 2 min, increased to 60 % in the following 3 mins, increased to 99 % in 1 min and held for 1.5 min, then come back to 5 % mobile phase B witnin 0.1 min, held for 2.4 min. The analytical conditions were as follows, column temperature, 40 °C; flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; injection volume, 4 μL; Another aliquot was using negative ion conditions and was the same as the elution gradient of positive mode.</p>	<p>All the methods alternated between full scan MS and data dependent MSn scans using dynamic exclu- sion. MS analyses were carried out using electrospray ionization in the positive ion mode and negative ion mode using full scan analysis over m/z 75-1000 at 35000 resolution. Additional MS settings are: ion spray voltage, 3.5 KV or 3.2 KV in positive or negative modes, respevtively; Sheath gas (Arb), 30; Aux gas, 5; Ion transfer tube temperature, 320 °C; Vaporizer temperature, 300 °C; Collision energy, 30,40,50 V; Signal Intensity Threshold, 1*e6 cps; Top N vs Top speed, 10; Exclusion duration, 3s.</p>	<p>The raw data obtained from mass spectrometry was converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard, and peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using the XCMS program. Peaks with a missing rate &gt;50% in each group of samples were filtered out, and missing values were filled using KNN imputation + 1/5 of the minimum value (missing values &gt;50% were filled with 1/5 of the minimum value, while missing values &lt;50% were filled using KNN imputation).&nbsp;</p>	<p>The peak areas were corrected using the SVR method. The filtered peaks were then subjected to metabolite identification by searching a laboratory-built database, integrating public databases, predictive databases, and the metDNA method. Finally, metabolites with a comprehensive identification score above 0.5 and a QC sample CV value less than 0.3 were extracted. The positive and negative modes were merged (retaining the substance with the highest identification level and the smallest CV value), resulting in the file 'all sample data.xlsx.'</p>

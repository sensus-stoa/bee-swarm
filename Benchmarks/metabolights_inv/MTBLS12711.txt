Study Identifier	MTBLS12711
Study Title	Metabolomic analysis of seminal fluids in oligoasthenospermia individuals
Study Description	<p>In this study, we systematically characterized the metabolic signatures of oligoasthenospermia individuals and health controls using high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS/MS). Additionally, we performed functional validation of specific differential metabolites in terms of their roles in enhancing sperm vitality and providing cryoprotection. This study aimed to comprehensively analyze the metabolomic signatures of seminal plasma in men with oligoasthenospermia, identify novel markers and for diagnosing and ART treating, and to provide effective strategies for improving sperm motility during in vitro manipulation in cases of oligoasthenospermia, which facilitating a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying male factor infertility and the protective mechanisms of sperm damage.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Semen samples were collected via masturbation, which is the standard methodology for semen acquisition. All donors maintained abstinence for 3–7 days prior to providing specimens and washed their hands with soap and water before self-collection into sterile, wide-mouthed sample cups in a room adjacent to the andrology laboratory. Donors were instructed to collect only the initial ejaculate while ensuring no spilled semen was included. Caps were immediately applied after collection, and the specimens were promptly placed in a 37 °C water bath until complete liquefaction occurred prior to analysis.</p>	<p>1. Thaw the experimental sample at 4℃, vortex the sample for 1 min after thawing, and mix</p><p>evenly;</p><p>2. Accurately transfer an appropriate amount of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>3. Add 400 µL methanol and vortex for 1 min;</p><p>4. Centrifuge for 10 min at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C,take all the supernatant transfer it to a new 2 mL</p><p>centrifuge tube, concentrate and dry it;</p><p>5. Add 150 µL of 2-chloro-l-phenylalanine (4 ppm) solution prepared with 80% methanol water</p><p>to redissolve the sample, filter the supernatant by 0.22 μm membrane and transfer into the</p><p>detection bottle for LC-MS detection.</p>	<p>The LC analysis was performed using an Ultimate 3000 UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Chromatographic separation was carried out on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm) (Waters, Milford, MA, USA), maintained at a temperature of 40 °C. The flow rate and injection volume were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively. </p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection of metabolites was performed on Q Exactive(Thermo Fisher</p><p>Scientific, USA) with ESI ion source. Simultaneous MS1 and MS/MS (Full MS-ddMS2 mode,</p><p>data-dependent MS/MS) acquisition was used. The parameters were as follows: sheath gas</p><p>pressure, 40 arb; aux gas flow, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV and -2.50 kV for ESI(+) and ESI(-),</p><p>respectively; capillary temperature, 325 ℃ ; MS1 range, m/z 100-1000; MS1 resolving power,</p><p>70000 FWHM; number of data dependant scans per cycle, 10; MS/MS resolving power, 17500</p><p>FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30 eV; dynamic exclusion time, automatic</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry output files were converted into the mzXML file format using the MSConvert tool in the Proteowizard software package (v3.0.8789) . The R XCMS software package was employed for peak detection, peak filtering, and peak alignment . The parameter settings were bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, and method = 'centWave', resulting in a list of substance quantifications. Support vector regression correction based on quality control (QC) samples was used to eliminate systematic errors. Then, substances with a coefficient of variance (CV) less than 30% in the QC samples were retained for subsequent analysis. </p>	<p>The components eluted after chromatographic separation continuously enter the mass spectrometer, and the mass spectrometer continuously scans for data acquisition. One mass spectrum is obtained for each scan. By continuously plotting the ions with the greatest intensity in each mass spectrum, with the ion intensity as the ordinate and time as the abscissa, the resulting chromatogram is the Base Peak Chromatogram (BPC). </p>

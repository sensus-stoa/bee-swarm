Study Identifier	MTBLS12714
Study Title	The enduring effects of early-life HFHS exposure and microbiota-targeted interventions on blood metabolome in adult female and male mice
Study Description	<p>Blood metabolomics of 12-week-old female and male C57BL/6J mice that were exposed to either a control or an unhealthy high-fat/high-sugar (HFHS) diet during early-life, or early-life HFHS diet supplemented with a microbiota-targeted intervention (either a prebiotic combination (fructo-oligosaccharides and galacto-oligosaccharides), FOS+GOS) or a putative probiotic (<em>B. longum</em> APC1472)). The overall aim of the project is to investigate the potential of microbiota-targeted approaches, via prebiotic combination FOS+GOS or the putative probiotic <em>B. longum</em> APC147, to attenuate detrimental enduring effects of an early-life HFHS diet, as a model of western diet, on offspring’s feeding behavior in adulthood.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Blood samples of 12-week-old female and male (n=6/group/sex) mice were collected at the end of the study. C57BL/6J mice were exposed to either a <strong>control </strong>or an unhealthy high-fat/high-sugar (<strong>HFHS</strong>) diet during early-life, or early-life <strong>HFHS </strong>diet supplemented with a microbiota-targeted intervention (either a prebiotic combination (fructo-oligosaccharides and galacto-oligosaccharides), <strong>FOS+GOS</strong>) or a<strong> putative probiotic (B. longum APC1472)</strong>. Trunk blood was collected in tubes (VACUETTE, Greiner Bio-One) containing 500 KIU/mL aprotinin (Fisher Scientific) and 1x cOmplete Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (Roche). Blood was then centrifuged at 2000 x g for 15 min at 18 °C, following instructions of blood tubes’ manufacturers.&nbsp;Plasma samples were aliquoted and stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>Sample was added to a Phree Phospholipid Removal cartridge (Phenomenex) and homogenized with an equal mixture of acetonitrile and methanol (1:1, v/v), containing stable isotope-labelled internal extraction standards. The extract was recovered using centrifugation at 15,000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C and dried under a gentle flow of nitrogen. Samples were reconstituted in a mixture of mobile phase eluents A and B (9:1, v/v) containing additional stable isotope labelled standards. Injected extracts had a total dilution factor of 5.5.</p>	<p>Semi-polar metabolite profiling (reverse phase) of blood samples was analyzed with MS-Omics’ semi-polar metabolites method, a slightly modified version of the protocol previously described<strong> [1]</strong>. The analysis was carried out in a randomized sample order using a UPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Doneanu CE, Chen W, Mazzeo JR. UPLC/MS Monitoring of Water-Soluble Vitamin Bs in Cell Culture Media in Minutes | Waters [Internet]. [cited 2024 Oct 21]; Available from: https://www.waters.com/waters/library.htm?locale=en_US&amp;lid=134636355</p>	<p>The analysis was carried out in a randomized sample order using a high-resolution quadrupole-orbitrap mass spectrometer (Orbitrap Exploris 240 MS, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The ionization was achieved with an electrospray ionization interface operated in positive and negative ionization mode under polarity switching. A QC sample was analyzed in MS/MS fragmentation mode for the identification of compounds.</p>	<p>Metabolites that were found to be altered with the early-life exposure to a HFHS diet and restored by the microbiota-targeted intervention (individually by FOS+GOS or B. longum APC1472) were mapped to their corresponding Human Metabolome Database identifier and subjected to enrichment analysis using the MetaboAnalyst online pipeline for metabolites altered by the diet (Benjamini-Hochberg correction, P-adjusted p&lt;0.2), and restored by each intervention (Benjamini-Hochberg correction, P-adjusted p &lt; 0.2) on each sex with additional controls with False Discovery Rate (FDR), choosing the murine KEGG library as a reference.</p><p><br></p><p>Chong J, Wishart DS, Xia J. Using MetaboAnalyst 4.0 for Comprehensive and Integrative Metabolomics Data Analysis. CP in Bioinformatics 2019; 68:e86.&nbsp;</p><p>KEGG GENOME: Mus musculus (house mouse) [Internet]. [cited 2025 Feb 9]; Available from: https://www.genome.jp/kegg-bin/show_organism?menu_type=pathway_maps&amp;org=mmu</p>	<p>The Metabolite Annotation File (<strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>MAF</strong>) in this study contain metabolites that have been successfully identified. For the <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>complete list </strong>of analyzed features, please refer to the <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>DERIVED_FILES</strong> folder.</p><p><br></p><p>Data were processed and peak areas were extracted using Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and Skyline (23.1107)<strong>[1]</strong>. Out of the three levels of annotation for LC-MS/MS data, level 1 (annotations based on accurate mass, MS/MS spectra and known retention time compared against in-house standards analyzed on the same system) and level 2a (based on accurate mass and known retention time compared against in-house standards analyzed on the same system) were selected for further analyses, with the exclusion of non-dietary plant metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p>LOD The Limit of Detection (LOD): indicates the lowest value of a compound that can be differentiated from noise. LOD is determined as the standard deviation between the signal in the blanks multiplied by 3, plus the average of the blank samples (which is an estimation of a signal with a signal-to-noise level of 3. Compound ID: MS-Omics study-specific internal count for the compound, with prefix depending on the annotation source. This is the link between the various files and Excel sheets. MSID: Short for MS-Omics identifier, which are global identifiers for compounds in the MS-Omics chemical library. HMDB ID: The compound identifier used in the Human Metabolome Database. ChEBI ID: The compound identifier used in the Chemical Entities of Biological Interest database. ChemSpider ID: The compound identifier used in the ChemSpider database. PubChem ID: The compound identifier used in the PubChem database.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Adams KJ, Pratt B, Bose N, Dubois LG, St. John-Williams L, Perrott KM, Ky K, Kapahi P, Sharma V, MacCoss MJ, et al.

Study Title	Analysis of flavor formation and metabolite changes during production of buffalo milk Double-Layer Steamed Milk Custard
Study Description	<p>Metabolomics analysis showed that concentration of free amino acids and short-chain fatty acid lipids were increased in DLSMC, and they were more easily digested and absorbed. Key metabolic pathways, including alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolism, starch and sucrose metabolism, and glycerophospholipid metabolism, appear to be involved in forming essential flavors. Our research revealed that the manipulation of thermal processing and the oxidation of fatty acids in buffalo milk are crucial factors influencing the flavor profile of DLSMC.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study collected buffalo milk samples from the Buffalo Breeding Farm at the Buffalo Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences in Nanning, Guangxi, China. The DLSMC production follows traditional processing: raw milk is boiled for 10 minutes then cooled at 25°C for 30 minutes to form the first milk skin layer. After piercing a hole and pouring out the milk, this first layer adheres to the bowl bottom. Following addition of appropriate proportions of eggs and sugar, the mixture is steamed for 20 minutes to form the second milk skin layer. Samples were collected at four key production stages: raw buffalo milk designated as Group A; material after thermoprocessing at 100°C for 10 minutes designated as Group B; product after first milk skin formation at 25°C for 30 minutes designated as Group C; and final product after second milk skin formation at 100°C for 20 minutes designated as Group D.</p>	<p>Accurately weigh 200±5 mg of the sample and add a grinding bead. Add 800 µL of 80% methanol aqueous solution (methanol: water = 4:1 (v:v), HPLC grade) containing the internal standard solution L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL, _98%). The tissue samples are ground using a cryogenic tissue grinder for 6 minutes (-10_, 50 Hz) and then extracted by ultrasound at low temperature for 30 minutes (5_, 40 KHz). Place the sample at -20_ for 30 minutes, then centrifuge at 13,000 g and 4_ for 15 minutes, and remove the supernatant for machine analysis.</p>	<p>Additionally, an equal volume of the supernatant is taken from each sample and mixed as a QC sample. The instrument platform for analysis is the UHPLC-Exploris 240 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The 3 µL samples are separated using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm _ 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm Waters, Milford, USA) and then detected by mass spectrometry. The column temperature is set to 40 _.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Positive and negative ion scanning modes are used to collect the sample mass spectrum signals, with a mass scanning range of 70-1050 m/z. The flow rate of sheath gas is 60 psi, the flow rate of auxiliary gas is 20 psi, the heating temperature of auxiliary gas is 350 _, the positive mode ion spray voltage is set to 3400 V, the negative mode ion spray voltage is set to -3000 V, and the ion transfer tube temperature is 350_. Primary mass spectrum resolution is 60000, secondary mass spectrum resolution is 15000, and data is collected using DDA mode.</p>	<p>Primary mass spectrum resolution is 60000, secondary mass spectrum resolution is 15000, and data is collected using DDA mode.</p>	<p>The raw data was imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. The MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry information was matched with public databases, including HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), and a self-built database.</p>

Study Title	Multiple roles of extracellular vesicles in promoting microbial driven elemental cycle
Study Description	<p>Microbial driven manganese (Mn) reduction influences the geochemical cycling of Mn and the environmental fate of various organic and inorganic substances. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are known to impact microbial metabolic activities, but their specific role in Mn reduction remains arcane. Here, we investigated the potential and mechanistic roles of EVs in this process by integrating metagenomic analysis of environmental EVs with functional assays in representative dissimilatory metal-reducing bacteria. Microbial physiological assays and in situ electrochemical analyses revealed that the addition of EVs enhanced cellular metabolism, promoted biofilm formation, and facilitated extracellular electron transfer. The presence of various redox enzymes and metabolites in EVs contributed to more efficient substrate utilization and energy conservation. The electron transfer-promoting effect of EVs is primarily attributed to OmcA/MtrC, CymA, and FMN. These findings highlight the multifaceted role of EVs in microbial Mn reduction and suggest their broader involvement in elemental cycling and pollutant degradation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><em>Shewanella oneidensis</em>&nbsp;MR-1 were revived from _80 °C stocks, cultured in LB medium (30 °C, 150 rpm), harvested at the late logarithmic phase for EV extraction. The prepared bacterial culture was centrifuged at 10,000 _ g for 15 min at 4°C. After the supernatant was filtered through a 0.2 μM vacuum filter (Jinteng, Tianjin, China), the filtrate was concentrated by a tangential flow ultrafiltration system (100 kDa cutoff, Millipore). The retentate was again filtered through a 0.22 μM membrane (Merck Millipore, Ireland) to completely remove the bacteria. The filtrate was subsequently subject to ultracentrifugation at 100,000 _ g for 2 h at 4°C using a SW32Ti rotor (Beckman Coulter, USA). After removing the supernatant, the crude EVs pellet was washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) by a second round of ultracentrifugation (100,000 _ g, 4°C, 2 h) using a SW41Ti rotor (Beckman Coulter, USA). The crude EVs were further isolated by density gradient centrifugation (SW41Ti rotor, 100,000 _ g, 4°C, 16 h). EVs fraction was then recovered by diluting against at least 10-fold PBS and pelleted by ultracentrifugation (100,000g, 2 h, 4°C, SW 41 Ti rotor). The final pellet was suspended in PBS and stored at 4°C for short-term use and at -80°C for long-term storage.</p>	<p>EV samples were mixed with 4 times the volume of the extraction solution (methanol: acetonitrile = 1:1 (v/v)) and vortexed for 30 s after three rounds of homogenization (4 min, 35 Hz) and sonication in an ice-water bath for 5 minutes. Subsequently, the mixtures were performed with 10-minute sonication in an ice-water bath and 1-hour incubation at -40 °C. Following the centrifugation at 12,000 _ g for 15 min at 4 °C, the dried supernatants were reconstituted in the extraction solution (methanol: acetonitrile: water = 2:2:1, v/v) containing deuterated internal standards and vortexed for 30 s. The samples were further sonicated for 10 min (in ice water) and centrifuged at 12,000 _ g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatants were collected for untargeted metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were randomly injected on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm _ 50 mm, 1.7 μm) delivered through an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The mobile phase consisted of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia hydroxide in water (pH = 9.75) (A) and acetonitrile (B). The auto-sampler temperature and injection volume were set at 4 °C and 2 µL, respectively.</p>	<p>The LC was coupled to Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The full scan MS spectrum was continuously evaluated by the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo) with information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode using Orbitrap MS. The ESI source conditions were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary temperature as 320 °C, full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000, collision energy as SNCE 20/30/40, and spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV (negative).</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard. They were processed with an in-house program for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The data were processed with an in-house program. The program was developed using R and based on XCMS, for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. The metabolites were annotated by the R package and the BiotreeDB (V3.0).</p>

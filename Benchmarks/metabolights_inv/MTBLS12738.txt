Study Title	α-ketoglutarate maintains AMPK translation for energy sensing in human cancer cells_untargeted metabolites_flux_FAO
Study Description	<p>The energy sensor AMPK promotes tumor cell survival under stress, but how to prevent AMPK activation to blunt tumor progression remains unclear. Here we show that the metabolite α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) controls AMPK translation through a TET-YBX1 axis, which can be exploited to sensitize human cancer cells to energy stress. α-KG-deficient cells fail to activate AMPK under glucose starvation, which elicits cytosolic NADPH depletion and cell death . Mechanistically, α-KG insufficiency inhibits TET-dependent transcription of YBX1, an RNA-binding protein required for human-specific AMPK translation. Similarly, α-KG competitors including succinate and itaconate inhibit the YBX1-AMPK axis and sensitize cancer cells to glucose starvation. Finally, co-targeting oncogenic YBX1 and GLUT1 creates a synthetic lethality and therapeutically blunts tumor growth&nbsp;in vivo. Together, our findings link α-KG to energy sensing through AMPK translation, and propose that targeting α-KG-YBX1-dependent AMPK translation can sensitize human cancer cells to energy stress for treatment.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>FAO activity was measured by monitoring the conversion rate of [U-13C] PA (m+16) to [2-13C]-citrate (m+2) and [2-13C]-malate (m+2) as previously1. Briefly, Huh7 and H1299 cells were cultured for 4 h in glucose-free DMEM medium containing 10% delipidated serum (VivaCell, C3840) and 20 μM (Huh7) and 50 μM (H1299) [U-13C]-palmitate acid (m+16). The cells were then harvested for targeted metabolomics analysis following above protocol. The FAO activity was calculated by dividing the citrate (m+2) or malate (m+2) labeling rate by the palmitate (m+16) uptake rate as previously.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Cells were first washed twice with 1 mL PBS. To extract intracellular lipids, 1 mL of an ice-cold mixture of methanol: isopropanol: water (MeOH: IPA: H2O, 45:45:10, v/v/v) containing 0.3 M potassium hydroxide (KOH) was added to the culture dish. Cells were harvested by scraping and transferred into 4 mL glass vials. Samples were saponified by heating at 60 °C for 1 h. After cooling to room temperature, 100&nbsp;μl formic acid was added and mixed thoroughly, followed by the addition of 1 ml hexane. The mixture was vortexed for 1 minute and allowed to separate into layers. The upper organic layer was collected into a new 4 ml glass vial. An additional 1 ml of hexane was added to the remaining aqueous phase, vortexed for 1 minute, and allowed to separate again. The second upper layer was combined with the first extract. The pooled hexane layers were evaporated to dryness under a gentle stream of nitrogen. The dried residue was reconstituted in 100 μl methanol: isopropanol (1:1, v/v) and subjected to LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>&nbsp;Lipid extracts were analyzed using a Vanquish F UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled with a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on an Acclaim C30 column (3 μm, 2.1 × 150 mm) maintained at 45 °C. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (10 mM ammonium formate with 0.1% formic acid in water: acetonitrile 40:60, v/v) and solvent B (10 mM ammonium formate with 0.1% formic acid in isopropanol: acetonitrile 90:10, v/v). The flow rate was set to 0.26 ml/min with a gradient elution program as follows: 0–2 min, 15% B; 3 min, 30% B; 10.5 min, 45% B; 14 min, 97% B; 14–18 min, hold at 97% B; 18–18.1 min, return to 15% B; and 18.1–25 min, re-equilibration at 15% B. The injection volume was 10 μl, and the autosampler was maintained at 6 °C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed in negative ion mode using full scan with a scan range of m/z 200–1500. The resolution was set to 240,000 at m/z 200.</p>	<p>These metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html) , HMDB database(https://hmdb.ca/ metabolites).</p>	<p>These metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html) , HMDB database(https://hmdb.ca/ metabolites).</p>

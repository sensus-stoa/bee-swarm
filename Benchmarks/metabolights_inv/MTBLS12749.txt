Study Title	Integrated Physiological and Multi-Omics Analyses Reveal Nitrogen Fertilization Promotes Salix matsudana Growth and Soil Remediation
Study Description	<p> Soil contamination with heavy metals, particularly lead (Pb) and cadmium (Cd), has become a growing environmental concern due to rapid industrialization.&nbsp;Salix matsudana, a woody energy plant with notable tolerance to heavy metals, exhibits considerable potential for use in phytoremediation. Although nitrogen (N) fertilization is known to promote the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana, its dual role in enhancing plant development and improving soil conditions remains insufficiently understood. In this study, we systematically examined the effects of varying nitrogen fertilizer concentrations on the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana, heavy metal uptake, and rhizosphere soil characteristics through integrated physiological and multi-omics approaches. Our findings revealed that high nitrogen levels significantly increased plant biomass and the accumulation of Pb and Cd compared to control conditions. Detailed physiological analyses demonstrated enhanced heavy metal absorption in roots under nitrogen fertilization. Microbial community analysis further indicated that nitrogen application altered rhizosphere microbial activity, particularly enriching bacterial taxa associated with metal mobilization. Metabolomic profiling corroborated improvements in soil quality, showing significant changes in organic acid metabolites involved in metal chelation. These results provide new insights into the mechanisms by which nitrogen fertilization synergistically enhances both the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana&nbsp;and the efficiency of soil remediation, offering valuable guidance for optimizing phytoremediation strategies in heavy metal-contaminated environments.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The CK group functioned as the control group. In this group, Salix matsudana was planted in heavy metal-contaminated soil without the application of nitrogen fertilizer, and the maintenance measures were kept consistent with those of the other groups.</p><p><br></p><p>The HAM group involved the application of ammonium chloride to Salix matsudana cultivated in heavy metal-contaminated soil, aiming to investigate the effects of nitrogen fertilizer on the phytoremediation of heavy metal-polluted soil. The maintenance procedures were consistent with those of the other experimental groups.</p><p><br></p><p>The HCN group involved the application of CO(NH2)2to Salix matsudana cultivated in heavy metal-contaminated soil, aiming to investigate the effects of nitrogen fertilizer on the phytoremediation of heavy metal-polluted soil. The maintenance procedures were consistent with those of the other experimental groups.</p><p><br></p><p>The HNI group involved the application of sodium nitrate&nbsp;to Salix matsudana cultivated in heavy metal-contaminated soil, aiming to investigate the effects of nitrogen fertilizer on the phytoremediation of heavy metal-polluted soil. The maintenance procedures were consistent with those of the other experimental groups.</p><p><br></p><p>The HAN group involved the application of ammonium chloride and sodium nitrate in a 1:1 ratio, with Salix matsudana cultivated in heavy metal-contaminated soil. The study aimed to investigate the effects of these nitrogen fertilizers on the phytoremediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil, with maintenance protocols consistent across all experimental groups.</p><p><br></p><p>Baoding&nbsp;the Experimental Station of Hebei Agricultural University</p>	<p>50 mg solid sample was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead</p><p>was added. 400 μL of extraction solution&nbsp;(methanol:&nbsp;water = 4:1 (v:v))&nbsp;containing&nbsp;0.02&nbsp;mg/mL&nbsp;of&nbsp;internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine)&nbsp;was used for metabolite extraction. Samples were ground by the Wonbio-96c ( Shanghai wanbo biotechnology co., LTD) frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz). The samples were left at -20°C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4°C, 13000 g), and the supernatant was transferred to the injection vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a&nbsp;SCIEX UPLC-Triple&nbsp;TOF 5600 system equipped with an&nbsp;ACQUITY&nbsp;HSS T3&nbsp;column&nbsp;(100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm;&nbsp;Waters,&nbsp;USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile&nbsp;(95:5, v/v)&nbsp;(solvent&nbsp;A) and 0.1% formic acid in a :water&nbsp;(47.5:47.5,&nbsp;v/v)(solvent&nbsp;B). The flow rate was 0.40&nbsp;mL/min&nbsp;and the column temperature was 40°C.</p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a&nbsp;SCIEX UPLC-Triple&nbsp;TOF 5600 Mass Spectrometer</p><p>equipped with a electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative</p><p>mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 550oC ; curtain gas</p><p>(CUR) at 30 psi; both Ion Source Gas1 and Gas2 at 50 psi; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at</p><p>-4000V&nbsp;in negative mode and 5000V in positive mode, respectively; declustering potential at 80V;</p><p>collision energy(CE), 20-60 eV rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition was performed with the</p><p>Information Dependent Acquisition (IDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range</p><p>of 50-1000 m/z.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC/MS raw data was performed by&nbsp;Progenesis&nbsp;QI, Milford,&nbsp;USA) software, and a three-dimensional data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this three-dimensional matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed, and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, deredundant and peak pooled.&nbsp;</p>	<p>At the same time, the metabolites were identified by searching database, and the main databases were the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin ( https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China)</p>

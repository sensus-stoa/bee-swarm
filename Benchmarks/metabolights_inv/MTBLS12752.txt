Study Title	Rumen Fluid Metabolomics Analysis of Sheep Under Different Grazing Intensities with Supplementary Feeding
Study Description	<p>This dataset comprises untargeted LC-MS metabolomics profiles of rumen samples from 8 sheep under moderate grazing with supplementary feeding and 8 sheep under intensive grazing with supplementary feeding. Samples were analyzed using Thermo Fisher Scientific mass spectrometers in positive/negative ionization modes. Data were processed with Progenesis QI and are intended for identifying differential metabolites in the rumen between the two grazing intensities with supplementation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Rumen fluid was collected from the ventral sac of the rumen and the rumen contents were filtered through four layers of cheesecloth from 16 sheep, including 8 under moderate grazing with supplementary feeding and 8 under intensive grazing with supplementary feeding, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80°C until analysis.</p>	<p>Rumen fluid samples underwent extraction by combining 100 μL of fluid with 400 μL of a solution (methanol: acetonitrile, 1:1, v/v) containing deuterated internal standards. The mixture was vortexed briefly, sonicated for 10 minutes in a 4°C water bath, and incubated at -40°C for 1 hour for protein precipitation. Following centrifugation at 12,000 rpm (RCF = 13,800 × g, R = 8.6 cm) for 15 minutes at 4°C, the supernatant was transferred to a new glass vial for analysis. A quality control (QC) sample was created by combining equal volumes of supernatants from all samples.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analysis was conducted using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase used 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and ammonia hydroxide (pH 9.75) in water (solvent A) and acetonitrile (solvent B). The auto-sampler was set at 4°C with a 2 μL injection.&nbsp;The Orbitrap Exploris 120, controlled by Xcalibur in IDA mode, continuously evaluated the full scan MS.&nbsp;ESI conditions were: sheath gas 50 Arb, aux gas 15 Arb, capillary 320°C, full MS resolution 60,000, MS/MS resolution 15,000, collision energy 20/30/40 (SNCE), and spray voltage 3.8 kV (positive) or −3.4 kV (negative).</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analysis was conducted using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase used 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and ammonia hydroxide (pH 9.75) in water (solvent A) and acetonitrile (solvent B). The auto-sampler was set at 4°C with a 2 μL injection.&nbsp;The Orbitrap Exploris 120, controlled by Xcalibur in IDA mode, continuously evaluated the full scan MS.&nbsp;ESI conditions were: sheath gas 50 Arb, aux gas 15 Arb, capillary 320°C, full MS resolution 60,000, MS/MS resolution 15,000, collision energy 20/30/40 (SNCE), and spray voltage 3.8 kV (positive) or −3.4 kV (negative).</p>	<p>Raw data were converted to mzXML via ProteoWizard and processed by an in-house R program based on XCMS for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. Metabolite identification used R and BiotreeDB (V3.0). The final dataset (peak number, sample name, normalized peak area) was analyzed with SIMCA16.0.2 (Sartorius Stedim Data Analytics AB).</p>	<p>Lipids and lipid-like molecules, Nucleosides, nucleotides, and analogues, Amino acids and Peptides</p>

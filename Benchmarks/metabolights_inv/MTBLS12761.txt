Study Title	Interaction of Genetic Variants Activates Latent Metabolic Pathways in Yeast
Study Description	<p>Genetic interactions are fundamental to the architecture of complex traits, yet the molecular mechanisms by which variant combinations influence cellular pathways remain poorly understood. Here, we answer the question of whether interactions between genetic variants can activate unique pathways and if such pathways can be targeted to modulate phenotypic outcomes. The model organism&nbsp;Saccharomyces cerevisiae&nbsp;was used to dissect how two causal SNPs,&nbsp;<em>MKT1(89G)</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>TAO3(4477C)</em>, interact to modulate metabolic and phenotypic outcomes during sporulation. By integrating time-resolved transcriptomics, absolute proteomics, and targeted metabolomics in isogenic allele replacement yeast strains, we show that the combined presence of these SNPs uniquely activates the arginine biosynthesis pathway and suppresses ribosome biogenesis, reflecting a metabolic trade-off that enhances sporulation efficiency. Functional validation demonstrates that the arginine pathway is essential for mitochondrial activity and efficient sporulation only in the double-SNP background. Our findings show how genetic variant interactions can rewire core metabolic networks, providing a mechanistic framework for understanding polygenic trait regulation and the emergence of additive effects in complex traits.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Yeast samples were collected at three time points (0 h, 2 h 30 min, and 8 h) following transfer to sporulation medium at an OD(600) of 1.0.</p>	<p>Metabolism was rapidly quenched by adding four volumes of ice-cold 60% methanol (kept in –80°C), followed by incubation on ice for 5 min. Cells were pelleted by centrifugation at 1,000 × g for 3 min at 0°C and washed once with 700 µl of ice-cold 60% methanol (kept in –80°C). The pellet was then resuspended in 1 ml of 75% ethanol and incubated at 80°C for 3 min to extract intracellular metabolites. Samples were cooled on ice for 5 min and centrifuged at maximum speed for 10 min at 4°C. Supernatants were collected, dried in a vacuum concentrator, and stored at –80°C until analysis. LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on an Agilent 6495 Triple Quadrupole mass spectrometer operating in positive ionisation mode using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM). </p><p><br></p><p>For LC-MS/MS analysis, dried extracts were reconstituted in 200 µL of LC-MS-grade water containing 0.1% formic acid. Samples were vortexed for 30 seconds, spun briefly, and sonicated for 2 minutes. After centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, the supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube and diluted 5-fold with 0.1% formic acid. A 10 µL aliquot was injected into the LC-MS system. To monitor instrument performance and ensure data quality, three pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by combining equal aliquots from all experimental samples and were analysed at the beginning, middle, and end of the run. The coefficient of variation (CV) for each amino acid was calculated, and it was ensured that the CV remained below 15% for all analytes </p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC system equipped with a C18 column (4 µm, 4.6 × 150 mm). The mobile phases consisted of water with 0.1% formic acid (A) and methanol with 0.1% formic acid (B) at a 0.5 ml/min flow rate. The column oven and autosampler were maintained at 45 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The following gradient was applied: 0–3 min, 2% B; 3–12 min, linear increase to 35% B; 12–15 min, linear increase to 90% B; 15–16 min, 90% B; 16–16.1 min, decrease to 5% B; 16.1–20 min, re-equilibration at 5% B.</p>	<p>Detection was performed on an Agilent 6495 Triple Quadrupole mass spectrometer operating in positive ionisation mode using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM). </p>	<p>Data acquisition and quantification were conducted using Agilent MassHunter Workstation Quantitative Analysis software (version 10.1). </p>	<p>Data acquisition and quantification were conducted using Agilent MassHunter Workstation Quantitative Analysis software (version 10.1). </p>

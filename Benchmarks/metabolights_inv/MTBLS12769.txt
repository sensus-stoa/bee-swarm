Study Protocol Description	<p>This study aims to systematically collect fecal samples from thyroid cancer patients before and after treatment, storing and processing them according to stringent standardized protocols. Upon collection, samples are immediately frozen at -80°C in ultra-low temperature freezers. Subsequent processing follows aseptic operating procedures, including homogenization, DNA/RNA extraction, and quality assessment. All operations are conducted within a biosafety cabinet to ensure sample integrity and data reliability, laying the foundation for future gut microbiota metagenomic research. By comparing microbiome differences before and after treatment, this study seeks to reveal the impact of thyroid cancer therapies on gut microbial ecology.</p>	<p>Remove the sample from liquid nitrogen and thaw it in a refrigerator at 4 °C. Take the sample and place it in a 5 mL centrifuge tube. Add 500 μL PBS and 2 Ml chloroform methanol solution, vortex for 1 min, mix well, and centrifuge at 4 °C for 10 min. Take the centrifugal sample extraction solution, use a pipette to aspirate 100 μL of the supernatant, and transfer it to injection vials with inner liners for metabolomics analysis. Use a pipette to extract 5 μL of the metabolomic analysis sample solution into the same injection vial with an inner tube, and obtain QC samples.</p>	<p>The chromatographic column is HSS T3 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 μm); Column temperature 40 °C; Injector temperature 10 °C; Injection volume 2 μL; Flow rate 0.40 mL/min; Mobile phase A is a 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution and mobile phase B is a 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile solution, eluted according to a gradient.</p>	<p>Acquisition mode: Positive and negative ions are scanned separately (ES+/ES-), capillary voltage is 1 kV, cone hole voltage is 30 V, cone hole airflow is 50 L/h, source temperature is 150 °C, atomization temperature is 500 °C, atomization airflow is 800 L/h, acquisition mode MSE, primary voltage is 6 eV, secondary voltage is 10-50 eV, scanning range is 50-1200 m/z.</p>	<p>Firstly, handle missing values, outliers and noisy data. Then adjust the distribution of the data to make it within a unified range. Common methods include total normalization, median normalization and quantization normalization. Then perform appropriate transformations on the data, such as logarithmic transformation, to reduce the skewness of the data. Finally, scale the data to a specific range, such as mean standard deviation scaling and range scaling.</p>	<p>Import mass spectrometry data into Progenesis QI software to complete data alignment, grouping and peak detection. Use Progenesis QI software for metabolite identification, mainly using databases such as HMDB and KEGG.</p>

Study Title	Microbial -Enzymatic Mucin Metabolic Relay Suppresses Tumor Progression in Apcmin/+ mice
Study Description	<p>Compelling evidence established the gut microbiota as a pivotal regulator of colorectal cancer (CRC) progression, although stage-specific mechanisms remain unclear. Our investigation in&nbsp;Apcmin/+&nbsp;mice mapped tumor growth and microbial shifts across early, intermediate, and advanced stages. In&nbsp;Apcmin/+&nbsp;mice, tumor growth exhibited an initial rapid phase followed by a gradual deceleration. Our study elucidated a niche complementarity model of gut microbiota in suppressing CRC progression: both critical gut bacteria (Akkermansia muciniphila, Muribaculaceae and other taxa) and their enzymes (hexosaminidase and SusD) exhibited the relay pattern in metabolizing intestinal mucin and producing N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc). Furthermore, rectal administration of GlcNAc suppressed Wnt/β-catenin signaling activation, might potentially contributing to the deceleration of tumor growth in the mid-to-late stages of cancer. This interplay between gut bacteria may reflect the significance of functional redundancy in maintaining the stability of microbial function.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mice feces: the experimental mice were placed in transparent boxes and allowed to defecate freely. Fresh mouse feces were collected in EP tubes and stored in a -80℃&nbsp;refrigerator.</p><p>Bacterial supernatant: After culturing the bacteria in the medium for a certain period of time, remove the medium, centrifuge it, and then take the supernatant. Store it at -80℃&nbsp;in the refrigerator.</p>	<p>Pre-treat the mouse feces and the supernatant of the bacterial culture medium. First, take about 15 mg of freeze-dried fecal powder or 50&nbsp;μL of the supernatant of the bacterial suspension, add 1 mL of 80% methanol-water and steel beads, and then use a tissue homogenizer to grind them, followed by ultrasonic-assisted extraction. Centrifuge at 12000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4℃, transfer the supernatant to a new EP tube, and place the extract in a vacuum centrifugal concentrator to concentrate until the moisture is completely evaporated. The next step is to perform derivatization treatment of the sugar substances: first, add 20 mg/mL methoxyamine hydrochloride/piperidine 20&nbsp;mL to the EP tube of the concentrated sample, react at 25℃ for 2 hours, then add MSTFA 60&nbsp;mL, react at 40℃ for 1 hour, and finally add 100&nbsp;mL of n-hexane.</p>	<p>The gas phase conditions are as follows: 1&nbsp;μL of liquid sample is injected; the injection port temperature is 250℃; the injection mode is non-siphoning; the flow rate: helium gas, constant flow, 1.0 mL/min; the temperature control box: 80℃&nbsp;for 0.5 min, 15℃/min to 200℃, 3℃/min to 230℃, 20℃/min to 280℃&nbsp;for 5 min. The mass spectrometry conditions are as follows: transfer line temperature: 280℃; EI ion source 70 eV, 280℃; solvent delay 10 min. The final confirmed mass spectrometry conditions for targeted quantitative detection of Galactose, GlcNAc and GalNAc are Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) scanning mode: 319.1-129 collision energy 15 V, 319.1-157 collision energy 10 V, 205.1-167 collision energy 7 V.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry conditions are as follows: transfer line temperature: 280℃; EI ion source 70 eV, 280℃; solvent delay 10 min.

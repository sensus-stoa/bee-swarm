Study Protocol Description	<p>The cohort consist of 10 patients, who were randomly selected to integrated spatial multi-omics analysis (PDAC, n=5; PDAC with DM, n=5). Frozen tissue samples were fixed in 3 drops of distilled water during the cutting stage. The tissues were sectioned at 10 μm thickness using a Leica CM1950 cryostat (Leica Microsystems GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany) at -20 °C. Afterwards, the tissue sections were placed in groups on electrically conductive slides coated with indium tin oxide (ITO), and the slides with tissue sections were dried in a vacuum desiccator for 30 min. Desiccated tissue sections mounted on ITO glass slides were sprayed using an HTX TM sprayer (Bruker Daltonics, Germany) with 15 mg/mL DHB (2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid), dissolved in 90%:10% Acetonitrile:water. The sprayer temperature was set to 65 °C, with a flow rate of 0.12 mL/min, pressure of 5 psi. 26 passes of the matrix were applied to slides with 5 s of drying time between each pass.</p>	<p>The tissue was frozen and sectioned using a Leica CM1950 cryosecter, with a sectioning thickness of 10 microns. The tissue taken out of the -80°C refrigerator was placed in a slicer and balanced at -20°C for 1 hour. Fix the sample onto the sample holder and adjust the Angle and position of the sample. Then, fix the sample holder onto the positioning sample head of the microtome. Follow the operation instructions of the microtome to perform the sectioning. Use a pre-cooled brush to transfer the sectioned section onto a pre-cooled ITO slide. Press the back of the slide against the back of your hand and use the temperature of your hand to melt the tissue section until it becomes transparent. After becoming transparent, rub the back of the slide with your fingers. The temperature from the back will evaporate the moisture on the tissue on the front of the slide, and the tissue will change from transparent to white. After cutting, the ITO slides containing tissue sections should be dried for 30 minutes using a vacuum drying instrument.</p>	<p>MALDI timsTOF MSI experiments were performed on a prototype Bruker timsTOF flex MS system (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany) equipped with a 10 kHz smartbeam 3D laser. Laser power was set to 75% and then fixed throughout the whole experiment. The mass spectra were acquired in positive mode. The mass spectra data were acquired over a mass range from m/z 50 to 1300 Da. The imaging spatial resolution was set to 50 μm for the tissue, and each spectrum consisted of 400 laser shots. MALDI mass spectra were normalized with the Root Mean Square, and the signal intensity in each image was shown as the normalized intensity. MS/MS fragmentations performed on the timsTOF flex MS system in the MS/MS mode were used for further detailed structural confirmation of the identified metabolites.</p>	<p>Image all the identified target peaks to obtain the distribution of the target substance on the tissue. In order to improve the visual effect of the imaging map and provide the image after Gaussian smooth at the same time. Spatial Segmentation analysis can provide an overview of mass spectrometry imaging datasets and quickly detect significant features. This method determines the similarity between mass spectra in a given area through statistical methods and classifies similar mass spectra into one category. These mass spectra are assigned a specified color (Alexandrov et al., 2010). All the target peak data obtained from substance identification were used for Spatially aware nearest shrunken centroids clustering to obtain partition information for the subsequent Region of Interests. The selection of ROIs provides a reference (Bemis et al., 2016; Tibshirani et al., 2003.</p>	<p>The original imaging data was imported into the SCiLS Lab software for reading, and smoothing processing and root mean square standardization were carried out.</p>	<p>Among the mass spectrometry peaks obtained through peak marking, the target peaks with higher intensity (MS primary information) are subjected to in-situ secondary fragmentation on the tissue to obtain the secondary spectra collected on the tissue (MS/MS secondary fragment peak information). The secondary spectra collected on the tissue are compared by searching the self-built database and integrating the public database (HMDB) for substance identification. Due to the low intensity, the target peak (MS first-level information) of the second-level spectrum cannot be collected. Based on its first-level molecular weight, the substances in the self-built database and the integrated public database (HMDB) are retrieved within an error range of 10 ppm to find the substance with the molecular weight closest to that detected by the instrument. A total of 1435 substances were identified in this project, among which 846 substances were identified at the secondary level.</p>

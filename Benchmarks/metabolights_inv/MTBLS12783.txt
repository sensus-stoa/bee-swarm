Study Identifier	MTBLS12783
Study Title	CPT2 inhibition activates selective autophagy by enhancing glycerophospholipid biosynthesis in colorectal cancer
Study Description	<p>Metabolic reprogramming is one of the hallmark events in colorectal cancer (CRC). &nbsp;Tumor cells adapt to microenvironmental changes by regulating energy and biomaterial metabolism. This study identified abnormal lipid accumulation and significant downregulation of fatty acid metabolism in CRC. The core gene of this pathway, CPT2, critically impacts CRC patient survival and regulates tumor cell proliferation both in vitro and in vivo. Metabolite analysis demonstrated that CPT2 knockdown induced accumulation of glycerophospholipids (GPs), primarily phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), driven by enhanced long-chain fatty acid (LCFA)-dependent GP biosynthesis mediated by GPAT4. Transcriptomic analysis and subsequent experiments revealed that GP accumulation, as key components of vesicles and autophagosomes, promoted autophagosome maturation and elongation, thereby activating selective autophagy (lipophagy) in CRC cells and accelerating tumor progression. These findings enhance understanding of tumor metabolic characteristics and identify novel diagnostic and therapeutic targets for CRC.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>HCT116 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were treated with NC or siCPT2 for 48 hours. The cells&nbsp;were collected, washed three times with PBS, and preserved on dry ice. The samples were sent to the PTM BIO company for high-throughput metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>The extraction protocol for Hct116 cell samples (Class I) began with thawing frozen samples on ice. After complete suspension in 500 μL of an 80% methanol-water extraction solution containing internal standards via vortexing for 3 minutes, samples underwent three cycles of rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen (5 min), thawing on dry ice (5 min), followed by thawing on ice (5 min), with vortexing for 2 minutes between cycles. The extract was then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. Subsequently, 300 μL of the supernatant was transferred to a new tube, incubated at -20°C for 30 minutes, and centrifuged again under the same conditions for 3 minutes. Finally, 200 μL of the clarified supernatant was aliquoted into vials for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column (1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) maintained at 40°C with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min and an injection volume of 4 μL. A binary mobile phase system was employed: Mobile Phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and Mobile Phase B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution profile was as follows: 95% A / 5% B at 0.0 min, decreasing to 80% A / 20% B by 2.0 min, further decreasing to 40% A / 60% B at 5.0 min, then rapidly shifting to 1% A / 99% B at 6.0 min and held until 7.5 min. The system was re-equilibrated to the initial conditions (95% A / 5% B) by 7.6 min and held until 10.0 min.</p>	<p>Metabolite detection utilized an AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600+ mass spectrometer operating in both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI-) ionization modes for 10 minutes each. Key source parameters included IonSpray Voltages of +5000 V (ESI+) / -4000 V (ESI-), source temperatures of 550°C (ESI+) / 450°C (ESI-), Gas1 (nebulizer) and Gas2 (heater) pressures of 50 psi and 60 psi respectively for both modes, and a Curtain Gas pressure of 35 psi. Declustering Potentials were set to +60 V (ESI+) / -60 V (ESI-). MS1 scans covered m/z 50-1000 Da with an accumulation time of 0.2 seconds and Collision Energy of 10 V (ESI+) / -10 V (ESI-). Data-dependent MS/MS acquisition (18 candidate ions per cycle) scanned m/z 25-1000 Da using an accumulation time of 0.04 seconds, Collision Energy of 30 V (ESI+) / -30 V (ESI-), and a Collision Energy Spread of 15 V.</p>	<p>The extraction protocol for Hct116 cell samples (Class I) began with thawing frozen samples on ice. After complete suspension in 500 μL of an 80% methanol-water extraction solution containing internal standards via vortexing for 3 minutes, samples underwent three cycles of rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen (5 min), thawing on dry ice (5 min), followed by thawing on ice (5 min), with vortexing for 2 minutes between cycles. A binary mobile phase system was employed, Mobile Phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and Mobile Phase B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The system was re-equilibrated to the initial conditions (95% A / 5% B) by 7.6 min and held until 10.0 min.</p>	<p>Metabolite detection utilized an AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600+ mass spectrometer operating in both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI-) ionization modes for 10 minutes each. Declustering Potentials were set to +60 V (ESI+) / -60 V (ESI-). MS1 scans covered m/z 50-1000 Da with an accumulation time of 0.2 seconds and Collision Energy of 10 V (ESI+) / -10 V (ESI-). Peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using the&nbsp;XCMS&nbsp;program. The&nbsp;'SVR' method&nbsp;was applied to correct peak areas, and peaks with a missing rate &gt;50% across sample groups were filtered out. To minimize quantitative errors caused by variations in sample size, metabolomics data were calibrated based on protein concentrations according to the&nbsp;central dogma principle. Specifically:</p><p>1.Protein concentrations were measured using the&nbsp;BCA assay.</p><p>2.Metabolomics data from test samples were normalized using protein concentrations from parallel samples.</p>	<p>&nbsp;The corrected and filtered peaks were then annotated by searching against a laboratory-built database, integrated public databases, an AI-predicted database, the&nbsp;metDNA&nbsp;method.</p>

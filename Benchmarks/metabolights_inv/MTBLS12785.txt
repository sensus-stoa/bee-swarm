Study Identifier	MTBLS12785
Study Title	A longitudinal ¹H NMR-based urinary metabolomics dataset in a birth cohort for childhood atopic diseases
Study Description	<p>This dataset presents longitudinal urinary metabolomic profiles from 198 children at 1 month, 6 months, 1 year, 3 years and 5 years. It includes demographic data, feeding practices, parental atopy, household income and atopic disease status over time. Total and allergen-specific IgE (mite, egg, cow's milk) were measured to track allergic sensitization. Metabolomic data, obtained via ¹H-NMR spectroscopy and normalized for analysis, support investigations into the links between early development, metabolism and atopic disease progression.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Urine samples were longitudinally collected at five time points: 1 month, 6 months, 1 year, 3 years and 5 years of age.</p>	<p>Urine samples were stored at -80 °C in aliquots until required. For each use, an aliquot was thawed, used and any remnants discarded after completion of the experiment.</p>	<p>A 900 μL urine sample was combined with 100 μL of 1.5 M phosphate buffer prepared in deuterium water, which contained 0.04% 3-(trimethylsilyl)-propionic-2,2,3,3-d4 acid sodium salt (TSP) as a reference for chemical shift calibration. The mixture was gently swirled for 20 s, then subjected to centrifugation at 12,000 x g and 4 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation, 650 μL of the resulting supernatant was carefully transferred into a 5-mm NMR tube for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>The ¹H-NMR spectral data were recorded at 300K using a Bruker Avance 600 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker-Biospin GmbH, Karlsruhe, Germany) fitted with a 5-mm CPTCI ¹H cryoprobe.</p>	<p>Each NMR spectrum was acquired using 64 scans, with a spectral width of 10,000 Hz (10 ppm) and a relaxation delay of 4 s.</p>	<p>the spectra underwent pre-processing, including zero padding and exponential multiplication with a line broadening factor of 0.3 Hz. Subsequent manual phasing, baseline correction and chemical shift referencing to TSP (δ 0.0 ppm) were carried out using TopSpin 3.2 software (Bruker BioSpin, Rheinstetten, Germany) for further NMR data analysis.</p>	<p>Chenomx Inc. version 8.6 was used for metabolite identification</p>

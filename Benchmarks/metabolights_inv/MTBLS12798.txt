Study Identifier	MTBLS12798
Study Title	Gut symbiotic bacteria-derived nicotinic acid regulates Bactrocera dorsalis reproduction
Study Description	<p>Depletion of gut commensal bacteria significantly reduced nicotinic acid (NA) levels in the hemolymph and ovaries of Bactrocera dorsalis, leading to impaired ovarian development. This inhibition was reversed by exogenous NA supplementation. We identified Enterobacter hormaechei as a key NA-producing gut symbiont. Recolonization with E. hormaechei restored NA levels in both the hemolymph and ovaries, thereby rescuing ovarian development in B. dorsalis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>To test the ability of&nbsp;gut bacteria to produce nicotinic acid, equal amounts of bacteria strains were inoculated into M9 medium supplemented with 3 mM ASP and incubated in the dark for 36 h;&nbsp;For hemolymph samples, the samples were diluted 20–40 times with 80% methanol/water (v/v) and extracted at 4°C in the dark for 1 h; For ovarian tissue, the samples were homogenized in PBS and sonicated for 25 s.</p>	<p>The bacterial culture was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min.&nbsp;The supernatant was then filtered through a 0.22 µm membrane and subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis; For hemolymph and ovaries, lysates with equal protein concentrations were extracted in 80% methanol/water (v/v) at 4°C in the dark for 1 h. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 15 min, the supernatant was used for LC-MS/MS analysis; The calibration standard: LGC Standards, CAS Rn: 59-67-6) with methanol,&nbsp;work concentrations: 5, 25, 50, 100, 300 ng/mL for nicotinic acid.</p>	<p>Analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (Shimadzu LC-30A) coupled with an Qtrap 4000 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX). The LC separation was performed on a reversed-phase C18 column (150 mm × 2.1 mm, 5 µm particle size, GL Sciences). Solvent A was 0.1% (V/V) formic acid in water and solvent B was methanol with 2 nM ammonium acetate. The gradient was 0–2 min, 99%-99% A; 3–8 min, 5%–5% A; 8–15 min, 99%–99% A. Other LC parameters: flow rate 300 µL/min, column temperature 30, auto-sampler temperature 4°C and injection volume 10 µL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed with MRM mode, monitoring the transition of the precursor ion m/z 124 to the product ion m/z 80 for NA. Ion spray voltage was set to 4000 V, with a source temperature of 350°C.</p>	<p>The calibration curve was generated based on the peak area and the concentration of the standard. The sample concentration was calculated based on the calibration curve.</p>	<p>Ionization mode: Positive ion mode ([M H], m/z 124.04). Retention time (RT): 2.52 min</p>

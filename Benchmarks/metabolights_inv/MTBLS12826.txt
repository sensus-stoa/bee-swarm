Study Title	Celery (Apium graveolens L.) Juice Fermented with Lactiplantibacillus plantarum NCU116 Mitigates Type 2 Diabetes in Mice: Mechanistic Insights from Gut Microbiota and Metabolomics
Study Description	<p>This study aimed to investigate the metabolic effects of fermented celery juice (FCJ) and non-fermented celery juice (NFCJ) in a mouse model of type 2 diabetes. Fresh celery juice was fermented with Lactiplantibacillus plantarum NCU116 under controlled conditions. Male C57BL/6J mice were divided into normal, diabetic, and intervention groups receiving NFCJ or FCJ for 4 weeks. Colonic contents were collected and subjected to non-targeted metabolomics using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC–MS/MS). This dataset contains the raw LC–MS data of colonic metabolites, providing a resource for understanding the metabolic impact of fermented and non-fermented celery juice in diabetic mice.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Colonic contents were collected from C57BL/6J mice following a 4-week intervention with fermented or non-fermented celery juice. Mice were fasted for 12 h before sacrifice, and the colon contents were immediately harvested under sterile conditions, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Freeze-dried colonic content (10 mg) was resuspended in 200 μL of ultrapure water and homogenized at 60 Hz for 1 min. The mixture was sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min. Subsequently, 800 μL of cold methanol was added, followed by vortexing for 1 min. Samples were incubated at −20 °C overnight to precipitate proteins. The next day, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, and the resulting supernatants were collected for metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Nexera X2 UHPLC system (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA) maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B). The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–1 min, 5% B; 1–2 min, 5–20% B; 2–3 min, 20–40% B; 3–6 min, 40–60% B; 6–10 min, 60–85% B; 10–11 min, 85–95% B; 11–13 min, 95% B; 13–14 min, 95–5% B; 14–16 min, 5% B. The flow rate was 0.25 mL/min, and the injection volume was 3 μL.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Metabolite detection was performed on an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600 mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, Framingham, MA, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The instrument was operated in both positive and negative ion modes. Data were acquired in full scan mode over a mass range of 50–1000 m/z. The ion spray voltage was set to +5500 V/−4500 V, the source temperature was 500 °C, curtain gas at 35 psi, nebulizer gas (GS1) and heater gas (GS2) at 50 psi.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (v2.0, Waters) for peak detection, alignment, and normalization.</p>	<p>Metabolites were putatively annotated by matching accurate mass and MS/MS spectra against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB v4.0) and METLIN database.</p>

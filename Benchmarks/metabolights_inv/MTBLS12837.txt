Study Identifier	MTBLS12837
Study Title	Untargeted lipid profiling following mTOR inhibition
Study Description	<p>The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) is pivotal in programming cellular lipid homeostasis for the structural and functional integrity of cells. Dysregulation of mTOR and consequent altered lipid metabolism are common features in various diseases, including cancers, making mTOR signaling a promising therapeutic target. Therefore, understanding how mTOR activation and inhibition reprogram cellular lipid homeostasis is of physiological and pathological significance. Using mTOR inhibitors in A375 melanoma cells, we performed untargeted lipid profiling to assess changes in lipid composition following mTOR inhibition.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cell samples were spiked with an internal lipid standard mix, and ice-cold methanol and chloroform in a 1:2 v/v ratio were added directly to the samples. After sonication for 5 min, the samples were incubated on ice for 30 min and vortexed occasionally. Ice-cold water was added for a final ratio of chloroform:methanol:water of 8:4:3 (v/v/v), and the samples were incubated on ice for an additional 10 min. Following centrifugation at 10,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C to separate the aqueous and organic layers, the lower organic phase was carefully transferred to a new tube. The remaining upper aqueous layer was re-extracted and combined with the organic phases. The organic layer was then dried down under nitrogen at 30 °C. The dried lipid extract was reconstituted with a 1:1 mixture of methanol and chloroform and transferred to an LC vial with a 200 μL glass insert. A QC sample was prepared by mixing an aliquot of all samples. The QC sample and the blanks were injected during the sample analysis.</p>	<p>Cell samples were spiked with an internal lipid standard mix, and ice-cold methanol and chloroform in a 1:2 v/v ratio were added directly to the samples. The mass spectrometer was operated in a 2 GHz extended dynamic range mode employing precursor ion analysis for relative quantification experiments in positive ion mode. Internal references were used for calibration. Mobile phase A was 90:10 water:methanol v/v with 10 mm ammonium acetate plus 0.5 mm ammonium fluoride, and B was (5:3:2) isopropanol:methanol:acetonitrile v/v/v with 10 mm ammonium acetate plus 0.5 mm ammonium fluoride. The resuspended lipid extracts were loaded onto a 2.1 x 100 mm Agilent Poroshell C18, 2.7 μm column (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Separation was performed using an Agilent 1290 UPLC system using the following gradient: 70% B at 0.0-1.0 min, 86% B at 3.5-10.0 min and 100% B at 11.0-17.0 at a flow rate of 300 μL/min. A post-column equilibration time of 5 min was used for all runs. Source parameters were as follows: gas temperature (200 °C), drying gas (11 L/min), nebulizer (35 psi), sheath gas temperature (350 °C), sheath gas flow (12 L/min), VCap (3000 V) and fragmentor (145 V). Data was collected for relative quantification using a scan speed of 4 MS spectra/s. Pooled samples were used in lipid database creation, and an iterative MS/MS workflow was performed in the Mass Hunter acquisition software across multiple injections of the pooled samples with a scan speed of 10 MS and 3 MS/MS spectra/s.</p>	<p>MS analysis of the lipid extracts was performed using an Agilent 6545 Q-TOF LC-MS system controlled by the Agilent Mass Hunter acquisition software. Raw LC-MS data files were processed using the Profinder software (vB.10.00, Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Here, molecular features were extracted for peaks ≥ 5000 counts in the positive mode ([M + H]+ , [M + Na]+ , [M + NH4 ]+ , [M + H - H2 O]+, [M + Na - H2 O]+ , [M + NH4 - H2 O]+) using an isotope model of common organic molecules. The resulting compound list was further filtered for those having an absolute height of ≥10,000 counts, quality score of ≥60, and compounds having two or more isotopes present. Additionally, the retention time for each compound was aligned to ±0.1 min using a mass accuracy window of ≤5.0 ppm and peaks integrated using the Agile integrator in the Profinder software. Each integrated peak was manually reviewed for retention time and fragmentation matching. The processed data file was then exported and imported into the Mass Profiler Professional software (v15.1, Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA), where each data set was analyzed separately. All compound abundance values were baseline-corrected to the median abundance and normalized by the class of lipids in IS. If the class is unavailable in IS, it will use the closest IS by retention time.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data was used to create a fragmentation-based (MS/MS) library containing m/z precursors and retention times for all MS/MS identified lipids using the Lipid Annotator software (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) and the following settings: lipid species for positive ([M + H]+, [M + Na]+, [M + NH4]+, [M + H - H2O]+, [M + Na - H2 O]+, [M + NH4 - H2O]+), Q-Score ≥ 60 and mass deviation ≤ 10 ppm.

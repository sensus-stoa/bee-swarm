Study Title	Engineered Bacillus subtilis WB600/ZD reduces post-weaning diarrhea in piglets by modulating gut microbiota and aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling
Study Description	<p>Post-weaning diarrhea (PWD) causes significant economic losses to the pig farming industry. Our previous study demonstrated that the engineered Bacillus subtilis WB600/ZD (an engineered Bacillus subtilis strain expressing the antimicrobial defensin peptide from Zophobas atratus), alleviates intestinal inflammation, modulates gut microbiota, and maintains redox homeostasis in Salmonella-challenged mice, yet the precise mechanisms remain unclear. This study suggests that WB600/ZD has great potential as an antibiotic alternative for improving the intestinal health in newly weaned piglets.</p><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Engineered Bacillus subtilis WB600/ZD; Post-weaning diarrhea; Salmonella Infantis; Fecal microbiota transplantation; Ileitis; Aryl hydrocarbon receptor</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The 30 healthy piglets were further equally divided into two groups: the healthy group fed a basal diet (n = 15) and the healthy + WB600/ZD group (H + WB600/ZD) was gavaged with 2 × 109 CFU/mL WB600/ZD while being fed the same basal diet (n = 15). For the 20 diarrheal piglets were equally divided into two groups the post-weaning diarrhea group (PWD) fed a basal diet (n = 10) and the PWD + WB600/ZD group was gavaged with 2 × 109 CFU/mL WB600/ZD while being fed the same basal diet (n = 10). These piglets did not receive any antibiotic treatment.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Weigh&nbsp;100 mg&nbsp;of solid sample into a&nbsp;2 mL centrifuge tube&nbsp;and add one&nbsp;6 mm diameter grinding bead.</p><p>Add&nbsp;800 μL of extraction solvent&nbsp;(methanol:water&nbsp;= 4:1, v/v) containing&nbsp;four internal standards&nbsp;(including&nbsp;L-2-chlorophenylalanine, 0.02 mg/mL).</p><p>Homogenize samples in a&nbsp;cryogenic tissue grinder&nbsp;for&nbsp;6 min&nbsp;(-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by&nbsp;low-temperature ultrasonic extraction&nbsp;for&nbsp;30 min&nbsp;(5°C, 40 kHz).</p><p>Incubate samples at&nbsp;-20°C for 30 min, then centrifuge at&nbsp;13,000 × g for 15 min&nbsp;(4°C).</p><p>Transfer the&nbsp;supernatant&nbsp;to an&nbsp;autosampler vial with insert&nbsp;for&nbsp;LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>A 3 μL aliquot of the sample was separated on an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm) prior to mass spectrometric detection. The mobile phase system consisted of:</p><p>Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v)</p><p>Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v)</p><p>The separation was performed at a flow rate of 0.40 mL/min with the column temperature maintained at 40°C.</p><p>Key translation notes:</p><p>Maintained scientific notation (μL, mm, μm)</p><p>Used standard chromatography terminology (e.g., 'mobile phase' instead of 'flowing phase')</p><p>Kept consistent volume ratio formatting (v/v)</p><p>Specified 'prior to mass spectrometric detection' for clarity</p><p>Structured the description in proper analytical chemistry style</p>	<p>Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled with Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X), Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA</p><p>HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm), Waters Corporation, USA</p><p>JXDC-20 nitrogen blowing instrument, Shanghai Jingxin Industrial Development Co., Ltd., China</p><p>LNG-T88 benchtop rapid centrifugal concentrator, Taicang Huamei Biochemical Instrument Factory, China</p><p>Wonbio-96c high-throughput tissue disruptor, Shanghai Wonbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China</p><p>SBL-10DT ultrasonic cleaner (300W-10L), Ningbo Scientz Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China</p><p>Centrifuge 5430R high-speed refrigerated centrifuge, Eppendorf, Germany</p><p>NewClassic MF MS105DU electronic balance, Mettler Toledo, Switzerland</p>	<p>1. MS and MS/MS spectra were matched against:</p><p>HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/)</p><p>Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/)</p><p>Majorbio's in-house database</p><p>for metabolite identification.</p><p>2. Data Preprocessing:</p><p>The annotated data matrix was uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform (cloud.majorbio.com) for analysis. Preprocessing steps included:</p><p>Missing value handling using the 80% rule (retaining variables with non-zero values in ≥80% of samples in at least one group)</p><p>Gap filling with minimum observed values</p><p>Sum normalization to correct for technical variations</p><p>Removal of variables with RSD &gt;30% in QC samples</p><p>log10 transformation</p><p>yielding the final normalized matrix for downstream analysis.</p><p>3.

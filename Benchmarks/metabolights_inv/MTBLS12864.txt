Study Title	Multi-omics analysis reveals ToMMV-induced regulation of amino acid and phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathways affecting tomato fruit quality and resistance
Study Description	<p>Tomato mottle mosaic virus (ToMMV) is a major threat to cultivating tomatoes and other solanaceous plants. Here, an infectious&nbsp;clone of the ToMMV Huludao isolate&nbsp;was constructed, and ToMMV&nbsp;infection&nbsp;significantly&nbsp;reduces soluble sugar,&nbsp;soluble phenolic and vitamin C contents, while increases&nbsp;titratable acid content&nbsp;in Micro-Tom fruits, thus altering their&nbsp;flavor profile&nbsp;and quality. Integrated&nbsp;transcriptomic&nbsp;and metabolomic analysis&nbsp;indicate that ToMMV infection induced 2090 differentially expressed genes and 689 differentially accumulated metabolites in Micro-Tom fruits.&nbsp;Then, functional analysis using TRV-VIGS validated that <em>DHQ-SDH</em>&nbsp;and <em>GAPDH</em>&nbsp;induced by ToMMV in the amino acid biosynthesis pathway play critical roles in tomato fruit quality, while <em>4CL</em>, <em>PAL</em>&nbsp;and <em>SGT</em>&nbsp;in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis&nbsp;pathway were required for host resistance. Our results provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying ToMMV-induced alterations in fruit quality and highlight potential metabolic targets for improving virus resistance in tomatoes.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p _msttexthash='3982797' _msthash='343'>BMK240604-CB595-S01-001Q0001-01, ToMMV-HLD infected tomato fruits, 7 dpi. (Treatment)</p><p _msttexthash='2497378' _msthash='343'>BMK240604-CB595-S01-001Q0002-01, tomato fruits (ck), 7 dpi. (Control)</p>	<p>ToMMV-HLD inoculated Micro-Tom&nbsp;fruits were collected at 7 days post inoculation (dpi)&nbsp;and freeze-dried at -80 °C.&nbsp;A total of 1000 μL extraction solution containing the internal standard (methanol:acetonitrile:water, 2:2:1, internal standard concentration 20 mg/mL) was added to the 50 mg sample, swirled and mixed for 30 s, and ultrasonic was performed in the ice water bath for 10 min (45Hz). The samples were left to stand for 1 h at -20 °C, and then were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. Then 500 μL of supernatant was carefully removed to&nbsp;an EP tube, and the extract was dried in a vacuum concentrator. Next 160 μL of extract (acetonitrile:water, 1:1) was added to the dried metabolites, redissolved, vortexed for 30 s and sonicated in the ice water bath for 10 min.&nbsp;The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and 120 μL supernatant was carefully taken out in a 2 mL injection bottle. Each sample was mixed with 10 μL to form QC samples for machine testing.</p>	<p>The LC-MS system for metabolomics analysis is composed of Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-high performance liquid tandem Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high resolution mass spectrometer. The column used is purchased from Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 um 2.1 x 100 mm).</p><p>Positive ion mode: mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution; mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile.</p><p>Negative ion mode: mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution; mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile.</p><p>Injection volume 2 μL.</p>	<p>Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF high resolution mass spectrometer can collect primary and secondary mass spectrometry data in MSe mode under the control of the acquisition software (MassLynx V4.2, Waters). In each data acquisition cycle, dual-channel data acquisition can be performed on both low collision energy and high collision energy at the same time. The low collision energy is off, the high collision energy range is 10-40 V, and the scanning frequency is 0.2 s for a mass spectrum. The parameters of the ESI ion source are as follows: Capillary voltage: 2500 V (positive ion mode) or -2000 V (negative ion mode); cone voltage: 30 V; ion source temperature: 100 °C; desolvent gas temperature 500 °C; backflush gas flow rate: 50 L/h; Desolventizing gas flow rate: 800 L/h.</p>	<p>The raw data collected using MassLynx v4.2 is processed by Progenesis QI software for peak extraction, peak alignment and other data processing operations, based on the Progenesis QI software online METLIN database and Biomark’s self-built library for identification.</p>	<p>After normalizing the original peak area information with the total peak area, the follow-up analysis was performed. Principal component analysis and Spearman correlation analysis were used to judge the repeatability of the samples within group and the quality control samples. The identified compounds are searched for classification and pathway information in KEGG, HMDB and lipidmaps databases. According to the grouping information, calculate and compare the difference multiples, <em>T</em> test was used to calculate the difference significance P value of each compound. The R language package ropls was used to perform OPLS-DA modeling and 200 times permutation tests was performed to verify the reliability of the model. The VIP value of the model was calculated using multiple cross-validation. The method of combining the difference multiple, the <em>P</em> value and the VIP value of the OPLS-DA model was adopted to screen the differential metabolites. The screening criteria are FC &gt; 1, <em>P</em> value &lt; 0.05 and VIP &gt; 1. The difference metabolites of KEGG pathway enrichment significance were calculated using hypergeometric distribution test.</p>

Study Title	Exploring the Influence of Psychological Factors on the Comorbidity of Dental Caries and Obesity in Adolescents from the Perspective of the Oral-Gut-Brain Axis
Study Description	<p>This study explores the impact of psychological factors on the comorbidity of dental caries and obesity in adolescents through the oral-gut-brain axis. An epidemiological survey was conducted on 1,024 students from Beizhen No. 1 Junior High School, with 90 adolescents aged 12–15 included in biosample research. Methods such as 16S rRNA gene sequencing, untargeted metabolomics, and SourceTracker analysis were used. Results showed significant differences in oral and gut microbiota and metabolite concentrations among adolescents with different health statuses, highlighting the roles of the oral-gut and oral-brain axes in disease progression. This study provides new insights into the mechanisms underlying the comorbidity of dental caries and obesity in adolescents and offers theoretical foundations for prevention and treatment strategies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study explores the impact of psychological factors on the comorbidity of dental caries and obesity in adolescents through the oral-gut-brain axis. An epidemiological survey was conducted on 1,024 students from Beizhen No. 1 Junior High School, with 90 adolescents aged 12–15 included in biosample research. </p>	<p>Feces:</p><p>1. Weigh 50 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>2. Add 500&nbsp;μL of pre-cooled methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine), add 2 steel beads, and</p><p>vortex for 30 s;</p><p>3. Place in a high-throughput tissue grinder, 55 Hz, grind for 60 s. Repeat this step once; 4. Place in ultrasonic cleaning machine for 10 min;</p><p>5. Freeze in a -20°C&nbsp;refrigerator for 30 min;</p><p>6. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22&nbsp;μm filter membrane, and add the filtrate to the test bottle;</p><p>7. Take 10-20&nbsp;μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample to evaluate instrument stability and data reliability.</p><p><br></p><p>saliva:</p><p>1. Pipette 100&nbsp;μL of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>2. Add 400&nbsp;μL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) and vortex for 30 s;</p><p>3. Freeze in a -20°C&nbsp;refrigerator for 30 min;</p><p>4. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, take 400&nbsp;μL of the supernatant and concentrate to dryness</p><p>under vacuum;</p><p>5. Add 150&nbsp;μL 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine) to dissolve and vortex for 30</p><p>seconds;</p><p>6. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22&nbsp;μm filter membrane, and add the filtrate to the test bottle;</p><p>7. Take 10-20&nbsp;μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample to evaluate instrument stability and data reliability.</p>	<p>An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100A, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min, a column temperature of 40°C, an autosampler temperature of 8°C, and an injection volume of 2 μL.</p>	<p>Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source, spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325°C, auxiliary gas temperature 300°C, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT 100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s, secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto.</p><p>All formal samples and QC sample were loaded into the equipment according to the above-mentioned chromatography and mass spectrometry methods. Before the formal injection, QC sample were injected 2-4 times to balance the system. During the injection process, one QC sample was injected for every 5-10 samples for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p>The .raw format data was imported into the commercial software Compound DiscovererTM 3.3 (version 3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithm of the software, peak extraction, alignment, correction and other operations were performed. The unique peak quality rating calculation and filter greatly reduced the interference of background peaks and low-quality peaks. The peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the missing values of the undetected peaks were filled based on the software Fill Gaps algorithm, and the Sum total peak area was normalized.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The identification of metabolites was based on self-built library PSNGM Database, mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/) and NIST_2020_MSMS spectral library. The MS1 mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>

Study Title	Integrated transcriptome and metabolome dynamics with phenotypic traits reveal regulatory basis of male morphotype differentiation in giant freshwater prawn Macrobrachium rosenbergii
Study Description	<p>This integrated multi-omics resource delineates the molecular and phenotypic trajectories underlying male morphotype differentiation (Blue Claw [BC], Orange Claw [OC], Small Male [SM]) in <em>macrobrachium rosenbergii</em>&nbsp;during determinative developmental stages (100, 110, and 120 days post-stocking). The dataset comprises paired testicular transcriptomic profiles, hemolymph serum metabolomes, and quantitative morphological trait data (15 key metrics), thereby establishing a holistic framework capturing concurrent gene expression dynamics, metabolic flux alterations, and phenotypic manifestations throughout morphotype specification. The synergistic integration of these multidimensional layers enables mechanistic dissection of regulatory networks governing crustacean growth polymorphism, facilitates identification of heritable biomarkers associated with commercially advantageous morphotypes, and provides foundational insights into arthropod phenotypic plasticity.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male <em _mstvisible='13'>Macrobrachium rosenbergii</em>&nbsp;specimens were obtained from a single-family population at Jiangsu Shufeng Aquatic Seed Industry Co., Ltd. (Gaoyou, China). Individuals were categorized into three morphotypes—Blue Claw (BC), Orange Claw (OC), and Small Male (SM)—based on chelae coloration, spine morphology, behavior, and growth traits at 100, 110, and 120 days post-stocking (dps). Sample sizes comprised 18 BC, 18 OC, and 30 SM individuals, with the larger SM cohort size compensating for their reduced body mass to obtain sufficient biological material for analysis. Cohorts were reared in a 667 m² pond at 28–32°C and pH 7.5–8, fed twice daily with a specialized probiotic-free diet (Jiangsu Fuyuda Grain Products Co., Ltd., Gaoyou, China). Phenotypic traits (e.g., total weight, body length, chelae metrics) were measured using electronic balances (0.01 g precision) and digital calipers (0.01 mm precision) but were solely utilized as biological background for sample stratification, not as independent data groups. Hemolymph was extracted from the same individuals using sterile syringes, all samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 100 μL serum with 400 μL acetonitrile:methanol (1:1) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine (internal standard). After vortexing (30 s), sonication (30 min, 5°C), and centrifugation (13,000 g, 15 min, 4°C), supernatants were dried under nitrogen and reconstituted in 100 μL acetonitrile:water (1:1). Quality control (QC) samples were pooled from all extracts.</p>	<p> Metabolites were separated on an HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters) using a UHPLC-Q Exactive system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Mobile phases were: (A) 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95:5); (B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol (47.5:47.5). Gradient elution: 0–13.1 min, 0–100% B; 13.1–16 min, 0% B. MS detection used positive/negative ionization modes (70–1050 m/z), spray voltage 3500 V (+) / 2800 V (−), and resolution 70,000 (MS1)/17,500 (MS2). </p>	<p>The mass spectrometry (MS) signals were acquired using both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scanning range of&nbsp;m/z 70-1050. The ion spray voltages were set to&nbsp;3500 V (positive ion mode)&nbsp;and&nbsp;2800 V (negative ion mode). Other parameters were configured as follows: sheath gas at 40 psi, auxiliary heating gas at 10 psi, ion source heating temperature at 400°C, cyclic collision energy of 20-40-60 V, MS1 resolution at 70,000, and MS2 resolution at 17,500.</p>	<p> Raw data were processed in Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with HMDB (www.hmdb.ca) and MetLin (metlin.scripps.edu) databases. Peak alignment, missing value imputation (minimum value), and QC-based filtering (RSD &lt; 30%) preceded log10 transformation.</p>	<p>Differential analysis was performed on preprocessed matrix files. Using R package ropls (v1.6.2), PCA and OPLS-DA were conducted with 7-cycle cross-validation for model stability. Student’s t-test and fold change analysis were also performed. Differential metabolites were selected based on OPLS-DA-derived VIP values and t-test p-values (VIP &gt; 1, p &lt; 0.05). These were annotated via KEGG (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/pathway.html) for involved pathways. Python packages were used for pathway enrichment analysis, with Fisher’s exact test identifying key biological pathways linked to experimental treatment.</p>

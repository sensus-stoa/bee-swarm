Study Title	Metabolomics Analysis of Limoniastrum monopetalum Extract
Study Description	<p>Limoniastrum monopetalum is a halophytic plant traditionally used in folk medicine for its purported antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory properties. However, its chemical composition and bioactive constituents remain insufficiently characterized. In this study, we employed an untargeted metabolomics approach using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) to profile the phytochemical landscape of L. monopetalum extract. Data preprocessing and multivariate statistical analyses revealed a diverse metabolic fingerprint consisting of phenolic acids, flavonoids, tannins, and other secondary metabolites. Several compounds with known pharmacological relevance, including gallic acid derivatives, catechins, and flavonol glycosides, were identified and annotated against public metabolite databases. Pathway enrichment analysis further suggested the involvement of phenylpropanoid, flavonoid, and galactose metabolism, highlighting potential mechanisms underlying the plant’s bioactivities. Collectively, these findings provide the first comprehensive metabolomic characterization of L. monopetalum extract, offering a valuable resource for future pharmacological, nutraceutical, and biotechnological applications.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fresh aerial parts (leaves and stems) of Limoniastrum monopetalum were collected from [specify location, e.g., coastal saline habitats in Egypt. Collected samples were rinsed thoroughly with distilled water to remove dust and debris, shade-dried at room temperature (25 ± 2 °C) for 7–10 days, and subsequently ground into a fine powder using a laboratory mill. The powdered plant material was stored in airtight containers at –20 °C until extraction.</p>	<p>For metabolomics analysis, 25–50 mg of sample (plant, fungi, or bacterial material) was weighed, ensuring that the weight did not exceed 50 mg. The extraction was performed using a reconstitution buffer added at a ratio of 1000 µL per 50 mg of sample. Samples were initially vortexed for 2 minutes and shaken for an additional 2 minutes to ensure homogenization, followed by sonication for 20 minutes (80 kHz, 100 W) at ambient temperature while maintaining the temperature below 30 °C. Afterward, samples were shaken again for 5 minutes. From each extract, 50 µL was taken and diluted with 950 µL of reconstitution buffer, then subjected to a second round of sonication for 20 minutes (80 kHz, 30 °C). The resulting mixture was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the clear supernatant was carefully transferred to a fresh Eppendorf tube. Finally, 300 µL of the supernatant was aliquoted into LC vials, making the samples ready for LC–MS analysis</p>	<p>The metabolomics analysis of Limoniastrum monopetalum extract was conducted following previously established methods. The study utilized an ExionLC™ AC UHPLC system (AB SCIEX, Concord, Canada) equipped with an Acquity XSelect HSS T3 analytical column (2.1 × 150 mm, 2.5 µm; Waters Co, Milford, US), maintained at 40 °C. This setup was coupled with a Triple TOF 5600+ mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, Concord, Canada) for ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography high-resolution mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS) analysis.</p><p>For chromatographic separation, 10 µL of each sample was injected and analyzed over 35 minutes using a gradient elution at a constant flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of three solutions: Solution A: 5 mM ammonium formate in 1% methanol (pH 3.0) for positive mode elution. Solution B: 5 mM ammonium formate in 1% methanol (pH 8.0) for negative mode elution. Solution C: 100% acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was structured as follows: 0% C for the first 1.0 minute, increasing to 90% C over 20 minutes, held at 90% for 4 minutes, returning to 0% C within 1 minute, and finishing with 3 minutes of re-equilibration at 0% C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed in both positive-ion (ESI+) and negative-ion (ESI−) modes using a DuoSpray™ ion source [3].</p><p>For feature detection, Information-Dependent Acquisition (IDA) with dynamic background subtraction was employed. The TOF-MS scan covered a mass range of 50–1000 Da, with an accumulation time of 30 ms. The 15 most intense precursor ions (50–1000 Da) were selected for MS/MS analysis using a 50 Da fixed transition window. MS/MS acquisition was performed with an accumulation time of 50 ms per scan, and collision energies of 35 V and -35 V for positive and negative modes, respectively. The total cycle time was 0.6502 sec, and Analyst TF (v 1.7.1) software was used for data acquisition [4].</p>	<p>No data transformation. No data transformation. </p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using MS-DIAL 4.9.2. The Global Natural Product Social Molecular Networking (GNPS) database and RIKEN MSn spectral database for phytochemicals (ReSpect) were used as reference databases for the search.&nbsp;Manual validation, using PeakView 2.2 with MasterView 1.1 (AB SCIEX), was conducted to increase confidence in metabolite identifications at both the parent and fragment ion levels.&nbsp;The search criteria included a precursor ion XIC signal-to-noise ratio greater than 10, a sample-to-blank ratio greater than 5, and a precursor mass tolerance of 10 ppm.</p>

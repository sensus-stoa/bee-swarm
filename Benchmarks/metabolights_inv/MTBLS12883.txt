Study Title	Impaired Glycolysis-Derived Serine Metabolism as a Key Driver of Podocyte Injury with Senescence
Study Description	<p>Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a major health issue, with podocyte injury with senescence playing a central role in glomerulosclerosis. This study investigates the link between glycolysis-derived serine metabolism and podocyte injury with senescence, focusing on the role of phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK1) in the regulation of L-serine synthesis and podocyte homeostasis. Using in vivo and in vitro models, we examined the effects of angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced metabolic dysregulation on serine metabolism and its impact on podocyte function. The results demonstrate that Ang II downregulates PGK1 expression through the transcription factor FOXA1, leading to reduced L-serine biosynthesis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and increased cellular senescence in podocytes. Supplementing with L-serine or enhancing PGK1 expression in podocytes alleviated these pathological changes, restored mitochondrial function, and reduced senescence-associated phenotypes in CKD mouse models. Moreover, PGK1 was found to interact with keratin, type II cytoskeletal 1 (KRT1), stabilizing the cytoskeletal integrity of podocytes. These findings identify a novel metabolic pathway linking glycolysis, serine metabolism, and podocyte injury with senescence, suggesting that targeting the PGK1-serine axis may offer therapeutic potential for slowing podocyte senescence and CKD progression.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples were collected from three biological replicates of MPC-5 podocyte cell lines, which were cultured under standard conditions. To induce podocyte injury, cells were treated with Ang II (angiotensin II) for a defined period, and then supplemented with L-serine to investigate its protective effects. The samples were harvested at specific time points after treatment to analyze the metabolic changes induced by Ang II and the effects of L-serine supplementation. All cell samples were processed using standardized protocols and stored at -80°C to ensure the stability and preservation of metabolites for subsequent mass spectrometry and metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>The metabolite extraction was carried out using a methanol-chloroform extraction method, which is effective for isolating both polar and non-polar metabolites. Initially, cells were lysed in methanol to precipitate proteins, followed by phase separation using chloroform. The organic phase was carefully collected, dried under nitrogen, and reconstituted in appropriate solvents, such as methanol or acetonitrile, for further analysis. This extraction process enabled the efficient recovery of key metabolites, including glycolytic intermediates, serine, sphingolipids, and other lipids, which were essential to understanding the metabolic dysregulation in podocytes under Ang II-induced stress.</p>	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed using a reverse-phase liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) column, which allows effective separation of both polar and non-polar metabolites. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (water with 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid), and a gradient elution method was applied to achieve optimal separation of metabolites. The gradient was adjusted to improve the resolution of metabolites such as serine, phosphoglycerate (3-PG), deoxysphingolipids, and other glycolytic intermediates. The chromatographic conditions were optimized to enhance the separation of these metabolites, ensuring high-quality data for downstream mass spectrometry analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite analysis was performed using a Q-TOF (quadrupole time-of-flight) mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source, which is ideal for detecting both small and large molecules in complex biological samples. The analysis was conducted in both positive and negative ionization modes to optimize the detection of various metabolites, including glycolytic intermediates, serine, and lipids. Full-scan mode was used to acquire a broad range of data, covering the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) range from 50 to 1200 Da. This broad range allowed for the comprehensive detection of low- and high-abundance metabolites, providing a detailed metabolomic profile of the podocyte cells and the effects of Ang II and L-serine treatment.</p>	<p>The raw data from the mass spectrometry (MS) analysis were processed using the vendor's software, including peak detection, alignment, and intensity normalization. The data were then subjected to transformation steps such as logarithmic transformation and autoscaling to ensure comparability across samples. Metabolites were quantified by integrating peak areas, followed by statistical normalization to the internal standards used during the extraction process. This ensured that the relative abundances of metabolites were accurately represented, allowing for reliable comparisons between different treatment groups. We used software like XCMS and MetaboAnalyst for further data processing and statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching the detected m/z values and retention times with those from known databases such as HMDB, KEGG, and METLIN. The high-resolution MS data were further verified using tandem MS (MS/MS) spectra to confirm the identity of metabolites based on fragmentation patterns. To improve identification accuracy, we cross-referenced our results with literature reports on known metabolites related to serine metabolism and sphingolipid pathways. In cases where unknown metabolites were detected, additional spectral data were utilized for tentative identification. Software such as MassHunter and MetaboScape was used for data analysis and metabolite identification.</p>

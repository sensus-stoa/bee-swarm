Study Title	Lacto-N-tetraose mediates indole-3-lactic acid production by Bifidobacterium longum subsp. infantis to alleviate enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli-induced diarrhea in infants
Study Description	<p>Enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli (ETEC) is a leading cause of infectious diarrhea and poses a threat to infant health. Current treatments for ETEC infection primarily rely on antibiotics，which face limitations due to resistance and side effect. In this cross-sectional study, we analyzed fecal metabolic profiles in breastfed infants with and without diarrhea. We found a significant decrease in indole-3-lactic acid (ILA) levels in infants with diarrhea.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Participants for the study were recruited in Harbin, China. The aim of the study was to investigate the differences in consumption and metabolism of HMOs in breast milk between diarrhea and healthy infants, as well as the interaction of HMOs with the infant gut microbiota.&nbsp;92 fecal samples were collected for metabolomics analysis.Feces were collected from the infant's most recent bowel movement after human milk collection.</p>	<p> Metabolite extraction. Metabolites were extracted using 1000 μL acetonitrile: methanol: water (2:2:1,&nbsp;v/v) in a 50 mg sample. After vortexing for 30 s, 20 μL of internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine, 0.3 mg/mL, methanol configuration) was added.&nbsp;The extract was ultrasonicated in an ice bath for 10 min and allowed to stand at -20℃ for 2 h. The extract was then centrifuged at 14000g for 20 min. The supernatant was collected by centrifugation at 14,000g for 20 min and subsequently passed through a 96-well protein precipitation plate and dried in a vacuum centrifuge at 4℃. The sample was then dried in a vacuum centrifuge at 4℃. The samples were redissolved in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1,&nbsp;v/v) solvent and transferred to LC vials for LC- MS analysis. 50 μL of each sample was mixed to make QC, and one QC sample was inserted for every 10 samples analyzed for the assay.</p>	<p>The samples of intestinal contents were analyzed using UHPLC-Q/TOF equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm*2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, UK) coupled to a mass spectrometer system (Xevo G2-XS QTOF, Waters, UK).&nbsp;The column temperature was 50℃ and the flow rate was 0.4 ml/min. The metabolites were eluted by gradient elution using mobile phases A and B, where mobile phase A was water and 0.1% formic acid and mobile phase B was methanol and 0.1% formic acid</p>	<p>For small molecules eluted from the column, the positive and negative ion modes were acquired using high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry Xevo G2-XS QTOF, respectively.&nbsp;</p>	<p> The acquired peaks were analyzed using Progenesis QI (version 2.2) software, including peak pairing, peak extraction, normalization, inverse convolution and compound identification.</p>	<p> The acquired peaks were analyzed using Progenesis QI (version 2.2) software, including peak pairing, peak extraction, normalization, inverse convolution and compound identification.</p>

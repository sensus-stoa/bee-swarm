Study Title	Combined Mesenchymal Stem Cells and Metformin Therapy Modulates Key Macromolecular Pathways in Pulmonary Fibrosis: Evidence from Untargeted Metabolomics
Study Description	<p>Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive and fatal interstitial lung disease. Current treatment options remain limited, driving interest in novel therapeutic approaches. Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) possess immunomodulatory and regenerative properties, while metformin, traditionally used for type 2 diabetes, has recently demonstrated anti-fibrotic potential through its effects on cellular metabolism. Metabolomic profiling provides a comprehensive strategy to explore disease-associated biochemical alterations and therapeutic mechanisms. In this pilot study, pulmonary fibrosis was induced in rats using bleomycin. Animals were then treated with MSCs, metformin, or both. Plasma and lung tissue underwent untargeted LC-MS-based metabolomic analysis, supported by histopathological examination of lung architecture. Combination therapy resulted in the most substantial correction of metabolite disturbances, notable improvements observed in the levels of argininosuccinic acid, lysophosphatidylcholine (18:0/0:0), cytidine diphosphate, 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid, and chenodeoxycholic acid, reflecting restored to nitrogen balance, lipid metabolism, nucleotide biosynthesis, serotonin turnover, and bile acid signaling. PCA showed distinct clustering of the combination group near controls, while histology confirmed nearcomplete resolution of fibrosis. These findings provide preliminary evidence that combined MSCs and metformin therapy exerts synergistic effects on fibrotic and metabolic pathways. Identified metabolite alterations may serve as potential early biomarkers for disease progression and therapeutic response</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study consisted of two primary phases: an induction phase and a therapeutic phase.</p><p>Induction Phase (59 days): Pulmonary fibrosis was induced using bleomycin. Prior to induction, baseline serum samples were collected from 10 rats and stored at -80°C for subsequent metabolomic analysis (Control Group reference).</p><p><strong>Therapeutic Phase (40 days):</strong> Following the induction phase, rats were allocated to treatment groups. Treatments included metformin (65 mg/kg body weight, administered orally every 48 hours), MSCs (a single dose of 1 million cells), or a combination of both. Serum samples were collected post-treatment for metabolomic analysis.</p><p> </p><p> </p>	<p>The extraction protocol for plasma and lung tissue metabolites adhered to established procedures [1-3] to ensure high-quality samples for LC-MS analysis.</p><p>Tissue Samples: Lung tissue was homogenized in a 1:8 (w/v) ratio with pre-cooled protein precipitation buffer (chloroform:methanol:MilliQ water, 1:3:1).</p><p>Plasma Samples: Plasma samples were thawed and briefly vortexed. Plasma was then mixed with the protein precipitation buffer in a 1:8 (v/v) ratio and homogenized.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Processing:</strong> Both tissue and plasma homogenates underwent sonication (Elma Elmasonic Ultrasonic cleaning unit P, Elma, Germany) for 20 minutes at 80 kHz and 100% power (ambient temperature). The resulting supernatant was collected and evaporated using a speed vacuum (Concentrator plus, Eppendorf, Germany) for three hours (ambient temperature). The dried residue was re-suspended in reconstitution buffer (H2O:methanol:ACN, 2:1:1) at a volume proportional to the initial tissue weight or plasma volume. Following centrifugation (10,000 rpm, 10 min, 4°C; Tomy MX-207, Tomy Kogyo Co., Ltd, Japan), the final supernatant was transferred to LC vials for analysis.</p><p> </p><p> </p>	<p>Untargeted metabolomic analysis was performed using LC-MS/MS in both positive and negative ionization modes with Information Dependent Acquisition (IDA) [4]. An ExionLC™ AC UHPLC system (AB SCIEX, Concord, Canada) was coupled to a Triple TOF 5600+ mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, Concord, Canada). Chromatographic separation utilized an Acquity XSelect HSS T3 analytical column (2.1 × 150 mm, 2.5 µm; Waters Co, Milford, US).</p><p>Chromatography: A 10 µL sample injection volume was used. Separation occurred over 35 minutes at a constant flow rate of 0.3 mL/min via gradient elution. Mobile phase A was 5 mM ammonium formate in 1% methanol (pH 3.0, positive mode), mobile phase B was 5 mM ammonium formate in 1% methanol (pH 8.0, negative mode), and mobile phase C was 100% acetonitrile. The gradient profile was: 0% C (1 min), 0-90% C (linear gradient, 20 min), 90% C (4 min), 90-0% C (linear gradient, 1 min), and 0% C re-equilibration (3 min) [5, 6].</p>	<p>Mass Spectrometry: Data acquisition employed a DuoSpray™ ion source in both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI-) modes. IDA with dynamic background subtraction facilitated metabolite detection. A TOF-MS survey scan (m/z 50–1000) was acquired in 30 ms, followed by MS/MS scans on the 15 most intense precursor ions (m/z 50–1000) using a fixed 50 Da transition window. MS/MS acquisition time was 50 ms per spectrum, with collision energies of 35 V (ESI+) and -35 V (ESI-). The total cycle time was 0.6502 seconds. Analyst TF software (v 1.7.1) controlled data acquisition [5].</p>	<p>Data pre-processing, statistical analyses, and visualization were performed using R (version 4.2.2) [11]. Metabolite abundance data, generated by MS-DIAL and compiled into a feature table, underwent the following steps:</p><p>Normalization: Probabilistic Quotient Normalization (PQN) was applied across all samples before filtering [9].</p><p>Filtering: Features with missing values in any sample within a specific group were excluded. Duplicate features were removed, retaining the one with the highest intensity.</p><p>Imputation: Missing values were imputed using random values drawn from a range within ±10% of the group median [12, 13].</p><p>Scaling: Data were auto-scaled prior to statistical analysis [14].</p>	<p>Metabolite identification utilized MS-DIAL 4.9.2 and the custom in-house database [9]. Manual validation using PeakView 2.2 with MasterView 1.1 (AB SCIEX) increased confidence in annotations at both precursor and fragment ion levels [10]. Annotation criteria included a precursor ion extracted ion chromatogram (XIC) signal-to-noise ratio &gt; 10, a sample-to-blank intensity ratio &gt; 5, and a precursor mass tolerance of 10 ppm. Annotated data were subsequently used for pre-processing and downstream statistical analyses [5].</p>

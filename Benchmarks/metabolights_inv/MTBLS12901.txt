Study Identifier	MTBLS12901
Study Title	Mitochondrial Transplantation of Brown Adipocytes Interferes with the Metabolism of Tumor Stem Cells and Inhibits the Recurrence of Brain Glioma after Operation
Study Description	<p>Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) is highly aggressive, with treatment resistance and recurrence driven by metabolically plastic glioma stem cells (GSCs). Inspired by brown adipose tissue (BAT) thermogenesis, researchers transplanted BAT-derived mitochondria (BA-Mito) into GSCs, reducing stemness markers and enhancing chemosensitivity to temozolomide (TMZ). To improve delivery, a CD133 aptamer-modified liposome-coated mitochondrial system (Apt/Lipo-Mito Gel) was developed, using alginate hydrogel as a post-surgical reservoir for sustained mitochondrial release, offering a novel metabolic therapy strategy for GBM.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples included glioma stem cells (GSCs) and brown adipocyte mitochondria (BA-Mito). GSCs were isolated from GL261 mouse glioma cells using serum-free culture supplemented with bFGF, EGF, and B27, forming suspended 3D spheres (~100 μm in diameter) and validated by stemness markers (CD133, Nanog, Nestin, SOX2). BA-Mito was extracted from differentiated HIB1B mouse preadipocytes induced by insulin, dexamethasone, and IBMX. Samples were collected via centrifugation (1000 rpm, 5 min), washed with PBS, and stored at -80°C after liquid nitrogen freezing. Control samples included untreated GL261 cells and CCCP-treated mitochondria (as a positive control for inactivity).</p>	<p>BA-Mito was isolated using gradient centrifugation: cells were homogenized in ice-cold buffer, centrifuged at 1000 g (4°C, 10 min) to remove nuclei, and the supernatant was centrifuged at 3500 g (4°C, 10 min) to pellet mitochondria. Mitochondria were modified with chitosan (CS) by incubating with 1 mg/mL CS solution on ice for 5 min, followed by centrifugation at 10000 g (4°C, 10 min). Control samples included unmodified mitochondria, CCCP-treated mitochondria (for activity validation), and solvent blanks (PBS).</p>	<p>Liquid chromatography (LC) was used for untargeted metabolomics. Details included a reverse-phase column with a mobile phase consisting of aqueous and organic solvents (exact components not detailed). Gradient elution was applied, with parameters such as flow rate, column temperature, and injection volume optimized for metabolite separation.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry (MS) was coupled with LC for metabolomic analysis. The instrument utilized an electrospray ionization (ESI) source in positive/negative modes. The mass range (m/z) was set to cover small-molecule metabolites, with parameters including ion source temperature, voltage, and scan rate optimized for sensitivity. Mitochondrial complex activity (Complex I and II) was measured using commercial kits.</p>	<p>Raw transcriptomic data (from RNA sequencing) and metabolomic data (from LC-MS) were processed using bioinformatics pipelines. Transcriptome data were analyzed for differential gene expression using software (e.g., DESeq2), and metabolome data were normalized and transformed to quantify metabolite abundances. Statistical analyses (ANOVA, t-tests) were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by matching mass spectra and retention times against reference databases (e.g., KEGG). Transcriptome and metabolome data were integrated to enrich pathways, focusing on lipid metabolism (e.g., arachidonic acid metabolism). Software tools for pathway analysis (e.g., KEGG Mapper) and gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) were used to annotate metabolites and their biological functions.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS12925
Study Title	Metabolomics profiling of traditional bioformulations: calotropis ark, dashparni ark and neemboli ark
Study Description	<p> The growing shift toward sustainable agriculture has intensified interest in traditional bioformulations derived from botanicals and microbial consortia. This study presents an integrative metabolomic profiling of plant-based arks widely used in Indian natural farming practices: Calotropis Ark (CA), Dashparni Ark (DA), and Neemboli Ark (NA). Using untargeted metabolomics (GC-MS and LC-MS), distinct metabolite signatures across formulations were identified. GC-MS based identification revealed that in DA, NA, and CA most abundant class of compounds were organoheterocyclic compounds (22%), benzenoids (21%), and organoheterocyclic compounds (19%), respectively. GC-MS identified 44 core and over 180 unique metabolites across the formulations. Shared compounds in all bioformulations included benzoic acid, 1,3-propanediol, 2-pentenoic acid, D-glucitol, 2-propenoic acid, inositol, and 2-butenoic acid, etc. In LC-MS, the most abundant class of compounds was lipids and lipid-like molecules in NA (33%), DA (32%), and CA (28%), followed by organoheterocyclics and organic acids. LC-MS data showed 619 shared metabolites (e.g., jasmonic acid, benzothiazole, cinnamic acid, octanal, 17-octadecynoic acid, etc.). This will enhance the understanding of composition of such bioformulations in applications to the sustainable agricultural practices.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples of traditional plant-based bioformulations, calotropis ark, dashparni ark, and neemboli ark were collected from certified natural farming practitioners in Gujarat, India. All bioformulations were prepared following natural farming protocols with some modifications. For each bioformulation, samples were collected, transported on ice, stored at -20°C and processed in triplicate.</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>The 200 μl of each sample was mixed with 1000 μl of solvent (ethyl acetate and methanol (1:1)), vortexed for 3 min, and sonicated for 15 min. After centrifugation (3000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), supernatants were dried in a SpeedvacTM vacuum concentrator.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>Samples were derivatized using BSTFA (N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide) with 1% TMCS (Trimethylchlorosilane) at 70 °C for 1h.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>LC-MS</p><p>Samples for LC-MS were processed as described by (Krishnareddy et al., 2022). For each sample, 200 µl was mixed with 1000 µl of a multi-solvent (methanol: acetonitrile: water; 2:2:1), sonicated (20 min), centrifuged (12,000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 µm syringe filter. A diluted aliquot (450 µl acetonitrile + 50 µl filtered sample) was used for LC-MS.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Instrument : Clarus 680 GC</p><p>Instrument : Agilent 1290 Infinity II</p><p><br></p><p>Column GC: Elite-5MS</p><p>Column LC: Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (3.0×100 mm, 1.8 µm)&nbsp;</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>GC–MS was performed on a PerkinElmer Clarus 680 GC coupled with a Clarus SQ8C MS using an Elite-5 ms column. The oven temperature program set as 60 °C (2 min) followed by 300 °C (10 °C/min), and 300 °C for 6 min. A 1 µl sample was injected (1:50 split ratio) at 250 °C. MS scanned from 50–600 m/z between 5–32 min.</p>	<p>msconvert Proteowizard was used to convert raw files to .mzML files</p>	<p>Annotation of GC-MS metabolites was done using MS-DIAL with NIST2014 library</p><p><br></p><p>Annotation of LC-MS metabolites was done using XCMS online using inbuilt METLIN library</p>

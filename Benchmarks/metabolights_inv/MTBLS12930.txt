Study Protocol Description	<p>Sample collection using oral sampler after morning feeding</p>	<p>Pipette 100 μL of liquid sample into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, add 400 μL of extraction solution (acetonitrile:methanol = 1:1) containing 0.02 mg/mL internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine), and vortex mix for 30 seconds. Subsequently, perform ultrasonic extraction for 30 minutes under low-temperature conditions (5°C, 40 KHz), and let the sample stand at -20°C for 30 minutes. Centrifuge at 4°C and 13,000 g for 15 minutes, transfer the supernatant, and evaporate to dryness under nitrogen stream. Reconstitute with 100 μL of reconstitution solution (acetonitrile:water = 1:1), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 5 minutes (5°C, 40 KHz). Centrifuge at 4°C and 13,000 g for 15 minutes, transfer the supernatant, and evaporate to dryness under nitrogen stream. Reconstitute with 100 μL of reconstitution solution (acetonitrile:water = 1:1), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 5 minutes (5°C, 40 KHz). Centrifuge again at 4°C and 13,000 g for 10 minutes, and finally transfer the supernatant to an injection vial with an insert for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>The 10 μL sample was separated using an HSS T3 chromatographic column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) and subsequently introduced into the mass spectrometer for detection. Mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (95/5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B was composed of acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5/47.5/5, v/v/v) with 0.1% formic acid.</p><p>For positive ion mode, the elution gradient was programmed as follows: 0–3 min, mobile phase B increased from 0% to 20%; 3–4.5 min, increased from 20% to 35%; 4.5–5 min, increased from 35% to 100%; 5–6.3 min, maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, decreased from 100% to 0%; 6.4–8 min, maintained at 0%.</p><p>For negative ion mode, the gradient profile was: 0–1.5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0% to 5%; 1.5–2 min, increased from 5% to 10%; 2–4.5 min, increased from 10% to 30%; 4.5–5 min, increased from 30% to 100%; 5–6.3 min, maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, decreased from 100% to 0%; 6.4–8 min, maintained at 0%.</p><p>The flow rate was set at 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data acquisition was performed in both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scan range of 70–1050 m/z. The sheath gas flow rate was set at 50 psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate at 15 psi, and the auxiliary gas heater temperature at 400°C. The capillary temperature was maintained at 350°C. The ion spray voltage was configured at 3400 V for positive mode and -2800 V for negative mode. Normalized collision energy was applied using a stepped energy setting of 20–40–60 eV. Full MS resolution was set to 60,000, and MS/MS resolution to 15,000. Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode.</p>	<p>Following instrumental analysis, the raw LC-MS data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. This process generated a data matrix comprising retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. Simultaneously, MS and MS/MS spectra were matched against public metabolite databases HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as Meiji's in-house library, to obtain metabolite identification.</p>	<p>Following instrumental analysis, the raw LC-MS data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment.

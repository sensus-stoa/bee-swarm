Study Title	Gut microbiota dysbiosis and altered fecal metabolome in patients with age-related cataract
Study Description	<p>Age-related cataract (ARC) is a leading cause of vision loss whose underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Emerging evidence suggests the gut microbiota can influence ocular health via systemic metabolic and inflammatory pathways, yet the gut–eye connection in cataract has not been explored. We aimed to characterize gut microbial communities and fecal metabolic profiles in ARC patients.</p><p><br></p><p>Fecal samples from 30 ARC patients and 30 age- and sex-matched healthy controls were collected and analyzed. Gut microbiota composition was profiled by 16S rDNA gene sequencing, and untargeted fecal metabolomics was performed by liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS). Additionally, eight short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) were quantified by targeted gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS). Differences in microbial taxa and metabolites were identified using multivariate statistics (PCA, PLS-DA/OPLS-DA) and differential abundance testing (VIP&gt;1, p&lt;0.05 after FDR correction).</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Stool samples were collected from patients and healthy people who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria at their preferred locations. Samples were stored in a refrigerator at 4 ° C, delivered to our laboratory within 4 to 6 hours, then divided into sterile enzyme-free frozen tubes and immediately stored in a refrigerator at -80 ° C until use.</p>	<p>GC:25mg of fecal sample was weighed in a 2ml grinding tube and 500 μL of water (containing 0.5% phosphoric acid) was added. Samples were frozen and ground twice for 3 min (50HZ), followed by sonication for 10 min and centrifugation at 13000g for 15 min at 4 ° C. 200 μL of the supernatant solution was put into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, and then 200 μL of n-butanol solvent (containing 10 μg/mL of internal standard 2-ethylbutyric acid) was added for extraction. After vortexing for 10s, ultrasonication at low temperature for 10 min, centrifugation at 13000g at 4 ° C for 5 min, the supernatant solution was taken into the injection vial and put on the machine.</p><p><br></p><p>LCMS:Take 50 mg of solid sample in a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and add one grinding bead with a diameter of 6 mm. Add 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol: water = 4:1 (v/v)) containing 0.02 mg/mL of internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine) for metabolite extraction. The sample solution is ground in a frozen tissue grinding instrument for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz). The sample is allowed to stand at -20°C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4°C, 13,000 g), and the supernatant is transferred to an injection vial with an insert for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>Please upLCMS:The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was a Thermo Vanquish ultra-high performance liquid chromatograph in series with Q Exactive HF-X modular quadrupole Orbitrap mass spectrometer. A 3 μL sample was separated on an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) before entering the mass spectrometer for detection. Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water + 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), while mobile phase B consisted of 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid).</p><p>The gradient program for positive ion mode separation was as follows:</p><p>0-3 min, mobile phase B increased from 0% to 20%;</p><p>3-4.5 min, mobile phase B increased from 20% to 35%;</p><p>4.5-5 min, mobile phase B increased from 35% to 100%;</p><p>5-6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%;</p><p>6.3-6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100% to 0%;</p><p>6.4-8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%.</p><p>The gradient program for negative ion mode separation was as follows:</p><p>0-1.5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0% to 5%;</p><p>1.5-2 min, mobile phase B increased from 5% to 10%;</p><p>2-4.5 min, mobile phase B increased from 10% to 30%;</p><p>4.5-5 min, mobile phase B increased from 30% to 100%;</p><p>5-6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%;</p><p>6.3-6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100% to 0%;</p><p>6.4-8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%.</p><p>The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was set at 40°C.</p><p><br></p><p>GCMS:The Agilent 8890B-5977B gas chromatography-mass spectrometry instrument was used for GC-MS targeted metabolomics analysis of the samples.date this protocol description</p>	<p>GCMS:The Agilent 8890B-5977B gas chromatography-mass spectrometry instrument was used for GC-MS targeted metabolomics analysis of the samples.</p><p>Mass spectrometry conditions: electron impact ion source (EI), ion source temperature 230 ° C, quadrupole temperature 150 ° C, transmission line temperature 230 ° C, electron energy 70 eV. The scan mode was selected as ion scan mode (SIM).</p><p><br></p><p>LCMS:The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was a Thermo Vanquish ultra-high performance liquid chromatograph in series with Q Exactive HF-X modular quadrupole Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Mass spectrometry data acquisition was performed in both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scan range of 70–1050 m/z. The sheath gas flow rate was set to 50 psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate to 13 psi, and the auxiliary gas heater temperature to 425°C. The spray voltage was set to 3500 V for positive mode and –3500 V for negative mode. The ion transfer tube temperature was maintained at 325°C, and normalized collision energy was applied using stepped energies of 20, 40, and 60 V. The full MS resolution was 60,000, and the MS/MS resolution was 7,500. Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode.</p><p><br></p>	<p>GCMS:The default parameters of Masshunter quantitative software (Agilent, United States, version: v10.0.707.0) were used to automatically identify and score the ion fragments of the target SCfas, and the manual inspection was assisted.</p><p><br></p><p>LCMS:Following data acquisition, the raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for automated peak picking, alignment, and deconvolution. The resulting data matrix, containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity, was subsequently subjected to a rigorous preprocessing pipeline:</p><p>Missing Value Handling: Features with more than 20% missing values across all samples were removed using the 80% rule. The remaining missing values were imputed with the minimum value of the original data matrix.</p><p>Normalization: To minimize systematic errors from sample preparation and instrument variability, the peak intensity data were normalized using the sum normalization method.</p><p>Quality Control (QC) Filtering: Variables with a relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% in the QC samples were considered unreliable and were removed from the dataset.</p><p>Data Transformation: The normalized and filtered data matrix was finally log10-transformed to improve normality and homoscedasticity for subsequent statistical analysis.</p><p>This preprocessing pipeline yielded the final data matrix used for all multivariate and univariate analyses.</p>	<p>GCMS:The detection concentration of each sample was calculated by the standard curve, and the actual content of SCfas in the sample was calculated.</p><p><br></p><p>LCMS:Metabolite identification was performed by matching the acquired MS and MS/MS spectra against multiple metabolic databases.</p><p>Software: Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) was used for the initial spectral matching.</p><p>Databases: Public databases including HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/) were queried. Additionally, the Majorbio in-house database was used to enhance identification rates, particularly for gut microbiota-related metabolites.</p><p>Process: Putative annotations were assigned based on the accurate precursor mass (typically within a 10-ppm tolerance) and the comparison of experimental MS/MS fragmentation patterns with theoretical or reference spectra in the databases.</p><p>Sourcing Analysis: Further (sourcing analysis) for differential metabolites was conducted by querying against the Majorbio Gut Metabolism in-house database to infer potential microbial origins.</p><p>The annotated data matrix was then uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform (cloud.majorbio.com) for comprehensive statistical analysis.</p>

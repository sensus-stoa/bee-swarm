Study Identifier	MTBLS12952
Study Title	Decoding the Regulatory Networks of Proteus mirabilis Under Succinic Acid Stress: A Multi-Omics Approach
Study Description	<p>Proteus mirabilis, a key catheter-associated urinary tract infection pathogen that forms antibiotic-resistant crystalline biofilms, was shown via multi-omics analyses to be multimodally inhibited by succinic acid. At 15 mM, succinic acid reduced the bacterium’s growth by 70% and biofilm formation by 50%. Metabolomics revealed dysregulation in tryptophan/arginine metabolism, nucleotide biosynthesis, and the tricarboxylic acid cycle; transcriptomics showed downregulated ribosomal genes, oxidative phosphorylation, and efflux pumps plus upregulated arginine transport; and proteomics demonstrated suppressed T6SS virulence factors and iron acquisition proteins. Mechanistically, succinic acid is proposed to reduce K6 acetylation of the histone-like nucleoid structuring protein, enhancing its oligomerization to repress T6SS genes and inhibit biofilm formation. By targeting metabolism, virulence, and stress adaptation, succinic acid circumvents single-target resistance, offering a strategy to combat multidrug-resistant P. mirabilis via biofilm disruption and pathogenicity suppression.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Single colonies of Proteus mirabilis from Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) plates were inoculated into Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB), incubated overnight at 37°C, centrifuged, resuspended to OD 550 =0.1, then treatment groups were mixed with MIC-level succinic acid (1:1 volumetric ratio) and control groups with sterile triple-distilled water, incubated at 37°C for 24 h, and finally centrifuged to collect pellets stored at -80°C.</p>	<p>Each 50 mg aliquot was mixed with an extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1, containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard), then processed via frozen grinding (Wonbio-96c) and low-temperature ultrasonication.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column. Mobile phase B (acetonitrile:isopropanol:water = 47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v) was used with gradient elution programs optimized for positive and negative ion modes, ensuring efficient separation of metabolites.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted on a Q Exactive HF-X spectrometer with an ESI source operating in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 60,000, and MS/MS scans at 7,500, with ion-spray voltage and collision energy optimized for metabolite detection.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were preprocessed using Progenesis QI to generate CSV matrices of metabolite features. These matrices were filtered (requiring detection in 80% of samples per group), imputed for missing values, sum-normalized, and uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform for multivariate analyses including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by matching experimental mass spectra and retention times against databases including HMDB, Metlin, and the Majorbio Database. Differential metabolites were screened using criteria of variable importance in projection (VIP) &gt; 1 and p &lt; 0.05, followed by KEGG pathway enrichment analysis to elucidate metabolic pathway alterations.</p>

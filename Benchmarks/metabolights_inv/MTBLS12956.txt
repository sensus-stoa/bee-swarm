Study Protocol Description	<p>The culture of Bacillus 541 was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 minutes, and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane filter. Used high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) technology (Agilent 1260, HPLC, CA, USA) to separate the bacterial-cell-free supernatant (CFS). The mobile phase consists of water and acetonitrile, with a total run time of 16 minutes and an injection volume of 20 μL. Collected the samples at 2-minute intervals, a total of 8 times. Then, the bacterial supernatant of each time group was freeze-dried and used to treat intestinal organoids to determine the retention time of the effective metabolites. The supernatant of the time group containing the active metabolites was sent to Shanghai Bioprofile Technology Company Ltd for non-target metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>The samples were thawed at 4 °C and vortexed thoroughly. An aliquot of 600 μL from each sample was transferred to a microcentrifuge tube and dried under vacuum in a high-speed centrifugal concentrator. Then, 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol-water (4:1, v/v) was added, followed by mixing and sonication in an ice bath for 20 min. After standing at -20 °C for 1 h, the mixture was centrifuged at 16,000 × g and 4 °C for 20 min. The resulting supernatant was collected and concentrated to dryness again under vacuum. For LC–MS analysis, the residue was reconstituted in 100 μL of pre-chilled methanol-water (1:1, v/v), centrifuged at 20,000 × g and 4 °C for 15 min, and an appropriate volume of the supernatant was injected for analysis.</p>	<p>Throughout the entire analytical process, samples were maintained at 4 °C in the autosampler. Separation was performed on a SHIMADZU-LC30 ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The injection volume was 6 µL, the column temperature was set at 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was applied as follows: 0–2 min, 0% B; 2–6 min, 0% to 48% B; 6–10 min, 48% to 100% B; 10–12 min, 100% B; 12–12.1 min, 100% to 0% B; and 12.1–15 min, 0% B.</p>	<p>Each sample was analyzed in both positive (+) and negative (-) ion modes using electrospray ionization (ESI). After UPLC separation, mass spectrometry was performed on a Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with a HESI ion source. The ionization parameters were set as follows: spray voltage: 3.8 kV (+) and 3.2 kV (-); capillary temperature: 320 °C; sheath gas: 30 arb; aux gas: 5 arb; probe heater temperature: 350 °C; S-lens RF level: 50. Mass spectrometry data were acquired under the following settings: acquisition time: 15 min; full scan range: m/z 75–1050; MS1 resolution: 70,000 at m/z 200; AGC target: 3e6; maximum ion injection time (IT) for MS1: 100 ms. For MS/MS analysis, the top 10 most intense precursor ions from each full scan were selected for fragmentation. The MS2 settings were: resolution: 17,500 at m/z 200; AGC target: 1e5; maximum IT: 50 ms; activation type: HCD; isolation window: 2 m/z; normalized collision energy (stepped): 20, 30, 40.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using the MSDIAL software for peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching exact mass (mass tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS spectra (mass tolerance &lt; 0.01 Da) against public databases including HMDB, MassBank, and GNPS, as well as an in-house Bepu Metabolite Library (BP-DB).</p>

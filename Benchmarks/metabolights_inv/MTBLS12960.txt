Study Title	Systematic evaluation of SPE cartridges in a 12-second SPE-IM-MS workflow for untargeted intracellular metabolomics
Study Description	<p>Increasingly large-scale biological studies require rapid metabolomics workflows. Conventional chromatography followed by mass spectrometry workflows remain limited by long chromatographic separation times. Ion mobility mass spectrometry (IM-MS) is an attractive alternative as it enables milliseconds-scale gas-phase separations. Incorporation of a front-end solid-phase extraction (SPE) step can help reduce matrix effects prior to analysis. Although SPE-IM-MS workflows have been applied to biological fluids, their application to intracellular metabolomics and the influence of SPE cartridge selection have not been systematically evaluated. Here, an automated SPE-IM-QTOF MS workflow was used to analyse intracellular yeast metabolites with a sample-to-sample duty cycle of 12 seconds. Flow injection analysis (FIA) was compared with SPE-IM-MS, and the performance of five SPE cartridges (C18, C8, Cyano, Phenyl and HILIC) was evaluated based on feature detection, metabolite annotation and analytical reproducibility. Incorporation of an automated SPE cleanup step reduced spectral noise and improved analytical performance compared with FIA. C18 provided the broadest analytical coverage, while cartridge-dependent differences reflected differences in metabolite selectivity. This study provides a systematic evaluation of SPE cartridges for a SPE-IM-MS workflow applied to untargeted intracellular metabolomics. The results demonstrate the potential of automated SPE-IM-MS for rapid intracellular metabolomics analysis.</p>

Study Protocol Type Term Source REF	MTBLS	MTBLS	MS	CHMO	OBI	MI	CHMO
Study Protocol Description	<p>Preculture was prepared by inoculating YPD medium (2% (w/v) dextrose) with Saccharomyces cerevisiae reference strain BY4741 (Accession number: Y00000) from the yeast deletant library, provided by EUROSCARF (Giaever and Nislow, 2014). The preculture was incubated overnight at 30°C, 220 rpm. 100 µL preculture was then transferred to Eppendorf tubes and the cells were washed in SC media (6.7 g/L Yeast Nitrogen Base [Sigma-Aldrich], 0.79 g/L Complete Supplement Mixture [MP Biomedicals], 20 g/L dextrose, 75 µg/L Ampicillin) twice. The washed cells were then used to inoculate the main cultivations, which were performed in 250 mL wide-necked baffled shake flasks, with a working volume of 40 mL SC medium. Each culture was inoculated with an initial OD600 of 0.05 and subsequently incubated for 24 hours at 30°C, 220 rpm. 6 biological replicates were prepared like this.</p><p>The cells were quenched by adding pre-chilled absolute methanol (-80°C) to equal volume of cell culture. The cells were then centrifuged for 5 minutes at 4,000 g and 4 °C. The supernatant was discarded and the cell pellets stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction, 80%&nbsp;boiling ethanol was added to the cell pellets with the ratio of 1 mL boiling ethanol per mg of biomass. Biomass was estimated from optical density at 600 nm (0.5 mg/mL for 1 OD600). The tubes were vortexed for a minute and then placed in a water bath preheated to 90 °C &nbsp;for 3 minutes after loosening the caps. The tubes were cooled at 4 °C&nbsp;for 15 min &nbsp;and then centrifuged for 20 min at 4000 g and 4 °C. The supernatants were collected, filtered through 0.45 μm nylon filters and then stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>The high-throughput solid phase extraction (SPE) system Agilent RapidFire 365 was used. The system sequentially aspirates samples from 96-well or 384-well plates. It then performs cleanup and injects the samples into the mass spectrometer. We ran our samples on C8, C18, HILIC, Phenyl and Cyano cartridges. The total time required for cleaning up a sample was only 12 seconds. Sample was aspirated, loaded onto the cartridge, a wash was performed, and finally the metabolites were eluted from the cartridge by an elution buffer. The cartridge was re-equilibrated for 1 second to make it ready for the next sample.</p><p>The order of sample injections was randomized. The extraction solvent (80% ethanol) was included as blank. 4 blanks were injected after every 6 sample injections. The first blank was treated as a cartridge wash; data obtained from succeeding 3 blank injections was retained for further processing and analysis.</p>	<p>The samples were analysed on Agilent 6560 ion mobility quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (IM-QTOF). We used nitrogen as the drift gas. The instrument was operated in 4-bit multiplexing mode.</p>	<p>Running via RapidFire results in creation of a single .d raw file for each sequence. This file must be split to get .d raw files for individual injections. As the data was collected with 4-bit multiplexing, the split files were next demultiplexed using PNNL PreProcessor 3.0 (v2021.04.21).</p><p>The IM-MS instrument measures drift time (i.e. the time taken by the ion to cross the drift tube), which is then used to calculate collisional cross section (CCS) values. Calibrant ions with known CCS values are run under the same settings on the same day, and this data is used for CCS calibration. The calibration solution had the following composition: 10 mL Agilent G1969-85000 ESI-L tuning mix, 1.5 mL water, 88.5 mL acetonitrile and 3 microL of 0.1 mM HP-0321 (Agilent biopolymer reference mass solution).</p><p>After CCS calibration, Agilent MassHunter Mass Profiler (v10.0) was used for unidentified features (peaks) detection. The quality score threshold was set at 70. Peaks with average abundance lower than the average abundance in blank injections were filtered out.</p><p>The demultiplexed and CCS calibrated injection files were also fed into Skyline (v25.1.0.142). Skyline is useful for searching using both m/z and CCS values. The library consisting of m/z and CCS values was created from the unified CCS Compendium hosted by the McLean lab (https://mcleanresearchgroup.shinyapps.io/CCS-Compendium/) (Picache et al., 2019). Only metabolites classified into categories such as xenobiotic, primary metabolite or secondary metabolite were kept. Xenobiotics, inorganic compounds and human metabolites that are not commonly found in yeast were excluded. Ions with charge states of up to ±3 were kept. The library used for peak identification in Skyline is given in Supplementary Material. For each cartridge, peaks detected in less than 50% of sample injections were removed. Average of blank injections was subtracted from the area of each peak, then only peaks with areas more than or equal to 100 were kept.</p>	<p>The demultiplexed and CCS calibrated injection files were also fed into Skyline (v25.1.0.142). Skyline is useful for searching using both m/z and CCS values. The library consisting of m/z and CCS values was created from the unified CCS Compendium hosted by the McLean lab (https://mcleanresearchgroup.shinyapps.io/CCS-Compendium/) (Picache et al., 2019). Only metabolites classified into categories such as xenobiotic, primary metabolite or secondary metabolite were kept. Xenobiotics, inorganic compounds and human metabolites that are not commonly found in yeast were excluded. Ions with charge states of up to ±3 were kept. The library used for peak identification in Skyline is given in Supplementary Material. For each cartridge, peaks detected in less than 50% of sample injections were removed. Average of blank injections was subtracted from the area of each peak, then only peaks with areas more than or equal to 100 were kept.</p>	<p>For validation, the yeast extracts were also analysed by Waters Xevo MRT mass spectrometer using a C18 column (Acquity UPLC HSS T3 1.8 µm particles, 2.1 x 100 mm column). The UPLC system used was Waters Acquity Premier. A 12 minute gradient was run. Buffer A was water + 0.1% formic acid and buffer B was acetonitrile + 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. The gradient was as follows: B was increased from 5% to 100% from 0 to 6 min, B was kept at 100% from 6 to 10.5 minutes, then again 5% B from 10.51 minutes to 12 minutes. Column temperature was kept as 45 °C. Desolvation temperature was 550 °C and source temperature was 120 °C. Capillary voltage was 1.5 kV, whereas cone voltage was 30 V. Desolvation gas flow rate was 800 L/hr, whereas cone voltage was 10 L/hr. Scan range was 50-1200 m/z. Scan rate was 10 Hz. The data was acquired in data independent MSE&nbsp;mode of Waters (data independent acquisition, DIA). Ramping collision energy was used for MS2 fragmentation. Low collision energy was 6 V, high energy ramp was from 20-50 V.&nbsp;</p><p>The data was analysed by MS-DIAL. SWATH analysis was done as the data was acquired in data independent mode. Positive and negative ion mode ESI-MS/MS spectral libraries of authentic standards were used for annotation. The positive ion mode library consisted of 16232 unique compounds, whereas the negative ion mode library consisted of 8887 unique compounds. The results obtained from MS-DIAL were filtered. Only MS2 assigned peaks were considered. Further filtering was kept similar to filtering done for SPE-IM-MS data. Peaks detected in less than 50% of sample injections were removed. Average of blank injections was subtracted from the area of each peak, then only peaks with areas more than or equal to 100 were kept.&nbsp;</p>

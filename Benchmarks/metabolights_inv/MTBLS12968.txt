Study Identifier	MTBLS12968
Study Title	PCOS preterm plasma metabolome
Study Description	<p>This study investigated mid-trimester plasma metabolomic profiles in pregnant women to identify biomarkers associated with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and preterm birth. A total of 149 participants were categorized into four groups: 32 with PCOS and preterm delivery, 34 with PCOS and term delivery, 42 without PCOS with preterm delivery, and 41 without PCOS with term delivery. Plasma samples were analyzed using targeted metabolomics via UHPLC-QTRAP MS/MS with HILIC and C18 chromatography. Multivariate statistical analyses including OPLS-DA were employed to identify significantly altered metabolites. The findings aim to provide insights into metabolic mechanisms underlying PCOS and preterm birth, potentially contributing to improved risk prediction strategies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Plasma samples were obtained from pregnant women who underwent routine prenatal examinations at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Peking University First Hospital between August 2022 and December 2024. Blood samples were collected during the mid-trimester (24–28 weeks of gestation) using EDTA-containing tubes. Following collection, samples were centrifuged on the same day to separate plasma. The supernatant plasma was aliquoted and immediately stored at -80°C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Prior to LC-MS analysis, metabolites were extracted from plasma samples using a standardized protocol. Briefly, 400 μL of cold methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) was added to each plasma aliquot to precipitate proteins and extract metabolites. The mixture was vigorously vortexed and then centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and completely dried under vacuum. For LC-MS analysis, the dried metabolites were reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v), followed by centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4 °C. The final supernatant was transferred for injection.</p><p><br></p><p>A pooled quality control (QC) sample was generated by combining equal volumes of all individual samples to monitor analytical reproducibility throughout the sequence. Solvent blanks were also prepared to assess background contamination. For absolute quantification, stable isotope-labeled internal standards were added during the extraction process to correct for matrix effects and variations in sample preparation.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on an UHPLC system (1290 Infinity LC, Agilent Technologies) using two columns: a HILIC column (Waters UPLC BEH Amide, 2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) and a reversed-phase C18 column (Waters UPLC BEH C18, 2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 μm).</p><p><br></p><p>For HILIC separation, the column temperature was maintained at 35 °C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 10% acetonitrile in water containing 2 mM ammonium formate and (B) acetonitrile with 0.4% formic acid. The gradient elution program was as follows: 85% B (0–1 min), 80% B (3–4 min), 70% B (6 min), 50% B (10–15.5 min), and 85% B (15.6–23 min), at a flow rate of 300 μL/min. The injection volume was 2 μL.</p><p><br></p><p>For RPLC separation, the column temperature was set to 40 °C. Mobile phase A consisted of water with 5 mM ammonium acetate, and mobile phase B was 99.5% acetonitrile. The gradient program was: 5% B (0 min), 60% B (5 min), 100% B (11–13 min), and 5% B (13.1–16 min), with a flow rate of 400 μL/min. The injection volume was 2 μL. All samples were maintained at 4 °C during the analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a QTRAP 6500+ system (AB Sciex) coupled with the UHPLC system. The instrument was operated in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode with positive and negative ionization switching.</p><p><br></p><p>The electrospray ionization (ESI) source conditions were set as follows:</p><p><br></p><p>Positive ion mode: IonSpray Voltage: +4500 V; Source Temperature: 580 °C; Ion Source Gas 1 (GS1): 45; Ion Source Gas 2 (GS2): 60; Curtain Gas (CUR): 35.</p><p><br></p><p>Negative ion mode: IonSpray Voltage: -4500 V; Source Temperature: 580 °C; Ion Source Gas 1 (GS1): 45; Ion Source Gas 2 (GS2): 60; Curtain Gas (CUR): 35.</p><p><br></p><p>The mass spectrometer was calibrated following manufacturer specifications prior to analysis. Specific MRM transitions for each metabolite were optimized and applied for quantitative detection. Throughout the entire analysis, pooled quality control (QC) samples were regularly injected to ensure system stability and reproducibility.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were processed using MultiQuant software (Version 3.0.3, AB Sciex) for peak integration, calibration, and quantification of targeted metabolites. Isotope-labeled internal standards were used to correct for variations in sample preparation and instrument response. The resulting quantitative data were then normalized and scaled using in-house scripts in R (version 4.2.1). Specifically, data were preprocessed by log-transformation and Pareto scaling prior to multivariate statistical analysis to improve normality and reduce heteroscedasticity. Quality control samples with a coefficient of variation (CV) &lt; 30% were retained to ensure reproducibility.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using a targeted approach based on the predefined multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions established with authentic chemical standards. The MRM ion pairs and corresponding collision energies were optimized and validated prior to the analysis. Metabolites were confirmed by matching the retention time and fragmentation patterns with those of reference standards analyzed under identical LC-MS conditions.</p><p><br></p><p>The identification process was supported by the use of stable isotope-labeled internal standards for precise retention time alignment and quantification. Further annotation and pathway mapping were conducted using major public metabolite databases including HMDB and KEGG.</p><p><br></p><p>All data processing and metabolite identification were performed using MultiQuant software (AB Sciex) and verified through manual inspection to ensure accuracy.</p>

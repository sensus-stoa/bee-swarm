Study Identifier	MTBLS12969
Study Title	Multi-omics analysis reveals urea promoting Salix matsudana growth and facilitating soil remediation
Study Description	<p>In this manuscript, we monitored the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana&nbsp;and measured the biomass of its various organs. Concurrently, we analyzed the rhizosphere metabolome and microbiome. The results demonstrated that urea application promotes both the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana&nbsp;and the accumulation of heavy metals. Moreover, urea induced significant changes in the rhizosphere microbial community and metabolic profiles. Integrated metabolomic and microbiome analyses further revealed the interactive effects of urea on the metabolic processes within the rhizosphere soil and the resident microbiota. Collectively, this study employs a multi-omics approach to elucidate the mechanism by which urea enhances the growth of&nbsp;S. matsudana&nbsp;and promotes soil remediation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The CK group functioned as the control group. In this group, Salix matsudana was planted in heavy metal-contaminated soil without the application of nitrogen fertilizer, and the maintenance measures were kept consistent with those of the other groups.</p><p>In the LCN group, urea (CO(NH2)2 was applied. Salix matsudana was planted in heavy - metal - contaminated soil, and CO(NH2)2 was applied at a concentration of 60 kg·hm-2·year-1&nbsp;to observe the influence of low - level urea on the phytoremediation of heavy - metal - contaminated soil. The maintenance conditions were the same as those of other groups.</p><p>In the HCN group, urea (CO(NH2)2) was applied. Salix matsudana was planted in heavy - metal - contaminated soil, and CO(NH2)2 was applied at a concentration of 200 kg·hm-2·year-1&nbsp;to observe the impact of high - level urea on the phytoremediation of heavy - metal - contaminated soil. The maintenance conditions were consistent with those of other groups.</p><p>In the HHCN group, urea (CO(NH2)2) was applied. Salix matsudana was planted in heavy - metal - contaminated soil, and CO(NH2)2 was applied at a concentration of 300 kg·hm-2·year-2&nbsp;to observe the impact of ultra - high - level urea on the phytoremediation of heavy - metal - contaminated soil. The maintenance conditions were the same as those of other groups.</p>	<p>100 mg solid sample was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead</p><p>was added. 800 μL of extraction solution&nbsp;(methanol:&nbsp;water = 4:1 (v:v)&nbsp;containing&nbsp;four&nbsp;internal standards&nbsp;( 0.02&nbsp;mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine,&nbsp;etc.) were used for metabolite extraction. Samples were ground by the Wonbio-96c ( Shanghai wanbo biotechnology co., LTD) frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz). The samples were left at -20°C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4°C, 13000 g), and the supernatant was transferred to the injection vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a&nbsp;UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris&nbsp;240 system equipped with an&nbsp;ACQUITY&nbsp;HSS T3&nbsp;column&nbsp;(100 mm&nbsp;×&nbsp;2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm;&nbsp;Waters,&nbsp;USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile&nbsp;(2:98, v/v)&nbsp;(solvent&nbsp;A) and 0.1% formic acid in a B). The flow rate was 0.40&nbsp;mL/min&nbsp;and the column temperature was 40°C. The injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a&nbsp;UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris&nbsp;240 system Mass</p><p>Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode</p><p>and negative mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 400°C ;</p><p>sheath gas flow rate at 40 arb; Aux gas flow rate at 10 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at</p><p>-2800V&nbsp;in negative mode and 3500V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy ,</p><p>20-40-60V&nbsp;rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent</p><p>Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of&nbsp;70-1050&nbsp;m/z.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC/MS raw data was performed by&nbsp;Progenesis&nbsp;QI, &nbsp;Milford,&nbsp;USA) software, and a three-dimensional data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this three-dimensional matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed, and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, deredundant and peak pooled.</p>	<p>At the same time, the metabolites were identified by searching database, and the main databases were the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin ( https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China)</p>

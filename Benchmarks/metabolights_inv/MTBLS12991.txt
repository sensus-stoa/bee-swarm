Study Identifier	MTBLS12991
Study Title	Exploring the variations in intestinal microbial communities and metabolite profiles between heat-sensitive and heat-tolerant chickens through a comprehensive multi-omics approach
Study Description	<p>Heat stress (HS) compromises poultry health by disrupting intestinal integrity and microbiota homeostasis. This study compared heat stress-susceptible (HS-SUS) and heat stress-resistant (HS-RES) White Leghorn chickens under chronic cyclic HS (35±2°C, 8 h/day). HS-RES chickens demonstrated superior growth performance (increased weight gain, reduced feed conversion ratio), decreased oxidative damage (lower MDA), and attenuated inflammation (reduced IL-6/TNF-α). Intestinal barrier integrity was preserved in HS-RES, evidenced by intact villi and upregulated tight junction proteins (ZO-1, Claudin-1, Occludin). 16S rRNA sequencing revealed higher microbial richness in HS-RES with enriched beneficial taxa (Lactobacillus, Alistipes) and depleted pathogenic Erysipelatoclostridium. Metabolomics identified HS-RES-specific enrichment in lysine/riboflavin biosynthesis and anti-inflammatory pathways, strongly correlating with beneficial microbiota. Critically, Lactobacillus abundance positively correlated with α-tocopherol, while Alistipes associated with lysine/riboflavin metabolism. These results establish microbial-metabolite synergy as a core mechanism for HS resilience, informing probiotic strategies to enhance thermotolerance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A cohort of 90 specific-pathogen-free (SPF) female White Leghorn chickens (14 days old; body weight 140 ± 10 g) were provided with standard antibiotic-free feed sourced from the National Poultry Experimental Animal Resource Bank, Harbin Veterinary Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences.Based on mean body weight per replicate, one representative chicken per replicate was selected at trial end. Blood (subcutaneous venipuncture), intestinal tissues, and cecal contents were collected, then stored at -80°C for analysis.</p>	<p>800 uL of cold methanol/acetonitrile (1:1,v/ was added to remove the protein and extract the metabolites. The mixture was colected into a new centrifuge tube,and centrifuged at 14000 g for 5 min at 'c to collect the supermatant. The supernatant was dried in a vacuum centrifuge. For LC-MS analysis,the samples were redissolved in 100 uL acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) solvent.</p>	<p>Hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography on a 2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 µm BEH Amide column is coupled to a TripleTOF 5600/6600 mass spectrometer; analytes are resolved at 25 °C with a 0.4 mL min-1 gradient from 95 % acetonitrile/5 % 25 mM ammonium acetate–ammonia aqueous buffer to 40 % acetonitrile over 9 min, followed by re-equilibration, delivering broad polar-metabolite coverage for serum, cells and tissues.</p>	<p>Metabolites eluted from the UPLC column are analysed on an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600/6600 high-resolution system operated in full-scan MS and data-dependent MS/MS mode; electrospray ionisation is run at 5500 V in both positive and negative polarity with a 600 °C source temperature, 30 psi curtain gas and 60 psi nebuliser gas, ensuring high sensitivity and broad coverage across the metabolome.</p>	<p>The raw MS data (wif.scan files) were converted to MzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert before importing into freely avallable XCMS software.</p>	<p>For peak picking, the folowing parameters were used: centWave m/2 = 25 ppm, peakwidth = c(10, 60,prefiter = c(10, 100. For peakgrouping, bw = 5, mzwid =0.025, minfrac = 0.5 were used.lin the extracted ion featres,only the variables having more than 50% ofthe nonzero measurement values in at least one groupwere kept. Compound identification of metabolites by MS/MS spectra with an in-house database established with available authentic standards.</p>

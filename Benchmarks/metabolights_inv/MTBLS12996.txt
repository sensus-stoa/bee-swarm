Study Identifier	MTBLS12996
Study Title	Proteomics and metabolomics analysis based on exosomes revealed the response mechanism of Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris to acid stress
Study Description	<p>Bacteria secrete exosomes containing stress-related molecules such as proteins and nucleic acids, which facilitate adaptation to challenging environments. In order to elucidate the role of A. acidoterrestris exosomes in its acid stress response, this study combined proteomics and metabolomics to analyze the function and biological pathway of exosomes in A. acidoterrestris against acid stress The results of exosome proteomics showed that spore coat protein D, SecA, YidC, chemotactic protein CheA, methyl-accepting chemotactic protein (MCP), serine-type D-alanine-D-alanine carboxypeptidase protein, and lipid II glutamine synthetase subunit GatD protein were significantly up-regulated. These proteins are mainly enriched in ABC transporters, two-component systems, quorum sensing, peptidoglycan biosynthesis and other pathways. The results of exosomal metabolomics showed that 83 and 68 differential metabolites were detected in positive and negative ion modes, respectively. These metabolites were mainly enriched in ABC transporters, carbohydrate metabolism and amino acid metabolism. This study showed that the exosome proteins and metabolites secreted by A. acidoterrestris under acid stress changed significantly, revealing the overall response of A. acidoterrestris to acid stress, and pointing out the possible future direction for the effective control and application of A. acidoterrestris</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris DSM 3922T was purchased from Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (DSMZ, Brunswick, Germany). According to the method of Jiao , A. acidoterrestris medium (AAM) was prepared and adjusted appropriately. Some of the bacterial cultures were taken as the control group (pH 4.0, R0-1, R0-2 and R0-3 as parallel controls). The remaining bacterial cultures were acid-shocked and resuspended in AAM medium at pH 2.5 (S-1, S-2 and S-3 were parallel samples). The samples were cultured with the control group at 250 r / min 45℃ for 12 hours to a logarithmic phase OD value of about 0.6-0.8, and frozen in liquid nitrogen and frozen at −80℃ for subsequent exosome extraction. All treatments were cultured independently and repeated 3 times.</p>	<p>The frozen samples were dissolved in a water bath at 37°C and transferred to a new centrifuge tube. The sample were then centrifuged at 4 °C, 2000 × g, 30 min, and the supernatant was centrifuged again at 4°C, 10000 × g, 45 min to remove larger vesicles. The obtained supernatant was filtered by 0.45 μm filter membrane, and the filtrate was 100000 × g, centrifuged again for 70 min, and the supernatant was discarded. After re-suspension with 10 mL pre-cooled 1 × PBS, 100000 × g, centrifuged again for 70 min, discarded the supernatant, re-suspended with 100 μL pre-cooled 1 × PBS, and stored the exosomes at -80°C.</p>	<p>After the sample of 2.1 was slowly thawed at 4°C, an appropriate amount of sample was added to a pre-cooled methanol / acetonitrile / water solution (2 : 2 : 1, v / v), vortexed and mixed, low-temperature ultrasound for 30 min, standing at -20 °C for 10 min, centrifuged at 14000 g 4°C for 20 min, and the supernatant was vacuum dried. During mass spectrometry analysis, 100 μL acetonitrile aqueous solution (acetonitrile : water = 1 : 1, v / v) was added to redissolve, vortexed, centrifuged at 14000 × g 4°C for 15 min, and the supernatant was taken for analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32059222' _msthash='226'>Chromatographic conditions : Agilent 1290 Infinity LC (UHPLC) equipped with HILIC chromatographic column. Parameters : column temperature 25°C, flow rate 0.5 mL / min, injection volume 2 μL. Mobile phase A : water + 25 mM ammonium acetate + 25 mM ammonia ; B: Acetonitrile. Gradient elution : 0-0.5 min, 95 % B ; 0.5-7 min, B decreased from 95 % to 65 % ; 7-8 min, B decreased from 65 % to 40 % ; 8-9 min, B maintained 40 % ; 9-9.1 min, B increased from 40 % to 95 % ; 9.1-12 min, B maintained 95%. The samples were stored at 4°C, randomly sequenced, and inserted into QC samples for system stability and data reliability monitoring.</p><p _msttexthash='32059222' _msthash='226'>Q-TOF MS conditions : Primary and secondary spectra were collected using an AB Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer. The samples were separated by UHPLC and detected by ESI positive and negative ion mode. ESI parameters : Gas 160, Gas 260, CUR 30 psi, ion source temperature 600°C, ISVF ± 5500 V. The detection range of m / z was 60-1000 Da for the first level and 25-1000 Da for the second level. Scanning time : first order 0.20 s / spectra, second order 0.05 s / spectra. The secondary mass spectrometry was performed in IDA mode with DP ± 60 V and collision energy of 35 ± 15 eV. IDA setting : dynamic exclusion range of 4 Da, 10 fragments were collected each time.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32059222' _msthash='276'>The original data were converted into.mzXML format by ProteoWizard software, and then peak alignment, retention time correction and peak area extraction were performed by XCMS software. The extracted data were first subjected to metabolite structure identification and data preprocessing, followed by experimental data quality evaluation, and finally data analysis. Data analysis included univariate statistical analysis, multivariate statistical analysis, differential metabolite screening, differential metabolite correlation analysis and KEGG pathway analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32059222' _msthash='318'>The Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) obtained by the OPLS-DA model can be used to measure the influence intensity and explanatory power of the expression pattern of each metabolite on the classification and discrimination of samples, so as to mine the differential metabolites with biological significance. In general, metabolites with VIP &gt; 1 are considered to have a significant contribution in model interpretation. In this study, VIP &gt; 1 and p-value &lt; 0.05 were used as the screening criteria for significant differential metabolites, and the results were shown in Fig. 11.</p>

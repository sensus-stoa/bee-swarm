Study Title	Integrated Gut Microbiome and Metabolomics Analysis Reveals Microbial-Metabolic Cross-talk in Allergic Rhinitis
Study Description	<p> Background:&nbsp;Emerging evidence indicates a link between gut dysbiosis and allergic rhinitis (AR) pathogenesis. Nevertheless, the mechanistic role of gut microbiota in AR progression requires further characterization. To address this, we employed an integrated multi-omics strategy to delineate gut microbial composition and metabolic signatures in AR patients.</p><p> Methods:&nbsp;Fecal specimens from 23 AR patients and 15 matched healthy controls (total n = 38) were subjected to 16S rRNA gene sequencing to assess bacterial community structure, alongside untargeted metabolomic profiling of microbial metabolites. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was applied to evaluate microbiota-metabolite interactions.</p><p> Results:&nbsp;AR patients showed reduced gut microbial diversity (ACE index, PD whole tree; p &lt; 0.05) with depletion of SCFA-producing&nbsp;Faecalibacterium&nbsp;and enrichment of&nbsp;Escherichia&nbsp;and&nbsp;Fusobacterium. Metabolomics revealed impaired butyrate metabolism (p = 0.03) and decreased anti-inflammatory metabolites (quercetin, caffeic acid). Spearman correlations linked&nbsp;Faecalibacterium&nbsp;to butyrate (ρ = 0.62, p = 0.002) and&nbsp;Gemella&nbsp;to pro-inflammatory 12,13-DHOME (ρ = −0.58, p = 0.005), implicating microbial-metabolic crosstalk.</p><p><strong>Conclusions:&nbsp;</strong>Gut dysbiosis in AR drives immune-metabolic imbalance via SCFA depletion and pro-inflammatory mediators, highlighting microbiota-targeted therapies as potential interventions.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from all participants by retaining the mid-to-late portion of bowel movements. Using sterilized spoons, the inner layer of fecal material was carefully sampled, and all specimens were transferred into sterile plastic tubes under aseptic conditions, with precautions taken to avoid contamination from urine or contact with toilet surfaces. Additionally, all female participants provided stool samples exclusively during their non-menstrual phase. Within 2 hours post-collection, samples were transported to the laboratory in ice-packed coolers to maintain a cold chain, followed by immediate storage at −80℃ until subsequent analyzes.</p>	<p>(1) Thaw the sample slowly on ice.</p><p>(2) Weigh 50 mg of the sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, add 800 μL of 80% methanol, vortex thoroughly to mix, grind at 65 Hz for 180 s, and ultrasonicate at 4 °C for 30 min.</p><p>(3) Let it stand at -40 °C for 1 h, vortex for 30 s, and then let it stand at 4 °C for 0.5 h.</p><p>(4) Centrifuge at 4 °C and 12,000 rpm for 15 min.</p><p>(5) Transfer all the supernatant into a centrifuge tube and let it stand at -40 °C for 1 h.</p><p>(6) Centrifuge at 4 °C and 12,000 rpm for 15 min.</p><p>(7) Pipette 200 μL of the supernatant, add 5 μL of internal standard (0.20 mg/mL dichlorophenylalanine), mix well, and transfer into an injection vial.</p>	<p>Instrumental Analysis Platform: LC-MS (Waters, UPLC; Thermo, Q Exactive)</p><p>Chromatographic Column: ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1*100 mm, 1.8 μm)</p><p>Chromatographic Separation Conditions:</p><p>Column temperature: 40 °C; Flow rate: 0.300 mL/min;</p><p>Mobile phase composition: A: water (0.05% formic acid), B: acetonitrile;</p><p>Injection volume: 5 μL; Autosampler temperature: 4 °C.</p>	<p>Positive mode: Heater temperature 300 °C; Sheath gas flow rate: 45 arb; Auxiliary gas flow rate: 15 arb; Sweep gas flow rate: 1 arb; Electrospray voltage: 3.0 kV; Capillary temperature: 350 °C; S-Lens RF level: 30%.</p><p>Negative mode: Heater temperature 300 °C; Sheath gas flow rate: 45 arb; Auxiliary gas flow rate: 15 arb; Sweep gas flow rate: 1 arb; Electrospray voltage: 3.2 kV; Capillary temperature: 350 °C; S-Lens RF level: 60%.</p><p>Scanning mode: Full scan (m/z 70–1050) with data-dependent MS² acquisition (dd-MS², TopN = 10);</p><p>Resolution: 70,000 (MS¹) &amp; 17,500 (MS²).</p><p>Collision mode: Higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD).</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC/MS raw data was performed by&nbsp;Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation Milford, USA)&nbsp;software, and a three-dimensional data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this three-dimensional matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed, and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, deredundant and peak pooled.&nbsp;</p>	<p>At the same time, the metabolites were identified by searching database, and the main databases were the&nbsp;HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin ( https://metlin.scripps.edu/)&nbsp;&nbsp;and Majorbio Database .&nbsp;</p>

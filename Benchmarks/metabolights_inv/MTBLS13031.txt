Study Title	LC-MS-Based Serum Metabolomics Reveals Distinct Metabolic Signatures in Patients with Cerebral Infarction
Study Description	<p>This study applied serum metabolomics to compare patients with cerebral infarction (n=10) and controls (n=10). A total of 3,160 metabolites were identified, with distinct class distributions between groups. Cerebral infarction patients showed elevated benzene derivatives, organic acids, and bile acids, while amino acids and glycerophospholipids were decreased. PCA and OPLS-DA confirmed clear metabolic separation, and 329 differential metabolites were identified. Network and enrichment analyses indicated disruptions in lipid, amino acid, energy, and inflammation-related pathways. These findings highlight marked metabolic reprogramming in cerebral infarction and provide potential biomarkers for disease mechanisms and diagnosis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 20 subjects were enrolled, including 10 patients with clinically and radiographically confirmed cerebral infarction (C001–C010) and 10 healthy controls (D001–D010) (Table S1). Inclusion criteria were: age 18–70 years, confirmed cerebral infarction, and provision of written informed consent. Exclusion criteria included recent infection, pregnancy or lactation, recent use of metabolism-altering drugs, severe organ dysfunction, malignancy, or psychiatric illness. Venous blood samples were collected for metabolomics analysis. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee (Approval No.: XXXX), and all participants provided informed consent.</p>	<p>After overnight fasting (8 h), 5 mL of peripheral blood was collected by venipuncture. Serum was separated by centrifugation and stored at 80 °C until use. For metabolomics analysis, proteins were precipitated with pre-chilled methanol/acetonitrile (20% v/v), followed by centrifugation. The supernatant was collected, re-centrifuged, and 180 μL of the clear extract was used for LC–MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Shimadzu LC-30A UPLC system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm). The mobile phases were 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B), delivered at 0.4 mL/min. The column temperature was set at 40 °C with an injection volume of 4 μL. The gradient program was as follows: 0.0 min, 95% A/5% B; 2.0 min, 80% A/20% B; 5.0 min, 40% A/60% B; 6.0–7.5 min, 1% A/99% B; 7.6–10.0 min, return to 95% A/5% B.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed on an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 6600 system in positive and negative ESI modes. The spray voltage was +5000 V/4000 V, source temperatures 550 °C/450 °C, and gas pressures 50/60/35 psi (nebulizer/heater/curtain). MS 1 scans (m/z 50–1000) were acquired at 0.2 s, and MS 2 scans (m/z 25–1000) at 0.04 s, with a collision energy of ±30 V. Dynamic exclusion was applied after three triggers within 3 s.</p>	<p>Raw MS data were converted to mzML using ProteoWizard, followed by peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction with XCMS. Peaks with &gt;50% missing values were removed, and remaining missing values were imputed using a hybrid strategy (KNN or one-fifth of the minimum intensity). Peak areas were normalized by support vector regression, and metabolites were annotated against in-house, public, and predicted spectral libraries. Features with identification score &gt;0.5 and QC CV &lt;0.3 were retained, and positive/negative mode data were merged to generate the final dataset.</p>	<p>Metabolites were annotated by matching features to in-house, public, and predicted spectral libraries. Only features with identification scores &gt;0.5 and QC CV &lt;0.3 were retained.</p>

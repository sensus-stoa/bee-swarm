Study Title	Identification of traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage associated metabolites using untargeted metabolomics
Study Description	<p>This study applied serum metabolomics to compare 10 traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage (TICH) patients with 10 controls. LC-MS/MS identified 3,183 metabolites, with increased benzene derivatives, organic acids, and amino acids, and decreased fatty acids, glycerophospholipids, and sphingomyelins in TICH patients. Multivariate analysis revealed differential metabolites involved in amino acid, lipid, and aromatic compound metabolism. Enrichment and network analyses indicated associations with lipid and amino acid metabolism, neural signaling, and inflammatory pathways. Mapping to HMDB suggested potential links to TICH pathogenesis, providing insights for biomarker discovery and therapeutic strategies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>TICH patients (experimental group) and non-TICH individuals (control group) were enrolled (Table S1). Inclusion criteria were age 18–65 years, TICH confirmed by clinical and imaging assessments, and provision of written informed consent. Exclusion criteria included recent infection, pregnancy or lactation, use of metabolism-altering drugs within one month, severe organ dysfunction or malignancy, and psychiatric or other conditions affecting compliance.</p>	<p>Serum Sample Preparation:</p><p>After overnight fasting (8 h), 5 mL of peripheral blood was collected by venipuncture. Serum was separated by centrifugation and stored at 80°C. For metabolomics analysis, proteins were precipitated with 20% acetonitrile in methanol, followed by centrifugation and purification at 20°C. The clarified supernatant (180 μL) was transferred to autosampler vials for LC–MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Shimadzu LC-30A UPLC system with a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B), delivered at 0.4 mL/min at 40 °C, with a 4 μL injection. Gradient elution was 95% A/5% B (0–2 min) to 80% A/20% B, 2–5 min to 40% A/60% B, 6–7.5 min to 1% A/99% B, then re-equilibrated to 95% A/5% B (7.6–10 min).</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed on an AB TripleTOF 6600 (SCIEX, USA) in both positive and negative ESI modes, acquiring MS^1 and MS^2 data over 10 min. Key parameters were: ESI voltage ±5000 V, source temperature 550/450 °C (ESI+/ESI-), gas pressures 50/60/35 psi (nebulizer/heater/curtain), declustering potential ±60 V, collision energy MS^1 ±10 V, MS^2 ±30 V (spread 15 V), scan ranges m/z 50–1000 (MS^1) and 25–1000 (MS^2), and accumulation times 0.2 s/0.04 s. Dynamic exclusion selected the top 18 precursor ions three times with a 3 s exclusion window.</p>	<p>Raw MS data were converted to mzML using ProteoWizard, then processed with XCMS for peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction. Peaks with &gt;50% missing values were removed, and remaining missing values were imputed using KNN or one-fifth of the minimum intensity. Peak areas were normalized by support vector regression, and metabolites were annotated against in-house, public, and predicted spectral libraries. Features with identification scores &gt;0.5 and QC CV &lt;0.3 were retained, and positive and negative mode data were merged to produce the final dataset.</p>	<p>Processed serum metabolome data from TICH patients and controls were analyzed in R. Univariate comparisons were performed using the limma package to calculate log 2 fold changes and significance, with FDR controlled by the Benjamini-Hochberg method. Non-normally distributed metabolites were tested using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Differential metabolites were defined as VIP &gt;1.0, adjusted p &lt;0.05, and |log 2 FC| &gt;1.0. Data visualization included volcano plots (limma), and metabolite classification and functional annotation were performed using MetaboAnalystR.</p>

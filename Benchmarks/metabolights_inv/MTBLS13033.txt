Study Identifier	MTBLS13033
Study Title	Effects of Glycyrrhizic Acid on growth performance and hepato-intestinal health of American bullfrogs (Aquarana catesbeiana)
Study Description	<p> High-density aquaculture of the American bullfrog (Aquarana catesbeiana) frequently result in&nbsp;hepatic lipid accumulation and intestinal damage. This study investigated the effects of Glycyrrhizic Acid (GA) supplementation in feed of bullfrogs on the growth performance and hepato-intestinal health. A total of 1500 juvenile bullfrogs were randomly assigned to five dietary groups, receiving diets supplemented with 0g/kg (GA0), 0.3g/kg&nbsp;(GA1), 1.5g/kg&nbsp;(GA2), 3g/kg&nbsp;(GA3), and 5g/kg&nbsp;(GA4) Glycyrrhizic Acid over a 35-day period.&nbsp;Our findings revealed that GA supplementation significantly&nbsp;enhanced the weight gain and survival rates of bullfrogs, while exerting beneficial effects on their hepato-intestinal health.&nbsp;In the intestine, GA improved morphology and bolstered barrier function, as indicated by increased villus height, goblet cell density, and upregulation of the expression of tight junction protein genes (zo-1,zo-2,zo-3,&nbsp;occludin&nbsp;and claudin-7). In the liver, GA bolstered antioxidant capacity, evidenced by elevated activities of antioxidant enzymes (SOD,CAT,GSH-Px) and reduced levels of the lipid peroxidation product malondialdehyde (MDA). Histological assessments also showed that GA supplementation diminished the number and size of hepatic lipid droplets, effectively mitigating hepatic lipid deposition. Molecularly, this corresponded with downregulated lipogenic genes (acc1,pparγ) and the upregulated lipolytic gene (atgl). Notably, these positive effects generally increased with GA levels up to a peak at 1.5g/kg&nbsp;(GA2) before declining. This research offers a scientific foundation for utilizing GA&nbsp;as a functional feed additive in bullfrog aquaculture, potentially improving growth, feed efficiency, and overall health.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Bullfrog (Aquarana catesbeiana) tissues from the feeding trial were collected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until extraction. Target tissues: liver. Pooled QC was prepared later from equal aliquots of all extracts (see Extraction).</p>	<p>Approximately 50 ± 5 mg tissue was placed in a precooled tube and extracted with methanol:water = 4:1 (v/v) containing internal standards ( 2-Chloro-L-phenylalanine 0.02 mg/mL). Samples were ground for 6 min at −10 °C (50 Hz), sonicated for 30 min at 5 °C (40 kHz), precipitated at −20 °C for 30 min, and centrifuged at 13,000 g, 4 °C, 15 min. Supernatants were collected for LC-MS. A pooled QC sample was prepared by mixing 20 µL from each extract.</p>	<p>UPLC system: WATERS ACQUITY. Column: ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3, 100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm (Reverse-phase). Mobile phase A: water/acetonitrile 95/5 (v/v) with 0.1% formic acid. Mobile phase B: acetonitrile/isopropanol/water 47.5/47.5/5 (v/v/v) with 0.1% formic acid. Flow rate 0.4 mL min-1; column temperature 45 °C; injection volume 10 µL</p>	<p>Instrument: AB SCIEX TripleTOF 6600 with ESI. Polarity: positive and negative (recorded as two assays). Full-scan range m/z 50–1200. Source settings: IonSpray +5500 V / −4500 V, Source Temp 500 °C, Gas1 50 psi, Gas2 50 psi, CUR 35 psi. Declustering potential DP 80 V. MS/MS collision energy CE 40 ± 20 eV (rolling/stepped). Interface heater: ON.</p>	<p>Raw SCIEX files (.wiff + .wiff.scan) were processed in Progenesis QI (v3.0). The pipeline included peak detection, de-isotoping/adduct grouping, retention-time alignment, and feature integration. Features were filtered by data-quality rules (presence in a sufficient proportion of samples and QCs). Missing values were imputed using the minimum-value approach. Intensities were total-ion normalization (TIC). Features with QC-RSD &gt; 30% were removed. Prior to statistics the data were log10-transformed and autoscaled (mean-centered, unit variance) for multivariate analysis.</p>	<p>Putative annotation was performed in Progenesis QI by matching precursor masses (tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS fragments against HMDB, METLIN, and an in-house library, with isotope/adduct patterns considered. Where available, identification confidence followed the MSI framework: Level 2 (MS/MS library match without authentic standard), Level 3 (putative class), and Level 1 if matched to an authentic standard (none unless otherwise stated). Reported IDs include database accession, adduct form, retention time, and match score.</p>

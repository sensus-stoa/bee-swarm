Study Title	Exploring the molecular intersection for hypertension, hyperlipidemia and their comorbid conditions through multi-omics approaches
Study Description	<p>Abstract</p><p>Background: Hypertension and hyperlipidemia are interconnected conditions that heighten cardiovascular risk, yet their intricate multi-scale molecular signatures remain inadequately mapped. This study aimed to conduct an integrated multi-omics investigation to unravel the key pathways and biomarkers underlying hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and both conditions.&nbsp;</p><p>Methods: Metabolomic analysis was performed on serum samples and metagenomic analysis on fecal samples collected from individuals with hypertension (n = 16), hyperlipidemia (n = 19), or both conditions concurrently (n = 20). In addition, 20 healthy individuals were recruited as controls.</p><p>Results: Metabolomics uncovered altered levels of sphingolipids, phosphatidylcholines, glycylprolines, and nucleic acid metabolites, which may be associated with changes in vascular tone, lipid and protein homeostasis, and thyroid signaling. Metagenomics showed depletion in the abundance of the Fibrobacteres phylum. Altered abundances of Escherichia coli and Bacteroides vulgatus were also observed, which were correlated with deviations in lipid and carbohydrate metabolism. Sphingomyelin d18:1/16:0 and sphingomyelin d18:1/24:1(15Z) were the key metabolites that were identified as potential diagnostic biomarkers across conditions. Microbial taxa such as Enterococcus cecorum, Lachnospiraceae bacterium, Prevotella histicola, and Flavobacterium discriminated these diseases. Pathway analysis revealed glycoxylate, amino acid, purine, and sphingolipid metabolism alterations intersecting hypertension and hyperlipidemia.&nbsp;</p><p>Conclusions: This multi-omics landscape of comorbid disease pathways and biomarkers lays the foundation for precision diagnosis and treatment of prevalent cardiovascular conditions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fasting blood samples were collected between 7:00 and 8:00 a.m., with participants required to fast and abstain from water for 8–12 hours prior to sampling. Study participants were instructed to avoid taking probiotics or antibiotics within one month before sample collection. The collected blood samples were centrifuged at 3,500 rpm for 15 minutes on site to separate the supernatant, which was used for biochemical assays and metabolite analysis. All samples were stored at –80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>100 μL of sample was transferred to an EP tube. After the addition of 400 μL of extract solution (acetonitrile: methanol = 1: 1, containing isotopically-labelled internal</p><p>standard mixture), the samples were vortexed for 30 s, sonicated for 10 min in ice-water</p><p>bath, and incubated for 1 h at -40&nbsp;to precipitate proteins. Then the sample was</p><p>centrifuged at 12000 rpm(RCF=13800(×g),R= 8.6cm) for 15 min at 4 . The resulting</p><p>supernatant was transferred to a fresh glass vial for analysis. The quality control (QC)</p><p>sample was prepared by mixing an equal aliquot of the supernatants from all of the samples.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher</p><p>Scientific) with a UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to Q Exactive</p><p>HFX mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted of 25 mmol/L</p><p>ammonium acetate and 25 ammonia hydroxide in water (pH = 9.75) (A) and acetonitrile (B). The auto-sampler temperature was 4 , and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The QE HFX mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on</p><p>information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software</p><p>(Xcalibur, Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the full</p><p>scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following: sheath gas flow rate as</p><p>30 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 25 Arb, capillary temperature 350 , full MS resolution as</p><p>60000, MS/MS resolution as 7500, collision energy as 10/30/60 in NCE mode, spray Voltage as</p><p>3.6 kV (positive) or -3.2 kV (negative), respectively.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed with an in-house program, which was developed using R and based on XCMS, for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration.</p>	<p>Then an in-house MS2 database (BiotreeDB) was applied in metabolite annotation. The cutoff for annotation was set at 0.3.</p>

Study Title	Artemisiae Scopariae Herba (Yinchen) suppresses ferroptosis in osteoporosis mice via the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway
Study Description	<p _msttexthash='40138319' _msthash='329'>With the aging of the population, the incidence of postmenopausal osteoporosis (PMOP) is increasing. Artemisiae Scopariae Herba (Yinchen, YC) extracts have been reported to promote osteogenic differentiation and bone formation, but the specific mechanism is still unclear. Aim of the study: To investigate the effect and mechanism of YC on PMOP. Methods: Ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry was used to determine the main potential mechanism of YC, and an ovariectomized (OVX) mouse model was established to evaluate the effects of YC on PMOP and to explore its potential mechanisms. First, the therapeutic effect of YC on PMOP was assessed by CT bone analysis, pathological observation and ELISA detection. Combined with serum ELISA, qRT-PCR and immunohistochemical staining, the potential key anti-PMOP pathway of YC was further explored.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Remove the sample from the -80 ℃ refrigerator and thaw it until there are no ice cubes in the sample.</p>	<p>After thawing on ice, the samples were vortexed for 10 s to ensure homogeneity. An aliquot of 100 μL of each sample was transferred to a 2.0 mL centrifuge tube and mixed with 100 μL of an extraction solution containing 70% methanol and internal standards. The mixture was vigorously vortexed for 15 min to facilitate extraction, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 3 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was carefully collected, passed through a 0.22 μm microporous membrane filter, and transferred to an injection vial for subsequent LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The sample extracts were analyzed using an UPLC-ESI-MS/MS system (UPLC, ExionLC™ AD, https://sciex.com.cn/) and Tandem mass spectrometry system (https://sciex.com.cn/). The analytical conditions were as follows, UPLC: column, Agilent SB-C18 (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm); The mobile phase was consisted of solvent A, pure water with 0.1% formic acid, and solvent B, acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. Sample measurements were performed with a gradient program that employed the starting conditions of 95% A, 5% B. Within 9 min, a linear gradient to 5% A, 95% B was programmed, and a composition of 5% A, 95% B was kept for 1 min. Subsequently, a composition of 95% A, 5.0% B was adjusted within 1.1 min and kept for 2.9 min. The flow velocity was set as 0.35 mL per minute; The column oven was set to 40°C; The injection volume was 2 μL. The effluent was alternatively connected to an ESI-triple quadrupole-linear ion trap (QTRAP)-MS.</p>	<p> The ESI source operation parameters were as follows: source temperature 550°C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (positive ion mode)/-4500 V (negative ion mode); ion source gas I (GSI), gas II(GSII), curtain gas (CUR) were set at 50, 60, and 25 psi, respectively; the collision-activated dissociation(CAD) was high. QQQ scans were acquired as MRM experiments with collision gas (nitrogen) set to medium. DP(declustering potential) and CE(collision energy) for individual MRM transitions was done with further DP and CE optimization. A specific set of MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the metabolites eluted within this period.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard software. Subsequent preprocessing, including peak detection, retention time alignment, and correction, was performed using the XCMS package in R. Metabolic features with a missing rate exceeding 50% in any group were excluded from further analysis. The remaining missing values were imputed using the k-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm. Intensity drift correction was then applied using Support Vector Regression (SVR) methodology.</p>	<p>Putative metabolite identification was conducted through a multi-tiered approach: (1) matching against an in-house spectral database; (2) querying public repositories including HMDB and METLIN; (3) consulting in silico prediction databases; and (4) applying metDNA analysis for network-assisted annotation.</p><p>Identifications were filtered based on the following criteria: a composite identification score ≥ 0.5 and a coefficient of variation (CV) &lt; 50% in quality control samples. For metabolites detected in both positive and negative ionization modes, we retained the entry demonstrating the highest identification confidence level and smallest CV value. The final curated dataset was exported to all_sample_data.xlsx for subsequent statistical analysis.</p>

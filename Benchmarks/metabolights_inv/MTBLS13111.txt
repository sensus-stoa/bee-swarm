Study Title	Mutations-Upstream-of-Metabolomic-Signature Based Functional Analyses Identify Prognostic Biomarkers and Therapeutic Targets in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma
Study Description	<p>Cross-talk among downstream regulatory networks of genome mutations has been the major hypothetical mechanism underlying poor prognosis and low-responsive rates for targeted therapy of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). By applying a causal inference-based mutation-upstream-of-the-metabolomic-signature (MUMS), we demonstrated a potential application of prognostic relevant serum metabolomic signature as an indicator to aggregate crosstalk among respective regulatory network downstream of genome mutations, and their collective interference on tumor progression. Moreover, a feature selection of 9-serum metabolites panel also highlights the clinical potential for predicting survival outcomes across multiple PDAC cohorts. Notably, integrating causal inference-based MUMS analysis identified and functional verified GRPEL1 as a novel tumour promoting gene, which shares a common down-stream metabolic signature with mTOR/PI3K/Akt signaling, sensitizing PDAC cells to mTOR inhibition-induced proliferation arrest. Our MUMS based functional cross-talk analysis provided proof-of-concept evidence that serum metabolic signatures reflect crosstalk among tumour mutational landscape, and such co-regulations would provide a novel aspect for identifying neo-targets for targeted therapy and rational based combined therapy design.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>From 2017 to 2022, consecutive samples were collected from patients with PDAC at two independent centres, namely, Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (CICAMS) and Peking Union Medical College Hospital. A final diagnosis of pancreatic cancer was made by a pathologist based on histological examination (either on a resection specimen or using pancreatic fine-needle aspiration), and patients without PDAC were excluded. Those who had received radiation or chemotherapy or had a history of other cancers were also excluded. PDAC was staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer and the International Union for Cancer Control tumour size, node, and metastasis (TNM) staging system. All patients with PDAC enrolled in this study were followed for at least 24 months to obtain the OS. Blood samples were collected prior to cancer treatment. Briefly, pre-treatment blood samples were obtained after overnight fasting using evacuated blood collection tubes (BD, NJ, America) without anticoagulant. Whole blood was incubated at room temperature for 60 min, followed by centrifugation at 1,500 × g for 15 min (4–8°C) to obtain serum. The serum-containing supernatant was mixed in a new centrifugation tube through inversion, split into 0.5-mL aliquots, and stored at -80 °C in 2 mL screw-cap tubes. The samples were then shipped on dry ice to an analytical laboratory.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 60 μL of previously thawed serum through the addition of 240 μL of acetonitrile:isopropanol (3:1 by volume, Thermo Fisher), followed by the addition of 60 μL of ammonium formate (0.5 g/mL, Thermo Fisher) and 6 μL of internal standard solution containing 100 µg/mL I-tyrosine-(phenyl-3, 5-d2) (Sigma-Aldrich), 10 µg/mL 13C-cholic acid (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories), and 60 µg/mL doxercalciferol (Med Chem Express) to precipitate serum proteins. After vortexing for 4 min and centrifugation at 17,949 × g for 5 min, 200 μL of the supernatant was transferred to another tube and ice-dried using Centrivap cold-trap centrifugation at -60 ℃. Finally, the dried metabolite extracts were reconstituted with 75 μL of 55% methanol (Thermo Fisher) containing 0.1% formic acid (Thermo Fisher). Equal volumes of serum derived from each patient in the matched tissue-serum cohort were pooled to obtain a QC sample. Each batch contained three QC samples. The accuracy and precision of the semi-quantitative untargeted metabolomic profiling were estimated as described in the data analysis section.Please update this protocol description</p>	<p>The metabolic extracts were analysed using reversed-phase liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (MS) in both positive- and negative-ionisation modes. Five microliters of the sample were injected into the Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to an UltiMate 3000 UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a CORTECS (Waters) C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.6 μm). The column temperature was maintained at 35 °C with a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phases consisted of (A) acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid (FA) and (B) water containing 0.1% FA (Merck Millipore). The following gradient elution program was applied: 5% A (initial), increased linearly to 45% A from 0.5 to 14 min, to 75% A at 32 min, to 80% A at 42 min, and then to 100% A from 50 to 55 min, followed by a return to 5% A and holding for 5 min for column re-equilibration. Data were acquired within the mass/charge ratio (m/z) range of 130–1200 Da at a resolution of 700,000 in the full MS scan mode. To acquire reliable peaks, we retained values up to one decimal place for retention times and three decimal places for m/z data to aggregate the metabolites. To filter out background signals, metabolites with an abundance &lt;50000 in all patients or zero abundance in &gt;85% of patients were filtered out. To eliminate batch-to-batch differences, an R preprocess Core software package (v1.47.1) was used for robust multiarray average (RMA) normalisation, and the abundance ratio of metabolites was calculated. The coefficient of variation (CV) for each metabolic feature was calculated based on its abundance in QC samples from each batch. Metabolites with a CV &gt;30% were also excluded.Please update this protocol description</p>	<p>MS/MS spectra of the QC samples were acquired at different fragmentation energies (25 and 50 NCE) for the highest 10 parent ions. Metabolite annotation was performed as previously described, with some modifications1. Briefly, the QC MS1/MS2 spectra were imported into the MS-DIAL 4.24 program and searched against public databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, https://hmdb.ca/), and MassBank (https://massbank.eu/MassBank/). Finally, a manual check of the reference database was performed to confirm and distinguish between similar readouts. For metabolites with MS/MS data that could not be reliably acquired, the exact m/z values were checked in relation to the Metlin, HMDB, and BioML databases to determine their potential identities, and the cutoff was set to &lt;10 ppm2. The confidence levels for metabolite annotation were as follows: the highest confidence identification, a validated identification (Level 1), confirms a structure with a minimum of two orthogonal properties from a pure reference standard acquired under identical analytical conditions. A lack of reference standard acquisition but predictive or externally acquired structure evidence, namely MS/MS data, exhibiting diagnostic fragments or neutral losses consistent with a specific structure would be considered a putative identification (Level 2). Preliminary identifications (Level 3) arise when accurate mass and isotopic distribution patterns produce tentative structures from database searches. Molecular formula candidates (Level 4) and a deconvoluted experimental m/z (Level 5) complete the less confident annotation classifications.</p>

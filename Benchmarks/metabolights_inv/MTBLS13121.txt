Study Title	Multi-omics Mechanistic Analysis of Efficient Lignocellulose Degradation by Pholiota adiposa Strain YAHS
Study Description	<p>Abstract This study focused on elucidating the lignocellulose degradation mechanism of Pholiota adiposa strain YAHS, aiming to provide theoretical basis and microbial resources for straw biorefining. Using the aniline blue-guaiacol plate screening method, 11 fungal strains were isolated from the Loess Plateau of northern Shaanxi. The highly efficient degrading strain P. adiposa YAHS was identified through DNS-based enzyme activity assays for cellulase and ligninase, combined with ITS sequence analysis. Whole-genome sequencing was performed using a hybrid approach integrating Illumina NovaSeq and Nanopore MinION platforms. Transcriptome-wide differential gene expression analysis was conducted via DESeq2, and untargeted metabolomics was carried out using UPLC-QTOF-MS. Multi-omics data were integrated to dissect the degradation pathways. Results showed that the genome of P. adiposa YAHS is 55.2 Mb in size, encoding 719 carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes), with glycoside hydrolases (GHs) accounting for 37.4%. Multi-omics analysis revealed that this strain degrades lignocellulose into carbohydrates such as monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and sugar alcohols through key enzymatic genes (e.g., exoglucanase, β-glucosidase, β-xylosidase, β-mannanase, monooxygenase) and metabolic pathways (e.g., sucrose/starch metabolism, fructose/mannose metabolism, anthranilate degradation). we preliminarily elucidated the lignocellulose degradation mechanism of fungi in the genus Pholiota through integrated multi-omics analysis, revealed the critical roles of key cellulolytic enzymes in this process, and provided important microbial resources and theoretical support for the development of novel biorefining technologies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The original sample of Pholiota adiposa strain YAHS was fungal fruiting bodies, collected from Laoshan Forest Park in Ganquan County, Yan'an City, Shaanxi Province (Yan'an area is located between 35°21′N-37°31′N latitude and 107°41′E-110°31′E longitude, with a terrain that is higher in the northwest and lower in the southeast, an average altitude of approximately 1200 meters, and a plateau continental monsoon climate). After a three-step disinfection process (soaking in 20% benzalkonium bromide solution for 5 minutes, 2% sodium hypochlorite for 3 minutes, and 70% ethanol for 1 minute, followed by rinsing with sterile water 5 times), the samples were inoculated onto a fungal fermentation medium, and cultured upright at 30°C until mycelia formed. Pure strains were obtained through multiple purifications using the streak plate method, and then preserved in the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC 5.2265). The wheat straw used in the experiment was purchased from Surui Agricultural Products Co., Ltd., which was washed, crushed through a 40-mesh sieve, and dried at 60°C to a constant weight for later use. The samples for metabolomic sequencing were mycelia cultured at 37°C for 3 days (with 3 biological replicates set: YAHS1-3 for the wheat straw carbon source group and YAHS-ctr1-3 for the glucose carbon source group), which were directly sent for sequencing.</p>	<p>In the metabolomic analysis of Pholiota adiposa strain YAHS, the metabolite extraction and pre-analysis sample preparation strictly followed standardized procedures, and quality control (QC) samples were set up simultaneously to ensure data reliability. Firstly, sample culture and collection were grouped based on two carbon sources: the experimental group using wheat straw as the lignocellulosic carbon source (YAHS1-3, with 3 biological replicates) and the control group using glucose as the easily utilizable carbon source (YAHS-ctr1-3, with 3 biological replicates). The strains were all cultured at 37°C for 3 days until reaching the vigorous growth stage, after which the mycelia were collected as the original samples for metabolite extraction.</p><p>The metabolite extraction adopted the mixed solvent method with methanol-water-chloroform (3:1:1, v/v/v). The specific steps were as follows: the collected mycelia were fully mixed with the mixed solvent, then subjected to ultrasonic disruption at 40 kHz for 30 minutes to ensure complete cell lysis and release of internal metabolites; after ultrasonic treatment, the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000×g for 10 minutes to separate the supernatant (containing target metabolites) from the precipitate (cell debris); finally, the supernatant was freeze-dried to remove the solvent, obtaining lyophilized metabolite powder, which served as the sample to be tested for subsequent ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-QTOF-MS) analysis.</p><p>To monitor the stability of the analysis process and the reliability of data, QC samples were prepared in the experiment: equal volumes of metabolite extraction supernatants from all experimental groups (YAHS1-3) and control groups (YAHS-ctr1-3) were mixed to prepare QC samples. These QC samples followed the identical extraction, freeze-drying, and UPLC-QTOF-MS detection procedures as the samples to be tested. They were used to evaluate the detection accuracy of the instrument (such as the repeatability of retention time and peak intensity) and the consistency of detection across different batches, thereby verifying the reliability of the experimental data.</p>	<p>In this study, an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system (Agilent 1290 InfinityII LC, Agilent Technologies, Inc., USA) was used for metabolite separation, equipped with a ZORBAX Eclipse Plus C18 column (specifications: 1.8 μm, 2.1×100 mm, Agilent Technologies, Inc., USA). The mobile phase consisted of Phase A (ultrapure water containing 0.1% formic acid, v/v) and Phase B (acetonitrile, chromatographic grade). The gradient elution program was set as follows: the proportion of Phase B was linearly increased from 5% to 95% within 0–10 minutes. The column temperature was controlled at room temperature (no specific temperature was clearly specified, and it was set to conform to the conventional operating temperature range of the instrument by default). The flow rate was set to 0.3 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL. All mobile phases were filtered through a 0.22 μm organic phase filter membrane and subjected to ultrasonic degassing before use.</p>	<p>For mass spectrometry (MS) detection, a quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (QTOF-MS), which is coupled with the Agilent 1290 InfinityII LC (Agilent Technologies, Inc., USA), was used. The ion source was an electrospray ionization (ESI) source, and the positive ion mode (ESI+) was selected as the ionization mode. The MS parameters were set as follows: the m/z scanning range was 50–1200; the temperature of the drying gas (nitrogen) was 350°C, and the flow rate of the drying gas was 10 L/min; the nebulizer gas pressure was 45 psi; the capillary voltage was 4000 V; the cone voltage was 60 V; the scanning rate was 2 spectra/s, and the full-scan mode was adopted for data acquisition. Data acquisition and preliminary processing were performed using MassHunter Workstation software (Agilent Technologies, Inc.).</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were first subjected to quality control using Fastp software (v0.23.2), which included filtering low-quality reads (bases with a Q value &lt; 20), removing adapter sequences and duplicate sequences to ensure data reliability. Subsequently, Agilent MassHunter Workstation software was used for peak extraction, peak alignment, and peak area normalization. These processes converted the raw mass spectrometry signals into a quantifiable metabolite feature table, which contains information on retention time, m/z values, and corresponding peak intensities. For the subsequent differential metabolite analysis, further data standardization (adopting auto-scaling) was performed via the MetaboAnalyst 5.0 online platform to eliminate the impact of concentration differences among different metabolites on the analysis results. Finally, a standardized data matrix for statistical analysis was obtained.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification adopted the strategy of 'feature matching + database comparison', with the specific workflow as follows: First, based on the metabolite features (retention time, accurate m/z value) obtained by UPLC-QTOF-MS detection, the MassHunter Workstation software was used to match with the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG, Release 107), and the open-source Metabolomics Database (Metlin) to initially screen candidate metabolites. Second, the metabolite structure was further verified by combining the secondary mass spectrometry fragment information of differential metabolites (if available) and the metabolomic characteristics of Pholiota strains reported in the literature. Finally, differential metabolites were identified based on the Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) model (screening criteria: R²Y &gt; 0.9, Q² &gt; 0.5), Variable Importance in Projection (VIP &gt; 1), and statistical significance (P &lt; 0.05). For example, the identified lignin degradation intermediates (5-carboxyvanillic acid, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde) and carbohydrate metabolites (xylose, xylitol, sedoheptulose, etc.) were all confirmed by comparing with the standard information in the databases through the above-mentioned method.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS13128
Study Title	Identification of Cetobacterium somerae as a key contributor to muscle quality improvement through intestinal microbiota transplantation from faba bean-fed donors in Yellow River carp (Cyprinus carpio)
Study Description	<p>Background&nbsp;The gut microbiota plays a significant role in modulating the growth and function of host muscle, and microbiota transplantation experiments provide compelling evidence of its capacity to improve muscle quality. Feeding faba beans improves the muscle quality of Yellow River carp. However, the changes in gut microbiota, along with the specific microorganisms, metabolic pathways, and regulatory mechanisms linked to the enhancement of muscle quality following faba bean consumption remain to be elucidated.</p><p>Results&nbsp;After a 6-week feeding trial with faba beans, growth performance decreased, but muscle texture improved (P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05). Gut microbiota structure also changed, with increased relative abundances of&nbsp;Aeromonas,&nbsp;ZOR0006,&nbsp;Cetobacterium, and&nbsp;Atopobium. Following 8 weeks of whole-intestinal microbiota transplantation (WIMT) from faba bean-fed donors to basal diet-fed recipients of Yellow River carp, growth performance remained unchanged (P&nbsp;&gt; 0.05), while muscle texture improved (P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05). This improvement was mainly due to increased small-diameter muscle fibers, higher collagen&nbsp;levels, and reduced muscle fat content (P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05), which partially replicated the muscle texture of donor fish.&nbsp;Moreover, WIMT promotes intestinal structure and barrier integrity, with significant changes in gut microbiota structure and metabolic profile. WIMT improved the muscle quality of Yellow River carp by regulating mitochondrial autophagy and adipocytokine signaling pathways through the gut-muscle axis.&nbsp;Cetobacterium somerae&nbsp;(C. somerae) and its metabolites, such as acetic acid, played a crucial role in this process. Further feeding experiments demonstrated that&nbsp;C. somerae&nbsp;and acetic acid reduced the crude fat content of muscle while increasing the crude protein and collagen (P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05).&nbsp;C. somerae&nbsp;also mitigated muscle protein degradation under inflammatory and enhanced collagen (P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05), thereby improving muscle texture.</p><p>Conclusion&nbsp;This study establishes that gut microbiota enhance muscle quality in Yellow River carp through WIMT, identifies&nbsp;C. somerae&nbsp;and its metabolite acetic acid as key contributors.&nbsp;The findings provide novel evidence for fish gut-muscle axis research and offer new scientific basis for improving Yellow River carp muscle quality.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The experimental fish were starved for 12 hours followed by anesthesia with MS-222. After dissection, intestinal contents were harvested and promptly flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for metabolomics.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The samples (1g) were placed in the EP tubes and and resuspended with prechilled 80%</p><p>methanol by well vortex; Then the samples were incubated on ice for 5 min and</p><p>centrifuged at 15,000 g, 4°C for 15min, collected the supernatant, centrifuged at</p><p>15,000g and 4°C for 20 minutes repeatedly; Finally, the solution was injected into the</p><p>LC-MS/MS system analysis.</p>	<p>UHPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system</p><p>(ThermoFisher, Germany) coupled with an Orbitrap Q ExactiveTM HF mass</p><p>spectrometer, Orbitrap Q ExactiveTM HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Germany) in Novogene Co., Ltd. (Beijing,China). Samples were injected onto a Hypersil Goldcolumn (100×2.1 mm, 1.9μm)</p><p>using a 12-min linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The eluents for the</p><p>positive and negative polarity modes were eluent A (0.1% FA in Water) and eluent B</p><p>(Methanol).The solvent gradient was set as follows: 2% B, 1.5 min; 2-85% B, 3 min;</p><p>85-100% B, 10 min; 100-2% B, 10.1 min; 2% B, 12 min.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Q ExactiveTM HF mass</p><p>spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with spray voltage of</p><p>3.5 kV, capillary temperature of 320°C, sheath gas flow rate of 35 psi and aux gas</p><p>flow rate of 10 L/min, S-lens RF level of 60, Aux gas heater temperature of 350°C.</p>	<p>The data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed by XCMS to perform</p><p>peak alignment, peak picking, and quantitation for each metabolite. Then, based on</p><p>adduct ions and setting mass deviation to 10 ppm, a comparison was made between</p><p>these data and the self-built high-quality secondary spectrum database (NovoMetDB)</p><p>to obtain results for metabolite identification. After eliminating background ions</p><p>according to blank samples, the original quantitative results were normalized by the</p><p>following formula to obtain relative peak areas: Relative peak areas = Raw</p><p>quantitative value of samples/ (The sum of quantitative value of samples/ The sum of</p><p>quantitative value of QC1); Compounds with a coefficient of variation (CV) of</p><p>relative peak areas in QC samples greater than 30% were removed.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Finally, the identification and relative quantitation results of metabolites were obtained. Data</p><p>processing is based on the Linux operating system (CentOS version 6.6), using R and</p><p>Python. For details on specific packages and software versions used, please refer to</p><p>the README file included in the results.</p>

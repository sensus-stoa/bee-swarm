Study Title	Comprehensive Metabolite Characterization of Beejamrit Using GC–MS and LC–MS
Study Description	<p>Indian natural farming practices which are mainly based on various bioformulations emerged as a sustainable alternative to conventional farming. Beejamrit is one such cow-based biostimulant which is used as a seed treatment to enhance seed germination, seed vigor, and resistance to pathogens. In this study untargeted metabolomics (GC-MS and LC-MS) were employed to comprehensively characterize the metabolic profiles of beejamrit samples. GC-MS analysis identified organoheterocyclic compounds (23%) as a most abundant metabolite class followed by organic acids and derivatives (18%) and benzenoids (15%). In LC-MS analysis, lipids and lipid-like molecules (26%) were highly abundant followed by organoheterocylclics (18%), and organic acids compounds (18%). Furthermore, GC-MS and LC-MS analysis identified a wide range of metabolites, including amino acids, organic acids, phenolics, and fatty acids, many of which are associated with antioxidants, antimicrobial, signaling and plant growth promoting functions. </p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples of Beejamrit were collected from Sardarkrushinagar Dantiwada Agricultural University and natural farming practitioners in Gujarat, India. All bioformulations were prepared following natural farming protocols with some modifications. For each bioformulation, samples were collected, transported on ice, stored at -20 °C and processed in triplicate</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>The 200 μL of each sample was mixed with 1000 μL of solvent (ethyl acetate and methanol (1:1)), vortexed for 3 min, and sonicated for 15 min. After centrifugation (3000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), supernatants were dried in a Speedvac vacuum concentrator. Samples were derivatized using BSTFA (N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide) with 1% TMCS (Trimethylchlorosilane) at 70 °C for 1 h.</p><p><br></p><p>LC-MS</p><p>For each sample, 200 µL was mixed with 1000 µL of a multi-solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water; 2:2:1), sonicated (20 min), centrifuged (12,000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 µm syringe filter. A diluted aliquot (450 µL acetonitrile + 50 µL filtered sample) was used for LC-MS.</p>	<p>GC-MS Instrument: Clarus 680 GC</p><p>LC-MS Instrument: Agilent 1290 Infinity II</p><p><br></p><p>Column GC: Elite-5MS</p><p>Column LC: Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (3.0 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm)</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>GC-MS was performed on a PerkinElmer Clarus 680 GC coupled with a Clarus SQ8C MS using an Elite-5 ms column. The oven temperature program set as 60 °C (2 min) followed by 300 °C (10 °C/min), and 300 °C for 6 min. A 1 µL sample was injected (1:50 split ratio) at 250 °C. MS scanned from 50-600 m/z between 5-32 min.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>LC-MS</p><p>LC-MS performed on a Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (3.0 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm) with a 0.3 mL/min flow rate. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid (FA) in water and Mobile phase B was 0.1% FA in acetonitrile. UHPLC run was carried out in gradient mode: 2% B (0-1 min), 30% B (2-10 min), 60% B (11 min), 95% B (13-25 min), followed by 1 min post run wash. Flush port was kept for 15 s and needle was washed for 15 min before each injection.&nbsp;The oven temperature was kept at 40 °C and injection volume was 5 µL. The MS parameters: positive ion mode, 50-1200 m/z; gas temperature: 250 °C; drying gas: 10 L/min; sheath gas: 350 °C, 10 L/min; nebulizer: 35 psi; capillary voltage: 3000 V; nozzle voltage: 1000 V</p>	<p>msconvert Proteowizard was used to convert raw files to .mzML files</p>	<p>Annotation of GC-MS metabolites was done using MS-DIAL with NIST2014 library</p><p>Annotation of LC-MS metabolites was done using XCMS online using inbuilt METLIN library</p>

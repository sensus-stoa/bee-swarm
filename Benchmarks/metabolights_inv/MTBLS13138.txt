Study Title	Multi-omics uncovers formononetin's protection against antibiotic-induced liver injury via microbiota-metabolite-transcriptome coordination
Study Description	<p>Tetracycline antibiotics (TETs) cause hepatotoxicity via gut-liver axis disruption and oxidative stress. This study evaluated whether formononetin (FMN), a natural isoflavone, alleviates TET-induced liver injury through microbiota-metabolite-transcriptome coordination. Using network toxicology and pharmacology, we predicted key targets and pathways involved. A mouse model of TET-induced injury (150 mg/kg, 3 days) was treated with FMN (50 or 100 mg/kg) for 14 days. Multi-omics analyses revealed that FMN attenuated hepatic steatosis, reduced ALT/AST levels, enhanced gut microbial diversity (enriched Limosilactobacillus), and restored intestinal barrier integrity. Mechanistically, FMN activated the Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathway, modulated PPARα/CYP2E1-dependent lipid metabolism, and inhibited ferroptosis. FMN demonstrates multi-target hepatoprotection by coordinating microbiota restoration, oxidative stress reduction, and metabolic reprogramming, supporting its potential as a dietary intervention against antibiotic-related liver damage.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male ICR mice (4 weeks old, body weight range 18–22 g) were purchased from the Yangzhou University (Yangzhou, China). All experiments complied with animal welfare guidelines. Post-acclimatization (7 days), mice were stratified into four group by random number assignment: Control group (CN), received daily oral gavage of deionized water; Model group (Model): Administered deionized water by gavage; Low-dose FMN group (FMNL): Treated with 50 mg/kg FMN solution daily; High-dose FMN group (FMNH): Treated with 100 mg/kg FMN solution daily. Formononetin have been used in this study purchased from Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) (FMN, purity 98%). After 14 days of treatment, the DILI model was induced starting from day 12. The CN group received an intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of sterile saline (0.1 mL/10 g body weight), while the other groups were injected with tetracycline solution (150 mg/kg, 0.1 mL/10 g body weight). On day 15, blood was collected via retro-orbital bleeding under ether anesthesia liver samples were harvested for further analysis.</p>	<p>Biological specimens were processed using optimized extraction methodologies: tissue samples (100 mg) underwent cryogenic homogenization in 500 μL methanol/water (80:20), followed by phase separation (15,000g, 20 min, 4°C) and solvent adjustment to 53% methanol.

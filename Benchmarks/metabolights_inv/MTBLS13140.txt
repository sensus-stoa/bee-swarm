Study Title	Astaxanthin Protects Against Ferroptosis and Oxidative Stress via Retinoic Acid-Mediated SLC7A11 Regulation by Lepagella muris
Study Description	<p>Oxidative stress and ferroptosis are central to the pathogenesis of many diseases, including those characterized by intestinal damage, where excessive lipid peroxidation and iron accumulation trigger cellular dysfunction. In this study, we investigate the protective effects of astaxanthin (ASTA), a potent antioxidant carotenoid, against dexamethasone (DEX)-induced oxidative stress and ferroptosis in the intestine. We demonstrate that ASTA supplementation significantly restores antioxidant defenses, enhancing key antioxidant enzymes. Furthermore, ASTA alleviates ferroptosis by upregulating GPX4 expression and reducing iron accumulation, thereby mitigating cellular damage. Using 16S rRNA sequencing and metagenomic analysis, we identify shifts in gut microbiota composition induced by ASTA, with a notable enrichment of Lepagella muris and other beneficial bacteria. These shifts correlate with the upregulation of retinol metabolism pathways, particularly those involving enzymes such as β-carotene monooxygenase 1 (BCMO1) and aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH). Metabolomic profiling further confirms that L. muris metabolizes ASTA to promote retinoic acid biosynthesis, which targets key ferroptosis-related genes, including SLC7A11, thereby inhibiting the activation of the ferroptosis pathway. RETINOIC ACID-mediated activation of SLC7A11 restores cellular redox balance, protecting against ferroptosis. Our findings provide mechanistic insights into how ASTA, through gut microbiota-mediated retinoic acid biosynthesis, modulates oxidative stress and ferroptosis pathways, offering a novel therapeutic approach for diseases associated with these processes. These results underscore the potential of microbiota-targeted interventions to enhance the efficacy of antioxidant therapies in treating oxidative stress-induced disorders.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mix 100μl of bacteria at a concentration of 22 - 550 × 100000 CFU/mL with 100μl of astaxanthin at a concentration of 15 μg/ml. Incubate under anaerobic conditions at 37°C for 24 hours. The medium containing only astaxanthin is defined as the blank control. Conduct metabolomic assays on the entire culture and the blank control.</p>	<p>Samples were extracted for metabolites by using the</p><p>&nbsp;Starlidworkstation. The 50 ul sample was mixed with 200 ul of extraction</p><p>&nbsp;solution(methanol:acetonitrile=1:1 (v/v), including internal standards),</p><p>&nbsp;shaken at 750 rpm for 5 min and settled down for 5 min, then filtered</p><p>&nbsp;through a protein precipitation plate and the filtrate was collected. The</p><p>&nbsp;quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing an equal aliquot of the</p><p>&nbsp;filtered liquid of samples</p>	<p>For polar metabolites, LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an</p><p>&nbsp;UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY</p><p>&nbsp;UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to Orbitrap Exploris</p><p>&nbsp;120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted</p><p>&nbsp;of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 ammonia hydroxide in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The auto-sampler temperature was 4 , and</p><p>&nbsp;the injection volume was 2 μL. The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer</p><p>&nbsp;was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent</p><p>&nbsp;acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur,</p><p>&nbsp;Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the</p><p>&nbsp;full scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following:</p><p>&nbsp;sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary</p><p>temperature 320 , full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000,</p><p>&nbsp;collision energy: SNCE 20/30/40, spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV</p><p>&nbsp;(negative), respectively.</p>	<p>For polar metabolites, LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an</p><p>&nbsp;UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY</p><p>&nbsp;UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to Orbitrap Exploris</p><p>&nbsp;120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted</p><p>&nbsp;of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 ammonia hydroxide in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The auto-sampler temperature was 4 , and</p><p>&nbsp;the injection volume was 2 μL. The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer</p><p>&nbsp;was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent</p><p>&nbsp;acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur,</p><p>&nbsp;Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the</p><p>&nbsp;full scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following:</p><p>&nbsp;sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary</p><p>temperature 320 , full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000,</p><p>&nbsp;collision energy: SNCE 20/30/40, spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV</p><p>&nbsp;(negative), respectively.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard</p><p>&nbsp;and processed with an in-house program. which was developed using R and</p><p>&nbsp;based on XCMS, for feature detection, extraction, alignment, and integration.</p><p>&nbsp;The R package and the BiotreeDB (V3.0) were applied in metabolite</p><p>&nbsp;identification.</p>	<p>&nbsp;In order to visualize group separation and find significantly changed</p><p>&nbsp;metabolites, supervised orthogonal projections to latent structures</p><p>discriminate analysis (OPLS-DA) was applied. Then, a 7-fold cross validation</p><p>&nbsp;was performed to calculate the value of R2 and Q2. R2 indicates how well the</p><p>&nbsp;variation of a variable is explained and Q2 means how well a variable could</p><p>&nbsp;be predicted. To check the robustness and predictive ability of the OPLS-DA</p><p>&nbsp;model, a 200 times permutations was further conducted. Afterward, the R2</p><p>&nbsp;and Q2 intercept values were obtained. Here, the intercept value of Q2</p><p>&nbsp;represents the robustness of the model, the risk of overfitting and the</p><p>&nbsp;reliability of the model, which will be the smaller the better.</p><p>&nbsp;Furthermore, the value of variable importance in the projection (VIP) of</p><p>&nbsp;the first principal component in OPLS-DA analysis was obtained. It</p><p>&nbsp;summarizes the contribution of each variable to the model. The metabolites</p><p>&nbsp;with VIP&gt;1 and p&lt;0.05 (student t test) were considered as significantly</p><p>&nbsp;changed metabolites. In addition, commercial databases including KEGG</p><p>&nbsp;(http://www.genome.jp/kegg/) and MetaboAnalyst</p><p>&nbsp;(http://www.metaboanalyst.ca/) were used for pathway enrichment analysis</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS13151
Study Title	Impact of dietary serine and glycine availability on plasma and tissue metabolite composition in mice
Study Description	<p>This study examines how altering systemic serine and glycine availability through dietary intervention affects breast cancer metastasis in vivo. Female NOD-SCID-gamma (NSG) mice were maintained on either a control amino acid–replete diet or a serine/glycine-enriched diet for two weeks before and during metastatic progression of human breast cancer cells. By integrating dietary manipulation with in vivo metastasis assays, this study tests whether changes in circulating and tissue nutrient levels can influence metastatic colonization and tumor growth across organs. These data provide insight into how nutrient availability shapes the metabolic environment and metastatic potential of cancer cells.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Plasma and tissue samples were collected from female NOD-SCID-gamma (NSG) mice (6–8 weeks old). Mice were randomly assigned to one of two dietary groups: a control amino acid–replete diet (TD.110839, Envigo) or a serine/glycine-enriched diet (TD.160575, Envigo). Diets were administered ad libitum for two weeks prior sample collection. Samples were collected at a fixed time of day to minimize circadian variation. Plasma was obtained by facial cheek bleed into EDTA-coated tubes and centrifuged at 800 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. Tissues were rapidly dissected, rinsed in cold saline, blotted dry, and snap frozen in liquid nitrogen. All plasma and tissues were stored at –80 °C until extraction and analysis by LC/MS.</p>	<p>Frozen tissues were ground into powder using a mortar and pestle and metabolites were extracted in 50 uL extraction mix (80% methanol + containing 500 nM 13C and 15N amino acid standards (Cambridge Isotope Laboratory, MSK-A2-1.2)) per mg of tissue. Samples were vortexed for 15 min, centrifuged at maximum speed for 10 min, dried down under nitrogen gas then stored at -80 °C. For analysis, samples were resuspended in HPLC-grade water and analyzed by LC/MS.</p><p>For plasma, 5 µL of fluid was extracted in 45 µL of 80% methanol containing 500 nM 13C and 15N amino acid standards (Cambridge Isotope Laboratory, MSK-A2-1.2). Samples were vortexed for 15 min, centrifuged at maximum speed for 10 min, and supernatant transferred to vials and analyzed by LC/MS.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Dionex UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). 2 μL of each sample was injected onto a SeQuant® ZIC®-pHILIC 150 x 2.1 mm analytical column equipped with a 2.1 x 20 mm guard column (both 5 mm particle size; EMD Millipore). Buffer A was 20 mM ammonium carbonate, 0.1% ammonium hydroxide; Buffer B was acetonitrile. The column oven and autosampler tray were held at 25 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The chromatographic gradient was run at a flow rate of 0.150 mL min-1 as follows: 0-20 min: linear gradient from 80-20% B; 20-20.5 min: linear gradient form 20-80% B; 20.5-28 min: hold at 80% B.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was conducted on a QExactive benchtop orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with an Ion Max source and a HESI II probe. The mass spectrometer was operated in full-scan, polarity-switching mode, with the spray voltage set to 3.0 kV, the heated capillary held at 275 °C, and the HESI probe held at 350 °C. The sheath gas flow was set to 40 units, the auxiliary gas flow was set to 15 units, and the sweep gas flow was set to 1 unit. MS data acquisition was performed in a range of m/z = 70-1000, with the resolution set at 70,000, the AGC target at 1x106, and the maximum injection time at 20 msec.</p>	<p>Raw LC/MS data were processed using XCalibur v2.2 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Peak detection and integration were performed using a mass accuracy tolerance of 5 ppm and a retention time window of 0.5 min. Peak areas were normalized to ^13C/^15N-labeled amino acid internal standards (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, MSK-A2-1.2). For plasma data, external standard pools were used to generate calibration curves and determine absolute concentrations, as described in Sullivan et al., eLife 2019 (PMID: 30990168).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed in XCalibur v2.2 using a 5 ppm mass accuracy and 0.5 min retention time window. Identification was based on matching experimental m/z and retention time to those of authentic external chemical standards analyzed under identical chromatographic conditions. For tissue samples, identities were confirmed using an in-house reference library of retention times and m/z values established from standards. Annotation confidence corresponds to MSI Level 1 (confirmed with authentic standards).</p>

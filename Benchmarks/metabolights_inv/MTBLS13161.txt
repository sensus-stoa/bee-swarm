Study Title	Multi-omics uncovers formononetin's protection against antibiotic-induced liver injury via microbiota-metabolite-transcriptome coordination
Study Description	<p>Tetracycline antibiotics (TETs) cause hepatotoxicity via gut-liver axis disruption and oxidative stress. This study evaluated whether formononetin (FMN), a natural isoflavone, alleviates TET-induced liver injury through microbiota-metabolite-transcriptome coordination. Using network toxicology and pharmacology, we predicted key targets and pathways involved. A mouse model of TET-induced injury (150 mg/kg, 3 days) was treated with FMN (50 or 100 mg/kg) for 14 days. Multi-omics analyses revealed that FMN attenuated hepatic steatosis, reduced ALT/AST levels, enhanced gut microbial diversity (enriched Limosilactobacillus), and restored intestinal barrier integrity. Mechanistically, FMN activated the Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathway, modulated PPARα/CYP2E1-dependent lipid metabolism, and inhibited ferroptosis. FMN demonstrates multi-target hepatoprotection by coordinating microbiota restoration, oxidative stress reduction, and metabolic reprogramming, supporting its potential as a dietary intervention against antibiotic-related liver damage.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male ICR mice (4 weeks old, body weight range 18–22 g) were purchased from the Yangzhou University (Yangzhou, China). All experiments complied with animal welfare guidelines. Post-acclimatization (7 days), mice were stratified into four group by random number assignment: Control group (CN), received daily oral gavage of deionized water; Model group (Model): Administered deionized water by gavage; Low-dose FMN group (FMNL): Treated with 50 mg/kg FMN solution daily; High-dose FMN group (FMNH): Treated with 100 mg/kg FMN solution daily. Formononetin have been used in this study purchased from Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) (FMN, purity 98%). After 14 days of treatment, the DILI model was induced starting from day 12. The CN group received an intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of sterile saline (0.1 mL/10 g body weight), while the other groups were injected with tetracycline solution (150 mg/kg, 0.1 mL/10 g body weight). On day 15, fecal samples were harvested for further analysis.</p>	<p>(1) After thawing the sample, weigh 20 mg of the sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube and add a small steel bead;</p><p>(2) Add 1000 μL of phosphoric acid solution (0.5%, v/v), grind with a 30 Hz ball mill for 1 minute, vortex at 2500 r/min for 10 minutes, sonicate at 4°C for 5 minutes, and centrifuge at 4°C and 12000 r/min for 10 minutes;</p><p>(3) Transfer 100 μL of the supernatant to a corresponding numbered 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, add 500 μL of MTBE extraction solution containing an internal standard, vortex at 2500 r/min for 3 minutes, sonicate at 4°C for 5 minutes, and centrifuge at 4°C and 12000 r/min for 10 minutes;</p><p>(4) Pipette 200 μL of the supernatant into an injection vial liner tube, store it in a -20°C refrigerator, and wait for GC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph coupled to a 7000D mass spectrometer with a DB-FFAP column (30 m length × 0.25 mm i.d. × 0.25 μm film thickness, J&amp;W Scientific, USA) was employed for GC-MS/MS analysis of SCFAs.</p>	<p>Helium was used as carrier gas, at a flow rate of 1.2 mL/min. Injection was made in the split mode with a split ratio 5:1 and the injection volume was 1 μL. The oven temperature was held at 50°C for 1 min, raised to 220°C at a rate of 18°C/min and held for 5 min. All samples were analyzed in multiple reaction monitoring mode. The injector inlet and transfer line temperature were 250 °C and 230 °C, respectively</p>	<p>Quantification is completed using the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode of triple quadrupole mass spectrometry, as shown in the figure below. In the MRM mode, the quadrupole first screens for precursor ions (parent ions) of the target substance, excluding ions corresponding to other molecular weights to preliminarily eliminate interference; after collision cell induced ionization, the precursor ion breaks into multiple fragment ions, which are then filtered through the triple quadrupole to select the required characteristic fragment ions, eliminating non-target ion interference and making quantification more precise and reproducible. After obtaining mass spectrometry analysis data from different samples, all chromatographic peaks of the target substances are integrated, and quantitative analysis is performed through a standard curve.</p>	<p>Quantification is completed using the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode of triple quadrupole mass spectrometry, as shown in the figure below.

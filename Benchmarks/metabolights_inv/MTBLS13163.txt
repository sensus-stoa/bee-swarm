Study Title	Multi-omics Analysis of Metabolic Characteristics and Validation of HBEGF-Mediated Regulatory Mechanisms of Metabolism during Catch-Up Growth in Small-for-Gestational-Age Infants
Study Description	<p>Small-for-gestational-age (SGA) is a globally recognized public health concern. Infants born SGA may experience metabolic disturbances. This study elucidated the key regulatory factors and mechanisms underlying catch-up growth (CUG) and metabolic homeostasis in SGA infants. High-throughput targeted metabolomics were employed to compare the serum profiles of SGA and appropriate-for-gestational-age (AGA) neonates, identifying key metabolic pathways and associated regulatory signaling pathways enriched with differentially expressed metabolites. Liver tissue samples were collected from neonatal SGA and AGA rats. Subsequently, mRNA sequencing to identify and analyze differentially expressed genes. Heparin-binding epidermal growth factor-like growth factor (HBEGF)— a gene critically involved in metabolic processes—was significantly upregulated in SGA rat liver tissues. HBEGF silencing can suppress CUG in SGA rats and influence carbohydrate and lipid metabolism. Furthermore, metabolomic profiling was performed to investigate the role of HBEGF in metabolic regulation during CUG in individuals with SGA. Finally, the impact of HBEGF on the AKT/GSK-3β (protein kinase B/glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta) signaling pathway was explored in both in vivo and in vitro settings. This study confirms metabolic differences between SGA and AGA individuals are evident at early-life. HBEGF may be crucial in CUG progression in SGA by modulating metabolic processes via AKT/GSK-3β signaling pathway.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>&nbsp;3–7 days after birth, with adequate feeding, an additional 1 mL blood was collected once per newborn during routine hospital testing at 9 am. Blood samples were collected using EDTA tubes and stored at 4°C prior to centrifugation at 4°C for 15 min at 3,000 rpm. The resulting plasma was aliquoted into Eppendorf tubes in 0.2 mL volumes and stored at 80°C until metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Liquid sample pretreatment steps:</p><p>1. Thaw the sample on ice and vortex for 30 seconds to mix.</p><p>2. Add 50 µL of the sample to a 2 mL EP tube, then add 250 µL of water and vortex to mix.</p><p>3. Add 1200 µL of liquid extraction solution (methanol:acetonitrile = 1:1, containing internal standard, pre-cooled at -40°C) and vortex to mix.</p><p>4. Ultrasonicate in an ice-water bath for 15 minutes, vortex to mix, and then let stand at -40°C for 2 hours.</p><p>5. Centrifuge at 12000 rpm (centrifugal force 13800(×g), radius 8.6 cm) at 4°C for 15 minutes. Transfer 1200 µL of the supernatant to a new EP tube and centrifuge to concentrate (8 hours) until dry.</p><p>6. Resuspend with 120 µL of acetonitrile:water, v:v = 6:4, vortex until the dry powder is dissolved, ultrasonicate in an ice-water bath for 30 seconds, then centrifuge at 12000 rpm (centrifugal force 13800(×g), radius 8.6 cm) at 4°C for 15 minutes.</p><p>7. Carefully transfer 70 µL of the supernatant to an LC injection vial and analyze on the instrument.</p>	<p>This project utilized an ACQUITY UPLC H-Class (Waters) ultra-performance liquid chromatograph to perform chromatographic separation of the target compounds through a Waters Atlantis Premier BEH Z-HILIC Column (1.This project utilized an ACQUITY UPLC H-Class (Waters) ultra-performance liquid chromatograph to perform chromatographic separation of the target compounds through a Waters Atlantis Premier BEH Z-HILIC Column (1.7 µm, 2.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 150 mm). The sample tray temperature was set at 8 , and the injection volume was 1 μL.</p>	<p>This project utilized an ACQUITY UPLC H-Class (Waters) ultra-performance liquid chromatograph to separate the target compounds through a Waters Atlantis Premier BEH Z-HILIC Column (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 150 mm). The A phase of the liquid chromatography was ultra-pure water: acetonitrile = 8:2, containing 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate, and the B phase was acetonitrile: ultra-pure water = 9:1, also containing 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate; both A and B phases were adjusted to pH 9 with ammonia water. The sample tray temperature was 8 , and the injection volume was 1 μL.</p><p>This project employed a SCIEX 6500 QTRAP+ triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an IonDrive Turbo V ESI ion source for mass spectrometry analysis in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The ion source parameters were as follows: Curtain Gas = 35 psi, IonSpray Voltage = +5000 V / -4500 V, Temperature = 500 , Ion Source Gas 1 = 50 psi, Ion Source Gas 2 = 50 psi.</p><p>All mass spectrometry data acquisition and quantitative analysis of target compounds in this project were accomplished through SCIEX Analyst Work Station Software (1.7.2) and Data Driven Flow (v-1.0.1).</p>	<p>The quantitative results of each target metabolite in the sample are shown in the attached table. C (Metabolite Concentration, nmol/L) represents the concentration of the target metabolite in the sample, which is equal to the final measured concentration C multiplied by the concentration factor CF in the pretreatment process and then multiplied by the final volume V (Final Volume, μL), and divided by the sample volume V (Volume, mL), with the unit of nmol/L. NA indicates that no target metabolite was detected in the sample.

Study Title	Time-dependent stability of plasma and liver interstitial fluid metabolites during sample handling in mice
Study Description	<p>This study assesses the stability of metabolite levels in plasma and liver interstitial fluid (IF) from mice subjected to different handling times prior to extraction. Blood and tissues from female C57BL/6J mice were collected and either processed immediately or held on ice for 5, 10, or 30 minutes before analysis. Metabolite concentrations were quantified by LC/MS to determine how delays in processing influence metabolite integrity. The resulting data establish time-dependent changes in metabolite abundance during pre-analytical handling.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>We performed a time-course experiment in female C57BL/6J mice aged 7–8 weeks to assess the stability of metabolites during sample handling. For plasma, blood was collected via cheek bleed directly into EDTA-coated tubes and either processed immediately or held on ice for 5, 10, or 30 minutes before processing. Samples were centrifuged at 800 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the resulting plasma supernatant was flash-frozen and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p><p>For liver interstitial fluid (IF), one liver was collected from each mouse and either processed immediately or held on a chilled metal block on ice for 5, 10, or 30 minutes prior to IF isolation. Livers were briefly rinsed in ice-cold saline, blotted to remove excess liquid, and placed in a 50 mL conical vial lined with a 20 µm nylon mesh filter (Spectrum Labs, 148134) containing 0.25 µL of 0.5 M EDTA (pH 8.0). Samples were centrifuged at 400 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the flow-through was collected and centrifuged again under the same conditions before being flash-frozen and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite quantification in murine fluid samples was performed as described previously (PMID: 30990168). In brief, 5 μL of sample or external chemical standard pool (ranging from ~5 mM to ~1 μM) was mixed with 45 μL of acetonitrile:methanol:formic acid (75:25:0.1) extraction mix including isotopically labeled internal standards. Samples were vortexed for 15 min at 4 °C and insoluble material was sedimented by centrifugation at 16,000 x g for 10 min at 4 °C. 20 µL of the soluble polar metabolite extract was taken for LC/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Dionex UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). 2 μL of each sample was injected onto a SeQuant® ZIC®-pHILIC 150 x 2.1 mm analytical column equipped with a 2.1 x 20 mm guard column (both 5 mm particle size; EMD Millipore). Buffer A was 20 mM ammonium carbonate, 0.1% ammonium hydroxide; Buffer B was acetonitrile. The column oven and autosampler tray were held at 25 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The chromatographic gradient was run at a flow rate of 0.150 mL min-1 as follows: 0-20 min: linear gradient from 80-20% B; 20-20.5 min: linear gradient form 20-80% B; 20.5-28 min: hold at 80% B.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was conducted on a QExactive benchtop orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with an Ion Max source and a HESI II probe. The mass spectrometer was operated in full-scan, polarity-switching mode, with the spray voltage set to 3.0 kV, the heated capillary held at 275 °C, and the HESI probe held at 350 °C. The sheath gas flow was set to 40 units, the auxiliary gas flow was set to 15 units, and the sweep gas flow was set to 1 unit. MS data acquisition was performed in a range of m/z = 70-1000, with the resolution set at 70,000, the AGC target at 1x106, and the maximum injection time at 20 msec.</p>	<p>External standard pools were used to generate calibration curves and determine absolute concentrations, as described in Sullivan et al., eLife 2019 (PMID: 30990168).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed in XCalibur v2.2 using a 5 ppm mass accuracy and 0.5 min retention time window. Identification was based on matching experimental m/z and retention time to those of authentic external chemical standards analyzed under identical chromatographic conditions.</p>

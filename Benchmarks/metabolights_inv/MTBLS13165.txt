Study Identifier	MTBLS13165
Study Title	Quantitative metabolomics of plasma and tissue interstitial fluids from mice
Study Description	<p>This study presents quantitative metabolomic measurements of plasma and tissue interstitial fluids (IF) collected from healthy C57BL/6J mice. Using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) and external chemical standards, absolute concentrations were determined for over one hundred polar metabolites spanning major biochemical pathways. Samples were obtained from plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, and interstitial fluids of multiple organs, including liver, lung, pancreas, kidney, and mammary fat pad. These data provide a quantitative reference for extracellular metabolite levels in physiologic mouse tissues and enable comparison across systemic and local microenvironments relevant to metabolic physiology and cancer biology.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Interstitial fluid (IF), plasma, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) were collected from female NSG or female C57BL/6J mice aged 6–9 weeks maintained on an ad libitum diet to quantify extracellular metabolite levels across tissues. All mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation at the same time of day to minimize circadian variation and metabolic artifacts associated with CO2 exposure.</p><p>For tissue interstitial fluid, organs from five mice were pooled per sample, and each pooled IF was analyzed as an individual data point. Tissues—including liver, lung, kidney, pancreas, and mammary fat pad—were kept on a chilled metal block throughout dissection, briefly rinsed in ice-cold saline (Azer Scientific, 16005), blotted on filter paper (VWR, 28298–020), and placed in 50 mL conical vials lined with 20 µm nylon mesh filters (Spectrum Labs, 148134) containing 0.25 µL of 0.5 M EDTA (pH 8.0). Samples were centrifuged at 400 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the flow-through was collected and centrifuged again under the same conditions before flash freezing and storage at −80 °C until LC/MS analysis.</p><p>For plasma, blood was collected from live mice by facial cheek bleed into EDTA-coated tubes (Sarstedt, 41.1395.105), centrifuged at 800 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the supernatant was flash frozen and stored at −80 °C. Equal plasma volumes from each mouse in a cohort were pooled for quantitative analysis.</p><p>For cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) collection, mice were anesthetized with ketamine (90 mg/kg) and xylazine (9 mg/kg) via intramuscular injection, and CSF was drawn from the cisterna magna using a sharpened glass capillary (inner diameter 0.75 mm, outer diameter 1.0 mm). Samples were visually inspected for blood contamination, centrifuged at 800 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the supernatant was flash frozen and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Following CSF collection, animals were euthanized by cervical dislocation, and blood was collected by cardiac puncture into EDTA-coated tubes (Sarstedt, 41.1395.105). Blood samples were centrifuged at 800 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and the resulting plasma was flash frozen and stored at −80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite quantification in murine fluid samples was performed as described previously (PMID: 30990168). In brief, 5 μL of sample or external chemical standard pool (ranging from ~5 mM to ~1 μM) was mixed with 45 μL of acetonitrile:methanol:formic acid (75:25:0.1) extraction mix including isotopically labeled internal standards. Samples were vortexed for 15 min at 4 °C and insoluble material was sedimented by centrifugation at 16,000 x g for 10 min at 4 °C. 20 µL of the soluble polar metabolite extract was taken for LC/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Dionex UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). 2 μL of each sample was injected onto a SeQuant® ZIC®-pHILIC 150 x 2.1 mm analytical column equipped with a 2.1 x 20 mm guard column (both 5 mm particle size; EMD Millipore). Buffer A was 20 mM ammonium carbonate, 0.1% ammonium hydroxide; Buffer B was acetonitrile. The column oven and autosampler tray were held at 25 °C and 4 °C, respectively. The chromatographic gradient was run at a flow rate of 0.150 mL min-1 as follows: 0-20 min: linear gradient from 80-20% B; 20-20.5 min: linear gradient form 20-80% B; 20.5-28 min: hold at 80% B.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was conducted on a QExactive benchtop orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with an Ion Max source and a HESI II probe. The mass spectrometer was operated in full-scan, polarity-switching mode, with the spray voltage set to 3.0 kV, the heated capillary held at 275 °C, and the HESI probe held at 350 °C. The sheath gas flow was set to 40 units, the auxiliary gas flow was set to 15 units, and the sweep gas flow was set to 1 unit. MS data acquisition was performed in a range of m/z = 70-1000, with the resolution set at 70,000, the AGC target at 1x106, and the maximum injection time at 20 msec.</p>	<p>External standard pools were used to generate calibration curves and determine absolute concentrations, as described in Sullivan et al., eLife 2019 (PMID: 30990168).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed in XCalibur v2.2 using a 5 ppm mass accuracy and 0.5 min retention time window. Identification was based on matching experimental m/z and retention time to those of authentic external chemical standards analyzed under identical chromatographic conditions.</p>

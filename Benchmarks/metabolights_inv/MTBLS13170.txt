Study Title	Peroxin Pex8 couples stress responses, antifungal tolerance and virulence regulation in Candida albicans
Study Description	<p><em>Candida albicans</em>, a World Health Organization critical-priority fungal pathogen, represents the predominant cause of candidemia. Therapeutic failure is sometimes driven by antifungal tolerance, a phenotype distinct from resistance, whose underlying mechanisms remain incompletely defined. Here, we identify the peroxisomal protein Pex8 as a key regulator of tolerance to both azoles and amphotericin B. Although neither deletion nor overexpression of <em>PEX8</em> altered minimum inhibitory concentrations, both modifications significantly reduced drug tolerance, as demonstrated by reduced fractional growth (FoG20) and impaired survival under drug pressure. Mechanistically, RNA-seq analysis revealed that <em>PEX8</em> overexpression suppresses ergosterol biosynthesis genes and reprograms stress-response pathways, providing a molecular basis for the observed azole tolerance attenuation. Complementary lipidomic profiling demonstrated that <em>PEX8</em> genetic manipulations induce extensive membrane lipid remodeling, characterized by specific alterations in ceramide and lysophospholipid subclasses, thereby revealing an ergosterol-independent mechanism underlying amphotericin B tolerance attenuation. Phenotypically, <em>PEX8</em> overexpression attenuated serum-induced hyphal morphogenesis and reduced virulence in a Galleria mellonella infection model, consistent with downregulation of hyphal-associated and virulence-related genes. Our findings establish Pex8 as a central coordinator of oxidative stress adaptation, membrane homeostasis, filamentation, and pathogenicity, revealing a promising target for combating antifungal tolerance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Candida albicans&nbsp;SN250 (wild-type) and its isogenic&nbsp;mutants pex8Δ/Δ, and PEX8OE were cultured in YPD broth at 37 °C with agitation (200 rpm) to mid-log phase. Cells were treated with amphotericin B (AMB, 0.25 μg/mL) or PBS (vehicle control) for 30 minutes, generating 6 experimental groups: (1) WT + AMB, (2) WT + PBS, (3)&nbsp;pex8Δ/Δ&nbsp;+ WT, (4)&nbsp;pex8Δ/Δ&nbsp;+ PBS, (5) PEX8OE + AMB, (6) PEX8OE + PBS, each with 3 biological replicates (total n=18). Following treatment, cells were harvested by centrifugation, washed twice with ice-cold PBS, and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen prior to storage at -80 °C for subsequent omics analyses.</p>	<p>Lipid extraction was performed using a methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE)-based protocol. Briefly, frozen samples were spiked with a lipid internal standard mixture (e.g., Avanti SPLASH LIPIDOMIX) and homogenized with 200 μL ultrapure water and 240 μL methanol. After vortexing for 30 s, 800 μL MTBE was added to induce phase separation, followed by ultrasonication in an ice-water bath and static incubation at room temperature (30 min). Phase separation was achieved by centrifugation (14,000 x g, 15 min, 10 °C), and the upper organic phase containing lipids was collected. The lipid extracts were dried under a nitrogen stream and stored at -80 °C until LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using reversed-phase liquid chromatography on a CSH C18 column (1.7 μm particle size, 2.1 x 100 mm; Waters) maintained at 45 °C. Lipid extracts were reconstituted in 200 μL of 90% isopropanol/acetonitrile (v/v), centrifuged (14,000 x g, 15 min, 10 °C), and 3 μL of supernatant was injected. Mobile phases consisted of (A) acetonitrile/water (6:4, v/v) and (B) acetonitrile/isopropanol (1:9, v/v), both supplemented with 0.1% formic acid and 0.1 mM ammonium formate. The gradient elution program was as follows: 30% B (0.0-2.0 min), 30-100% B (2.0-25.0 min, linear), 100% B (25.0-28.0 min), and re-equilibration at 5% B (28.0-38.0 min), with a constant flow rate of 300 μL/min.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes using a Q Exactive Plus hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The ESI parameters were optimized as follows: ion source temperature, 300 °C; capillary temperature, 350 °C; ion spray voltage, 3.0 kV; and S-lens RF level, 50%. Full-scan spectra were collected over a mass range of&nbsp;200-1800 m/z with a resolving power of 70,000 (at&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;200) in profile mode. Instrument calibration was performed weekly using a Pierce LTQ ESI Positive and Negative Ion Calibration Solution (Thermo Scientific) to ensure mass accuracy &lt;2 ppm.</p>	<p>In this project, mass spectrometry data were statistically analyzed using the commercial software LipidSearch (developed by Thermo Fisher Scientific), which includes a built-in database containing more than 30 lipid classes and over 1,500,000 fragment ions. Lipid identification was performed based on this database.</p>	<p>Lipid species were identified using&nbsp;LipidSearch software (version 4.2, Thermo Scientific)&nbsp;with a curated database encompassing &gt;30 lipid classes (e.g., phospholipids, sphingolipids, sterols) and &gt;1.5 million annotated MS/MS spectra. Identification criteria included: 5 ppm mass accuracy thresholds for both precursor and fragment ions, retention time alignment (±0.3 min) and HCD-specific fragmentation patterns.</p>

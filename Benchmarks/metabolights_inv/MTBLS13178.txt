Study Title	GRSF1 Protects Against Heart Failure by Maintaining BCAA Homeostasis
Study Description	<p>BACKGROUND: Imbalances in cardiac branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) metabolism and mitochondrial homeostasis are implicated in the onset and development of heart failure. However, the mechanisms triggering the downregulation of cardiac BCAA metabolism in heart failure remain unclear. Here, we identify a novel role of RNA-binding protein GRSF1 (guanine-rich RNA sequence binding factor 1) in post-transcriptionally regulating cell-intrinsic BCAA metabolic pathways, ultimately contributing to the pathogenesis of heart failure.</p><p>METHODS: We examined GRSF1 expression in the heart tissues of patients with dilated cardiomyopathy and generated mice with cardiomyocyte-specific deletion or overexpression of GRSF1 in vivo to investigate its role in heart failure. The effect of GRSF1 on BCAA homeostasis was assessed through untargeted and targeted metabolomics and mitochondrial function analysis. To elucidate the mechanisms underlying GRSF1-mediated metabolic regulation, we employed mice with cardiomyocyte-specific deletion of BCKDHB, and mice with cardiomyocyte-specific expression of GRSF1 lacking a quasi-RNA recognition motif.&nbsp;</p><p>RESULTS: GRSF1 expression was significantly decreased in the hearts of patients with heart failure and failing murine hearts. Cardiomyocyte-specific GRSF1 deletion resulted in cardiac dysfunction, spontaneous progression to dilated cardiomyopathy, and heart failure, accompanied by increased cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis. Conversely, GRSF1 overexpression attenuated cardiac remodeling and heart failure induced by transverse aortic constriction. Mechanistically, GRSF1 maintained BCAA homeostasis and mitochondrial function by directly interacting with the G-tracts in coding region of BCKDHB mRNA through a quasi-RNA recognition motif to promote the stability of BCKDHB mRNA at the post-transcriptional level, thereby increasing its protein expression. Functional recovery mediated by GRSF1 overexpression in cardiomyocytes was partially blocked upon cardiac-specific deletion of BCKDHB.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>CONCLUSIONS: Our study identified GRSF1 as a cell-intrinsic metabolic checkpoint that maintains cardiac BCAA homeostasis by regulating BCKDHB mRNA turnover. Targeting GRSF1 may offer therapeutic benefits for heart failure and other cardiometabolic diseases requiring BCAA manipulation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>&nbsp;Eight-week-old male C57BL/6J mice received a single injection via the tail vein with adeno-associated virus of serotype 9 (AAV9) to induce overexpression of GRSF1 under the transcriptional control of the cardiomyocyte-specific troponin T promoter (cTNT, 1.4 × 1011 vg/mouse) in a volume of 100 µL per mouse. Six-week-old male BCKDHBfl/fl and BCKDHBCM mice (C57BL/6J background) were injected with AAV-Ctrl or AAV-GRSF1 under the transcriptional control of the cTNT promoter (1.4 × 1011 vg/mouse) in a volume of 100 µL per mouse. Six-week-old male GRSF1CM mice were injected with AAV-Ctrl, AAV-GRSF1 wild type (WT) or AAV-GRSF1 mutant (MUT) under the transcriptional control of the cTNT promoter (1.4 × 1011 vg/mouse) in a volume of 100 µL per mouse. Six-week-old male GRSF1fl/fl and GRSF1CM mice were injected with AAV-Ctrl or AAV-cTNT-BCKDHB to induce expression of BCKDHB (4.7 × 1010 vg/mouse) in a volume of 100 µL per mouse. At 2 weeks after AAV9 injection, the mice underwent transverse aortic constriction (TAC) surgery under isoflurane anesthesia according to established procedures. The process began with an incision of the proximal sternum to expose the chest cavity. Subsequently, the aortic arch was isolated between the innominate artery and the left common carotid artery and constricted using a 6-0 silk suture tied tightly around a 27-gauge blunted needle for TAC. After ligation, the needle was promptly removed. In contrast, sham-operated mice were subjected to identical surgical interventions, with the exception of aortic constriction. In the isoproterenol (ISO)-induced heart failure model, 10-week-old C57BL/6J mice were administered daily subcutaneous injections of ISO (5 mg/kg/day) for 12 weeks. Echocardiography was utilized to evaluate cardiac function lesions. Mice were deeply anesthetized with isoflurane (5% for induction and maintained at 2-3%) and then euthanized by transcardial perfusion with PBS for subsequent experiments. Death was confirmed by respiratory arrest and the absence of a pedal reflex.</p>	<p>The heart tissue samples were thawed at 4, and approximately 50 mg was precisely weighed before being homogenized for 1 min with one steel bead, 10 ceramic beads, and 100 µL of water; subsequently, 100 µL of water and 800 µL of methanol were added, and the mixture was shaken for 30 min. The tube was then centrifuged at 4 and 12,000 rpm for 10 min, after which 300 µL of the supernatant was transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and dried under reduced pressure at a low temperature. The dried extract was reconstituted in 100 µL of 50% methanol by sonication, and 80 µL of the resulting supernatant was placed into an HPLC vial equipped with a 250 µL insert for subsequent untargeted metabolomic analysis using an Ultimate 3000 Rapid Separation Liquid Chromatography system coupled to a Q Exactive hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p>	<p>Reversed-phase chromatographic separation utilized a C18 column (Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3, 2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) with mobile phases composed of 0.1% formic acid in water (phase A) and 0.1% formic acid in methanol (phase B). For hydrophilic interaction chromatography (HILIC) separation, a HILIC column (Waters UPLC BEH Amide, 2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) was employed, with a gradient comprising 5 mmol/L ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile/water (95:5) (phase A) and 0.1% formic acid in water (phase B).</p>	<p>Full-scan mass spectrometry (MS) analysis was conducted on a Q Exactive hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a HESI-II probe, operated in both positive (3.7 kV) and negative (3.5 kV) ion modes.</p>	<p>MS data acquisition was conducted using Xcalibur software (v2.2 SP1.48), and raw MS data were processed with Progenesis QI (v2.1) for peak alignment, peak picking, deconvolution, and subsequent feature database searching.</p>	<p>First, unsupervised principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on both quality control (QC) samples and experimental samples to assess data quality, using SIMCA 14.1 (Umetrics AB, Umeå, Sweden). The analysis then proceeded in two parts: PCA and orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). Potential biomarkers were screened by combining variable importance in projection (VIP) values (VIP &gt; 1) with results from t-tests (P &lt; 0.05). The structures of these selected differential metabolites were subsequently annotated using multistage mass spectral data, with the aid of the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, http://www.hmdb.ca) and LIPID MAPS database (http://www.lipidmaps.org).</p>

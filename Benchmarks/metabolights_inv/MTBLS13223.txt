Study Title	Integrated Multi-omics Elucidation of the Uric Acid Degradation Pathway in Limosilactobacillus fermentum M5e
Study Description	<p>Hyperuricemia is a growing global health concern associated with gout, renal diseases, and cardiovascular disorders. Current pharmacotherapies are often limited by side effects, underscoring the need for alternative strategies. In this study, we isolated&nbsp;Limosilactobacillus fermentum&nbsp;M5e from human feces, which demonstrated exceptional uric acid (UA) degradation efficiency, reaching 85.81% after 4 days under high UA conditions (6.40 mmol/L). Genomic analysis revealed key genes involved in uric acid degradation, including&nbsp;APRT,&nbsp;XPT, and&nbsp;UapA. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses under uric acid stress showed significant changes in gene expression and metabolite levels, particularly in pathways related to purine metabolism, nitrogen assimilation, and energy production. The findings suggest that M5e can effectively utilize uric acid as a nitrogen source while mitigating oxidative stress. This integrated multi-omics analysis not only provides a comprehensive understanding of M5e's metabolic response to uric acid but also highlights its potential as a probiotic for managing hyperuricemia and related disorders. These insights establish&nbsp;L. fermentum&nbsp;M5e as a promising probiotic candidate for the management of hyperuricemia and provide a foundational framework for the development of microbiome-based therapeutics.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Limosilactobacillus fermentum&nbsp;M5e was anaerobically incubated in de Man, Rogosa and Sharpe (MRS) medium (Qingdao Haibo, China) supplemented with 6400 μmol/L uric acid at 37°C for 48 hours, using an anaerobic chamber (Don Whitley Scientific, UK) with an atmosphere of 85% N2, 10% CO2, and 5% H2. Bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation at 3,000 × g for 10 min.The conditioned medium (1&nbsp;mL per sample) was then collected, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen for 5 minutes, and stored at -80°C.</p>	<p>T3 Chromatographic Conditions</p><p>(1) Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm;</p><p>(2) Mobile phase: Phase A – ultrapure water (0.1% formic acid), Phase B – acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid);</p><p>(3) Flow rate: 0.4 ml/min; Column temperature: 40°C; Injection volume: 2 µl (for broadly targeted detection), 5 µl (for non-targeted detection).</p>	<p>Untargeted Detection Mass Spectrometry Conditions</p><p>The data acquisition instrument system primarily consists of an Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD,&nbsp;https://sciex.com.cn/) coupled with a Quadrupole-Time of Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (TripleTOF 6600, AB SCIEX).</p>	<p>Untargeted Detection Mass Spectrometry Conditions</p><p>The data acquisition instrument system primarily consists of an Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD,&nbsp;https://sciex.com.cn/) coupled with a Quadrupole-Time of Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (TripleTOF 6600, AB SCIEX).</p>	<p>All sample extracts were mixed in equal volumes to create a quality control (QC) sample. Untargeted detection was performed on the LC-QTOF-MS/MS platform. Accurate qualitative analysis was conducted based on the self-built standard database MWDB (including MS/MS spectra and retention time RT), the integrated public database DB-all (including Metlin, HMDB, KEGG, etc.) and the AI-predicted library from Metware, as well as MetDNA.</p>	<p>The multi-ion pairs and retention time (RT) information of the identified metabolites were extracted. Combined with Metware's self-built targeted database, a project-specific new library was established. Finally, using the Q-Trap instrument platform, MRM-based precise quantification of metabolites in the new library was performed for all samples.</p>

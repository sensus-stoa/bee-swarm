Study Title	GPR109a-AMPK Axis Mediates the Attenuation of Uric Acid-Induced M1 Macrophage Polarization by β-hydroxybutyrate from Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus M2b
Study Description	<p> Hyperuricemia (HUA) is a metabolic disorder marked by increased blood uric acid (UA) levels, closely linked to conditions such as gout. UA-induced macrophage M1 polarization fundamentally exacerbates inflammatory pathophysiology, but current HUA-specific immunoregulatory treatments are inadequate. We investigated&nbsp;β-hydroxybutyrate&nbsp;(BHB), a UA-degrading metabolite generated from&nbsp;Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus&nbsp;M2b, for its ability to inhibit UA-induced macrophage M1 polarization through the activation of the GPR109a-AMPK axis, uncovering new targets for the intervention of HUA inflammation. BHB was identified as a significant molecule by untargeted metabolomics in conjunction with CCK8 and ELISA assays. Subsequently, the M1 polarization model was developed by activating RAW264.7 cells with 1mM UA, establishing the Control group, UA group, UA+BHB group, and UA+M2b sterile supernatant group (UA+M2b_CM group). The findings indicated that both BHB and M2b_CM markedly suppressed UA-induced M1 polarization, reduced the expression levels of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and iNOS, and stimulated AMPK phosphorylation (enhanced the ratio of p-AMPK/AMPK) (P&lt;0.05). The transcriptome analysis of the GEO database verified that the AMPK pathway was markedly enhanced by BHB intervention. Functional validation experiments showed that specific silencing of GPR109a expression through siRNA interference or intervention with AMPK inhibitors (Compound C) completely eliminated the anti-inflammatory effects of BHB. These data offer substantial evidence that GPR109a functions as the principal molecular target facilitating the anti-inflammatory effects of BHB. In summary,&nbsp;Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus&nbsp;M2b-derived BHB suppressed UA-induced macrophage M1 polarization via the activation of the GPR109a-AMPK signaling pathway, presenting a novel approach for utilizing intestinal microbiota metabolites to address HUA-related inflammation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus&nbsp;M2b&nbsp;was anaerobically incubated in de Man, Rogosa and Sharpe (MRS) medium (Qingdao Haibo, China) supplemented with 6400 μmol/L uric acid at 37°C for 48 hours, using an anaerobic chamber (Don Whitley Scientific, UK) with an atmosphere of 85% N2, 10% CO2, and 5% H2. Bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation at 3,000 × g for 10 min.The conditioned medium (1&nbsp;mL per sample) was then collected, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen for 5 minutes, and stored at -80°C.</p>	<p>(1) Take the sample out of the -80°C freezer and place it on ice to thaw until no ice remains in the sample (all subsequent operations must be carried out on ice);</p><p>(2) After the sample has thawed, vortex for 10 seconds to mix thoroughly, then transfer 50 μL of the sample into a correspondingly labeled centrifuge tube;</p><p>(3) Add 150 μL of 20% acetonitrile-methanol internal standard extraction solution, vortex for 3 minutes, and centrifuge at 12,000 r/min for 10 minutes at 4°C;</p><p>(4) After centrifugation, transfer 150 μL of the supernatant into another correspondingly labeled centrifuge tube and let it stand in a -20°C freezer for 30 minutes;</p><p>(5) Centrifuge again at 12,000 r/min for 3 minutes at 4°C, then transfer 120 μL of the supernatant into the corresponding insert of the sample vial for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>T3 Chromatographic Conditions</p><p>(1) Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm;</p><p>(2) Mobile phase: Phase A – ultrapure water (0.1% formic acid), Phase B – acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid);</p><p>(3) Flow rate: 0.4 ml/min; Column temperature: 40°C; Injection volume: 2 µl (for broadly targeted detection), 5 µl (for non-targeted detection).</p><p>HILIC Chromatographic Conditions</p><p>(1) Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH HILIC Column, 1.7 µm, 1 mm * 100 mm;</p><p>(2) Mobile phase A: 20 mM ammonium formate, 10% water, 10% methanol, 80% acetonitrile, pH adjusted to 10.6 with ammonia water; Mobile phase B: 20 mM ammonium formate, 60% water, 40% acetonitrile, pH adjusted to 10.6 with ammonia water;</p><p>(3) Instrument column temperature: 40°C; Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min; Injection volume: 2 µl (for broadly targeted detection), 5 µl (for non-targeted detection).</p>	<p>Untargeted Detection Mass Spectrometry Conditions</p><p>The data acquisition instrument system primarily consists of an Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD,&nbsp;https://sciex.com.cn/) coupled with a Quadrupole-Time of Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (TripleTOF 6600, AB SCIEX).</p><p><br></p><p>Broadly Targeted Detection Acquisition Conditions</p><p>The data acquisition instrument system primarily consists of an Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD,&nbsp;https://sciex.com.cn/) coupled with a Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) system (QTRAP®,&nbsp;https://sciex.com/).</p>	<p>All sample extracts were mixed in equal volumes to create a quality control (QC) sample. Untargeted detection was performed on the LC-QTOF-MS/MS platform. Accurate qualitative analysis was conducted based on the self-built standard database MWDB (including MS/MS spectra and retention time RT), the integrated public database DB-all (including Metlin, HMDB, KEGG, etc.) and the AI-predicted library from Metware, as well as MetDNA.</p>	<p>The multi-ion pairs and retention time (RT) information of the identified metabolites were extracted. Combined with Metware's self-built targeted database, a project-specific new library was established. Finally, using the Q-Trap instrument platform, MRM-based precise quantification of metabolites in the new library was performed for all samples.</p>

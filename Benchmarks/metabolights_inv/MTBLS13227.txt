Study Title	Metabolic study of Rumen and ileum in Yanbian cattle
Study Description	<p>The high-concentrate feeding mode in intensive ruminant production often induces gastrointestinal dysfunction, characterized by microbial dysbiosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation, which compromise animal health and productivity. Glycerol monodecanoate (GMD), a medium-chain fatty acid derivative, has shown promise as a feed additive. However, its mechanisms of action in the ruminant gastrointestinal tract have not been fully clarified. This study investigated the mechanisms by which GMD modulates gastrointestinal health in Yanbian cattle fed a high-concentrate diet, via microbiome, metabolome, and transcriptome analyses. Thirty cattle were randomly assigned to a basal diet or a basal diet supplemented with 60 g/cattle/day GMD for 100 days.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>At the end of the experiment, following a 12-hour fasting period, 20 randomly selected bulls were transported to a commercial slaughterhouse (Yanji City, Jilin Province, China) for slaughter. Rumen fluid (filtered with four layers of gauze) and ileal digesta were quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and transferred to -80℃ for subsequent analysis of liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) metabolomics.</p>	<p>Solid sample protocol: 50 mg tissue + 1000 μL extraction solvent (MeOH:ACN:H2O = 2:2:1, v/v/v, with internal standard). Homogenized with beads (45 Hz, 10 min), followed by sonication in ice-water bath (10 min).</p><p>Liquid sample protocol: 100 μL sample + 500 μL extraction solvent (MeOH:ACN = 1:1, v/v, with internal standard). Vortexed and sonicated.</p><p>Post-extraction: All extracts were incubated at -20°C for 1 h, centrifuged (4°C, 12000 rpm, 15 min). The supernatant was dried in a vacuum concentrator. The dried metabolites were reconstituted in 160 μL ACN:H2O (1:1, v/v).</p><p>Internal standard: 2-Chloro-L-phenylalanine.</p>	<p>Chromatography type: Reversed-phase ultra-performance liquid chromatography (RP-UPLC).</p><p>Column: Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm x 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm).</p><p>Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water.</p><p>Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile.</p><p>Gradient program:</p><p>0.0 - 0.5 min: 5% B</p><p>0.5 - 5.5 min: 5% B to 50% B</p><p>5.5 - 9.0 min: 50% B to 95% B</p><p>9.0 - 10.5 min: 95% B</p><p>10.5 - 12.0 min: 95% B to 5% B</p><p>Flow rate: 400 μL/min.</p><p>Injection volume: 2 μL.</p>	<p>Platform: QTOF mass spectrometer (Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF).</p><p>Ionization: Electrospray ionization (ESI), positive and negative modes.</p><p>Data acquisition mode: MSe (low and high collision energy data acquired simultaneously).</p><p>MS scan range: m/z 50-1200.</p><p>Scan rate: 0.2 sec/spectrum.</p><p>Collision energy: Low energy: off; High energy ramp: 10-40 eV.</p><p>ESI source parameters:</p><p>Capillary voltage: 2500 V (positive), -2000 V (negative)</p><p>Cone voltage: 30 V</p><p>Source temperature: 100°C</p><p>Desolvation temperature: 500°C</p><p>Desolvation gas flow: 800 L/h</p><p>Cone gas flow: 50 L/h</p>	<p>Software: Progenesis QI (Waters).</p><p>Data processing steps: Raw data files were imported for peak picking (feature detection), alignment, and normalization.</p><p>Normalization: Normalized by total peak area.</p><p>Output: A data matrix of compound features (retention time, m/z) with normalized peak areas across all samples.</p>	<p>Identification strategy: Metabolites were identified by comparing accurate mass (MS1) and fragmentation spectra (MS/MS) against reference databases.</p><p>Databases used: Online METLIN database, Biomark's self-built library, KEGG, HMDB, and LipidMaps.</p><p>Software: Progenesis QI.</p><p>Annotation level:</p><p>Level 1: Identified by MS/MS spectrum match against reference standard (when available from self-built library).</p><p>Level 2: Putatively annotated by accurate mass and MS/MS spectrum match against public database (e.g., METLIN).</p><p>Level 3: Putatively characterized by accurate mass match only.</p><p><br></p>

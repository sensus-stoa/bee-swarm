Study Identifier	MTBLS13230
Study Title	A microfluidic chip recapitulating the human neurovascular unit with a functional blood-brain barrier for modeling gut-brain interactions
Study Description	<p>The blood-brain barrier (BBB) is essential for central nervous system homeostasis, but most current in vitro models lack structural and functional fidelity. We developed a physiologically relevant human neurovascular unit microfluidic chip (hNVU-on-a-chip) incorporating brain microvascular endothelial cells, astrocytes, and microglia to reconstruct a biomimetic BBB microenvironment. Barrier function was confirmed by low apparent permeability (Papp) and active P-glycoprotein (P-gp) efflux, with performance superior to Transwell models. Transcriptomic profiling revealed endothelial maturation with upregulated barrier and transport genes and downregulated proliferative pathways. Introducing gut microbial metabolites altered brain-side neurotransmitter metabolism, elevating biogenic amines and reducing precursors, consistent with enhanced turnover. Together, the hNVU-on-a-chip recapitulates BBB architecture and function, and provides a robust platform to investigate gut-brain axis interactions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fresh fecal samples were collected from healthy individuals and immediately suspended in sterile anaerobic phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Samples were processed in an anaerobic workstation to prepare a 20% (w/v) fecal slurry, which was filtered to remove debris and designated as the gut microbiota inoculum. The inoculum was supplemented with glycerol as a cryoprotectant, aliquoted, and stored at –20 °C until use.</p>	<p>The samples were added to water by well vortexing as the diluted sample. Then 100 μL of it was taken and homogenized with 400 μL of acetonitrile/water (8:2) which contained mixed internal standards by well vortexing. Next, put it on ice for 30min. After that, centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. Finally, the supernatant was injected into the LC-MS/MS system for analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters XSelect HSS T3 column (2.1 × 150 mm, 2.5 µm) held at 45 °C. The mobile phases were (A) 5 mM ammonium acetate in water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.30 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 µL. The gradient was: 0.0–2.0 min, 95% A; 2.0–4.0 min, linear to 60% A; 4.0–7.0 min, linear to 30% A; 7.0–7.5 min, to 0% A (100% B); 7.5–9.0 min, hold at 0% A; 9.1 min, return to 95% A; and 9.1–11.0 min, re-equilibration at initial conditions.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer was operated in negative multiple reaction mode (MRM) mode. Parameters were as follows: IonSpray Voltage (-4500 V), Curtain Gas (35 psi), Ion Source Temp (550°C), Ion Source Gas of 1 and 2 (60 psi).</p>	<p>Identification followed the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) criteria for targeted assays. All reported neurotransmitters (n = 23) achieved MSI level 1 by matching to authentic reference standards analyzed under identical LC and MS conditions. For each analyte, an in-house MRM library (one quantifier + ≥1 qualifier transition with optimized DP/CE) generated from standards was used. Peaks in samples were accepted only when: (i) retention time matched the standard within ±0.15–0.20 min (after routine RT check with pooled QC); (ii) qualifier/quantifier ion-ratio deviated by ≤20% from the reference; (iii) signal-to-noise &gt; 10 with appropriate peak shape; and (iv) back-calculated concentrations fell within the validated calibration range (R² ≥ 0.99; accuracy ±15%, ±20% at LLOQ). For a subset of metabolites, product-ion (enhanced product ion) scans from standards and representative samples were compared to reference spectra to further support identity. Final metabolite names were cross-referenced to KEGG.</p>	<p>Metabolomic data were first subjected to principal component analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction and visualization, enabling the assessment of overall distribution patterns and clustering tendencies among experimental groups. Differential metabolites were identified based on the thresholds of absolute log2 fold change&gt; 1 and P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05 (Student’s t-test). The identified differential metabolites were subsequently analyzed for pathway enrichment using the MetaboAnalyst online platform (https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/), in order to systematically evaluate their enrichment within relevant metabolic pathways and to explore their potential biological significance.</p>

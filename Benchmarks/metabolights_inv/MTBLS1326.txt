Study Identifier	MTBLS1326
Study Title	Metabolomics of red-light-induced stomatal opening in Arabidopsis thaliana: Coupling with abscisic acid and jasmonic acid metabolism
Study Description	Environmental stimuli-triggered stomatal movement is a key physiological process that regulates CO<sub>2</sub> uptake and water loss in plants. Stomata are defined by pairs of guard cells that perceive and transduce external signals, leading to cellular volume changes and consequent stomatal aperture change. Within the visible light spectrum, red light induces stomatal opening in intact leaves. However, there has been debate regarding the extent to which red-light-induced stomatal opening arises from direct guard cell sensing of red light versus indirect responses as a result of red light influences on mesophyll photosynthesis. Here we identify conditions that result in red-light-stimulated stomatal opening in isolated epidermal peels and enlargement of protoplasts, firmly establishing a direct guard cell response to red light. We then employ metabolomics workflows utilizing gas chromatography mass spectrometry and liquid chromatography mass spectrometry for metabolite profiling and identification of Arabidopsis guard cell metabolic signatures in response to red light in the absence of the mesophyll. We quantified 223 metabolites in Arabidopsis guard cells, with 104 found to be red light responsive. These red-light-modulated metabolites participate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle, carbon balance, phytohormone biosynthesis and redox homeostasis. We next analyzed selected Arabidopsis mutants, and discovered that stomatal opening response to red light is correlated with a decrease in guard cell abscisic acid content and an increase in jasmonic acid content. The red-light-modulated guard cell metabolome reported here provides fundamental information concerning autonomous red light signaling pathways in guard cells.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>A. thaliana</strong> used for <strong>guard cell protoplasts (GCP)</strong> isolation were grown in a short-day growth chamber with an 8 h/16 h day/night cycle. The white light intensity was 300 µmol/m^·s and the temperature cycle was 22°C/20°C day/night. Fully expanded leaves from 4-5 wks old <strong>Arabidopsis </strong>were used for <strong>guard cell </strong>isolation as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, ~300 fully expanded leaves were excised and then blended 4-5 times for 30 s each in cold tap water using a blender (Oster Inc., USA). After blending, epidermal peels were washed thoroughly with tap water and transferred to 200 mL of the first enzyme solution: 0.1% (w/v) PVP-40, 0.25% (w/v) BSA fraction V, 0.3% Cellulase R-10, 0.3% Cellulysin cellulase Trichoderma viride, and 0.02 % Macerozyme R-10, prepared in 55% (v/v) basic solution. We prepared basic solutions <strong>with or without K+ </strong>to test the effect of K+ on the red light-regulated<strong> guard cell </strong>responses. <strong>Basic solution without K+</strong> contains 0.55 M sorbitol, 0.5 mM CaCl2, 0.5 mM MgCl2, 0.5 mM ascorbic acid, 10 mM MES-Tris, pH 5.5; basic solution with K+ contains ~0.51 M sorbitol (amount adjusted each time of preparation to ensure the same osmolality as the basic solution without K+), 0.5 mM CaCl2, 0.5 mM MgCl2, 20 mM KCl, 0.5 mM ascorbic acid, 10 mM MES-Tris, pH 5.5. Peels were digested for ~50 min at 28-29 °C with a shaking speed of 140 rpm. Peels were then retained by filtration using 100 µm mesh and transferred to 200 mL of the second enzyme solution: 0.25 % (w/v) BSA fraction V, 0.8% (w/v) Onozuka RS cellulase, and 0.02% (w/v) Pectolyase Y-23 prepared in 100% basic solution. Digestion was for ~1 h at 20 °C with a shaking speed of 50 rpm. Peels were then retained on a 20 µm nylon mesh and rinsed with 600-800 mL basic solution, resulting in a filtrate containing the <strong>GCPs</strong>. Protoplasts were collected by centrifugation at 150 g for 5 min and carefully layered on top of the same volume of Histopaque (-1077, Sigma-Aldrich Co., USA), followed by centrifugation at 150 g for 15 min. Intact <strong>GCPs </strong>retained between the 2 phases were collected using a transfer pipette and washed thoroughly with basic solution. The protoplasts were finally resuspended in 1 mL of basic solution. The yield of protoplasts was estimated with a hemocytometer (Hausser Scientiﬁc, USA).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GCPs </strong>were allowed to recover on ice for 1 h in darkness, then brought back to room temperature for another 15 min. Aliquots (0.2 mL) of <strong>GCPs </strong>were treated under red light (provided by an ABI LED red light bulb, wavelength 660 nm, intensity 125 μmol/m^2·s, JacobsParts Inc., USA) or in darkness for 0, 30, and 60 min, respectively. At the end of the treatment, <strong>GCPs </strong>were collected by centrifugation at 150 g for 5 min. Each GCP pellet was frozen with liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for future analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Zhu M, Jeon BW, Geng S, Yu Y, Balmant K, Chen S, Assmann SM. Preparation of Epidermal Peels and Guard Cell Protoplasts for Cellular, Electrophysiological, and -Omics Assays of Guard Cell Function. Methods Mol Biol. 2016;1363:89-121. doi:10.1007/978-1-4939-3115-6_9. PMID:26577784</p>	<p>For metabolomic analyses, we used a total of 16,000 leaves for ~50 GCP preparations, which yielded ~200 million Arabidopsis GCPs in total. Before metabolite extraction, pellets from 4-6 individual GCP preparations were pooled to yield one biological replicate, which contained ~4 million GCPs. 5 replicates were prepared for LC-MS/MS analysis. Metabolites were extracted by adding 100 µL pre-chilled extraction solution (methanol: chloroform: water (v/v/v) 3:1:1) and continuously vortexed at 4 °C in the dark for 20 min. Extraction was repeated 3 times and the extracts were combined and lyophilized for each sample. Metabolite extracts were dissolved in 45 µL of 50% methanol (with lidocaine as positive mode internal standard and 10-camphorsulfonic acid as negative mode standard) and vortexed for 15 min at room temperature. After centrifugation at 4 °C, 13,000 g for 15 min, the supernatants were transferred to glass vials for HPLC-MRM-MS analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>For the MRM analysis on the QTRAP 4000 (AB Sciex Ltd., USA), samples were separated on an Eclipse XDB-C18 column (diameter 4.6 mm, length 250 mm, particle size 5 μm) with 0.1% formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B. The HPLC gradient started at 1% solvent B for 5 min, followed by ramping from 1% B to 99.5% B over 41.5 min, then held at 99.5% B for 4.5 min, and then return to 1% B in 0.3 min and held at 1% B for 8.7 min. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer conditions were: 30 psi curtain gas, 50 psi GS1, 55 psi GS2, ion source voltage 4,500 V, with a TurboIon electrospray ionization (ESI) interface temperature of 350 °C. MRM transitions for ~340 metabolites of both plant primary and secondary metabolism were optimized using commercial standards and distributed in 3 acquisition methods, 2 under positive mode and 1 under negative mode. Each acquisition method contains several time periods, each with selected MRM-transitions based on the compounds’ retention time, to increase the number of detected metabolites in the acquisition method.</p>	<p>No data transformation information was supplied for this study. </p>	<p>For analyses on a targeted metabolomics platform (QTRAP 4000), peak area of each metabolite from each sample was measured using <strong>MultiQuantTM </strong>software (AB Sciex Ltd., USA), then normalized to the internal standards. The normalized peak areas represented metabolite abundance were used for relative quantification.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>Paired mass/charge (m/z) ratios of the precursor ion and a selected daughter ion along with the chromatographic retention time, as acquired from analysis of authentic compounds (standards), were employed to identify a given metabolite in experimental samples.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS13295
Study Title	Family-based multi-omics reveal gut dysbiosis and circulatory metabolic disruption in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
Study Description	<p>Background: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a fatal neurodegenerative disorder with unclear etiology and no effective cure. Accumulating evidence implicates gut microbiota dysbiosis and circulatory metabolic al-terations in ALS pathogenesis, potentially driving disease progression and severity.</p><p>Methods: We enrolled 36 ALS patients and 32 healthy household relatives of these ALS patients as controls to minimize environmental confounders. Fecal (n=61) and serum (n=68) samples were analyzed using metagenomic sequencing for gut microbiota composition and untargeted metabolomics for fecal and serum metabolic profiles. Statistical and machine learning approaches, including principal coordinate analysis (PCoA), linear discriminant analysis effect size (LEfSe), and random forest modeling, identified differentially abundant taxa, metabolites, and biomarkers.</p><p>Results: ALS gut microbiota showed distinct beta diversity but unchanged alpha diversity. Pathogenic Proteobacteria (Enterobacteriaceae, Desulfovibrioaceae) increased, while beneficial Faecalibacterium and Clostridium decreased. A random forest model identified 20 microbial genera (AUC=0.89) and species (AUC=0.92) as ALS biomarkers. Microbial functions indicated hyperactive carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism and suppressed fatty acid metabolism. Fecal metabolomics revealed 722 altered metabolites (670 downregulated, e.g., lipids; 52 upregulated), disrupting phenylpropanoid biosynthesis and linoleic acid metabolism. Serum metabolomics showed lipid and choline metabolism perturbations. Reduced creatine and 2-hydroxybutyrate correlated with lower ALS Functional Rating Scale-Revised scores, while elevated diacylglycerols tracked disease severity. Symbiotic network analysis linked pathogenic Erysipelatoclostridium to reduced beneficial metabolites. Eubacterium was a central node, influencing metabolomes and BMI, with higher BMI correlating with Firmicutes enrichment (e.g., Ruminococcus).</p><p>Conclusion: ALS patients exhibit gut microbiota dysbiosis and circulatory metabolic disruptions, shaped by host factors like BMI and disease severity, correlating with disease progression. The microbiota-metabolite axis, elucidated through multi-omics and network analyses, offers targets for non-invasive biomarkers and therapies like microbiota modulation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This cohort study enrolled first-degree relatives of ALS patients, with samples collected at Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital Shanghai between October 2022 and March 2025. ALS patients n36 were diagnosed using the Gold Coast Criteria, while controls n32 were family members without personal or family history of neurodegenerative diseases. All participants provided written informed consent, legally authorized representatives consented for incapacitated individuals. Plasma was drawn from non-fasting ALS patients due to ethical concerns over fasting discomfort. Extensive metadata were documented: demographics sex, age, height, weight, BMI, ALS-specific clinical features onset segment, phenotype, diagnosis timeline, ALSFRS-R severity, constipation levels, and sleep status. The protocol was approved by the hospitals Ethics Committee.</p>	<p>Fecal protocol: Precisely 50±5 mg stool was extracted in 400 μL methanol:water 4:1 v:v containing four undisclosed internal standards including confirmed L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL, homogenized via cryogenic grinding -10°C, 50 Hz, 6 min, ultrasonicated 5°C, 40 kHz, 30 min, incubated -20°C, 30 min, and centrifuged 13,000 g, 4°C, 15 min.</p><p>Plasma protocol: 100 μL plasma underwent protein precipitation with 300 μL methanol:acetonitrile 1:1 v:v, same internal standards, followed by vortexing, ultrasonication, incubation, centrifugation, nitrogen drying risk of volatile metabolite loss, and reconstitution in 100 μL acetonitrile:water 1:1 v:v with secondary sonication.</p><p>Controls: A pooled QC was generated from 20 μL of each samples supernatant, one QC injected every 5–15 samples to monitor reproducibility. Solvent blanks were not described.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Fisher UltiMate 3000 UHPLC system (autosampler model not specified) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, C18). No guard column was documented. The binary mobile phase consisted of:</p><p>Mobile phase A: Acidified aqueous solution with acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid).</p><p>Mobile phase B: Acidified mixture of acetonitrile, isopropanol, and water (0.1% formic acid).&nbsp;Critical omissions: The gradient program (e.g., temporal variation of mobile phase B) was not defined, preventing method reproduction. Confirmed parameters: Injection volume 3 μL, column temperature 40°C; flow rate remained undisclosed.</p>	<p>Analysis employed a Thermo Fisher Q Exactive HF-X hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer with HESI-II electrospray ionization. Dual-polarity scanning positive/negative switching covered m/z 70–1050. Key settings:</p><p>Ion source: Sheath gas 50 arb, auxiliary gas 13 arb, heater 425°C, capillary 325°C, spray voltages ±3500 V, S-Lens RF 50.</p><p>Fragmentation: Stepped higher-energy collisional dissociation HCD at 20/40/60 eV.</p><p>Resolution: Full MS 60,000 FWHM at m/z 200, MS/MS 7,500 FWHM. No scan rate or AGC target details provided.</p>	<p>Raw data processing used Progenesis QI v2.3 (Waters) for peak picking (noise threshold undisclosed), alignment (retention time tolerance undisclosed), and integration. Post-processing included:</p><p>Normalization: Sum normalization followed by transformation to minimize batch effects.</p><p>Filtering: Features with &gt;30% RSD in pooled QC samples were discarded to reduce technical noise.</p><p>Multivariate analysis: OPLS-DA via R&nbsp;ropls v1.6.2&nbsp;with VIP &gt;1 for feature prioritization.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Annotations leveraged tiered confidence levels:</p><p>Level 1: MS/MS spectral match cosine similarity over 0.8 and exact mass under 5 ppm error against HMDB and MetLin databases.</p><p>Level 2: Mass-only match under 5 ppm from Majorbio Database.</p><p>Statistical significance required dual criteria: VIP over 1 from OPLS-DA and p under 0.05 Students t-test. Pathways were mapped to KEGG using Fishers exact test via Python scipy.stats. Derivatization was not performed, limiting coverage of certain metabolite classes</p>

Study Title	Multi-omics insights into high-carotenoid hybrid potato lines: enhanced nutritional quality and metabolic regulation for food applications
Study Description	<p>Modern potato varieties often exhibit low carotenoid content, limiting their nutritional value and processing performance. This study employed a multi-omics approach (physicochemical analysis, metabolomics, mineral profiling, transcriptomics, and microbiome sequencing) to investigate the molecular basis of high-carotenoid biofortification in two elite hybrid potato lines (H4 and H14) compared with four commercial cultivars. H4 and H14 showed higher starch (beneficial for processing), zeaxanthin and lutein, elevated phenolic acids (vanillic and salicylic acid), optimal K/Ca, and reduced reducing sugars, improving storage. Key pathways in starch metabolism, carotenoid biosynthesis, and amino acid metabolism were reprogrammed, with β-carotene hydroxylase (BCH2) and transcription factors (MYB, bHLH, NAC, WRKY) regulating carotenoid accumulation. Microbiome analysis revealed enriched&nbsp;Bacillus and&nbsp;Lactobacillales in H4/H14, suggesting a role in metabolic support. These findings highlight metabolic and microbial mechanisms underlying high-carotenoid, high-nutrition, and processing-optimized potatoes, offering a strategy for biofortified cultivar development to enhance nutrition and sustainability.​</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Four commercial potato varieties ('Atlantic' [A], 'Favorita' [F], 'Mira' [M], and 'Zhongshu No. 347' [Z]) and two hybrid lines ('H4' [H4] and 'H14' [H14]) were cultivated under controlled field conditions at the Agricultural Genomics Institute Shenzhen Experimental Station (China). All genotypes received NPK fertilizer (120-60-80 kg/ha) and were harvested at commercial maturity (120 days after planting) .</p><p>Tubers were transported to the laboratory within 2 hours post-harvest and subjected to standardization procedures: (1) surface decontamination was performed using deionized water; (2) tubers were equilibrated at 4°C (85% relative humidity) for 24 hours. For multi-omics analyses, uniform tubers (10–15 tubers per biological replicate, with 3 independent replicates) were aseptically peeled, cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen and archived at -80°C (solely the flesh tissue retained). All processing steps adhered to RNase-free protocols, utilizing analytical-grade reagents (Sigma-Aldrich) and HPLC-grade solvents (Fisher Scientific).</p>	<p>Weigh 0.1 g of the sample accurately into a 2 mL tube. Add 1000 μL of extraction solution (75% methanol:chloroform [9:1] + 25% H2O) and a steel bead, then homogenize the mixture in a tissue homogenizer at 50 Hz for two cycles of 60 seconds each. After sonication (30 minutes, 25°C) and incubation on ice (30 minutes), centrifuge the mixture (12,000 rpm, 10 minutes, 4°C). Dry the resulting supernatant and reconstitute it in 200 μL of 50% acetonitrile containing 4 ppm 2-chloro-L-phenylalanine.</p>	<p>LC analysis was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA) was used and maintained at 40 °C. The flow rate and injection volume were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively.</p><p>For LC-ESI(+)-MS analysis, the mobile phase consisted of (B2) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid (v/v) and (A2) water with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). The elution gradient was as follows: 0–1 min, 10% B2; 1–5 min, 10%–98% B2; 5–6.5 min, 98% B2; 6.5–6.6 min, 98%–10% B2; 6.6–8 min, 10% B2.</p><p>For LC-ESI(-)-MS analysis, the analytes were separated using (B3) acetonitrile and (A3) 5 mM ammonium formate. The separation gradient was: 0–1 min, 10% B3; 1–5 min, 10%–98% B3; 5–6.5 min, 98% B3; 6.5–6.6 min, 98%–10% B3; 6.6–8 min, 10% B3.</p>	<p>Metabolites were detected by mass spectrometry using an Orbitrap Exploris 120 instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with an ESI ion source. Data acquisition was performed in both MS1 and MS/MS modes (Full MS-ddMS2 mode, data-dependent MS/MS acquisition). The key parameters were set as follows: sheath gas pressure, 40 arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV for ESI(+) and -2.50 kV for ESI(-); capillary temperature, 325 °C; MS1 scanning range, m/z 100-1000; MS1 resolution, 60,000 FWHM; number of data-dependent scans per cycle, 4; MS/MS resolution, 15,000 FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30%; and dynamic exclusion time, auto.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data files were converted from the original instrument format to the mzXML file format using the MSConvert tool from the ProteoWizard software package (v3.0.8789).</p>	<p> Peak detection, peak filtering, and peak alignment were then performed using the R XCMS package. The parameter settings were as follows: bw=2, ppm=15, peakwidth=c(5, 30), mzwid=0.015, mzdiff=0.01, method='centWave', resulting in a quantitative list of compounds</p>

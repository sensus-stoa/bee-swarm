Study Title	Spermine is an endogenous iron chelator that inhibits ferroptosis
Study Description	<p>Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of cell death driven by lipid peroxidation. Here, we identify spermine—a polyamine derived from spermidine—as an endogenous iron chelator that directly suppresses ferroptosis. Integrating metabolomics, stable isotope tracing, and biophysical studies of the interaction between spermine and Fe2+ ions, we demonstrate that aldehyde dehydrogenase 18 family member A1 (ALDH18A1) promotes an alternative glutamine-dependent pathway for de novo spermine synthesis, thereby limiting iron availability and lipid peroxidation in hepatocellular carcinoma. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of ALDH18A1—by knockout, AAV-delivered shRNA, or the small molecule inhibitor YG1702—triggers ferroptosis and impairs both spontaneous and chemically induced hepatocarcinogenesis. Conversely, supplementation of spermine protects against ferroptosis-associated ischemia–reperfusion injury across multiple tissues, including the liver, intestine, and kidneys. These findings uncover a pathophysiologically relevant metabolic circuit in which spermine-mediated iron chelation suppresses ferroptosis.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Liver tissues from LTsc1-/-Pten-/-(KO) and LTsc1+/+Pten+ /+(WT) mice at the age of 16 weeks, HCC tissues from LTsc1-/-Pten-/-(KO) mice and liver tissues from LTsc1+/+Pten+ /+(WT) mice at the age of 20 weeks;</p><p>Liver tissues from DEN/CCL4 and vehicle mice at the age of 4 months, HCC tissues from DEN/CCL4 mice and liver tissues from control mice at the age of 6 months</p>	<p>The tissue samples were minced and homogenized, followed by the addition of 80% methanol aqueous extraction solvent. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and sonicated for 10 min. After standing at -40 °C for 1 h, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were transferred into autosampler vials for LC-MS analysis. Additionally, equal volumes of supernatants from all samples were pooled to generate a quality control (QC) sample for system performance evaluation.</p>	<p>For the analysis of polar metabolites, chromatographic separation of target compounds was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column. The mobile phase consisted of water (phase A) and acetonitrile (phase B). The autosampler temperature was maintained at 4 °C and the injection volume was 2 μL.For the analysis of non-polar metabolites, chromatographic separation of target compounds was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Phenomenex Kinetex C18 column. The mobile phase consisted of water (phase A) and isopropanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) as phase B. The autosampler temperature was maintained at 4 °C and the injection volume was 2 μL. Mass spectrometric detection was carried out on an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer under the control of Xcalibur software (version 4.4, Thermo Fisher Scientific), with both MS1 and MS/MS data acquisition.

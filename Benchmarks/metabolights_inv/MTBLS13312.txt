Study Title	Integrated Proteomic and Metabolomic Profiling Reveals Potential Virulence Factors and Secondary Metabolites in the Secretome of Fusarium verticillioides
Study Description	<p>Fusarium verticillioides (F. verticillioides) is an important fungal pathogen known to infect a variety of economically critical crops, especially maize, causing substantial yield reductions and economic losses worldwide. In addition to its direct damage to agricultural productivity, F. verticillioides threatens public health through producing/secreting potent compounds, including the well-known fumonisins (FUMs), which pose significant health threats to both livestock and humans due to their toxicity and carcinogenicity. However, current knowledge about the materials secreted/produced by F. verticillioides, such as secreted proteins and additional secondary metabolites remains limited. In the present study, we conducted an integrated secretome analysis of F. verticillioides at exponential growth stage by using proteomic and metabolomic technologies. The results of the present study showed that proteomic analysis identified 185 proteins, including 138 fungus-specific proteins, GO enrichment of these 138 fungus-specific proteins yielded 24 significant terms spanning carbohydrate/polysaccharide and aminoglycan metabolic/catabolic processes, extracellular and membrane-anchored components, and hydrolase/peptidase activities. Meanwhile, KEGG analysis identified starch and sucrose metabolism as the sole significantly enriched pathway. Metabolomic analysis of medium supernatant showed that a total of 2,352 metabolites were identified, with 110 unique to the medium supernatant of fungal group, including fumonisins (A1, B2, B3, B4), fatty acids, and other bioactive compounds. KEGG pathway enrichment highlighted key metabolic pathways such as TCA cycle, unsaturated fatty acid biosynthesis, and arachidonic acid metabolism. These findings provide new insights into the pathogenic mechanisms of F. verticillioides, suggesting potential virulence factors and metabolic adaptations that contribute to its pathogenicity.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>2.1 Fungal Strain&nbsp;</p><p>F. verticillioides strains were isolated from Yunnan Province, and stored by the Yunnan Key Laboratory of Veterinary Etiological Biology. The fungus was preserved in 30% (v/v) glycerol solution and stored at 80°C for long-term cryopreservation.&nbsp;</p><p>2.2 Growth Curve Measurement</p><p>F. verticillioides was cultivated on potato dextrose agar (PDA) medium at 25°C in darkness. Agar plugs (5 mm diameter) were transferred into 150 mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing 100 mL modified potato dextrose broth (PDB), incubated at 25 °C with shaking at 150 rpm for 7 days. At 24-hour intervals, one flask was removed for analysis. Mycelia were harvested by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 10 min, dried at 60 °C to constant weight, and biomass (g/day) was calculated.&nbsp;</p><p>In parallel, spore production was monitored daily. Aliquots from each flask were taken at 24-hour intervals, diluted, and the number of spores was quantified using a hemocytometer under a microscope. A spore count growth curve was constructed with time as the x-axis and spore number as the y-axis.</p>	<p>2.3 Secretome Collection</p><p>Based on the above measurements, the culture supernatant corresponding to the rapid growth phase of F. verticillioides and the non-inoculated control medium were selected. The fungal or non-inoculated control medium supernatant was collected on day 5. The samples were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes, and the supernatant was carefully collected. The collected supernatant was then sent to a specialized service provider for proteomic and metabolomic sequencing.</p>	<p>hroughout the entire analysis process, the sample was placed in a 4 autosampler, and the sample was SHIMADZU-LC30 ultra-high grade</p><p>High-performance liquid chromatography system (UHPLC), using ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1×100 mm, 1.8</p><p>µm (Waters, Milford, MA, USA) chromatographic column. Among them, the injection volume is 6 μL, the column temperature is 40, and the flow rate is 0.3 mL/min. Chromatographic mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution, B: 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile solution; Chromatographic gradient washing</p><p>The process is as follows: 0- 2 minutes, 0 B; Within 2 to 6 minutes, B linearly changed from 0% to 48%. 6 to 10 minutes, B</p><p>Linearly change from 48% to 100%; For 10 to 12 minutes, B remains at 100%. 12 to 12.1 minutes, B from 100%</p><p>Linear change to 0%, 12.1- 15 minutes, B remains at 0%.</p>	<p>Each sample was detected in both positive ion (+) and negative ion (-) modes by electrospray ionization (ESI).</p><p>The samples were separated by UPLC and then analyzed by mass spectrometry using a QE Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific)</p><p>The HESI source is ionized, and the ionization conditions are as follows: Spray Voltage: 3.8kv (+) and 3.2kv (-);</p><p>Capillary Temperature: 320 (±) Sheath Gas: 30 (±); Aux Gas: 5 (±);</p><p>Probe Heater Temp: 350 (±); S-Lens RF Level: 50.</p><p>The Settings for mass spectrometry acquisition are as follows:</p><p>Mass spectrometry acquisition time: 15 minutes.

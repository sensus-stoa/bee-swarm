Study Description	<p _msttexthash='376091651' _msthash='550'> Liver samples of southern catfish were sent to Majorbio Bio-pharm Technology Co., Ltd. for untargeted metabolomic sequencing&nbsp;referring to previous study [27]. Grinding beads were added to the liver samples, and metabolites were extracted via low-temperature ultrasound using an extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing four internal standards (including L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL). The samples were then incubated at -20℃ for 30 min, centrifuged at 13,000 g for 15 min at 4℃, and the supernatant was transferred to injection vials with inner liners for instrumental analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of metabolites from all samples, and one QC sample was inserted every 5–10 samples during instrumental analysis to evaluate the repeatability of the entire analytical process. LC-MS analysis was performed on a Thermo Fisher Scientific ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris 240) system.&nbsp;After instrumental analysis, the raw LC-MS data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. Finally, a data matrix including retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity was obtained. Meanwhile, LC-MS spectral information was matched against public metabolomic databases (HMDB:&nbsp;http://www.hmdb.ca/; Metlin:&nbsp;https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and Majorbio’s in-house database to acquire metabolite information.</p><p _msttexthash='376091651' _msthash='550'><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p> Three hundred southern catfish (4.02±1.03 g) were purchased from a local farm in Huzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. One week prior to the experiment, all fish were reared on commercial feed in a recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) containing filtered freshwater with sufficient dissolved oxygen at 28±1.0℃.Five experimental diets were formulated: one basal diet as the control and four <em>Candida utilis</em>-containing diets replacing fish meal at different ratios. for the basal diet, fish meal, soybean meal, chicken meal, and corn gluten meal were used as protein sources, wheat flour as the carbohydrate source, and soybean oil as the lipid source. Four <em>Candida utilis</em>&nbsp;diets (YP10, YP20, YP30, YP40)&nbsp; were prepared by substituting 10%, 20%, 30%, and 40% of fish meal with Candida utilis, respectively. The basal diet was formulated with a feed composition containing 35% fish meal. For the Candida utilis&nbsp;diets, Candida utilis&nbsp;served as the alternative protein source, with the fish meal proportions adjusted to 31.5%, 28%, 24.5%, and 21% accordingly.&nbsp;All ingredients were ground through a 200-µm sieve, weighed according to the formula, and then mixed with water using a blender. Subsequently, pellets were produced using a commercial pelletizer (KCHL-10, Kcth Group, Beijing, China). The experimental feeds were then dried in a cool, dry place away from light and stored at -20℃ for future use. The crude protein, crude lipid, and gross energy of the experimental diets were determined according to the Kjeldahl method .</p><p> After one week of acclimatization, 300 southern catfish were randomly divided into five groups for the feeding trial (note: adjusted to five groups to match the five diets mentioned earlier). Each group consisted of 60 fish, which were further divided into 3 aquariums (300 liters of filtered recirculating freshwater per aquarium) as three replicates. Prior to the start of the experiment, all fish were weighed to record their initial body weight.&nbsp;Throughout the experiment, the parameters of the recirculating aquaculture water were monitored daily: temperature was maintained at 28±1.0℃, pH ranged from 6.8 to 7.5, dissolved oxygen level was above 5.0 mg/L, ammonia nitrogen was below 0.1 mg/L, and nitrite level was below 0.1 mg/L. The five groups of fish were hand-fed twice a day (at 9:00 and 17:00) with the basal diet and the four <em>Candida utilis</em>-containing diets, respectively. The daily feed consumption was also recorded after feeding. The feeding trial was conducted under these conditions for 56 days.</p><p> At the end of the feeding trial, after a 24-hour fasting period, the southern catfish in each aquarium were counted and sampled. Prior to euthanasia and sampling, all fish were anesthetized with MS-222. All fish were then weighed and measured to calculate. After weighing, blood samples were collected from the caudal vein using a sterile syringe and allowed to coagulate overnight at 4℃. Subsequently, serum was collected by centrifugation at 3000 g for 15 minutes at 4℃ and stored at -80℃ for biochemical analysis. Following blood collection, the fish were dissected to isolate the liver and intestine. The isolated liver was weighed to calculate the hepatosomatic index (HSI). The sampled liver was immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and then stored at -80℃ for biochemical assays and metabolomic sequencing. The sampled intestine was also snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately and stored at -80℃ for 16S rRNA sequencing. In addition, liver tissues were collected and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution (Servicebio, Wuhan, China), then stored in 70% ethanol solution for histopathological analysis. For blood and liver biochemical assays as well as intestinal digestive enzyme activity detection, five biological replicates were performed in this study. Five biological replicates were also conducted for 16S rRNA sequencing and metabolomic sequencing.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>100 mg solid sample was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>was added. 800 μL of extraction solution (methanol: water = 4:1 (v:v) containing four internal </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>standards ( 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine, etc.) were used for metabolite extraction. </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>Samples were ground by the Wonbio-96c ( Shanghai wanbo biotechnology co., LTD) frozen tissue </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>(5°C, 40 kHz). The samples were left at -20°C for 30 min, centrifuged for 15 min (4°C, 13000 g), </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28679'>and the supernatant was transferred to the injection vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>The LC-MS/MS analysis of sample was conducted on a UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris 240 </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The mobile phases </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (2:98, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>acetonitrile (solvent B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was 40℃. </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28739'>The injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>The UPLC system was coupled to a UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris 240 system Mass </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>and negative mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 400℃ ; </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>sheath gas flow rate at 40 arb; Aux gas flow rate at 10 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>-2800V in negative mode and 3500V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy , </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>20-40-60V rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='28818'>Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC/MS raw data was performed by Progenesis QI software, and a three-dimensional data matrix in CSV format was exported. The</p><p>information in this three-dimensional matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and</p><p>mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive</p><p>peaks (including noise, column bleed, and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data</p><p>matrix, deredundant and peak pooled. At the same time, the metabolites were identified by</p><p>searching database, and the main databases were the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin</p><p>( https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio</p><p>Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China)</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>The data matrix obtained by searching database was uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>(https://cloud.majorbio.com) for data analysis. Fistly, the data matrix was pre-processed, as</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>follows: At least 80% of the metabolic features detected in any set of samples were retained. After </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>filtering, the minimum value in the data matrix was selected to fill the missing value and each </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>metabolic signature was normalized to the sum. To reduce the errors caused by sample preparation </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>and instrument instability, the response intensities of the sample mass spectrometry peaks were </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>normalized using the sum normalization method, to obtain the normalized data matrix. Meanwhile, </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>the variables of QC samples with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% were excluded and </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>log10 logarithmicized, to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.Perform variance </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>analysis on the matrix file after data preprocessing. </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>The R package “ropls”(Version 1.6.2) was used to perform principal component analysis </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>(PCA) and orthogonal least partial squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), and 7-cycle </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>interactive validation evaluating the stability of the model. The metabolites with VIP&gt;1, p&lt;0.05 </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>were determined as significantly different metabolites based on the Variable importance in the </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>projeciton (VIP) obtained by the OPLS-DA model and the p-value generated by student’s t test. </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>Differential metabolites among two groups were mapped into their biochemical pathways </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>through metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis based on KEGG database (http://www. </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>genome.jp/kegg/). These metabolites could be classified according to the pathways they involved </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>or the functions they performed. Enrichment analysis was used to analyze a group of metabolites </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>in a function node whether appears or not. The principle was that the annotation analysis of a </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>single metabolite develops into an annotation analysis of a group of metabolites. Python packages </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>“scipy.stats” (https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/ ) was used to perform enrichment analysis to obtain </p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='29058'>the most relevant biological pathways for experimental treatments.</p>

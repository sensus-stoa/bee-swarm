Study Title	Mycelium of Diaporthe nobilis treated with 2% cell-free supernatant (CFS) from Bacillus velezensis A4 for 12 hours
Study Description	<p>Mycelia of Diaporthe nobilis(50 mg) treated with CFS were collected,Each sample was homogenized in 400 µL of pre-cooled 80% methanol aqueous solution containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine (internal standard). The mixture was ground and subjected to ultrasonication at 4 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation, the filter supernatants were analyzed using a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). Progenesis QI software was used for the analysis and processing of raw LC-MS data. Metabolite identification was performed by comparing with the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Metlin, and Majorbio databases. ANOVA, PCA, and OPLS-DA analyses were conducted with seven-cycle cross-validation. Metabolites with Variable Importance (VIP) &gt;1 and *p* &lt; 0.05 were classified as significantly different metabolites, and functional enrichment analysis was performed based on the KEGG database.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mycelium of Diaporthe nobilis treated with 2% cell-free supernatant (CFS) from Bacillus velezensis A4 for 12 hours. Mycelia of Diaporthe nobilis(50 mg) treated with CFS were collected,Each sample was homogenized in 400 µL of pre-cooled 80% methanol aqueous solution containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine (internal standard).</p>	<p>1. Accurately weigh 50±5 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and add a grinding bead with a diameter of 6 mm;</p><p>2. Add 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1 (v:v)) containing 0.02 mg/mL internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine);</p><p>3. Grind with a frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10, 50 Hz);</p><p>4. Perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5, 40 KHz);</p><p>5. Let the sample stand at -20 for 30 min;</p><p>6. Centrifuge for 15 min (13000 g, 4), transfer the supernatant to an autosampler vial with an insert for analysis;</p><p>7. Additionally, take 20 μL of supernatant from each sample, mix them together as a quality control sample.</p>	<p>The liquid chromatography system used is the Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon system; the chromatographic column is ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, USA); mobile phase A is 95% water and 5% acetonitrile (with 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B is 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol, and 5% water (with 0.1% formic acid). The injection volume is 3 μL, and the column temperature is 40°C. Mobile phase elution gradient for positive ion mode: 0 min: 100% A, 0% B; 3 min: 80% A, 20% B; 4.5 min: 65% A, 35% B; 5 min: 0% A, 100% B; 6.3 min: 0% A, 100% B; 6.4 min: 100% A, 0% B; 8 min: 100% A, 0% B. Mobile phase elution gradient for negative ion mode: 0 min: 100% A, 0% B; 1.5 min: 95% A, 5% B; 2 min: 90% A, 10% B; 4.5 min: 70% A, 30% B; 5 min: 0% A, 100% B; 6.3 min: 0% A, 100% B; 6.4 min: 100% A, 0% B; 8 min: 100% A, 0% B</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer is a Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive HF-X; the samples were ionized by electrospray and mass spectrometry signals were collected using both positive and negative ion scanning modes. The scan range is 70-1050 (m/z); the sheath gas flow rate is 50 arb; the auxiliary gas flow rate is 13 arb; the capillary temperature is 325°C; the spray voltage is 3500 V for both positive and negative modes; the S-Lens RF level is 50; the normalized collision energy is 20, 40, 60 eV; the resolution (Full MS) is 60,000; and the resolution (MS2) is 7,500.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, and other processes, ultimately obtaining a data matrix containing information such as retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. Subsequently, the software was used for feature peak library identification, matching MS and MS/MS spectral information with metabolite databases. The MS mass error was set to be less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on secondary mass spectrometry matching scores. The main databases used were mainstream public databases such as http://www.hmdb.ca/ and https://metlin.scripps.edu/, as well as a self-built database.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, and other processes, ultimately obtaining a data matrix containing information such as retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity.

Study Title	Gut Microbiota and Metabolomic Changes Across Preterm Stages: Potential Associations with Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia
Study Description	<p>The coordinated postnatal development of the gut microbiome and metabolome is essential for preterm infant health, yet its disruption is increasingly linked to adverse outcomes such as bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD). In this study, we performed an integrated multi-omics analysis of fecal samples collected from perterm infants to characterize temporal changes in gut microbial and metabolic profiles and explore their potential associations with BPD development. This study observed a distinct trajectory of the phylum&nbsp;Bacteroidota&nbsp;as a hallmark of normal gut maturation, with its abundance progressively declining across non-BPD infants. In contrast, infants who later developed BPD exhibited early depletion followed by irregular enrichment of&nbsp;Bacteroidota. Correlation analysis revealed that&nbsp;Streptococcus&nbsp;abundance was positively associated with elevated cysteic acid, a metabolite linked to oxidative stress. Together, these findings suggest that altered&nbsp;Bacteroidota succession and&nbsp;Streptococcus-associated oxidative imbalance may reflect early microbial-metabolic perturbations in infants at risk of BPD. This work provides preliminary, hypothesis-generating insights into gut-associated signatures potentially relevant to BPD pathogenesis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fresh fecal samples were collected from neonates immediately after defecation by trained nurses using sterile, DNA-free collection tubes. To minimize contamination, material was obtained from the inner portion of the stool and care was taken to avoid contact with urine or the diaper surface. Approximately 0.5–2 g of feces was transferred into pre-labeled sterile cryovials, placed on ice within 30 minutes of collection, and transported to the laboratory under cold-chain conditions. Samples were stored at −80 °C until further processing.</p>	<p>After the samples were slowly thawed at 4°C, an appropriate amount of each sample was added to a pre-cooled methanol/acetonitrile/water solution (2:2:1, v/v/v), vortex-mixed, and subjected to low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 minutes. The mixture was then allowed to stand at -20°C for 10 minutes, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 g and 4°C for 20 minutes. The supernatant was collected and vacuum-dried. For mass spectrometry analysis, the residue was reconstituted in 100 μL of an acetonitrile/water solution (acetonitrile:water = 1:1, v/v), vortexed, and centrifuged at 14,000 g and 4°C for 15 minutes. The resulting supernatant was taken for injection analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation&nbsp;was performed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC system equipped with a hydrophilic interaction chromatography (HILIC) column (25 ℃&nbsp;column temperature; flow rate: 0.5 mL/min; injection volume: 2 μL). The mobile phases consisted of 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonia hydroxide in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The gradient elution was programmed as follows: 0-0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5-7 min, 95% to 65% B; 7-8 min, 65% to 40% B; 8-9 min, hold at 40% B; 9-9.1 min, 40% to 95% B; 9.1-12 min, hold at 95% B. Samples were kept at 4 ℃&nbsp;in the autosampler throughout the analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>After separation using the Vanquish LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system, the samples were analyzed by an Orbitrap Exploris™ 480 mass spectrometer (Thermo) in both electrospray ionization (ESI) positive and negative ion modes. The ESI source and mass spectrometry parameters were set as follows: sheath gas (Gas1): 50, auxiliary gas (Gas2): 2, ion source temperature: 350°C, spray voltage (ISVF): 3500 V for positive ion mode and 2800 V for negative ion mode; primary mass-to-charge (m/z) detection range: 70–1200 Da, resolution: 60,000, scan accumulation time: 100 ms. For secondary MS, a data-dependent acquisition (DDA) method was applied with an m/z range of 70–1200 Da, resolution: 60,000, scan accumulation time: 100 ms, and dynamic exclusion time: 4 s.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to .mzXML format using ProteoWizard, followed by peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction using XCMS software. For the data extracted by XCMS, metabolite identification and data preprocessing were first performed, including: null value filtering (removing ion peaks with &gt;50% missing values), null value imputation (KNN algorithm), and data filtering (removing features with RSD &gt;50%). Subsequently, experimental data quality assessment was conducted, followed by data analysis.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to .mzXML format using ProteoWizard, followed by peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction using XCMS software.

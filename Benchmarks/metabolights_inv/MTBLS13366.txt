Study Title	Drought Stress-Induced Physiological and Molecular Changes in Strawberries: An Integrated Transcriptomic and Metabolomic Perspective
Study Description	<p>This study identifies drought-responsive differential metabolites in two strawberry cultivars ('Benihoppe' and 'Kaorino') via metabolomics, uncovering their genotype-specific metabolic mechanisms of drought tolerance. It provides metabolic-level evidence for breeding drought-resistant strawberries and optimizing water-saving cultivation, addressing the production constraints of strawberry's high drought sensitivity.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The two strawberry cultivars used in this study, ‘Benihoppe’ and ‘Kaorino’,&nbsp;were&nbsp;commercial seedlings from&nbsp;Yunnan Shangri-La Zangmei Agricultural Technology Co., Ltd. (Shangri-La, elevation 3,400&nbsp;m). After 30 d of acclimation in a smart greenhouse (50 m², Kunming University,&nbsp;24°58′30.98″N, 102°47′57.98″E) equipped with&nbsp;an automated drip-irrigation system (Netafim, 2L h−1&nbsp;per dripper), uniform seedlings (5-6 true leaves, height 12 ±&nbsp;1 cm) were selected. The growth&nbsp;conditions were&nbsp;maintained at (&nbsp;25 ±&nbsp;3)&nbsp;°C / (18 ±&nbsp;3)°C (day/night), relative humidity&nbsp;(RH)&nbsp;65%&nbsp;±&nbsp;5%, and CO2 400 ±&nbsp;20 ppm. Plants were potted individually in 1.2&nbsp;L plastic pots (10.8&nbsp;cm Ø&nbsp;×&nbsp;13.1&nbsp;cm&nbsp;height) containing&nbsp;a sterilized substrate (peat: composted earthworm castings, 2:1 v/v; pH 6.0 ± 0.2, EC 0.45 dS m−1).Metabolites were extracted from 50 mg&nbsp;of&nbsp;finely&nbsp;ground root powder with 1 mL&nbsp;of&nbsp;80% methanol (pre-cooled at −20°C, containing 0.1 mg mL−1&nbsp;lidocaine as&nbsp;an&nbsp;internal standard).</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 50 mg&nbsp;of&nbsp;finely&nbsp;ground root powder with 1 mL&nbsp;of&nbsp;80% methanol (pre-cooled at −20°C, containing 0.1 mg mL−1&nbsp;lidocaine as&nbsp;an&nbsp;internal standard). Following vortexing (30s) and sonication (10 min, 4°C), the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 ×&nbsp;g&nbsp;for10 min&nbsp;at&nbsp;4°C). Supernatants were filtered through 0.22 µm PVDF&nbsp;membranes&nbsp;and analyzed on&nbsp;a Vanquish UHPLC system coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap MS (Thermo Fisher).</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Hypersil GOLD C18 column (2.1 ×&nbsp;100 mm, 1.9 µm) at 40 °C. The mobile&nbsp;phases&nbsp;consisted of&nbsp;0.1 % formic acid in water&nbsp;(A) and acetonitrile&nbsp;(B), with the following gradient: 0-1 min,&nbsp;5% B;&nbsp;1-9 min,&nbsp;5-95 % B;&nbsp;9-11 min,&nbsp;95 % B;&nbsp;11-11.1 min,&nbsp;95-5 % B;&nbsp;11.1-14 min,&nbsp;5&nbsp;% B.The&nbsp;flow rate&nbsp;was&nbsp;0.3 mL min−1. MS parameters&nbsp;included a&nbsp;full-scan range of&nbsp;70-1,050 m/z, resolution of 120,000, spray voltages&nbsp;of&nbsp;+3.5/−2.8 kv, capillary temperature of 320°C, and probe heater temperature of&nbsp;350°C.</p>	<p>Supernatants were filtered through 0.22 µm PVDF&nbsp;membranes&nbsp;and analyzed on&nbsp;a Vanquish UHPLC system coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap MS (Thermo Fisher).</p>	<p>Raw files were processed using&nbsp;Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo) with a 5 ppm mass tolerance against HMDB, and METLIN and an in-house Fragaria&nbsp;spectral library. Peak areas were normalized to the internal standard and fresh weight. Differential metabolites were identified by OPLS-DA (SIMCA 16.0) with VIP &gt; 1 and P&nbsp;&lt; 0.05 (Student’s t-test, FDR-adjusted).</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into Progenesis QI v3.0 metabolomics processing software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. This process generated a data matrix containing retention time (RT), mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity information. Subsequently, the software was further utilized for feature peak library searching and compound identification by matching MS and MS/MS spectra with metabolic databases. The mass accuracy tolerance was set at &lt;10 ppm for MS data, while metabolites were annotated based on MS/MS spectral matching scores. The primary reference databases included HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), and other mainstream public repositories, supplemented by an in-house database.</p>

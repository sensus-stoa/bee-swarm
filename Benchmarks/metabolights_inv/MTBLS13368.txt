Study Description	<p>Common vetch (Vicia sativa&nbsp;L.) is an important annual leguminous forage crop commonly used for green manure, fodder, and soil improvement. It is widely cultivated as a green manure and forage crop in Yunnan Province during winter and spring. However, the dry conditions and minimal rainfall during these seasons greatly limit common vetch growth. Therefore, screening for drought-tolerant, high-yielding common vetch varieties is a critical objective in breeding programs.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Leaves of drought-tolerant and high-yielding common vetch (Vicia sativa L.) varieties were collected from field-grown plants in Yunnan Province during the winter-spring growing season. Healthy, fully expanded leaves at the same growth stage were selected, rapidly rinsed with deionized water to remove surface contaminants, blotted dry with sterile filter paper, and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen to preserve metabolite integrity. Samples were stored at -80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Frozen leaf samples (100 mg) were ground into a fine powder using a mortar and pestle under liquid nitrogen. The powder was transferred to 2 mL centrifuge tubes, and 1 mL of methanol/water (80:20, v/v) containing internal standards was added. The mixture was vortexed vigorously for 1 min, sonicated at 4 °C for 30 min, and then centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully collected, filtered through a 0.22 μm organic phase filter membrane, and transferred to amber sample vials for subsequent UHPLC-Q Exactive analysis.</p>	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters UHPLC system equipped with an autosampler (Waters Sample Manager). The analytical column used was a Waters BEH C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 µm), and a Waters BEH C18 guard column (5 mm × 2.1 mm., 1.7 µm) was installed to protect the analytical column. The column type was reversed-phase C18, optimized for the separation of diverse metabolites in Vicia sativa L. leaves.</p>	<p>Metabolite detection was conducted using a Thermo Scientific UHPLC-Q Exactive Fourier transform mass spectrometry system. The ion source employed was an electrospray ionization (ESI) source, operated in both positive and negative scan polarities. The mass analyzer was a high-resolution orbitrap mass analyzer, with a scan m/z range set from 50 to 1500 to cover the molecular weights of target metabolites in Vicia sativa L. leaves.</p>	<p>The LC-MS raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. A data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity was finally generated.&nbsp;The variable importance in projection (VIP) values derived from the OPLS-DA model and p-values from Student’s t-test were used to identify significantly differential metabolites: those with a VIP &gt; 1 and p &lt; 0.05 were defined as significantly differential metabolites.</p>	<p>the MS and MS/MS spectral information were matched against the self-built plant metabolite-specific database MJDBPM (Meiji Bioinformatics) to obtain metabolite annotations.</p>

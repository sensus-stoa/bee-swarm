Study Title	Metabolome Analyses Provides Insights into Fruit Symptom Development Caused By Huanglongbing in Citrus reticulata Blanco cv. Wokan
Study Description	<p> Citrus Huanglongbing (HLB), caused by the phloem-limited bacterium ‘Candidatus Liberibacter asiaticus’ (CLas), is the most devastating diseases threatening citrus production worldwide. Infected fruit exhibited lopsided morphology and color inversion (‘Red-nose’ fruit), but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we investigated the dynamic CLas-host interactions associated with fruit symptom development through integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses of fruit pith and pericarp from 30 to 240 days after flowering (DAF). “Red-nose” symptoms appeared at 180 DAF and intensified as CLas titers peaked at 240 DAF. CLas transcriptomes profiling revealed that the early colonization (30–90 DAF) was characterized by upregulated genes involved in proliferation, nutrient uptake, stress tolerance and secretion systems, while later stages (180–240 DAF) showed broad downregulation of core metabolism with sustained transporter and surface‑structure gene expression. CLas infection shifted host metabolism by enhancing sucrose degradation, reducing sucrose synthesis, disrupting auxin/cytokinin pathways, and perturbing pigment pathways. Host defense metabolites were depleted early (30/90 DAF) but accumulated during ripening (180/240 DAF). These findings revealed how CLas temporally manipulated host growth and defense, driving HLB‑associated fruit disorders.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p> HLB-affected and healthy six-years old Citrus reticulata Blanco cv Wokan trees were originally collected from the citrus orchard located in Conghua, Guangzhou city, Guangdong Province (23°64′19″N, 113°50′18″E). Both HLB-affected and healthy citrus fruits were collected monthly from May (30 days after flowering, DAF) to December (240 DAF), 2021 (Figure 1A). At each developmental stage, 10 to 15 fruits per tree from five independent trees were collected. The fruit pith and fruit pericarp were collected every month and immediately put into liquid nitrogen before bringing back to laboratory. Leaf samples adjacent to the fruit sample from the same branch of HLB-affected plant were also collected for confirmation of CLas infection. The representative samples of CLas-infected and healthy fruit samples from different growing stage, including fruit cell division (30 DAF), fruit cell expansion (90 DAF and 180 DAF) and fruit ripening (210 DAF and 240 DAF), were selected for dual transcriptome and metabolome analyses. For each sample group, three biological replicates were analyzed. Each replicate was prepared from pooled tissues of five fruits collected from different trees to ensure biological independence and reduce individual variation.</p>	<p>&nbsp;A total of 100 mg fruit pith tissues were individually grounded with liquid nitrogen and the homogenate was resuspended with prechilled 80% methanol by well vortex. The samples were incubated on ice for 5 min and then centrifuged at 15,000 g, 4°C for 20 min. The supernatant was then diluted to final concentration containing 53% methanol by LC-MS grade water and subsequently centrifuged at 15000 g, 4°C for 20 min. After centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and added into the detection bottle for liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analyses.</p>	<p>The LC-MS based untargeted metabolomics analyses were carried out on a Vanquish UHPLC system (ThermoFisher, Germany) in conjunction with an Orbitrap Q ExactiveTM HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Germany).</p>	<p> For the positive polarity mode, the eluents were eluent A (0.1% FA in water) and eluent B (methanol), while for the negative polarity mode, the eluents were eluent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9.0) and eluent B (methanol). The solvent gradient was configured as follows: 2% B at 1.5 min; 2% to 100% B within 3 min; 100% B for 10 min; 100% to 2% B at 10.1 min; and 2% B at 12 min. The Q ExactiveTM HF-X mass spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with a spray voltage of 3.5 kV, a capillary temperature of 320°C, a sheath gas flow rate of 35 psi, an aux gas flow rate of 10 L/min, an S-lens RF level of 60, and an aux gas heater temperature of 350°C.</p>	<p>The raw data files produced by UHPLC-MS/MS were subjected to processing with Compound Discoverer 3.1 (CD3.1, ThermoFisher) for conducting peak alignment, peak selection, and quantification of each metabolite. Subsequently, the peaks were matched against the mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/), mzVault, and MassList database to acquire the precise qualitative and relative quantitative outcomes. Statistical analyses were carried out utilizing the statistical software R (version 4.2.3), Python (version 2.7.6), and CentOS (release 6.6).&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html), HMDB database (https://hmdb.ca/ metabolites) and LIPID Maps database (http://www.lipidmaps.org/).</p>

Study Title	A Bidirectional Regulatory ACLY-NLRP3 Axis Integrates Metabolic and Inflammatory Signaling to Control NLRP3 Inflammasome Activation
Study Description	<p _msttexthash='40138319' _msthash='7409'>The NLRP3 inflammasome is critical for host defense against bacterial infection and is involved in the pathogenesis of various inflammatory diseases. The aggregation of its sensor protein NLRP3&nbsp;is a pivotal checkpoint for inflammasome activation. However, the regulatory mechanism of NLRP3 aggregation is not completely understood. Here, we identify the metabolic enzyme ATP-citrate lyase&nbsp;(ACLY) as a direct binding partner that constrains NLRP3&nbsp;aggregation, membrane translocation and activation.&nbsp;We demonstrate that ACLY interacts with the NLRP3-PYD independently of its enzymatic function, thereby sterically hindering NLRP3 aggregation&nbsp;and suppressing inflammasome assembly. Paradoxically, we find that NLRP3 binding to ACLY inhibits ACLY’s enzymatic activity&nbsp;and remodels cellular metabolism, which&nbsp;counteracts the stimulatory effect of ACLY on NLRP3 inflammasome activation.&nbsp;This reciprocal interaction&nbsp;reveals an essential role of ACLY as a brake for NLRP3 aggregation, and also establishes NLRP3 as a metabolic checkpoint to regulate ACLY-mediated metabolism. Furthermore, we develop a&nbsp;peptide that disrupts the ACLY-NLRP3 interaction&nbsp;and promotes NLRP3 inflammasome activation, which enhances host defense against bacterial infection. Our work delineates a self-limiting circuit for immunometabolic crosstalk, positioning the ACLY-NLRP3 axis as a new&nbsp;therapeutic target for inflammasome-driven pathologies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a QTRAP 6500+ system (AB Sciex) coupled to the UHPLC. The instrument was operated in both positive and negative ion modes with polarity switching. ESI source parameters: source temperature 580°C, Ion Source Gas 1 (GS1) 45, Ion Source Gas 2 (GS2) 60, Curtain Gas (CUR) 35, ion spray voltage +4500 V (positive mode) and -4500 V (negative mode). Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) was used for quantitative data acquisition. QC samples were analyzed throughout the batch to monitor system stability.</p>	<p>Peritoneal macrophages were isolated from 8-week-old male mice. Cells were washed with PBS, collected by centrifugation at 300g for 5 minutes at 4°C, and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Samples were stored at -80°C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted using 400 μL of cold methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) containing stable-isotope internal standards for absolute quantification. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly, centrifuged at 14,000g for 20 minutes at 4°C, and the supernatant was collected. The extract was dried under vacuum centrifugation at 4°C and reconstituted in 100 μL acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) for LC-MS analysis. Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of each sample extract.</p>	<p>For polar metabolites (amino.mzML):</p><p>UHPLC separation was performed on a Waters UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) at 35°C. Mobile phase A was 90% water + 2 mM ammonium formate + 10% acetonitrile, and mobile phase B was 0.4% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient program was: 85% B at 0-1 min, 80% B at 3-4 min, 70% B at 6 min, 50% B at 10-15.5 min, 85% B at 15.6-23 min. Injection volume was 2 μL and flow rate was 300 μL/min. Samples were maintained at 4°C in the autosampler.</p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a QTRAP 6500+ system (AB Sciex) coupled to the UHPLC. The instrument was operated in polarity switching mode. For HILIC analysis (amino.mzML), the MS was primarily operated in positive ion mode with selective MRM transitions for polar metabolites. For C18 analysis (C18.mzML), alternating positive/negative ion mode was used to cover both neutral lipids (positive mode) and acidic lipids (negative mode). Common ESI source parameters: source temperature 580°C, Ion Source Gas 1 (GS1) 45, Ion Source Gas 2 (GS2) 60, Curtain Gas (CUR) 35, ion spray voltage +4500 V (positive mode) and -4500 V (negative mode). Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) was used for quantitative data acquisition. QC samples were analyzed throughout the batch to monitor system stability. Data acquisition was controlled by Analyst software (AB Sciex).	<p>Raw MS data were processed using MultiQuant or Analyst software (AB Sciex) for peak integration and quantitative analysis. Metabolites in QC samples with coefficient of variation (CV) less than 30% were considered reproducible. Missing values were imputed using KNN algorithm. Data were normalized and subjected to multivariate statistical analysis using R package ropls, including Pareto-scaled principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified and quantified based on MRM transitions and retention times matching to reference standards with stable-isotope internal standards. Lipid classification was performed according to LIPID MAPS classification system. Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using KEGG database and MetaboAnalyst platform. Significantly changed metabolites were defined as VIP &gt; 1.0 from OPLS-DA model and p-value &lt; 0.05 from Student's t-test.</p>	<p>For lipids (C18.mzML):</p><p>UHPLC separation was performed on a Waters UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm) at 40°C. Mobile phase A was 5 mM ammonium acetate in water, and mobile phase B was 99.5% acetonitrile. The gradient program was: 5% B at 0 min, 60% B at 5 min, 100% B at 11-13 min, 5% B at 13.1-16 min. Injection volume was 2 μL and flow rate was 400 μL/min. Samples were maintained at 4°C in the autosampler.</p>	<p>UHPLC separation was performed on a Waters UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm) at 40°C using an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC system. Mobile phase A was 5 mM ammonium acetate in water, and mobile phase B was 99.5% acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was: 5% B at 0 min, 60% B at 5 min, 100% B at 11-13 min, and 5% B at 13.1-16 min. The flow rate was 400 μL/min and injection volume was 2 μL. Samples were maintained at 4°C in the autosampler throughout the analysis.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS13404
Study Title	Profiling aromatic amino acid metabolome in the human gut microbiota reveals Clostridioides difficile specific N-acyl amino acids
Study Description	<p>Owing to its compositional and chemical complexity, much of the gut microbiota metabolome remains poorly characterized. Aromatic amino acids (AAAs) have a history of being privileged substrates for the biosynthesis of diverse bioactive metabolites and thus represent a potentially rich source of bioactive molecules within the microbiota metabolome. In this study, we leveraged&nbsp;13C- and&nbsp;2H-labeled aromatic amino acid and untargeted&nbsp;liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry&nbsp;(LC-MS) to profile AAA-derived metabolites produced by 80 phylogenetically diverse human gut bacterial isolates. Collectively, we found 93 unique LC-MS features, 28 of which, predominantly produced by&nbsp;Clostridioides difficile,&nbsp;were identified as&nbsp;N-acyl amino acids.&nbsp;C. difficile&nbsp;produced the highest levels of the AAA-derived precursors phenylacetic acid and phenylpropionic acid, exceeding all Bacteroidetes and Proteobacteria strains in our panel.&nbsp;C. difficile’s&nbsp;uniquely diverse&nbsp;N-acyl amino acids&nbsp;have the potential to serve biomarkers for&nbsp;C. difficile&nbsp;colonization and mediators of&nbsp;C. difficile-specific host interaction.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All bacteria were cultured anaerobically in a vinyl anaerobic chamber (Coy Laboratory Products) with a gas mixture of 5% CO2, 5% H2, and 90% N2&nbsp;at 37 °C. BHI media consisted of 37 g/L brain-heart infusion (Hardy diagnostics), 5 g/L yeast extract, 200 mg/L MgSO4·7H2O, 100 mg/L MnCl2·4H2O. After being autoclaved, 1L of BHI media was supplemented with 5 ml of 200 g/L maltose, 10 ml of 100 g/L cellobiose, 25 ml of 20 g/L-cysteine, and 5 ml of 1 g/L hemin. All media were deoxygenated by placing in an anaerobic chamber for at least 24 hr prior to use. Bacteria from a glycerol stock were plated on BHI-agar, and a single colony was picked and cultured in BHI media. For isotope feeding experiments, 10 μL of the saturated 2-day culture was used to inoculate fresh 1 ml BHI media supplemented with the indicated amino acid isotopes, followed by incubation for 3 days.&nbsp;L-tryptophan-2H5&nbsp;and&nbsp;L-tryptophan-13C11&nbsp;were spiked at final concentration at 1 mM for tryptophan feeding; L-phenylalanine-2H5&nbsp;and&nbsp;L-phenylalanine-13C9&nbsp;were spiked at final concentration at 3 mM&nbsp;for phenylalanine feeding;&nbsp;L-tyrosine-2H4&nbsp;and&nbsp;L-tyrosine-13C9&nbsp;were spiked at final concentration at 0.5 mM&nbsp;for tyrosine feeding. These isotope concentrations were similar to endogenous amino acid concentrations in BHI medium. </p>	<p>After incubation, the culture was centrifuged (10 min, 3220 xg) to remove bacteria. 200 μL of the resulting culture broth was added to 2 μM of oligomycin in 800 μL methanol followed by incubation at -80 °C for 3 hr, and centrifugation (10 min, 3220 xg) to precipitate protein. From the resulting clarified supernatant, 800 μL was transferred to a 96-well plate, dried using a SpeedVac and resuspended in 160 μL of a 1:1 mixture of methanol:water for LC-MS analysis. </p>	<p>5 μL of spent culture was separated using a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH Shield RP18 1.7 μm 2.1 x 150 mm column maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phase consisted of buffer A (0.1% formic acid in water) and buffer B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile). The LC gradient was as follows: 0-2 min: 2% B, 2-3 min: 10% B, 3-13 min: linear gradient from 10% to 18% B, 13-17 min: linear gradient from 18% to 50% B, 17-22 min: 95% B, 22- 30 min: 2% B. The flow rate was set at 0.25 ml/min.&nbsp;Tryptophan labeled samples were analyzed using the following LC gradient:&nbsp;0-2 min: 2% B, 2-3 min: 10% B, 3-15&nbsp;min: linear gradient from 10% to 18% B, 15-23&nbsp;min: linear gradient from 18% to 50% B, 23-26&nbsp;min: 95% B, 26-30 min: 2% B. If indicated, a synthetic standard was used to create a standard curve and run in the same batch to perform absolute quantification. </p>	<p>The LC-MS analysis was performed using a Sciex X500R quadrupole time-of-flight MS coupled to&nbsp;an Exion LC. Turbo ESI source was set with the following parameters: curtain gas at 30 psi, ion source gas 1 at 50 psi, ion source gas 2 at 50 psi, temperature at 500 °C, positive polarity, spray voltage at 5500 V, CAD gas at 7, declustering potential at 80 V, collision energy at 5 V, TOF start mass at 100 Da, and TOF stop mass at 1000 Da. MS/MS spectra were acquired with the same parameters, except collision energy was set at&nbsp;30&nbsp;V, collision energy spread was set as&nbsp;10&nbsp;V, TOF start mass was set at 20 Da, and TOF stop mass was set at 500 Da.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Each LC-MS file in&nbsp;wiff2 format was converted to mzXML format using MSConvert (ProteoWizard).</p><p><br></p><p>MS Feature identification:&nbsp;To identify&nbsp;13C-labeled MS features (i.e., a&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;peak at specific retention time), the TracExtract module of MetExtract II (version 2.9.7) was used with the following parameters: atom count: 9, number of labeling atom to search for: 6-9, native isotopolog: 12C, native isotopolog enrichment: 98.93%, labeled isotopolog: 13C, labeled isotopolog enrichment: 99%, amount native compound: 50, amount labeled compound: 50, ratio deviation relative to theoretical signal ratio: 50% to 170%, scan range: 1-28 mins, intensity threshold: 10000, charge: 1, mass deviation: ±5 ppm, isotopologs native: 2, isotopologs labeled: 2, maximum ratio deviation native: ±50%, maximum ratio deviation labeled: ±50%, MZ clustering window: ≤10 ppm, MZ clustering minimum spectra: ≥ 3 scans, XIC extraction width: ±10 ppm, XIC smoothing window: Gaussian and ±3 scans, chromatographic separation: 15-70 scans, EIC artificial shift: 0 scan, center error: ≤10 scans, minimum corr: ≥85 %. Tryptophan features were searched using the same parameters except atom count was 11 and number of labeling atom to search for was 8-11.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>MS feature filtering:&nbsp;For each feature, expected&nbsp;2H-labeled masses were calculated from the native mass (3 to 5&nbsp;2H for phenylalanine and tryptophan labeling; 2 to 4&nbsp;2H for tyrosine labeling). Peak areas of&nbsp;13C-labeled, expected&nbsp;2H-labeled, and&nbsp;native masses were extracted from the same LC-MS sample where the feature was identified in MetExtract II using Mzmine 2 (version 2.5.3) with the following parameters: (1)&nbsp;mass detection: MS level: 1, centroid mode, noise level: 3E3; (2)&nbsp;targeted feature detection: intensity tolerance: 60%, noise level: 2E3, m/z tolerance: 20 ppm, retention time tolerance: 0.1 min (0.3 min for expected&nbsp;2H-labeled masses); (3)&nbsp;smoothing: filter width: 11. All features were then aligned across samples using (1)&nbsp;join aligner: m/z tolerance: 10 ppm, weight for m/z: 75, retention time tolerance: 0.3 min, weight for RT: 25; (2)&nbsp;peak finder (multithreaded): intensity tolerance: 100%, m/z tolerance: 10 ppm, retention time tolerance: 0.2 min, (3)&nbsp;peak filter: duration: 0.05 to 2.5 min, height: 2E3 to 1E8. Features were kept based on the following criteria: (1) the difference in retention time between&nbsp;13C-labeled and native masses was &lt; 0.1 min. (2) the peak area ratio of (native mass/&nbsp;13C-labeled mass) was between 3 and 0.3. (3) the retention time of&nbsp;2H-labeled mass minus the retention time of native mass was between 0.1 and -0.3 min. (4) the peak area ratio of (2H-labeled mass/&nbsp;13C-labeled mass) was between 3 and 0.3.&nbsp;2H-labeling number was determined by the largest peak area among all identified&nbsp;2H-labeled masses. Finally, a feature was removed if (1) its&nbsp;2H-labeled mass cannot be found. (2) its&nbsp;13C-labeled masswas found in the BHI medium. (3) its native&nbsp;mass appeared as fragments in the MS/MS (e.g.,&nbsp;due to&nbsp;in-source fragmentation) or matched a common adduct ions (e.g.,&nbsp;multimers and Na+) of another feature at (+/- 0.1 min) the same retention time.&nbsp;MS/MS spectra of native mass for each feature were extracted using (1)&nbsp;mass detection: MS level: 2, centroid, noise level: 1E1;&nbsp;(2) targeted feature detection: intensity tolerance: 60%, noise level: 2E3, m/z tolerance: 20 ppm, retention time tolerance: 0.3 min.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Molecular networking analysis: GNPS was used to analyze metabolite structural similarity among MS features based on MS/MS.&nbsp;The MS/MS was filtered by removing all MS/MS fragment ions within +/- 17 Da of the precursor m/z. The precursor ion mass tolerance was set to 0.02 Da and a MS/MS fragment ion tolerance of 0.02 Da. A network was then created where edges were filtered to have a cosine score above 0.4 and more than 4 matched peaks. Further, edges between two nodes were kept in the network if and only if each of the nodes appeared in each other's respective top 10 most similar nodes. Finally, the maximum size of a molecular family was set to 100, and the lowest scoring edges were removed from molecular families until the molecular family size was below this threshold. The spectra in the network were then searched against GNPS' spectral libraries. The library spectra were filtered in the same manner as the input data. All matches kept between network spectra and library spectra were required to have a score above 0.7 and at least 6 matched peaks.</p><p><br></p><p>All putative structures were identified either through comparing to a synthetic standard (level 1) or detailed MS/MS and isotope analyses (level 2 identification: putative compound based on MS/MS similarity to a standard).</p><p><br></p>

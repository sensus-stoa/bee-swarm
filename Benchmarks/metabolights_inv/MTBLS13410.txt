Study Title	How introgression affects leaf endophytic fungi: higher diversity but lower co-occurrence network complexity
Study Description	<p>Plant-leaf endophytic fungi mutualism is essential for plants to adapt to adverse environments. Though introgression has been extensively studied in many plants, the underlying effects of introgression on fungi-driven adaptability still remain unanswered. Here, we performed landscape-scale sampling of natural populations across its typical distribution range in China to disentangle how introgression effects endophytic fungal community within oak leaves. Our results showed that fungal diversity was decreased while fungal co-occurrence network complexity was increased with increasing introgression index, reflecting a “dual adaptation strategy” to respond to environmental challenges. This pattern was also linked to metacommunity: the lowest diversity occurred in metacommunity that characterized by the lowest mean annual temperature and annual precipitate, whereas the highest fungal network complexity appeared in metacommunity that had the highest mean annual temperature and optimal annual precipitate. Importantly, the differential metabolite intensities (e.g., organoheterocyclic compounds, and organoheterocyclic compounds) and climate factors mediated the leaf endophytic fungi-driven adaptability of oak trees. This work not only advances our understanding of plant-microbe interactions in the context of evolutionary ecology but also provides insight into the importance of fungal community restructuring in facilitating plant adaptation to environmental change.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mature, healthy and intact leaves of Quercus trees were collected in July–August 2022 from natural populations across 20 sites in China. For each individual tree, fully expanded sun-exposed leaves were sampled. Immediately after collection, leaves were placed into insulated containers pre-chilled with ice packs and transported to the laboratory on the same day.</p><p>In the laboratory, leaf surfaces were rinsed under running tap water to remove dust and debris, then sterilized by immersion in 75% ethanol for 3 min, rinsing with purified water for 1 min, and a final rinse in 75% ethanol for 30 s. After surface treatment, leaves were rapidly frozen and stored on dry ice / at −80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg of frozen leaf tissue was weighed into a centrifuge tube for metabolite extraction. A total of 400 µL extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as internal standard was added to each sample.</p><p>Samples were homogenized using a cryogenic tissue grinder at −10 °C and 50 Hz for 6 min, followed by ultrasonic extraction at 5 °C and 40 kHz for 30 min. The extracts were then incubated at −20 °C for 30 min to precipitate proteins and centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was transferred to autosampler vials for UHPLC-MS/MS analysis.</p><p>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of extract from all samples; one QC sample was injected after every 10 real samples to monitor system stability.</p>	<p>Untargeted metabolomics was performed on a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system equipped with an ACQUITY BEH C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.7 µm; Waters, USA). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p><p>Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water and 5% acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid (v/v), and mobile phase B consisted of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol, and 5% water with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). The flow rate and gradient of mobile phase B were as follows:</p><p>0-3.5 min: 0-24.5% B, 0.40 mL/min</p><p>3.5-5.0 min: 24.5-64% B, 0.40 mL/min</p><p>5.0-5.5 min: 65-100% B, 0.40 mL/min</p><p>5.5-7.4 min: 100% B, 0.40 to 0.60 mL/min</p><p>7.4-7.6 min: 100-51.5% B, 0.60 mL/min</p><p>7.6-7.8 min: 51.5-0% B, 0.60 to 0.50 mL/min</p><p>7.8-9.0 min: 0% B, 0.50 to 0.40 mL/min</p><p>9.0-10.0 min: 0% B, 0.40 mL/min</p><p>Samples were kept at low temperature in the autosampler before injection.</p>	<p>The UHPLC system was coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X high-resolution mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) operated in electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative modes. The scan range was m/z 70–1050.</p><p>Ion spray voltages were set to +3500 V (positive mode) and −3500 V (negative mode). The ion source temperature was 325 °C, with a sheath gas flow of 50 psi, auxiliary gas flow of 13 psi, and auxiliary gas heater temperature of 425 °C.</p><p>Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode, with a resolution of 60,000 for full MS scans and 7,500 for MS/MS scans. Normalized collision energies were set to 20, 40 and 60 V to generate fragment spectra for metabolite identification.</p>	<p>Raw UHPLC-MS/MS data were processed using the Majorbio Cloud platform. Feature detection, peak alignment and retention-time correction were performed, and only metabolite features with non-zero values in at least 80% of samples within any group were retained. Missing values were imputed using the minimum value observed in the original data matrix.</p><p>To correct for sample preparation and instrumental variation, peak intensities were normalized by total ion current (sum normalization). QC samples were used to evaluate data quality; variables with a relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% across QC injections were removed. The remaining data were log 10 transformed, yielding a normalized data matrix for subsequent statistical analyses.</p>	<p>Processed mass spectra were matched against public and in-house spectral libraries for metabolite annotation. Candidate features were compared to reference spectra in HMDB (Human Metabolome Database), Metlin, and a custom library provided by Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. Metabolite identification was based on accurate mass, retention time and MS/MS fragmentation patterns. Putative identities were assigned to metabolites that matched database entries within predefined mass-accuracy and spectral-similarity thresholds.</p>

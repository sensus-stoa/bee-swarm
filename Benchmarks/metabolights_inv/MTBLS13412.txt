Study Description	<p>A combination of bioinformatics and network pharmacology was used to screen curcumin's target genes and related signaling pathways in treating hepatocellular carcinoma. Curcumin-Containing Serum（CUR） was prepared. Cisplatin(CDDP) served as the positive control .The CCK-8 assay, scratch assay, and flow cytometry were used to assess curcumin's effects on HepG2 Cells cell proliferation, apoptosis, and cell cycle. ROS levels measurement, RT - qPCR, and Western blotting were conducted to explore curcumin's molecular mechanisms via TLR4/TRAF6/MAPKs signaling molecules.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Blood samples were collected from the experimental animals via venous puncture at predetermined time points after curcumin administration. The collected blood was transferred into heparinized centrifuge tubes and allowed to stand at room temperature for 30 min to promote coagulation. Subsequently, the samples were centrifuged at 3500 × g for 15 min at 4 °C to separate the serum. The supernatant serum was carefully transferred into clean polypropylene tubes and stored at -80 °C immediately to prevent the degradation of curcumin and its metabolites. Before sample preparation, the frozen serum was thawed slowly on ice to maintain the stability of the analytes.</p>	<p>An aliquot of 100 μL serum was pipetted into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 400 μL methanol (containing 0.1% formic acid, v/v) as the protein precipitant and extraction solvent. The mixture was vortexed vigorously for 2 min to ensure thorough mixing and protein denaturation. After standing at 4 °C for 10 min, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C to remove precipitated proteins and other macromolecular impurities. The clear supernatant was transferred into a new centrifuge tube and evaporated to dryness under a gentle stream of nitrogen at 37 °C. The residue was reconstituted with 100 μL of initial mobile phase (methanol:water = 30:70, v/v), vortexed for 1 min, and centrifuged again at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. Finally, the supernatant was transferred into autosampler vials with inner liners, and 5 μL was injected into the UHPLC-Q-TOF system for analysis.</p>	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed on an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC System equipped with an Agilent G7167B High-Speed Autosampler. A reverse-phase Agilent ZORBAX Eclipse Plus C18 column (4.6 × 150 mm, 3.5 μm) was used as the analytical column, coupled with a corresponding Agilent ZORBAX Eclipse Plus C18 guard column (4.6 × 12.5 mm, 5 μm) to protect the analytical column and improve separation stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was conducted using an Agilent 6546 LC/Q-TOF Mass Spectrometer coupled with an Agilent Jet Stream (AJS) Electrospray Ionization (ESI) source. The quadrupole-time of flight (Q-TOF) mass analyzer operated in both Positive Ion Mode (PI) and Negative Ion Mode (NI), with a scan range of m/z 100–1000 to cover curcumin and its major metabolites in serum samples.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed with Agilent MassHunter and Profinder software for peak detection (mass tolerance ±5 ppm, S/N ≥3) and alignment. Peak areas were normalized to the internal standard (curcumin-d6). Multivariate (PCA, PLS-DA) and univariate (t-test, fold change ≥2 or ≤0.5) analyses were used to screen differential metabolites (VIP ≥1.0, p &lt; 0.05).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based on accurate mass (±2 ppm), MS/MS fragmentation patterns (CID 10–40 eV), retention behavior, and matching with HMDB/MetLin databases. Structures were classified into Phase I (reduction) and Phase II (glucuronidation, sulfation) metabolites, following MSI Level 1 (authentic standard) or Level 2 (HRMS/MS + database) confidence criteria.</p>

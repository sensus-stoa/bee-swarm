Study Protocol Description	<p>MEM25 and FACHB-9 respectively represent salt-tolerant and freshwater species of the genus Chlorella. By setting salinity of 35 and 105 as the high and low salinity conditions for MEM25, and salinity of 0 and 35 as the high and low salinity conditions for FACHB-9, we measured the metabolome data. To seek the difference in salt tolerance between two classic chlorella species. The measurement conditions are publicized as follows. Approximately 500 mg of algal biomass was collected, followed by metabolite extraction.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Algal cells were suspended in 1 mL mixture of methanol and water (7/3, vol/vol) and kept at − 80 °C for 2 min.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The internal standard used was 2-chloro-l-phenylalanine (0.3 mg/mL).&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The mixture was vortexed at 60 Hz for 2 min, followed by ultrasonication at ambient temperature for 30 min.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Samples were then centrifuged at 13,000 rpm, 4 °C for 15 min.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The supernatants were collected using crystal syringes, filtered through 0.22 μm microfilters and transferred to LC vials, followed by storage at − 80 °C.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>	<p>Biological samples were vacuum freeze-dried and grinded (30 Hz,1.5 min) into powder using a grinding instrument (MM 400, Retsch). 100 mg of the powder were weighed and dissolved in 1.0 mL of the extract. After dissolution, the samples were stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C overnight, during which were vortexed three times to increase the extraction rate. After centrifugation (rotational speed x 10,000 g, 10 min), the supernatant was drawn and the sample was filtered with a microporous filter membrane (0.22 μm pore size), and stored it in the injection bottle for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>LC–MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (1290, Agilent Technologies) (Shim-pack UFLC SHIMADZU CBM30A), and an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm*100 mm (Waters) chromatographic column. The mobile phases consisted on the aqueous phase (ultrapure water, with 0.04% acetic acid added) and the organic phase (acetonitrile, with 0.04% acetic acid added). The elution gradient was as follows, water/acetonitrile (95:5 V/V) at 0 min, 5:95 V/V at 11.0 min, 5:95 V/V at 12.0 min, 95:5 V/V at 12.1 min, and 95:5 V/V at 15.0 min. Flow rate was 0.4 ml/min, column temperature was 40 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μl.</p>	<p>Tandem mass spectrometry (MS) Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) was performed using an Applied Biosystems 6500 QTRAP. The main mass spectrometry conditions include: Electrospray ionization (ESI) temperature of 500 °C, mass spectrometry voltage at 5500 V, and the curtain gas (CUR) at 25 psi. The collision-activated dissociation (CAD) parameter was set to high. In the triple quadrupole (QQQ), each ion pair is scanned and detected based on the optimized declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE) (Chen et al., 2013).</p><p><br></p>	<p>Qualitative and quantitative principles of metabolites</p><p>Based on the self-built database MWDB (metware database) and the public database of metabolite information, qualitative analysis of substances was conducted based on secondary spectral information. During the analysis, isotope signals, repetitive signals containing K+ ions, Na+ ions, and NH4+ ions, as well as repetitive signals of fragment ions that are themselves from other substances with larger molecular weights were removed. The quantification of metabolites was accomplished through the analysis using the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode of triple quadrupole mass spectrometry, as shown in the following figure. In the MRM mode, the quadrupole first screens the precursor ions (parent ions) of the target substance and eliminates the ions corresponding to other molecular weight substances to initially eliminate interference. The precursor ions are induced to ionize in the collision chamber and then break to form many fragment ions. These fragment ions are then filtered through a triple quadrupole to select the required characteristic fragment ion, eliminating the interference of non-target ions, making the quantification more accurate and the repeatability better. After obtaining the spectral analysis data of metabolic substances from different samples, the peak area of the mass spectrometry peaks of all substances was integrated, and the mass spectrometry peaks of the same metabolite in different samples were integrally corrected (Fraga et al. 2010).</p><p><br></p><p><br></p><p>Qualitative and quantitative analysis of metabolites</p><p>Process mass spectrometry data using software Analyst 1.6.3. The following figure shows the Total ion current diagram (TIC, that is, the spectrum obtained by continuously drawing the sum of the intensities of all ions in the mass spectrum at each time point) of the mixed sample quality control QC sample and the multi-peak diagram of MRM metabolite detection (ion current spectrum of multi-substance extraction, XIC). The horizontal axis represents the Retention time (Rt) of metabolite detection, and the vertical axis represents the ion current intensity (the intensity unit is cps, count per second) of ion detection. Based on the local metabolic database, the metabolites of the samples were qualitatively and quantitatively analyzed by mass spectrometry.</p>	<p>Qualitative and quantitative principles of metabolites:</p><p><br></p><p>Based on the self-built database MWDB (metware database) and the public database of metabolite information, qualitative analysis of substances was conducted based on secondary spectral information.

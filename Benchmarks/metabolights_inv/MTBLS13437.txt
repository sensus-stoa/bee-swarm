Study Identifier	MTBLS13437
Study Title	A synthetic cell with integrated DNA self-replication and lipid biosynthesis
Study Description	<p>The emergence, organization, and persistence of cellular life are the result of the functional integration of metabolic and genetic networks. Here, we engineer phospholipid vesicles that can operate three essential functions, namely transcription-translation of a partial genome, self-replication of this DNA program, and membrane synthesis. The synthetic genome encodes six proteins, and its compartmentalized expression produces active liposomes with distinct phenotypes demonstrating successful module integration. Our results reveal that genetic factors exert a stronger control over DNA replication and membrane synthesis than metabolic crosstalk or module co-activity. By showing how genetically encoded functions derived from different species can be integrated in liposome compartments, our work opens new avenues for the construction of autonomous and evolving synthetic cells.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples are collected from liposomes populations. Original lipid composition consisted of 49.5% DOPC, 33.7% DOPE, 12% DOPG, 3.8% 18:1 CL, and 1% DSPE-PEG-biotin mass composition, and liposomes were formed by gentle swelling using In Vitro Transcription Translation reactions using PUREfrex2.0 (GeneFrontier).</p><p>Samples consisted of liposomes containing DNA and negative controls where DNA was replaced by water.</p><p>2 - 4 ul samples were taken at time zero and following 16 hours of incubation at&nbsp;34°C. Samples were stored for maximum 2 weeks before analysis at -25 °C.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Two to four microliters of liposome suspension were diluted 10x in a sample preparation solution consisting of a 1:1:98 ratio of 0.5 M EDTA:200 mM acetylacetone:100% methanol. The solution was sonicated for 10 min, and centrifuged at 16.1 g and room temperature for 5-10 min. 15 μL of the supernatant with the soluble lipid phase were transferred to a 25 μL glass insert within a 2 mL LC-MS glass vial, further diluted 4× with the sample preparation solution, and stored under argon at −20 °C 850 Deleted: x until use (less than a week).</p>	<p>10&nbsp;μL sample kept at 4 °C were injected into an Agilent 6460 Triple Quad LC-MS stocked with a&nbsp;ACQUITY UPLC Peptide CSH C18 Column with a mobile phase A of 0.05% ammonium hydroxide and 2 mM acetylacetone in water, and a mobile phase B of 80% 2-propanol, 20% acetonitrile, 0.05% ammonium hydroxide and 2 mM acetylacetone, at a flow rate of 300 μL min^–1 and column temperature of 60 °C. An A:B ratio of 70:30 was set to equilibrate the column. Upon sample injection, the A:B ratio was slowly changed to 100% mobile phase B and kept for two min. Then, the 70:30 ratio was gradually restored and kept until the end of each sample run.</p>	<p>Samples were injected into an&nbsp;Agilent 6460 Triple Quadripole LC-MS operating in negative ionisation mode at a flow rate of 300 μL min^–1&nbsp;and column temperature of 60 °C.</p>	<p>Data were analyzed with Skyline-daily. Peak areas were exported and normalized to DOPG peak areas. When two injections were done per sample, the averaged peak area was considered.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Transitions were used based on previous studies (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0163058 and https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3923781) as determined from purified standards. Data were analyzed with Skyline-daily. Peak areas were exported and normalized to DOPG peak areas. When two injections were done per sample, the averaged peak area was considered.&nbsp;</p>

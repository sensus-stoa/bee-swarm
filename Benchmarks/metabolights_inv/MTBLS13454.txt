Study Description	<p>Early-life experiences profoundly shape brain development and long-term cognition. Environmental enrichment (EE) and social isolation (SI) represent opposing forms of stimulation and deprivation, yet the metabolic mechanisms connecting these conditions to cognitive outcomes remain poorly defined. In this study, we investigated how EE and SI during adolescence influence hippocampal metabolism and cognition in adult C57BL/6 mice.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>After behavioral testing, hippocampal tissue was carefully dissected from each mouse under aseptic conditions to avoid contamination.The samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen to preserve metabolic activity and stored at -80°C until further analysis. The tissue samples were processed for non-targeted metabolomics analysis to examine the metabolic profiles of the hippocampus.</p>	<p>Frozen hippocampal tissues (80 °C) were thawed on ice, and all subsequent procedures were performed under the same conditions. Each sample was minced and homogenized, and approximately 20 mg (±1 mg) of tissue was transferred into pre-labeled microcentrifuge tubes. A stainless-steel bead was added to each tube, and homogenization was performed using a bead mill (30 Hz, 20 s). Samples were briefly centrifuged (4 °C, 3,000 rpm, 30 s) to collect material at the bottom. An extraction solution (400 µL, 70% methanol in water containing internal standards) was added, vortexed at 1,500 rpm for 5 min, and kept on ice for 15 min to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation (4 °C, 12,000 rpm, 10 min), 300 µL of the supernatant was transferred to new tubes, stored at 20 °C for 30 min, and re-centrifuged (4 °C, 12,000 rpm, 3 min). Finally, 200 µL of the clarified supernatant was transferred into autosampler vials with glass inserts for LC–MS analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed in both positive and negative ion modes using identical chromatographic conditions. Separation was achieved on a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The mobile phases were: (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The column was maintained at 40 °C, with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min and an injection volume of 4 µL. The gradient program was as follows: 5% B (0.0 min), 20% B (2.0 min), 60% B (5.0 min), 99% B (6.0 min, held to 7.5 min), and 5% B (7.6–10.0 min). The same gradient was applied for both ionization modes.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed on a Q Exactive HF-X instrument operating in data-dependent acquisition mode, alternating between full-scan MS and MS². Data were collected in both positive and negative electrospray ionization modes over an m/z range of 75–1000 at a resolution of 35,000 (full scan). Source parameters were as follows: spray voltage, 3.5 kV (positive) or 3.2 kV (negative); sheath gas, 30 a.u.; auxiliary gas, 5 a.u.; ion transfer tube temperature, 320 °C; vaporizer temperature, 300 °C. Data-dependent MS² acquisition used stepped normalized collision energies (30, 40, 50), a top-10 precursor selection scheme, an intensity threshold of 1 × 100000, and a dynamic exclusion of 3 s. MS² resolution was set to 17,500 with an AGC target of 2 × 100000, while MS¹ used an AGC target of 1 × 1000000.</p>	<p>The raw data obtained from the non-targeted metabolomics analysis were processed using the ProteoWizard software suite, which allows for the conversion of various file formats (e.g., .raw, .mzML). The data were then subjected to XCMS for peak detection, alignment, and chromatogram extraction. For data normalization, scaling methods such as Pareto scaling and log transformation were applied to minimize technical variations and facilitate comparisons across samples. Quality control (QC) samples were included in the analysis to monitor instrument stability and ensure reliable data interpretation. The resulting processed data were then used for further statistical analysis, including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was carried out by first using XCMS and MetaboAnalyst software to detect significant features from the processed data. The identified features were then matched against reference databases such as HMDB (Human Metabolome Database), KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes), and MassBank to annotate the metabolites. In cases where the metabolite was not directly matched, a RT-m/z (Retention Time and m/z ratio) matching approach was applied. Further confirmation of metabolite identities was performed through mass spectrometry fragmentation patterns and comparison with known standards. Metabolite identification confidence was assigned according to the criteria provided by the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI).</p>

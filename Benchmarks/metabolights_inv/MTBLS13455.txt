Study Protocol Description	<p>Following approval by the Ethics Committee of Chongqing Municipal Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, all participants provided written informed consent prior to inclusion in the study. Venous blood samples were collected from two cohorts: patients clinically diagnosed with osteoarthritis (OA) and age- and sex-matched healthy control (HC) volunteers. Blood was drawn into EDTA-coated vacutainer tubes. Within one hour of collection, the samples were centrifuged at 3,000 g for 15 minutes at 4°C to separate the plasma. The resulting supernatant (plasma) was carefully collected, aliquoted into sterile cryovials, and immediately flash-frozen before being stored at -80°C until metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Plasma samples were thawed on ice. For each sample, 400 µL of a pre-chilled methanol/water solution (80:20, v/v) was added to 100 µL of plasma. The mixture was vortexed for 1 minute, incubated at -20°C for 60 minutes for protein precipitation, and then centrifuged at 14,000 g for 15 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant was carefully transferred to a new vial for LC-MS analysis. Quality Control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots (20 µL) from each sample.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC system. A Thermo Scientific Hypersil Gold column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.9 µm) was used and maintained at 40°C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B). A linear gradient was applied at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The injection volume was 5 µL. The total run time was 18 minutes per sample.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF Orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with a Heated Electrospray Ionization (HESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes in separate runs. The scan range was set to 100-1500 m/z. The full scan MS1 resolution was 70,000. For metabolite identification, data-dependent MS/MS (ddMS2) was also acquired on the QC samples.</p>	<p>The raw data files (.raw) generated by the Thermo mass spectrometer were converted to the open-standard mzML format using the msConvert tool, which is part of the ProteoWizard software suite. During the conversion process, vendor-specific peak picking algorithms were applied to centroid the data, preparing it for subsequent feature detection and analysis.</p>	<p>The converted mzML files were processed using an open-source pipeline, such as XCMS. The workflow included peak detection, retention time correction, and peak alignment across all samples. Metabolite annotation was performed by matching the accurate mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against public databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and KEGG, with a mass tolerance of less than 5 ppm.</p>

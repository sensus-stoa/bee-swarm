Study Protocol Description	<p>Tumor samples were collected from female 4-week-old BALB/c mice obtained from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. All procedures were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Fuzhou University (No. KS2023-SG-020). After a 7-day acclimation period, mice were assigned to three groups (control, low-dose BBP, and high-dose BBP), with BBP administered daily at 0.2 mg/kg/day or 20 mg/kg/day according to a human exposure-based multi-pathway dose conversion model.</p><p>After 18 weeks of exposure, 4T1-Luc cells were orthotopically implanted into the mammary fat pad of each mouse. Four weeks after tumor implantation, mice were anesthetized and injected intraperitoneally with 200 µL sodium fluorescein for in vivo fluorescence imaging. Tumor tissues were harvested immediately after dissection, following standardized protocols to ensure sample integrity, and stored appropriately for subsequent metabolomic and lipidomic analyses.</p>	<p>Tumor tissue samples were stored at −80 °C until analysis. After complete freezing, the tissues were sectioned at a thickness of 10 μm using a cryostat. Approximately 20 mg of central tumor tissue was accurately weighed and transferred into a grinding tube containing 600 μL of methanol and 150 μL of water, along with zirconia grinding beads.</p><p>The tissues were homogenized using a bead mill homogenizer at 70 Hz for 60 s, repeated for three cycles. Subsequently, 450 μL of chloroform and 150 μL of water were added to the homogenate, followed by vortex mixing for 1 min and a 5 min resting period. Phase separation was achieved by centrifugation at 12,000 g and 4 °C for 15 min.</p><p>The upper aqueous phase was collected for metabolomics analysis. The extract was dried under nitrogen, reconstituted with 50 μL methanol and 50 μL water, vortexed for 1 min, and centrifuged at 12,500 g and 4 °C for 15 min. The supernatant was transferred into LC vials for injection.</p><p>A 10 μL aliquot from each sample was pooled to generate quality control (QC) samples.</p>	<p>Ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC system. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (1.7 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm).</p><p>Mobile phase A consisted of water with 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was set to 0.3 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows:</p><p>0–1 min, 2% B;</p><p>1–19 min, 2% -100% B;</p><p>19–21 min, 100% B;</p><p>21–30 min, 2% B for column re-equilibration.</p><p>The column temperature was maintained at 35 °C, and the injection volume was 5 μL. Quality control (QC) samples were injected every four samples to monitor chromatographic stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed on a Waters Xevo G2-QTOF instrument equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes.</p><p>Capillary voltage was set to 2 kV for positive mode and 3 kV for negative mode. The cone gas, desolvation gas, and nebulizing gas were all nitrogen, while argon was used as the collision gas.</p><p>Source temperature was 150 °C, desolvation temperature was 600 °C, cone gas flow was 150 L/h, and desolvation gas flow was 1000 L/h.</p><p>Data were collected in MSE mode with a collision energy ramp of 6–66 eV. The scan time was 0.3 s, and data were acquired in centroid mode over an m/z range of 50–1200.</p>	<p>Raw high-resolution mass spectrometry data were processed using Progenesis QI software (Waters, USA) for peak detection, peak alignment, and peak area normalization. Metabolite identification was performed by matching features against the built-in METLIN database (https://metlin.scripps.edu/index.php) and the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, http://www.hmdb.ca). The software generated a comprehensive output file containing all detected features, including m/z values, retention times, and annotation information.</p>	<p>Peak signals extracted from the dataset were compared between the control and low-dose groups, or between the control and high-dose groups. Only features meeting both criteria—p &lt; 0.05 and fold change &gt; 1.2 or &lt; 0.8—were considered potential differential metabolites for further identification.</p><p>Based on the automatic annotation results generated by Progenesis QI using the METLIN and Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) libraries, an additional round of manual verification was performed through HMDB. During this process, only endogenous human metabolites or exogenous compounds with physiological relevance were retained, while industrial chemicals or substances not present in humans were removed.</p>

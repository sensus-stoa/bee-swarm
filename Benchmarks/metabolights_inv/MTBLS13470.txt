Study Title	Cholecystectomy inhibits fasting hepatic fatty acid oxidation in mice
Study Description	<p>Cholecystectomy is associated with an increased risk of metabolic syndrome (MetS); however, the underlying mechanism remains unknown. The gallbladder acts as a storage organ for hepatic bile and regulates the feeding/fasting cycles of bile acid (BA) flow in the enterohepatic circulation (EHC). In this study, we aimed to use C57BL/6 mice to investigate the effects of cholecystectomy in the regulation of glucose homeostasis and bile acid metabolism with metabolomics and quantitative RT-PCR. The results show that cholecystectomy increases fasting hepatic BA levels by enhancing EHC. Livers from cholecystectomized (XGB) mice displayed suppression of genes involved in fatty acid oxidation (FAO), abnormal lipid accumulation, and marked remodeling of their metabolomic profiles, particularly a reduction in FAO intermediate acylcarnitines. Many FAO genes were transcriptional targets of the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor α (PPARα), and BA inhibited PPARα, resulting in impeded FAO. Consistent with this, blocking intestinal BA uptake using an apical sodium-BA transporter inhibitor enhanced fasting hepatic FAO levels and ameliorated metabolic disorders in XGB mice. Analysis of patient peripheral blood revealed directional trends in bile acid and fatty acid metabolites that were consistent with the metabolic disruptions observed in mice. Nevertheless, these findings suggest that cholecystectomy could inhibit fasting hepatic FAO by disturbing the EHC of BA, and reveal the role of the gallbladder in coordinating PPARα-regulated FAO in the liver.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Serum samples were collected from C57BL/6 mice involved in a study of cholecystectomy effects. Eight-week-old male and female mice were randomly assigned to either cholecystectomy or sham operation groups. Following surgery, all mice were fed an experimental diet for 6 weeks under controlled light-dark cycles (12h:12h, dark phase 6:00 PM to 6:00 AM) with&nbsp;ad libitum&nbsp;feeding during the dark phase only. Blood was collected at the end of the 6-week dietary intervention.</p><p>Serum was subsequently separated by centrifugation, aliquoted, and immediately stored at -80°C upon receipt until analysis. For quality assurance, QC samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of experimental samples, and solvent blank samples were prepared using a 53% methanol-water solution.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from serum samples using a methanol-based protein precipitation method. Briefly, 100 μL of serum was mixed with 400 μL of ice-cold 80% methanol. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly, incubated on ice for 5 minutes, and then centrifuged at 15,000 g for 20 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant was collected and diluted with mass spectrometry-grade water to a final methanol concentration of 53%. The diluted supernatant was centrifuged again at 15,000 g for 20 minutes at 4°C. The final supernatant was transferred to an LC-MS vial for analysis. QC samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all experimental serum extracts. Solvent blank samples were prepared by substituting serum with 53% methanol and processing it identically through the extraction protocol.</p>	<p>Reverse phase liquid chromatography was performed using a Thermo Fisher Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with a Thermo Hypersil Gold C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.9 μm particle size). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C with a constant flow rate of 0.2 mL/min.</p>	<p>Instrument: Thermo Fisher Q Exactive™ HF Mass Spectrometer</p><p>Ion Source: Heated Electrospray Ionization (H-ESI)</p><p>Scan Range: *m/z* 100–1500</p><p>Spray Voltage: 3.5 kV</p><p>Sheath Gas Flow Rate: 35 psi</p><p>Auxiliary Gas Flow Rate: 10 L/min</p><p>Capillary Temperature: 320 °C</p><p>S-lens RF Level: 60</p><p>Auxiliary Gas Heater Temperature: 350 °C</p><p>Polarity: Positive &amp; Negative</p><p>Scan Mode: Data-dependent MS/MS scanning</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data files were processed using Compound Discoverer software (version 3.1). The processing pipeline included the following steps:</p><p>Peak Picking: Features were extracted with a mass tolerance of 5 ppm, a signal intensity tolerance of 30%, and a minimum signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio of 3.</p><p>Alignment: Peak lists from all samples were aligned using a retention time tolerance of 0.2 minutes and a mass tolerance of 5 ppm to correct for run-to-run variations.</p><p>Gap Filling: Missing peaks were re-integrated from the raw data to create a comprehensive feature table.</p><p>Normalization: For downstream statistical analysis, the data were normalized using the z-score method.</p><p>The output was a quantitative feature table containing peak areas across all samples.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using a multi-step approach:</p><p>Database Searching: Extracted features were searched against the mzCloud mass spectral library within the Compound Discoverer 3.1 software. Molecular formula prediction was performed using isotopic patterns and fragment ions.</p><p>Confidence Levels: Identifications were assigned confidence levels based on matching scores. A “Full match” in mzCloud for MS/MS spectra was considered a high-confidence match.</p>

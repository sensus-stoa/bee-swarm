Study Title	Antimicrobial Efficacy of Bifidobacterium longum FB1-4 Cell-Free Supernatant Against MRSA: Insights into Mechanisms and Food Matrix Application
Study Description	<p>The food safety landscape faces a significant challenge from methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), a virulent and antibiotic-resistant foodborne pathogen. This study investigates the antimicrobial properties and mechanisms of cell-free supernatant (CFS) from Bifidobacterium longum FB1-4 against MRSA. The CFS exhibited robust antibacterial activity, achieving a minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of 5.56 mg/ml and maintaining a bacterial elimination rate of over 80% for 8 hours at 2× MIC, demonstrating superior sustained bactericidal efficacy compared to vancomycin. Antibacterial component analysis revealed that while the initial effect is partly pH-dependent, the activity is largely attributable to heat-stable molecules, retaining 90% efficacy after high-temperature treatment. In a food matrix, FB1-4 CFS exhibited impressive bactericidal effects against three MRSA strains, leading to elimination rates of 94.64% at 2 hours, 98.02% at 4 hours, 99.90% at 6 hours, and nearly complete eradication (99.99%) by 8 hours. Furthermore, FB1-4 CFS significantly suppressed all three tested MRSA strains (MRSA-43300, MRSA-337371, and MRSA-361194); P&lt;0.05) mecA expression, increased antibiotic susceptibility, and diminished biofilm formation by 70%. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses revealed that FB1-4 CFS modulates two-component systems and quorum sensing, leading to reduced virulence factor expression. In vivo studies confirmed a 90% reduction in MRSA colonization in mouse intestines. These findings provide critical insights into how probiotic metabolites inhibit MRSA, underscoring their potential as natural therapeutic agents in food safety.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The sample used for metabolic assays is culture supernatant. Specifically, 1 mL of culture medium from a strain cultured for 18 h is transferred to a centrifuge tube. Centrifuge at 8000 rpm at 4 °C for 10 min. Remove the supernatant and filter it through a 0.22 μm membrane. Store the filtered supernatant at -80 °C to ensure sample quality.</p>	<p>The&nbsp;samples were incubated on ice for 5 min and then were centrifuged at 15,000 x g, 4 °C&nbsp;for 20 min. Some of supernatant was diluted to final concentration containing 53%&nbsp;methanol by LC-MS grade water. The samples were subsequently transferred to a&nbsp;fresh Eppendorf tube and then were centrifuged at 15,000 x g, 4 °C for 20 min. Finally,&nbsp;the supernatant was injected into the LC-MS/MS system analysis.</p>	<p>UHPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system&nbsp;(ThermoFisher, Germany) in Novogene Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). Samples were injected onto an&nbsp;ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide Column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm) using a 12-min linear&nbsp;gradient at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The eluents for the positive polarity mode were&nbsp;eluent A (0.1% FA in 95% ACN, 10 mM ammonium acetate) and eluent B (0.1% FA&nbsp;in 50% ACN, 10 mM ammonium acetate). The eluents for the negative polarity mode&nbsp;were eluent A (95% ACN, 10 mM ammonium acetate) and eluent B (50% ACN, 10 mM ammonium acetate). </p>	<p>UHPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer or Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (ThermoFisher, Germany) in Novogene Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with spray voltage of 3.5 kV, capillary temperature of 320 °C, sheath gas flow rate of 35 arb and aux gas flow rate of 10 arb, S-lens RF level of 60, Aux gas heater temperature of 350 °C.</p>	<p>The data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed by XCMS to perform&nbsp;peak alignment, peak picking and quantitation for each metabolite. Then, based on&nbsp;adduct ions and setting mass deviation to 10 ppm, a comparison was made between&nbsp;these data and the high-quality secondary spectrum database to obtain results for&nbsp;metabolite identification. After eliminating background ions according to blank&nbsp;samples, the original quantitative results were normalized by the following formula to&nbsp;obtain relative peak areas: Relative peakareas = Raw quantitative value of samples /&nbsp;(The sum of quantitative value of samples / The sum of quantitative value of QC1);&nbsp;Compounds with a coefficient of variation (CV) of relative peak areas in QC samples&nbsp;greater than 30% were removed. Finally, the identification and relative quantitation&nbsp;results of metabolites were obtained. Data processing is based on the Linux operating&nbsp;system (CentOS version 6.6), using R and Python.</p>	<p>These metabolites were annotated using the LIPIDMAPS database. Principal&nbsp;components analysis (PCA) and Partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA)&nbsp;were performed at metaX[6] (a flexible and comprehensive software for processing&nbsp;metabolomics data). We applied univariate analysis (t-test) to calculate the statistical&nbsp;significance (P-value). The metabolites with VIP &gt; 1 and P-value &lt; 0.05 and fold change ≥ 2 or FC ≤ 0.5 were considered to be differential metabolites. Volcano plots were used to filter metabolites of interest which based on log2(FoldChange) and -log10(p-value) of metabolites by ggplot2 in R language. For clustering heat maps, the data were normalized using z-scores of the intensity areas of differential metabolites and were ploted by Pheatmap package in R language. The correlation between differential metabolites were analyzed in R language (method=pearson). Statistically significant of correlation between differential metabolites were calculated in R language. P-value &lt; 0.05 was considered as statistically significant and correlation plots were ploted by corrplot package in R language. The functions of these metabolites and metabolic pathways were studied using the KEGG database. The metabolic pathways enrichment of differential metabolites was performed, when ratio were satisfied by x/n &gt; y/N, metabolic pathway were considered asenrichment, when P-value of metabolic enrichment. Reference pathway &lt; 0.05, metabolic pathway were considered as statistically significant&nbsp;enrichment.</p>

Study Title	Host–gut microbiota–bile acid axis integrates dietary starch to regulate milk fatty acid metabolism in dairy goats
Study Description	<p> Bile acids (BA) and BA-related microbiota are essential for ruminant lipid metabolism, yet targeted modulation to enhance production and health remain underexplored. Herein, we investigated the interplay between dietary rumen degradable starch, BA metabolism, and milk fatty acids (FAs) synthesis in dairy goats using a multi-omics approach. Lactating dairy goats of comparable milk yield and body weight were allocated to dietary treatments differing in rumen degradable starch to evaluate impacts on milk FAs profiles, gut BA metabolism, and gut microbiota. Additional BA supplementation was applied to assess its potential to mitigate high rumen degradable-rate starch (HRDS)-induced alterations, with targeted tissue sampling to investigate microbe–host interactions. Goats fed the HRDS diet exhibited significant lower milk FAs concentration and yield compared to those on LRDS. Targeted metabolomic analyses revealed that the HRDS diet led to marked deficiencies in five key small intestine BA-cholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid, lithocholic acid, hyocholic acid, and hyodeoxycholic acid. Macrogenomic sequencing identified Clostridium and Turicibacter as key microbial drivers involved in primary (ko00120) and secondary (ko00121) BA metabolism, with significant downregulation of 7α-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (7α-HSDH) in the HRDS group. BA supplementation restored both milk FAs concentration and yield. Additionally, HRDS reduced small intestine FAs concentration and decreased the abundance of Oscillospiraceae_bacterium, a taxon positively correlated with small intestine FAs concentration. Taurine-conjugated bile acids were found to mediate this effect. BA supplementation enhanced BA metabolism and increased activities of bile salt hydrolases and 7α-HSDH in the small intestine, and upregulation host FAs degradation and uptake responses, notably through increased FA binding protein 6 expression. In conclusion, our comprehensive investigation into the interplay between diets, gut microbes, and BA sheds light on the intricate mechanisms regulating milk FAs synthesis in dairy goats.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>At the conclusion of the experimental period, all dairy goats were humanely euthanized, and the internal organs were promptly dissected. Subsequently, the gastrointestinal tract of each goat was meticulously separated, and the luminal contents of the small intestine were thoroughly homogenized. Three replicate 5 mL aliquots of the homogenized luminal contents were aseptically collected from the small intestine regions&nbsp;</p>	<p>10 μL of a working solution containing an internal standard mixture (1 μg/mL) and 200 μL of methanol/acetonitrile (v/v = 2: 8) were added for homogenization. Following homogenization, the samples were shaken at 2500 r/min for 10 min, and then kept at -20°C for 10 min to precipitate proteins. Afterward, the samples underwent centrifugation for 10 min at 12,000 r/min and 4°C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography</p><p>Mobile Phase:</p><p>Phase A: Ultrapure water (containing 0.01% acetic acid and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate).</p><p>Phase B: Acetonitrile (containing 0.01% acetic acid).</p><p>Chromatographic Conditions:</p><p>Flow rate: 0.35 mL/min.</p><p>Column temperature: 40 °C.</p><p>Injection volume: 1 μL.</p>	<p>Tandem Mass Spectrometry</p><p>Mass Spectrometry Conditions:</p><p>Ion source: Electrospray Ionization (ESI).</p><p>ESI temperature: 550 °C.</p><p>Ion spray voltage: -4500 V.</p><p>Curtain Gas (CUR): 35 psi.</p><p>In the triple quadrupole, each ion pair was monitored according to the optimized Declustering Potential (DP) and Collision Energy (CE).</p>	<p> After unblinding and data release, the metabolite profiles underwent quality control checks and were preprocessed to ensure data quality, involving steps such as adjusting for batch effects, imputing missing values, and log-transformation.</p>	<p>The qualitative analysis of the mass spectrometry data was performed based on the MWDB (Metware Database) established using standard compounds.</p><p><br></p><p>Quantification was achieved using the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode of a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. In MRM mode, the first quadrupole filters the precursor ions (parent ions) of the target analyte, excluding ions corresponding to other molecular species to preliminarily reduce interference. These precursor ions are then fragmented into multiple product ions within the collision cell. Subsequently, the third quadrupole filters and selects the required characteristic fragment ions from these product ions, further excluding non-target ion interferences. This two-stage selection process enhances quantification precision and improves reproducibility. Following the acquisition of mass spectrometry data from all samples, the chromatographic peaks for all target compounds were integrated, and quantitative analysis was conducted based on standard curves.</p>

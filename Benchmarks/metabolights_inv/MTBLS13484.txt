Study Title	Atlas of one-carbon metabolism in conventional and germ-free mice reveals folate as a key determinant of biochemical pathways
Study Description	<p>Folates participate in the one-carbon metabolism (OCM) cycle, supporting many biochemical pathways. Existing methods to profile folate are limited in the diversity of vitamers they measure and the samples they profile. We present a metabolomics workflow for stable extraction, separation, and measurement of folates, along with precursors and products of OCM-associated pathways. We profiled these metabolites in 37 mouse tissues to chart the first ‘OCM atlas’, revealing vast heterogeneity across organs and a novel folate derivative. We discovered that in adult mice, the gut microbiota is a consumer of folate, and folate polyglutamylation in the host is not regulated by folate availability. Germ-free mice show tissue-specific shifts in methyl donor abundances relative to conventionally-raised mice, indicative of altered DNA methylation. Correlation analyses uncovered the central roles played by folates in potentially modulating other biochemical pathways in tissues, thus linking microbial folate consumption directly to its global impacts on host metabolism. (https://chaudharilab.com/folate-atlas/)</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Conventional C57BL/6 male and female mice (age 6-8 weeks) were purchased from Jackson Laboratory (Bar Harbor, ME), and germ-free male and female animals were purchased from the National&nbsp;Gnotobiotic&nbsp;Rodent Resource Center (NGRRC) at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and bred at University of Wisconsin-Madison. Conventional animals were fed standard chow (LabDiet 5008; LabDiet, St Louis, MO), while germ-free animals were fed autoclaved breeder chow (LabDiet 5021; LabDiet, St Louis, MO) and maintained in a controlled environment in plastic flexible film gnotobiotic isolators. Folate-replete (LabDiet 57W5; LabDiet, St Lous, MO) and folate-deficient (LabDiet 57W5 without added folate; LabDiet, St Lous, MO) diets were fed to conventional male mice (age 6-8 weeks) for 2 weeks. All animals were maintained in climate-controlled animal facilities with a 12 h light-dark cycle and ad libitum access to food and water. For organ collection, male and female animals at 6-8 weeks of age were fasted for 4 h prior to being euthanized by cervical dislocation. Red blood cells were collected via cardiac puncture and serum was separated by centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 20 min at RT. Stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, and colon tissues were harvested, and contents were collected by squeezing into Eppendorf tubes. Gastrointestinal tissues were then washed in 1X DPBS (GeneClone 25-508) prior to collection in Eppendorf tubes. Bone marrow was harvested from the hind limbs, tibia, and femur by flushing with 200 µL of cold, 1X DPBS using a needle and syringe. Other tissues (esophagus, portal vein, BAT, gWAT, iWAT, mWAT, heart, lung, skeletal muscle, gallbladder, skin, liver, thymus, kidney, brain, pancreas, spleen, urinary bladder, testicles, prostate, uterus, ovaries) were rapidly collected, and all tissues were snap frozen on dry ice immediately to prevent abiotic folate interconversion. Samples were stored at -80oC until processing. Experiments were approved by the IACUC at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. No mouse data points have been excluded from this study or analyses, unless lost during processing as indicated in figure legends. </p>	<p>Frozen tissues were cut on dry ice and weighed for extraction. Approximately 50 mg of tissue was used for extraction. For smaller tissues such as portal vein, gallbladder, ovary, and esophagus, all available tissue was used for each extraction. Sample was added to a 2 ml bead homogenizing tube (Fisher Scientific, Fitchburg, WI 02681374) with glass beads (Bertin Technologies, Montigny-le-Bretonneux, France P000928-LYSK0-A.0). 400 μL of folate extraction buffer (20 mM ascorbic acid, 20 mM ammonium acetate, 20 mM ß-mercaptoethanol (BME), pH 8.1 adjusted with sodium hydroxide, prepared fresh) was added to each tissue sample and homogenized for 1 minute at 4 m/s (Bead Ruptor Elite, Omni International, Kennesaw, GA). For blood, approximately 50 mg of packed red blood cells were weighed and processed as described above. For serum and marrow, 50 μL of sample was pipetted into folate extraction buffer and vortexed for 30 seconds. For all samples internal standards were spiked into extraction buffer prior to homogenization. Homogenates were kept on ice unless noted otherwise. Homogenates were centrifuged at 12,700 rpm for 5 minutes at 4°C. For monoglutamylated folate analysis, 200 μL of supernatant was aliquoted into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube and incubated at 37°C for 45 minutes. For total folate analysis, 200 μL of supernatant was aliquoted into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube, 5 μL of pooled rat serum (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX catalog sc-45135) was added, and sample was vortexed briefly to mix, and incubated at 37°C for 45 minutes. After incubation, tubes were stored on ice before being boiled at 100°C for 5 minutes. Samples were centrifuged at 12,700 rpm for 5 minutes to pellet the remaining debris. Supernatants were filtered by solid phase extraction (SPE) using a Strata-X 33 μM polymeric reverse-phase 96-well SPE plate (Phenomenex, Torrance, CA catalog 8E-S100-AGB). After each solvent addition, samples were passed through the column by centrifugation at 2500 rpm for 30 seconds. SPE wells were conditioned using 200 μL methanol and equilibrated using 200 μL of resuspension buffer (20 mM ammonium acetate, 20 mM BME, pH 8.1). Sample supernatant was passed through the column, and wells were rinsed using 200 μL resuspension buffer. SPE plates were moved to a clean 96-well plate, and metabolites were eluted using 2 pass-throughs of 100 μL elution buffer (50: 50 methanol: water, 20 mM ammonium acetate, 20 mM BME). Eluted samples were dried down in a speedvac (Thermo Scientific, Fitchburg, WI Savant SPD120, Savant UVS450) at 35°C for 90 minutes. Dried down samples were resuspended in 40 μL of resuspension buffer and were moved to autosampler vials (Fisher Scientific, Fitchburg, WI 3452232) for LC/MS analysis. Ascorbic acid and ammonium acetate for folate extraction purchased from Santacruz Biotechnology (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, sc-202686 sc-203818) BME purchased from MP Biomedicals (MP Biomedicals,190242, Illkirch, France). Water for folate extraction was obtained from a GenPure Pro water purification system (Thermo Scientific, 50131948, Fitchburg, WI Savant).</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>For adipose tissues, approximately 100 mg of tissues were homogenized in 350 µL of folate extraction buffer mixed with 300 µL of ethanol and 50 µL of methanol. Homogenates were boiled at 100°C for 5 minutes. The samples were then centrifuged at 4°C at 12,700 rpm for 15 minutes. The supernatants were filtered through SPE using Captiva EMR (Agilent, catalog 5190-1002, Santa Clara, CA). The samples were passed through the SPE column using vacuum, followed by elution using 100 µL of 2:1:1 folate extraction buffer:MeOH:EtOH mixture passed through twice into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube. Each sample was divided into half. 5 µL of rat serum was added to one fraction for polyglutamylation analysis. The samples were incubated at 37°C for 45 minutes. Samples were next boiled at 100°C for 5 minutes, followed by immediately cooling down on ice. Samples were then filtered using Spin-X (Corning Incorporated, catalog 8163, Salt Lake City, UT) by centrifuging at 12,700 rpm for 5 minutes. Dry down in speedvac and subsequent steps were identical to other tissues as described above. </p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>To extract folate from cecal bacterial cells, samples were treated the same as above with the following modifications. After initial weighing, 400 μL of folate extraction buffer prepared in 50:50 methanol:water was added to each sample. Samples were homogenized for 1 minute at 4 m/s before being dried down in a speedvac at 35°C for 120 minutes. After drying, samples were resuspended in folate extraction buffer and treated as above protocol for total folate extraction. Folate loss due to incubation, boiling, dry down, and abiotic interconversion was minimal (Extended Data Table 2). </p>	<p>LC/MS metabolomics was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system (Thermo Scientific, Fitchburg, WI) coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, Fitchburg, WI). Chromatography was performed using a reverse-phase polar C18 column (Phenomenex, Luna Omega Polar C18, 1.6 µM particle size, 2.1x100 mm column, 00D-4748-AN, Torrance, CA). The column chamber and preheater were set to 40°C. Solvent A was 95% H2O and 5% methanol with 5 mM dimethyl-hexylamine (DMHA) (Acros Organics, 314740250, Geel, Belgium) pH 8.1 adjusted with formic acid. Solvent B was 100% methanol with 5 mM DMHA. All solvents were LC/MS grade purchased from Fisher chemical (Thermo Scientific, CAS 67-56-1, Fitchburg, WI). The total run time was 12 minutes. Flow rate was held constant at 0.4 mL/min. The chromatography gradient was as follows: 5% solvent B for 0.5 minutes, linear increase to 85% B over 5.2 minutes, increase to 100% B for 1.3 minutes, decrease to 5% to equilibrate the column for 4 minutes. Eluent from the column was analyzed by MS from the start of the run until 5 minutes, after which flow was directed to waste for the remainder of the run. </p>	<p>LC/MS metabolomics was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system (Thermo Scientific, Fitchburg, WI) coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer. Eluent from the column was analyzed by MS from the start of the run until 5 minutes, after which flow was directed to waste for the remainder of the run. Ionization was performed using a heated electrospray ionization source (HESI) in negative mode at 2500V. Nitrogen gas flow was set to 50 (sheath), 10 (aux) and 1 (sweep) in arbitrary units. The ion transfer tube was maintained at 325°C with the vaporizer at 350°C. m/z range was 100-1200</p>	<p>Quantification was performed using Freestyle (ThermoFisher, Fitchburg WI) and MAVEN80 to identify and calculate integrated peak areas. Manual curation confirmed peak identification and parameters. All signals were normalized to internal standards (methotrexate, folic acid-d2, 5-m-THF-d3) for injection variation and compared to the standard curve before reporting molar concentrations normalized to sample mass. The standard curve was modeled using linear regression. Total measured folate in each sample were calculated by adding all detected and measured folate vitamers. Standard curves prepared with folate standards purchased from Schircks Laboratories (Bauma, Switzerland) at 200 µM and serially diluted until signal could not be detected to determine limit of detection and linear range. </p>	<p>Total measured folate in each sample were calculated by adding all detected and measured folate vitamers. Standard curves prepared with folate standards purchased from Schircks Laboratories (Bauma, Switzerland) at 200 µM and serially diluted until signal could not be detected to determine limit of detection and linear range. Identification of folates without available standards was performed using SIRIUS and comparisons to previously published results. Folates without standards were quantified using the standard curve of their most chemically similar folate with an available standard. Internal standards purchased from Schircks Laboratories (methotrexate, 16.411) and Cayman Chemicals (5-m-THF-d3, 1356019-94-7, folic acid-d2, 171777-72-3, Ann Arbor, MI). </p>

Study Title	Metabolic programming and functional remodeling by a c-di-GMP-centric hierarchy in aerobic granular sludge
Study Description	<p>This study systematically elucidates the hierarchical signaling regulatory network in aerobic granular sludge (AGS) by integrating dynamic analysis of quorum sensing signal molecules, microbial community structure profiling, and metabolomic characterization. The research reveals that c-di-GMP functions as the highest-priority signal directing systemic metabolism. AI-2 acts as a synergistic optimizer and the functional expression of AHLs is governed by upstream signals. This signaling hierarchy precisely controls central carbon metabolic fluxes, thereby determining the pollutant removal efficiency, structural stability, and system resilience of AGS. Experimental results demonstrate that the synergistic action of c-di-GMP and AI-2 significantly enhances short-chain AHLs production (C8-HSL reaching 22.6 nmol/L), while AI-2 alone predominantly regulates long-chain AHLs turnover. Signal combinations achieve metabolic reprogramming through specific enrichment of functional bacterial groups (e.g., Rhodocyclaceae increased by 2.7-52.9%, Comamonadaceae increased by 6.3-53.7%). Under different storage conditions, the system exhibits differentiated recovery strategies. In response to stress, refrigerated granules activate their metabolic network globally. In contrast, dried granules initiate a recovery process that prioritizes the repair of oxidative damage following desiccation. Correlation analyses further underscore the functional links between signal molecules and key metabolic activities, such as the positive relationship between C8-HSL and ICDHc activity (p &lt; 0.05), thereby validating the proposed hierarchical regulatory model. This study paves the way for targeted quorum sensing regulation strategies and the advancement of stable, efficient, next-generation wastewater treatment processes.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The aerobic granular sludge samples used in this study were collected from a laboratory-scale sequencing batch reactor operated in our lab.</p><p>Reactor Specifications: Cylindrical plexiglass reactor with an effective volume of 1.6 L, total height of 220 cm, and inner diameter of 60 mm.</p><p>Influent Characteristics: Synthetic simulated municipal wastewater was used, composed mainly of sodium acetate (contributing to a COD of 1500 mg/L), ammonium chloride (50 mg/L), and necessary trace element solutions.</p><p>Operational Parameters: The reactor was operated in a constant-temperature room at 30 ± 1°C. Each operational cycle lasted 4 hours, specifically consisting of: 5 minutes of feeding, 215 minutes of aeration, 15 minutes of settling, and 5 minutes of decanting.</p><p>Steady-State Sampling: After continuous operation for 30 days, when pollutant removal performance met the required standards, steady-state sampling was conducted.</p>	<p>This method aims to maximally extract intracellular metabolites of various classes (e.g., organic acids, amino acids, sugars, lipids) and is suitable for discovering differential metabolites.</p><p>1. Materials and Reagents</p><p>Pre-chilled Extraction Solvent: Methanol:water =&nbsp;4:1 (v/v)&nbsp;or Methanol:acetonitrile:water =&nbsp;2:2:1 (v/v/v), pre-chilled to&nbsp;-20°C.</p><p>Quenching Solvent:&nbsp;60% (v/v)&nbsp;pre-chilled methanol aqueous solution (pre-cooled to -40°C or with liquid nitrogen).</p><p>Phosphate Buffer:&nbsp;0.1 M, pH 7.4, pre-chilled to&nbsp;4°C.</p><p>Equipment: Refrigerated centrifuge, tissue homogenizer (or cryogenic grinding ball mill), vacuum concentrator, vortex mixer.</p><p>2. Extraction Procedure</p><p>Sample Preparation: Take approximately&nbsp;50 mg&nbsp;(wet weight) of granular sludge sample from the&nbsp;-80°C&nbsp;freezer and place it in a pre-chilled&nbsp;2 mL&nbsp;centrifuge tube (containing a grinding bead).&nbsp;Perform all steps on ice.</p><p>Metabolic Quenching: Immediately add&nbsp;1 mL&nbsp;of pre-chilled&nbsp;60% methanol quenching solution&nbsp;and vortex rapidly for&nbsp;10 seconds&nbsp;to quickly terminate all enzymatic activity.</p><p>Cell Disruption:</p><p>Grinding Method (Recommended): Place the sample tube in a pre-chilled tissue homogenizer and grind at&nbsp;60 Hz&nbsp;for&nbsp;2 minutes&nbsp;at low temperature.</p><p>Sonication Method: Place the sample tube in an ice-water bath and sonicate using an ultrasonic cell disruptor at&nbsp;200 W&nbsp;power for&nbsp;3 seconds&nbsp;with&nbsp;5-second intervals, for a total of&nbsp;10 cycles.</p><p>Metabolite Extraction: Add&nbsp;1 mL&nbsp;of pre-chilled&nbsp;extraction solvent&nbsp;(e.g., methanol:acetonitrile:water = 2:2:1) to the disrupted sample and vortex vigorously for&nbsp;30 seconds.</p><p>Low-Temperature Incubation: Allow the mixture to stand at&nbsp;-20°C&nbsp;for&nbsp;1 hour, vortexing every&nbsp;15 minutes&nbsp;during this period.</p><p>Centrifugation: Centrifuge at&nbsp;14,000 ×g&nbsp;for&nbsp;15 minutes&nbsp;at&nbsp;4°C.</p><p>Supernatant Transfer: Carefully transfer the supernatant (approximately&nbsp;1.8 mL) to a new&nbsp;2 mL&nbsp;centrifuge tube.&nbsp;Avoid disturbing the pellet.</p><p>Repeat Extraction (Optional): Add another&nbsp;0.5 mL&nbsp;of extraction solvent to the pellet and repeat steps 4-6. Combine the supernatants from both extractions to improve yield.</p><p>Concentration and Reconstitution: Dry the combined supernatant using a vacuum concentrator at&nbsp;room temperature or 4°C. After complete drying, reconstitute the sample in&nbsp;100 µL&nbsp;of an appropriate reconstitution solution (e.g.,&nbsp;acetonitrile:water = 1:1&nbsp;for LC-MS) based on subsequent instrumental analysis requirements, and vortex vigorously for&nbsp;30 seconds.</p><p>Filtration: Pass the reconstituted solution through a&nbsp;0.22 µm&nbsp;nylon or hydrophilic PTFE syringe filter into a sample vial (or insert) for LC-MS/MS or GC-MS analysis.</p><p>Quality Control (QC) Sample: Pool equal aliquots from each experimental group to prepare a&nbsp;QC sample. Inject this QC sample periodically throughout the analytical sequence to monitor system stability.</p>	<p>Chromatographic Conditions:</p><p>Column:&nbsp;ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 Column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 µm).</p><p>Column Temperature:&nbsp;40°C.</p><p>Mobile Phase:</p><p>Phase A:&nbsp;Water + 0.1% Formic Acid.</p><p>Phase B:&nbsp;Acetonitrile + 0.1% Formic Acid.</p><p>Flow Rate:&nbsp;0.3 mL/min.</p><p>Injection Volume:&nbsp;2 µL.</p><p>Autosampler Temperature:&nbsp;4°C.</p><p>Gradient Elution Program:</p><p>0–2 min:&nbsp;2% B.</p><p>2–15 min:&nbsp;B increased linearly from 2% to 95%.</p><p>15–18 min:&nbsp;95% B.</p><p>18–18.1 min:&nbsp;B decreased from 95% to 2%.</p><p>18.1–20 min:&nbsp;2% B (for system equilibration).</p>	<p>Ion Source:&nbsp;Heated Electrospray Ionization (HESI) Source.</p><p>Ionization Mode:&nbsp;Positive ion mode (ESI+) and negative ion mode (ESI-) (analyzed in separate injections).</p><p>Mass Scan Range:&nbsp;m/z 70–1050.</p><p>Resolution Settings:&nbsp;Full scan resolution: 120,000; MS/MS scan resolution: 30,000.</p><p>Key Ion Source Parameters:</p><p>Spray Voltage:&nbsp;+3.5 kV for ESI+; -3.0 kV for ESI-.</p><p>Capillary Temperature:&nbsp;320°C.</p><p>Auxiliary Gas Heater Temperature:&nbsp;350°C.</p><p>Sheath Gas Pressure:&nbsp;40 (arbitrary units).</p><p>Auxiliary Gas Pressure:&nbsp;10 (arbitrary units).</p><p>Data Acquisition Mode:&nbsp;Data-Dependent Acquisition (DDA) Mode.</p><p>Full MS Scan:&nbsp;Automatic Gain Control (AGC) target: 3e6; Maximum injection time: 100 ms.</p><p>MS/MS Scan:&nbsp;AGC target: 1e5; Maximum injection time: 50 ms.</p><p>Collision Energy:&nbsp;Stepped normalized collision energy (NCE) set to 20, 40, 60 eV.</p><p>Dynamic Exclusion Time:&nbsp;10.0 seconds.</p>	<p><br></p><p>pdp &lt;- PeakDensityParam(</p><p>sampleGroups = sample_groups,</p><p>bw = 30,</p><p>minFraction = 0.5,</p><p>minSamples = 1,</p><p>binSize = 0.025</p><p>)</p><p>Missing Value Imputation&nbsp;using&nbsp;fillChromPeaks&nbsp;with&nbsp;FillChromPeaksParam()&nbsp;to fill gaps in peak detection</p><p>Export Feature Table: Generated a matrix with features as rows (m/z_RT pairs) and samples as columns</p><p>2. Data Cleanup and Normalization</p><p>2.1 Noise Filtering and Background Subtraction</p><p>Removed features with RSD &gt; 30% in QC samples</p><p>Applied&nbsp;Blank Subtraction: Features with average intensity in blank samples &gt; 20% of average intensity in biological samples were removed</p><p>Filtered features present in &lt; 50% of samples per group</p><p>2.2 Normalization Methods Applied Sequentially:</p><p>Internal Standard Normalization: Using 12C/13C-labeled internal standards spiked in each sample</p><p>Probabilistic Quotient Normalization (PQN)&nbsp;to correct for dilution effects</p><p>Log Transformation&nbsp;(base 2) to reduce heteroscedasticity</p><p>Pareto Scaling&nbsp;for multivariate analysis</p><p>3. Metabolite Identification Pipeline</p><p>3.1 Level 1 Identification (Confirmed Standards)</p><p>Database:&nbsp;In-house MS/MS spectral library&nbsp;of 500+ authentic standards</p><p>Matching Criteria:</p><p>Mass accuracy &lt; 10 ppm</p><p>MS/MS spectral similarity &gt; 0.7 (dot product)</p><p>Retention time difference &lt; 0.1 min</p><p>3.2 Level 2 Identification (Putative Annotation)</p><p>Databases:</p><p>HMDB&nbsp;(Human Metabolome Database, version 4.0)</p><p>MassBank&nbsp;(North America and Europe repositories)</p><p>mzCloud&nbsp;(with minimum match factor of 70)</p><p>Annotation Tools:</p><p>SIRIUS&nbsp;(version 5.0) for molecular formula prediction</p><p>CSI:FingerID&nbsp;for compound structure prediction</p><p>3.3 Level 3 Identification (Compound Class)</p><p>Based on: MS/MS fragmentation patterns and characteristic neutral losses</p><p>Reference:&nbsp;LipidBlast&nbsp;for lipid identification</p><p>Classification: Using&nbsp;ClassyFire&nbsp;for chemical ontology</p><p>4. Statistical Analysis and Bioinformatics</p><p>4.1 Univariate Analysis</p><p>Software:&nbsp;MetaboAnalystR&nbsp;(version 3.0)</p><p>Tests:</p><p>Student's t-test or Mann-Whitney U test (two groups)</p><p>ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc (multiple groups)</p><p>False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction&nbsp;using Benjamini-Hochberg method</p><p>4.2 Multivariate Analysis</p><p>Principal Component Analysis (PCA): Using&nbsp;ropls&nbsp;package</p><p>Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA): With 7-fold cross-validation</p><p>Orthogonal PLS-DA (OPLS-DA): For improved interpretation</p><p>Model Validation: Permutation testing (n = 1000)</p><p>4.3 Pathway Analysis</p><p>Databases:</p><p>KEGG&nbsp;(Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes)</p><p>MetaboAnalyst&nbsp;pathway modules</p><p>Tools:</p><p>Mummichog&nbsp;(version 2.0) for pathway enrichment</p><p>Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)&nbsp;adapted for metabolites</p><p>5. Visualization and Reporting</p><p>Heatmaps:&nbsp;ComplexHeatmap&nbsp;package with hierarchical clustering</p><p>Network Analysis:&nbsp;Cytoscape&nbsp;(version 3.9) for metabolic networks</p><p>Interactive Tools:&nbsp;ShinyMetabo&nbsp;for web-based data exploration</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS/MS data were converted from proprietary .raw format to open mzML format using ProteoWizard MSConvert. Metabolic features were then detected, aligned across samples, and quantified using the XCMS package in R, applying the CentWave algorithm for peak picking and the Obiwarp method for retention time correction. A feature intensity matrix was generated and subjected to rigorous filtering, normalization (using internal standards and probabilistic quotient normalization), and scaling. For metabolite annotation, a multi-tiered strategy was employed: Level 1 identifications were made by matching against an in-house library of authentic standards using strict criteria for mass accuracy (&lt;5 ppm), retention time (&lt;0.1 min), and MS/MS spectral similarity. Level 2 putative annotations were obtained by querying public spectral databases (MassBank, GNPS, HMDB) and applying in-silico prediction tools (SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID). Level 3 classifications were assigned based on characteristic MS/MS fragmentation patterns and neutral losses. Statistical analysis and pathway mapping were subsequently performed using MetaboAnalyst and KEGG.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p><p><br></p>

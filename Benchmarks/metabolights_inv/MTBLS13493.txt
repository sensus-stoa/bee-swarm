Study Title	Metabolomic profiling of cow-based bioformulations used in sustainable agriculture: Jeevamrit and Ghanjeevamrit
Study Description	<p><em>Jeevamrit</em> (JV) and <em>Ghanjeevamrit</em> (GH) are traditional cow-based bioformulations used in natural farming practice, and this study provides a comprehensive characterization of metabolite profiles via GC-MS, and LC-MS analysis. Untargeted metabolomics revealed ~222 (GC-MS) and ~1049 (LC-MS) metabolites in <em>Jeevamrit</em> and ~96 (GC-MS) and ~1208 (LC-MS) metabolites in <em>Ghanjeevamrit</em>. Major metabolite classes included organoheterocyclic compounds, organic acids, lipids, benzenoids, and organic oxygen/nitrogen compounds. Key metabolites detected across formulations included benzoic acid, crotonic acid, L-alanine, L-glycine, dioonflavone, phosphoglycolic acid, glutathione, phenyllactic acid, 1,3-oxathiane, rutin, and benzothiazole, all associated with nutrient solubilization, microbial activity, regulating plant growth, and stress tolerance. Overall, the study demonstrates that <em>Jeevamrit</em> and <em>Ghanjeevamrit</em> are metabolically rich bioformulations, reinforcing their roles in enhancing soil health and plant growth.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Two sample groups for Jeevamrit (JV) and Ghanjeevamrit (GH) were collected for the study: Experimental preparation (EP) and Farmer preparation (FP). JV-1 to JV-6 represent EP (Experimental Preparation) samples, while JV-7 to JV-9 represent FP (Farmer Preparation) samples. GH-01 to GH-06 represent EP (Experimental Preparation) samples, while GH-07 to GH-11 represent FP (Farmer Preparation) samples. All samples were transported on ice from the collection site and stored at -20 °C before processing in triplicate.</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>The 200 μL of each sample was mixed with 1000 μL of solvent (ethyl acetate and methanol (1:1)), vortexed for 3 min, and sonicated for 15 min. After centrifugation (3000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), supernatants were dried in a Speedvac vacuum concentrator. Samples were derivatized using BSTFA (N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide) with 1% TMCS (Trimethylchlorosilane) at 70 °C for 1 h.</p><p><br></p><p>LC-MS</p><p>For each sample, 200 µL was mixed with 1000 µL of a multi-solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water; 2:2:1), sonicated (20 min), centrifuged (12,000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 µm syringe filter. A diluted aliquot (450 µL acetonitrile + 50 µL filtered sample) was used for LC-MS.</p>	<p>GC-MS Instrument: Clarus 680 GC</p><p>LC-MS Instrument: Agilent 1290 Infinity II</p><p><br></p><p>Column GC: Elite-5MS</p><p>Column LC: Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (3.0 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm)</p>	<p>GC-MS</p><p>GC-MS was performed on a PerkinElmer Clarus 680 GC coupled with a Clarus SQ8C MS using an Elite-5 ms column. The oven temperature program set as 60 °C (2 min) followed by 300 °C (10 °C/min), and 300 °C for 6 min. A 1 µL sample was injected (1:50 split ratio) at 250 °C. MS scanned from 50-600 m/z between 5-32 min.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>LC-MS</p><p>LC-MS performed on a Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (3.0 x 100 mm, 1.8 µm) with a 0.3 mL/min flow rate. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid (FA) in water and Mobile phase B was 0.1% FA in acetonitrile. UHPLC run was carried out in gradient mode: 2% B (0-1 min), 30% B (2-10 min), 60% B (11 min), 95% B (13-25 min), followed by 1 min post run wash. Flush port was kept for 15 s and needle was washed for 15 min before each injection.&nbsp;The oven temperature was kept at 40 °C and injection volume was 5 µL. The MS parameters: positive ion mode, 50-1200 m/z; gas temperature: 250 °C; drying gas: 10 L/min; sheath gas: 350 °C, 10 L/min; nebulizer: 35 psi; capillary voltage: 3000 V; nozzle voltage: 1000 V</p>	<p>msconvert Proteowizard was used to convert raw files to .mzML files</p>	<p>Annotation of GC-MS metabolites was done using MS-DIAL with NIST2014 library</p><p>Annotation of LC-MS metabolites was done using XCMS online using inbuilt METLIN library</p>

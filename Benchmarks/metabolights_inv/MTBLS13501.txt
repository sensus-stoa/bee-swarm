Study Title	Traditional Chinese medicine Dachaihu decoction alleviates abnormal behavior through regulating gut microbiota and metabolites in children with ASD
Study Description	<p>Traditional Chinese Medicine Dachaihu decoction (DCHD) has been reported to hold great promise to alleviate autism spectrum disorders (ASD), a generalized neurodevelopmental disease associated with gut microbiome dysbiosis. Health-promoting effects of DCHD on ASD children could be attributed to gut microbiota reconstruction. However, its underlying mechanism of action remains elusive, resulting in rare therapies have proposed for TCM-targeted modulation of gut microbiota for the treatment of ASD. This study aimed to investigate the mechanisms by which DCHD alleviates ASD severity and social disorder, with particular emphasis on gut microbiota composition and metabolism.</p>

Study Protocol Description	Serum and fecal samples collected from children with ASD at baseline and after 6 months of DCHD treatment	Pipette 100 μL of the liquid sample into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, add 400 μL of extraction solution (acetonitrile:methanol=1:1) containing four internal standards (such as L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL), etc.). Vortex mix for 30 seconds, then perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 minutes (5°C, 40 KHz). Let the sample stand at -20°C for 30 minutes. Centrifuge at 4°C, 13000 × g for 15 minutes, transfer the supernatant, and dry it under a stream of nitrogen. Reconstitute with 100 μL of reconstitution solution (acetonitrile:water=1:1), and perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 5 minutes (5°C, 40 KHz). Centrifuge at 4°C, 13000 × g for 10 minutes, and transfer the supernatant into an injection vial with an insert	3 μL of the sample was separated using an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) and then introduced into the mass spectrometer for detection. Mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (95/5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B was acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5/47.5/5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was set at 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C	The mass spectrometry signal acquisition for the samples was performed using both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scanning range of m/z 70-1050. The ion spray voltage was set at 3500 V for positive ions and -3000 V for negative ions. The sheath gas flow was 50 arb, the auxiliary heater gas flow was 13 arb, and the ion source heating temperature was 450°C. Stepped collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 V were applied	The LC-MS raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, and retention time correction. After peak alignment, a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity was generated	The MS and MS/MS spectral data were matched against public metabolome databases HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as the self-built database from Majorbio, to obtain metabolite identification information

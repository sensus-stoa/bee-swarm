Study Title	Validation: Multi-Omics and Machine Learning-Based Profiling of Severity Signatures in Mycoplasma Pneumoniae Infection in Children
Study Description	<p>Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is a common respiratory infection in children, yet the mechanisms driving its progression to severe disease remain poorly understood. This study employs a comprehensive proteomic and metabolomic approach to elucidate severity-related pathways and identify potential biomarkers for improved diagnosis and targeted therapy. By analyzing blood proteomes from 57 pediatric patients with varying MPP severities alongside 10 healthy controls, and integrating multi-omics data from bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). This study is a validation cohort by analyzing another 50 BALF proteome.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Bronchoalveolar&nbsp;lavage&nbsp;fluid&nbsp;(BALF)&nbsp;volumes&nbsp;collected&nbsp;were&nbsp;≥8&nbsp;mL&nbsp;and&nbsp;the&nbsp;sample&nbsp;was&nbsp;centrifuged&nbsp;at&nbsp;10&nbsp;min&nbsp;for&nbsp;12,000 x g&nbsp;to&nbsp;collect&nbsp;the&nbsp;supernatant.&nbsp;</p><p>Carefully&nbsp;remove&nbsp;and&nbsp;transfer&nbsp;the&nbsp;supernatant&nbsp;to&nbsp;a&nbsp;new&nbsp;centrifuge&nbsp;tube&nbsp;for&nbsp;subsequent&nbsp;metabolomics&nbsp;analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen liquid samples can be placed on ice to slowly defrost before extraction. Add 400 μL of MeOH/ACN (1:1, v/v) solvent mixture to 100 μL serum samples (keep 2:2:1 ratio, No H2O added). After that, samples were vortexed for 30 s and followed by sonication 10 min on ice using a high intensity ultrasonic processor. Then, samples were incubated at -20 °C for 1 h. The remaining debris was removed by centrifugation at 12,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min. The supernatant was collected and evaporated to dryness at room temperature by using a vacuum concentrator. Add 100 μL of ACN/H2O (1:1, v/v) solvent mixture to the dried samples. The samples were vortexed for 30 s and followed by sonication 10 min on ice using a high intensity ultrasonic processor. The remaining debris was removed by centrifugation at 12,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min. Finally, keep supernatant in -80 °C prior to LC/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The tryptic peptides were dissolved in solvent A, directly loaded onto a BEH&nbsp;C18&nbsp;Column (1.7&nbsp;µm,&nbsp;2.1&nbsp;mm x 100&nbsp;mm). The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (0.1% formic acid, 2% acetonitrile/in water) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile). 10 μl Peptides were separated with following gradient: 0.0-11.0 min, 2-98% B; 11.0-12.0 min, 98% B; 12.0-12.1 min, 98-2% B, 12.1-15.0 min, 2% B, and all at a constant flow rate of 400 uL/min on a Waters&nbsp;ACQUITY&nbsp;UPLC system. </p>	<p>The samples were subjected to capillary source followed by the timsTOF Pro mass spectrometry. The electrospray voltage applied was 4.5 kV. Precursors and fragments were analyzed at the TOF detector, with a MS/MS scan range between 50-1300 m/z. The timsTOF Pro was operated in parallel accumulation serial fragmentation (PASEF) mode. Precursors with charge states 0-1 were selected for fragmentation, and 2PASEF-MS/MS scans were acquired per cycle. The dynamic exclusion was set to 6 s. </p>	<p>The peak detection, alignment and retention time correction of the resulting data were performed by MetaboScape 2022. Mass error was set to 20 ppm for precursor and fragment ions. The structure and annotation information of metabolites were obtained by searching NIST, HMDB, self-built database and integrating public database.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data were search against metabolite database to obtain the quantative values of metabolites. After data filtration, metabolite abundances were ajusted by batch based on QC samples. </p>

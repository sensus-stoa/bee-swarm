Study Title	Dietary High Fiber and N-Carbamylglutamate Enhance Sow Reproductive Performance via Modulating Lactobacilli, Lipid Metabolites, and the PI3K-Akt Signaling Pathway
Study Description	<p>Background Both high fiber diet and N-Carbamylglutamate (NCG) individually contributes to improve reproductive efficiency in sow. However, the potential synergistic effects and the underlying mechanisms are not well-understood. Here, Landrace x Yorkshire sows were selected to investigate the synergistic effects of high fiber diet and 0.05% NCG on reproductive performance using metagenomic, metabolomic, and transcriptomic analysis.</p><p>Results The study revealed that high fiber with NCG (H+N) significantly increased litter weight at weaning and the apparent nutrient digestibility. Metagenomic analysis revealed H+N increased the abundance of lactobacilli across multiple taxonomic levels (from order to species level, including Lactobacillus sp. 910589175), which was positively correlated with elevated levels of the lipid metabolites L-α-glycerylphosphorylcholine and taurocholic acid. Subsequent transcriptomic profiling indicated activation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, which was associated with improved litter weight at weaning and the apparent nutrient digestibility.</p><p>Conclusion The synergistic effect of high-fiber diet and NCG supplementation enhances reproductive efficiency in sows by modulating gut microbiota (specifically lactobacilli), increasing beneficial lipid metabolites, and activating the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, thereby improving nutrient digestibility and litter weight.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p> A total of 400 healthy sows (Landrace x Yorkshire hybrid) with identical parity (second or third) were selected from Hunan Longhua Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Technology Co., Ltd. The sows were randomly assigned to four dietary treatment groups (n = 100 per group) as follows: low-fiber group (LF; 3.73% crude fiber); low-fiber + NCG group (L+N; 3.73% crude fiber + 0.05% NCG); high-fiber group (HF; 7.46% crude fiber); high-fiber + NCG group (H+N; 7.46% crude fiber + 0.05% NCG). The experimental was initiated on the day of breeding and continued throughout the entire gestation period of 114 days. At the end of the feeding experiment (day 114), approximately 10 mL of blood was collected from each sow via the tail vein and centrifuged at 3000 ×g for 10 min to separate plasm. The plasm was carefully transferred into 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes and stored at -20°C until analysis.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>1. Pipette 50 μL of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>2. Add 200 μL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) and vortex for 30 s;</p><p>3. Freeze in a -20℃ refrigerator for 30 min;</p><p>4. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, take 200 μL of the supernatant and concentrate to dryness</p><p>under vacuum;</p><p>5. Add 100 μL 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine) to dissolve and vortex for 30</p><p>seconds;</p><p>6. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22 μm filter membrane,</p><p>and add the filtrate to the test bottle;</p><p>7. Take 10-20 μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample for evaluating instrument stability</p><p>and data reliability.</p>	<p>An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used with a flow rate of</p><p>0.4 mL/min, a column temperature of 40°C, an autosampler temperature of 8°C, and an injection volume of</p><p>2 μL.</p>	<p>Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in</p><p>positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source,</p><p>spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325℃, auxiliary</p><p>gas temperature 300℃, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT</p><p>100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s,</p><p>secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto.</p><p>All formal samples and QC sample were loaded into the equipment according to the above-mentioned</p><p>chromatography and mass spectrometry methods. Before the formal injection, QC sample were injected 2-4</p><p>times to balance the system. During the injection process, one QC sample was injected for every 5-10</p><p>samples for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p>The .raw format data was imported into the commercial software Compound Discoverer™ 3.3 (version</p><p>3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithm of</p><p>the software, peak extraction, alignment, correction and other operations were performed. The unique peak</p><p>quality rating calculation and filter greatly reduced the interference of background peaks and low-quality</p><p>peaks. The peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the missing values of the undetected peaks were filled based on the software Fill Gaps algorithm, and the Sum total peak</p><p>area was normalized.</p>	<p>The identification of metabolites was based on self-built library PSNGM Database,</p><p>mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB</p><p>(https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/) and NIST_2020_MSMS spectral library. The</p><p>MS1 mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>

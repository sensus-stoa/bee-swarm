Study Title	Intraspecific competition drives orthogonal variation in root exudate metabolic and functional traits in a dominant plant species’ seedlings
Study Description	<p>1. Classical plant functional traits, often limited by intercorrelation and incomplete capture of dynamic variation, are insufficient for fully predicting ecological responses to environmental change. Quantifying independent functional dimensions, particularly those reflecting molecular and biochemical processes, is therefore essential for a comprehensive understanding of plant ecology.</p><p>2. We adapted the well-established community-weighted mean (CWM) concept from ecology to metabolomic data, developing a framework to quantify metabolic functional traits based on the intrinsic chemical properties of metabolites. We applied this framework to root exudate metabolomics of Haloxylon ammodendron seedlings undergoing intraspecific competition.</p><p>3. Our results show that intraspecific competition significantly altered the root exudate metabolome composition. We characterized the chemical space of identified metabolites using molecular descriptors, revealing three core dimensions of variation: molecular size/hydrogen bonding, acidity/structural complexity, and hydrophobicity/saturation. Plant metabolic functional traits, quantified as CWMs of these chemical properties, varied significantly with competitive interaction, exhibiting distinct adaptive patterns across treatments, ranging from investment in defense/allelopathy to nutrient mobilization or aggressive nutrient acquisition. Crucially, correlation analyses showed no significant relationship between metabolic CWMs and classical traits, and integrated PCA further revealed their orthogonal separation in multivariate space.</p><p>4. Synthesis: These findings demonstrate that metabolic CWMs represent a novel, independent functional dimension. This highlights the power and potential of integrating molecular-level functional traits to enhance our understanding of plant ecological strategies and phenotypic plasticity, particularly in capturing intraspecific variation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p> Intraspecific competition among Haloxylon ammodendron seedlings was investigated using five potted treatments (Fig. 1): single-plant (D-1), two-plant (D-2), and three-plant (D-3) for density variation. Root segregation treatments with two plants used a 30-μm nylon mesh (N) for chemical interaction or a plastic film (P) for complete root isolation. Seedlings from Ebinur Lake Basin (44°30′–45°09′ N, 82°36′–83°50′ E) were collected in May 2022. Detailed collection, handling, and initial root standardization procedures are in Supplementary Methods S1. As a typical desert plant with a relatively slow growth rate, H. ammodendron seedlings were transplanted into large pots (29 cm diameter × 43 cm height) filled with a 2:1:1 (v/v/v) mixture of peat soil, sieved (2 mm mesh) in-situ topsoil (0–10 cm), and perlite to minimize root restriction during the experiment. The 5-month experiment (May-October) had four replicates per treatment (n=4). Root exudates were passively collected from intact plants by rinsing roots and submerging them in 500 mL deionized water for 24h in darkness. Solutions were filtered (0.22 µm), concentrated (rotary vacuum evaporation to ~15 mL), and stored at -80°C for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Extraction/Preparation Methods:</p><p>The root exudate samples were subjected to a detailed extraction protocol prior to LC-MS/MS analysis. First, 100 μL of each concentrated exudate sample was aliquotted into a clean tube. Then, 500 μL of pre-chilled extraction solution (methanol:acetonitrile, 1:1 v/v, containing 20 mg/L 2-Chloro-L-phenylalanine as internal standard) was added. The mixture was thoroughly vortexed for 30 seconds, followed by 10 minutes of sonication in an ice-water bath. Samples were then incubated at -20°C for 1 hour. A first centrifugation was performed at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C. 500 μL of the supernatant was carefully transferred to a new EP tube and dried using a vacuum concentrator. The dried extract was reconstituted with 160 μL of acetonitrile:water (1:1 v/v) solution, vortexed for 30 seconds, and sonicated for 10 minutes in an ice-water bath. Finally, samples were centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C, and 120 μL of the final supernatant was transferred to a 2 mL autosampler vial for analysis.</p><p>Control

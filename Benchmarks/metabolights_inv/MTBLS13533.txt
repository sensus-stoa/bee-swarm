Study Title	Functional Remodeling of iNKT Cells by Sulfatide-Reactive Type II NKT Cells Reprograms Alveolar Macrophages to Alleviate Lung Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury
Study Description	<p>Lung ischemia-reperfusion injury (LIRI) is a serious complication in critical clinical situations. Despite its importance, the specific role of type II natural killer T (NKT) cells in LIRI remains unclear. In this study, we establish a LIRI mouse model and demonstrate that sulfatide-reactive type II NKT cells promote M2 polarization of alveolar macrophages (AMs) and alleviate LIRI through AMs-mediated mechanisms. This protective effect is absent in&nbsp;Jα18-/-&nbsp;mice, indicating the essential role of invariant NKT (iNKT) cells. Further analysis shows that interleukin-10 (IL-10) secreted by iNKT cells upregulates AT-rich interaction domain 3A (Arid3a) in macrophages, which then promotes the transcription of DNA damage inducible transcript 4 (DDIT4). This cascade enhances M2 polarization of macrophages, potentially contributing to lung protection and alleviating LIRI. These findings suggest that NKT cells may offer a therapeutic target for LIRI in the future.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>First, dissolve an appropriate amount of sulfatide and 5-TAMRA in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), ensuring both are completely dissolved. Next, add 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) as a coupling agent, along with N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) to enhance reaction efficiency.</p>	<p> The reaction is carried out at room temperature, with the pH adjusted to 7.0–7.5 using phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to maintain a stable reaction environment. After 6 hours, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) is employed to remove unreacted reagents and by-products, ultimately yielding the purified sulfatide-5-TAMRA complex.</p>	<p>Pump A :0.1%Triϐluoroacetic in 100% water</p><p>Pump B :0.1%Triϐluoroacetic in 100% acetonitrile&nbsp;</p><p>Total Flow :1ml/min&nbsp;</p><p>Wavelength :220nm&nbsp;</p><p>Analytical column type :SHIMADZU Inertsil ODS-SP(4.6*250mm*5um)</p><p>Dissolution method :100%H2O&nbsp;</p><p>Inj. Volume:15 uL&nbsp;</p>	<p>Dissolve the sample in pure water and filter it into an injection vial. Place the injection vial in the sample tray (e.g., position 3 in tray 1), input the corresponding sample position into the mass spectrometer, set the instrument to positive ion mode 'Positive,' and start the run (injection volume: 2 µl) for detection.</p>	<p>Raw data obtained from the LC-MS/MS analysis were first converted to the mzXML format using the MSConvert tool within the ProteoWizard software package (v3.0.8789). The converted mzXML files were then processed with XCMS, which was used for feature detection, retention time correction, and alignment. Signal drift and batch effects were corrected using robust LOESS signal correction for quality control (QC-RLSC). Only ion peaks with a relative standard deviation (RSD) below 30% in the quality control samples were retained to ensure reliable metabolite identification.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based on their accurate mass (within a tolerance of &lt;30 ppm) and MS/MS data, matched against comprehensive databases including HMDB, MassBank, LipidMaps, mzCloud, and KEGG. The processed data were normalized and scaled for downstream statistical and multivariate analyses.</p>

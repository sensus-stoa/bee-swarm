Study Title	Untargeted Plasma Lipidomics Identifies Oxidative Stress and Inflammation Signatures Associated with Recurrent Leg Ulcer Status in Sickle Cell Disease
Study Description	<p>Leg ulcers are among the most burdensome complications of sickle cell disease (SCD), yet the molecular determinants underlying ulcer susceptibility and recurrence remain incompletely characterized. We performed untargeted plasma lipidomics by LC–MS in 129 individuals with SCD from the REDS-III Brazil cohort, comparing participants with a recurrent sickle cell leg ulcer (SCLU) phenotype (n = 72) and SCD controls without ulcer history or follow-up episodes (n = 57). Data were split into a training set (80%; SCLUs n = 58, controls n = 46) and an independent validation set (20%; SCLUs n = 14, controls n = 11), with leakage-resistant preprocessing and variable selection restricted to the training set. After quality filtering, 2,419 features were retained. Univariate screening in the training set yielded 181 candidates, and 44 annotated metabolites were retained for visualization and modelling. Recurrent SCLUs showed higher lysophospholipids and oxylipins and lower sphingolipids and FAHFAs, consistent with inflammation, oxidative stress and membrane dysregulation. Random Forest achieved the best validation discrimination (AUC = 0.85; accuracy = 0.72; sensitivity = 0.86; specificity = 0.55). Untargeted plasma lipidomics defines an interpretable lipid signature linked to recurrent SCLUs and nominates candidates for targeted quantification and external validation to support individualized risk stratification.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Peripheral blood was collected in EDTA tubes and processed to separate cellular and plasma components, following standardized biobanking protocols. Samples were subsequently aliquoted and stored at the Biobank of the University of São Paulo.</p>	<p>Briefly, 200 μL aliquots of plasma were mixed with 800 μL of cold methanol:chloroform:methyl tert-butyl ether (1.33:1:1), vortexed for 30 s, and centrifuged (12,000 × g, 10 min, 4 °C). The apolar fraction (450 μL) was collected, concentrated, and resuspended in 200 μL of water:acetonitrile:isopropanol (1:1:2) prior to analysis. Quality control (QC) pools were prepared by combining aliquots from all study samples. QC injections were run every 10 analyses to monitor extraction reproducibility and instrument stability. Sample order was randomized to minimize technical bias.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The samples were separated by an ACQUITY UPLC and eluted through CSH C18 column (1.7 µm × 100 mm × 2.1 mm) column (Waters Ltd., Elstree, UK), and analysis was acquired in negative mode.The column oven was maintained at 40 °C throughout the chromatographic run.</p><p>For the apolar compound analysis was employed using mobile phase A, with ACN/H2O solution (60:40, v/v) and 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B, comprising isopropanol/ACN (90:10, v/v) with 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.4 mL min−1. The flow rate was 0.400 mL/min. The method started with 10% of solvent A, followed by a 18-minute ramp up to 70% of A, which was then held for 1,5 minutes. Shortly after, the system returned to the initial value of 10% of A, maintaining this condition for an additional 3,5 minutes for re-equilibration. The total run time was 22 minutes, with an injection volume of 2 μL.</p>	<p>Data was obtained in MSE mode employing 6 V for low collision energy and a ramp of 20–50 V for high collision energy, with the&nbsp;range set from 50 to 1200 Da and 0.5 s for scan duration. Leucine enkephalin (molecular weight = 555.62; 200 pg L−1 in 1:1 ACN:H2O, v/v) was used as the lock mass, and calibration was performed using 0.5 mM sodium formate solution. Additional settings included: source temperature of 140 °C, desolvation temperature of 550 °C, desolvation gas flow rate of 900 L h−1, capillary voltage of 2.5 kV, and cone voltage of 40 V.</p><p>A quality control (QC) sample was created by pooling from each study sample to monitor extraction and LC-MS system stability (injected after every 10 study samples). The order of sample extraction and analysis was randomized to mitigate potential instrument or biological bias.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI™ v3.0 (Nonlinear Dynamics) to extract features based on retention time, m/z, and peak area. Ion abundance data were corrected using the QC-based random forest signal correction (QC-RFSC) method25. The relative standard deviation (%RSD) was then calculated for the QC samples.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was achieved through MSE analysis, by evaluating fragmentation score, mass error, mass accuracy, and isotope similarity corresponding to level 2 (putatively annotated compounds), was enhanced using SDF2PQI, an in-house tool for matching Progenesis QI features with external SDF libraries (https://github.com/pedrohgodoys/sdf_to_pqi, accessed 10 February 2025). Spectral libraries included LipidMaps, the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), and MassBank of North America (MoNA).</p>

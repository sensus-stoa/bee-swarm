Study Title	Variations in carbon flux allocation among cassava (Manihot esculenta) cultivars arise from balanced competition between starch accumulation and structural component development
Study Description	<p>Cassava (Manihot esculenta Crantz) storage roots exhibit significant variation in starch content among cultivars, yet the metabolic and molecular mechanisms governing carbon allocation between storage and structural components remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated carbon partitioning in two cassava cultivars with distinct starch phenotypes: high-starch FX01 and low-starch SC16. Using 13C isotope labeling coupled with metabolomic analysis, we traced the pathway of carbohydrates through primary and secondary metabolism. The results revealed that SC16 exhibits enhanced photosynthetic capacity and elevated soluble sugar content in storage roots, whereas FX01 demonstrates superior starch synthesis due to its efficient glucose and fructose phosphorylation. Conversely, SC16 exhibits a faster conversion of 13C-labeled ferulic acid, directing carbon flow towards lignin biosynthesis via the phenylpropanoid pathway. Further, by silencing the MeCOMT8 gene, encoding a key enzyme in ferulic acid biosynthesis, we observed a reduction in lignin content and an increase in ADP-glucose levels in the MeCOMT8-silenced cassava plants, suggesting a regulatory link between these competing pathways. Our research elucidated that the variations in carbon allocation between starch and lignin biosynthesis among different cultivars are finely orchestrated though the specific-step alteration of metabolic flux. These findings provide potential candidate targeted points and valuable insights for high-starch breeding in cassava.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cassava plants were harvested respectively on the 3rd, 6th, 9th, and 12th days after undergoing a ten-hour labeling process. Three plants were collected for analysis at each sampling point. The roots, stems, and leaves were quickly separated and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen to arrest metabolic activity. Leaf samples were taken from the 5th and 6th fully expanded leaves, while stem segments (including lignified stems when present) were excised from the area between the 9th and 12th leaves. Root samples were categorized into storage root and fibrous roots, with each category being stored separately for subsequent analyses.</p><p><br></p>	<p>One hundred milligrams of fresh tissues (storage roots) were finely ground into powder using a mortar and pestle, subsequently dissolved in 1000 μL of a cold 75:20:5 (v/v/v) methanol: ddH2O: formic acid solution containing 30 μg mL-1 of DDTC, and allowed to soak at 4 for 16 h. The mixture was then vortexed and centrifuged at 10,000 × g at 4 for 15 min. The supernatant was carefully aspirated and subsequently dried using a vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf concentrator 5301, Eppendorf AG 22331, Hamburg, Germany) at a temperature of 30. Four hundred microliters of methanol were added to re-suspend the dried aspirate, which was then filtered through a 0.45 μm filter (Costar, Corning Inc.).</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was achieved using a binary mobile phase system consisting of solvent A (water containing 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (methanol). A linear gradient elution program was employed as follows: 0-2 min, 95% A; 2-13 min, 95-0% A; 13-16 min, 0% A; 16-16.1 min, 0-95% A, and 16.1-19 min, 95% A. The injection volume was set to 2 μL, with a flow rate of 0.3 mL min-1. The column compartment and autosampler tray were maintained at 30°C and 10°C, respectively, to ensure optimal chromatographic performance and sample stability.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer was operated under optimized conditions, with the following key parameters: spray voltage, 3 kV; capillary temperature, 320°C; sheath gas flow rate, 35 arbitrary units; and auxiliary gas flow rate, 10 arbitrary units. Nitrogen was used as both the sheath and auxiliary gas. The auxiliary gas was heated to 350°C to aid in solvent evaporation and improve ionization efficiency. Data acquisition was performed in Full MS and Full MS/data-dependent MS2 (Full MS/DD-MS2) scanning modes, covering a mass range of 20-2000 m/z. The resolution settings for the primary (Full MS) and secondary (MS2) scans were 70,000 and 17,500, respectively, ensuring high mass accuracy and resolving power for confident metabolite identification.</p>	<p>Raw data files were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.2 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for metabolite profiling. Individual metabolite peaks were identified by comparing their parent ion/daughter ion pairs and retention times with those of authentic standards analyzed under identical conditions.</p>	<p>Raw data files were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.2 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for metabolite profiling.

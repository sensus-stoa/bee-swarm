Study Protocol Description	<p>Soil samples were collected from contrasting forest types in a subtropical region of China. At each sampling site, surface soil (0–20 cm) was collected after removal of litter and debris. Multiple subsamples were taken within each plot and homogenized to form a composite sample. Fresh soils were placed into sterile containers, transported to the laboratory on ice, and immediately sieved (2 mm) to remove roots and stones. All samples were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Freeze-dried topsoil samples (100 mg) were weighed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube and extracted with 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v). Samples were homogenized using a cryogenic grinder at −10 °C and 50 Hz for 6 min, followed by low-temperature ultrasonication (5 °C, 40 kHz) for 30 min. The extracts were then incubated at −20 °C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were transferred to LC–MS vials for metabolomics analysis, and equal aliquots from each sample were pooled to prepare quality control (QC) samples. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal aliquots of all extracts and injected periodically during the LC–MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 × 50 mm, 1.7 μm). The mobile phase consisted of water containing ammonium acetate and aqueous ammonia (phase A) and acetonitrile (phase B). The flow rate was set to 0.5 mL / min with a gradient starting at high organic content and gradually increasing the aqueous phase. The column was re-equilibrated prior to the next injection.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted using a Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. Full MS scans were collected at high resolution, followed by data-dependent MS/MS acquisition. Instrument parameters, including spray voltage, capillary temperature, sheath gas, auxiliary gas, and normalized collision energy, were optimized according to the manufacturer’s recommendations.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data files were processed using appropriate metabolomics software for peak detection, retention time alignment, and feature extraction. A feature table containing m/z, retention time, and peak intensities was generated for all samples and used for downstream analyses.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were detected and aligned using appropriate data processing software. Putative metabolite annotation was performed based on accurate mass, retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation patterns by comparison with public databases and in-house spectral libraries. Metabolite identifications were reported at putative annotation levels.</p>

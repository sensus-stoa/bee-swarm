Study Title	TFAM-Associated Mitochondrial Dynamics and Metabolic Reprogramming Regulate Microglial Polarization: Temporal and Causal Perspectives
Study Description	<p>The polarization state of microglia exerts an influence on neuroinflammation and neural tissue repair after injury. Modulating microglial polarization is emerging as a potential therapeutic strategy for various types of neural injuries and neurodegenerative diseases. However, the causal relationship between microglial polarization and mitochondrial dynamics, which include mitochondrial fusion and fission, remains to be fully clarified. Our study demonstrates that mitochondrial fusion promoter M1 promotes mitochondrial fusion in mouse microglial cells, leading to reduced glycolysis and increased fatty acid oxidation, and this metabolic reprogramming impacts microglial polarization. Additionally, in both cellular and animal experiments, it was observed that knocking down mitochondrial transcription factor A (TFAM) results in increased mitochondrial fission, decreased fatty acid β-oxidation, enhanced glycolysis, and promotes the polarization of microglia towards the pro - inflammatory M1 phenotype. In conclusion, our study has, for the first time, provided evidence that TFAM may play a role in the regulation of mitochondrial dynamics. Furthermore, we provide a detailed elucidation of the chronological sequence and underlying causal relationships among mitochondrial dynamics, mitochondrial metabolic reprogramming, and microglial polarization. These findings offer novel targets and strategies for the treatment of various neural injuries and neurodegenerative diseases.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The study employed mouse-derived BV2 microglial cells. Two groups were established: BV2 (control) and BV2+M1 (M1-polarised). Each group contained three independent biological replicates (n = 3). After treatment, cells were immediately quenched with liquid nitrogen, harvested by scraping, and stored at –80 °C until analysis. No additional time-course sampling was performed.</p>	<p>Cell pellets (~1 × 10000000 cells per replicate) were suspended in 1 mL ice-cold MeOH : ACN : H2O (2 : 2 : 1, v/v/v) containing deuterated internal standards. The suspension underwent three freeze–thaw cycles (1 min liquid-nitrogen freezing followed by room-temperature thawing with 30 s vortexing), 10 min ice-bath sonication, and 1 h protein precipitation at –40 °C. After centrifugation (4 °C, 12 000 rpm, 15 min, 13 800 × g, 8.6 cm rotor radius), the supernatant was transferred to glass LC vials for analysis. A pooled QC sample was prepared by combining equal aliquots of all six cell extracts; no solvent blank or neat standard series were analysed.</p>	<p>UHPLC: Thermo Vanquish system.</p><p>Column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 µm) maintained at 30 °C.</p><p>Mobile phase: A = 25 mmol /L ammonium acetate + 25 mmol /L ammonia hydroxide in water (pH&nbsp;9.75); B = acetonitrile.</p><p>Gradient: started at 95 % B, returned to 95 % B after 10 min; total run time 12 min.</p><p>Flow rate: 0.3 mL/ min.</p><p>Autosampler: 4 °C; injection volume 2 µL.</p>	<p>MS: Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 (Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p><p>Ionisation: heated electrospray (HESI); sheath gas 50 arb, aux gas 15 arb; capillary temperature 320 °C.</p><p>Spray voltage: +3.8 kV (positive mode) / –3.4 kV (negative mode).</p><p>Scan modes: full MS (resolution 60 000) and data-dependent MS/MS (resolution 15 000) with stepped normalised collision energies 20/30/40 %.</p><p>Mass range: m/z 70–1 000 for both MS1 and MS2.</p>	<p>Raw .raw files were converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard. Peak detection, retention-time alignment, and integration were performed with an in-house R package based on XCMS. Missing peaks were imputed with half the minimum detected value, and data were normalised by the median intensity of deuterated internal standards. No batch correction or additional scaling was applied before multivariate modelling.</p>	<p>Features were matched against the commercial BiotreeDB (v3.0) containing RT, MS1 accurate mass, and MS/MS spectra. Confidence levels were assigned according to Metabolomics Standards Initiative criteria:</p><p>Level 1: RT, MS1 and MS2 match to an authentic standard.</p><p>Level 2: MS1 and MS2 match to a public spectral library entry.</p><p>Level 3: MS1 and MS2 match to a putative structure without RT evidence.</p><p>Level 4: unknown.</p><p>Putative annotations were cross-linked to KEGG COMPOUND, HMDB, and PubChem identifiers for downstream pathway analysis.</p>

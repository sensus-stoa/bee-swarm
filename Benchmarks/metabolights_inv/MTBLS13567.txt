Study Protocol Description	<p>Untargeted metabolomics profiling was performed to characterize metabolic alterations in EC cells. Cells were harvested at the designated time point and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, then stored at -80 °C until further processing.</p>	<p>Precooled methanol–water (4:1, v/v) was added to each sample to a total volume of 1 mL and transferred into a glass vial in two portions. Subsequently, 200 μL of chloroform was added and thoroughly dispersed by pipetting. The mixture was subjected to ice-bath ultrasonication for cell disruption at 500 W for 3 min with an on/off cycle of 6 s/4 s. All liquids were then transferred into centrifuge tubes and further extracted by ultrasonication in an ice-water bath for 20 min, followed by incubation at -40 °C overnight. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, 800 μL of the supernatant was collected into LC–MS vials and evaporated to dryness. The dried residues were reconstituted in 300 μL of methanol–water (1:4, v/v), vortexed for 30 s, ultrasonicated in an ice-water bath for 3 min, and incubated at -40 °C for 2 h. The samples were centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and 150 μL of the supernatant was withdrawn using a syringe, filtered through a 0.22 μm organic-phase syringe filter, and transferred into LC vials. Subsequently, 20 μL of mixed internal standards (0.1 mg/mL) was added, and the samples were stored at -80 °C until LC–MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of the extracted solutions from all samples. All extraction solvents were precooled at -20 °C prior to use.</p>	<p>Metabolomic analysis was performed using a liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) system consisting of a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class Plus coupled to a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF high-resolution mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C. The mobile phases consisted of solvent A (water containing 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (acetonitrile). The flow rate was set at 0.35 mL/min, and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>The mass range was from m/z 100 to 1,000. The resolution was set at 70,000 for the full MS scans and 17500 for HCD MS/MS scans. The Collision energy was set at 10, 20 and 40 eV. The mass spectrometer operated as follows: spray voltage, 3800 V (+) and 3200 V (-); sheath gas flow rate, 35 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow rate, 8 arbitrary units; capillary temperature, 320°C; Aux gas heater temperature, 350°C; S-lens RF level, 50.</p>	<p>The original LC-MS data were processed by software Progenesis QI V2.3 (Nonlinear, Dynamics, Newcastle, UK) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integral, retention time correction, peak alignment, and normalization. Main parameters of 5 ppm precursor tolerance, 10 ppm product tolerance, and 5% product ion threshold were applied. Compound identification were based on precise mass-to-charge ratio (M/z), secondary fragments, and isotopic distribution using The Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Lipidmaps (V2.3), Metlin, and self-built databases. The extracted data were then further processed by removing any peaks with a missing value (ion intensity = 0) in more than 50% in groups, by replacing zero value by half of the minimum value, and by screening according to the qualitative results of the compound. Compounds with resulting scores below 36 (out of 60) points were also deemed to be inaccurate and removed. A data matrix was combined from the positive and negative ion data.</p><p>The matrix was imported in R to carry out Principle Component Analysis (PCA) to observe the overall distribution among the samples and the stability of the whole analysis process. Orthogonal Partial Least-Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) and Partial Least-Squares-Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA) were utilized to distinguish the metabolites that differ between groups. To prevent overfitting, 7-fold cross-validation and 200 Response Permutation Testing (RPT) were used to evaluate the quality of the model. V ariable Importance of Projection (VIP) values obtained from the OPLS-DA model were used to rank the overall contribution of each variable to group discrimination. A two-tailed Student’s T-test was further used to verify whether the metabolites of difference between groups were significant. Differential metabolites were selected with VIP values greater than 1.0 and p-values less than 0.05. Differential metabolites were further used to for KEGG pathway (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/) enrichment analysis.</p>	<p>A two-tailed Student’s T-test was further used to verify whether the metabolites of difference between groups were significant.

Study Title	Metabolic study of eggs in L. xylina
Study Description	<p> The casuarina moth (Lymantria xylina) is a notorious forestry pest, posing severe ecological and economic threats due to its destructive defoliation outbreaks and high invasive potential. Despite its significance, a high-quality reference genome has been lacking, limiting molecular-level investigations into its biology and hindering the development of effective pest management strategies.&nbsp;In this study, we report the first chromosome-level genome assembly&nbsp;of L. xylina&nbsp;generated through a combination of illumina short-reads, Oxford Nanopore long-reads, and Hi-C scaffolding. The final assembly spans 977.74 Mb, with 95.17% anchored to 31 pseudo-chromosomes, achieving a scaffold N50 of 34.15 Mb. Importantly, telomeric sequences were identified at both ends of all 31 pseudo-chromosomes, underscoring the exceptional quality and completeness of this reference genome. Quality assessment further revealed a BUSCO completeness of 94.5% and a consensus QV of 31.72. We also annotated 18,484 protein-coding genes, 95.21% of which were functionally assigned, and characterized genome-wide repetitive elements (77.18%).</p><p> Beyond the genome assembly, we generated comprehensive RNA-seq and metabolomic datasets&nbsp;across multiple diapause stages, enabling insights into gene expression dynamics and metabolic regulation during egg development. Together, these resources provide a valuable foundation for studying the genetic basis of host adaptation, invasiveness, and interactions with natural enemies such as nucleopolyhedrovirus and Beauveria bassiana.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The egg masses of&nbsp;<em>L. xylina</em>&nbsp;were obtained from a casuarina plantation in Pingtan Comprehensive Experimental Zone, Fuzhou City, Fujian Province (25°27'02.9'N 119°47'58.3'E)&nbsp;and were maintained at 4 °C&nbsp;before hatching. Hatched larvae were fed with an artificial diet at 25±1 °C,&nbsp;14:10 (L:D) photoperiod and 65±5% relative humidity. The larvae, pupae, and adult moth were collected separately. The samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen and then stored at&nbsp;−80 °C.&nbsp; For metabolic analysis, samples from the four groups (DT, DTH, ED, and MD), with each group comprising five biological replicates, were thawed on ice.&nbsp;</p>	<p>For metabolic analysis, samples from the four groups (DT, DTH, ED, and MD), with each group comprising five biological replicates, were thawed on ice.&nbsp;Take 50 ±&nbsp;2 mg of one sample and add cold steel balls to the mixture and&nbsp;homogenate at 30 Hz for 3 min. Add 1 mL 70% methanol with internal standard extract to the homogenized&nbsp;centrifuge tube, Whirl the mixture for 5 min, and then centrifuge it with 12,000 rpm at 4 °C for 10 min. After&nbsp;centrifugation, draw 400 uL of supernatant into the corresponding EP tube and store in -20 °C refrigerator&nbsp;overnight, then centrifuge at 12000 r/min at 4 °C for 3 min, and take 2000 uL of supernatant in the liner of the&nbsp;corresponding injection bottle for on-board analysis.</p>	<p>The sample extracts were analyzed using an&nbsp;LC-ESI-MS/MS system (UPLC, ExionLC AD&nbsp;, https://sciex.&nbsp;com.cn/; MS, QTRAP® System,&nbsp;https://sciex.com/).&nbsp;The analytical conditions&nbsp;were as follows, UPLC: column,&nbsp;Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS&nbsp;T3&nbsp;C18&nbsp;(1.8μm,&nbsp;2.1 mm*100 mm);&nbsp;column&nbsp;temperature,&nbsp;40&nbsp;°C;&nbsp;flow&nbsp;rate,&nbsp;0.4&nbsp;mL/min;&nbsp;injection&nbsp;volume,&nbsp;2μL&nbsp;or&nbsp;5μL;&nbsp;solvent&nbsp;system,&nbsp;water&nbsp;(0.1%&nbsp;formic&nbsp;acid):&nbsp;acetonitrile&nbsp;(0.1%&nbsp;formic&nbsp;acid);&nbsp;gradient program,&nbsp;95:5 V/V&nbsp;at&nbsp;0 min,&nbsp;10:90 V/V&nbsp;at&nbsp;10.0 min,&nbsp;10:90 V/V&nbsp;at&nbsp;11.0 min,&nbsp;95:5&nbsp;V/V&nbsp;at&nbsp;11.1&nbsp;min, 95:5 V/V&nbsp;at&nbsp;14.0 min.</p>	<p>The&nbsp;Triple&nbsp;TOF mass&nbsp;spectrometer was used&nbsp;for&nbsp;its&nbsp;ability to&nbsp;acquire MS/MS&nbsp;spectra&nbsp;on&nbsp;an&nbsp;information-&nbsp;dependent basis (IDA) during an LC/MS experiment.&nbsp;In&nbsp;this&nbsp;mode,&nbsp;the&nbsp;acquisition&nbsp;software&nbsp;(TripleTOF&nbsp;6600,&nbsp;AB&nbsp;SCIEX)&nbsp;continuously&nbsp;evaluates&nbsp;the&nbsp;full&nbsp;scan&nbsp;survey&nbsp;MS&nbsp;data&nbsp;as&nbsp;it&nbsp;collects&nbsp;and&nbsp;triggers&nbsp;the&nbsp;acquisition&nbsp;of&nbsp;MS/MS&nbsp;spectra&nbsp;depending&nbsp;on&nbsp;preselected&nbsp;criteria.&nbsp;&nbsp;In&nbsp;each&nbsp;cycle,&nbsp;12&nbsp;precursor&nbsp;ions&nbsp;whose&nbsp;intensity&nbsp;greater&nbsp;than&nbsp;100 were&nbsp;chosen&nbsp;for&nbsp;fragmentation&nbsp;at&nbsp;collision&nbsp;energy&nbsp;(CE)&nbsp;of 30 V&nbsp;(12 MS/MS&nbsp;events with product ion&nbsp;accumulation time of 50 msec each).&nbsp;ESI&nbsp;source conditions were&nbsp;set as&nbsp;following:&nbsp;Ion&nbsp;source&nbsp;gas&nbsp;1&nbsp;as&nbsp;50&nbsp;Psi,&nbsp;Ion source gas 2 as 50 Psi, Curtain gas as 25 Psi, source temperature 500 °C, Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF) 5500 V or -4500 V in positive or negative modes, respectively.</p>	<p>The acquired MS data pretreatments including peak picking, peak grouping, retention time </p><p>correction, second peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts was performed using </p><p>XCMS software. LC−MS raw data files were converted into mzXML format and then processed </p><p>by the XCMS, CAMERA and metaX[2] toolbox implemented with the R software. Each ion was </p><p>identified by combining retention time (RT) and m/z data. Intensities of each peaks were recorded </p><p>and a three dimensional matrix containing arbitrarily assigned peak indices (retention time-m/z </p><p>pairs), sample names (observations) and ion intensity information (variables) was generated.</p>	<p>Qualitative and quantitative metabolite analysis was performed based on a local metabolic database. The Multi-Reaction Monitoring (MRM) metabolite detection multi-peak chromatogram illustrates the substances detected within the samples, where distinct colors represent different metabolites. Characteristic ions for each substance were screened using a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer, and their signal intensities (counts per second, CPS) were recorded by the detector. The raw mass spectrometry files were processed using MultiQuant software to perform chromatographic peak integration and correction. The area under each peak (Peak Area) represents the relative abundance of the corresponding metabolite. Finally, all integrated peak area data were exported and archived.</p>

Study Title	Differentiation Defect Repositions Sebaceous Glands as Instigators in the Early Pathogenesis of Hidradenitis Suppurativa
Study Description	<p>The role of sebaceous glands (SGs) in the early pathogenesis of hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is undefined, with unclear causal relationship to inflammatory sequelae. The aim of this study was to determine whether SG aberrations constitute a primary pathogenic driver in early HS and elucidate the underlying mechanism. Histological study, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), and in vitro functional assays were employed. Clinical specimens included non-lesional skin, early lesional skin from patients diagnosed with Hurley I HS, and healthy controls. Human SZ95 sebocytes were used for mechanistic studies, including gene knockdown with lentivirus, bulk RNA sequencing, and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry based lipidomic analysis.&nbsp;</p><p>The size of SG was significantly reduced in HS patients. Development aberration and significant downregulation of tight junction signaling (e.g., TJP1, OCLN, CLDN1) in HS SGs were revealed by scRNA-seq, associated with compromised barrier integrity and early CD45+ immune cell infiltration. Knockdown of CLDN1 in human SZ95 sebocytes recapitulated these findings, inducing a robust pro-inflammatory response and a shift toward keratinocyte-like lineage accompanied by metabolic reprogramming, specifically overproduction of lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC). Exogenous LPC directly promoted proliferation and inflammatory cytokine secretion in HaCaT keratinocytes. Collectively, these findings reposition SGs as instigators in the early pathogenesis of HS.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cultured cells were digested upon reaching 80% confluence with trypsin (25200056, Thermo Fisher) before centrifugation at 3000 rpm, 4°C for 5 minutes to remove supernatant. Pellet was resuspended in 4°C PBS ( SH30256.01, Hyclone), transfered to fresh labeled tubes and centrifuged at 3000 rpm, 4°C for 5 minutes to remove supernatant two times before resuspended with 4°C PBS. Cell counting was performed to ensure a sample size of more than 5E6 cells per sample before resuspension with 4°C PBS and centrifugation at 3000 rpm, 4°C for 5 minutes to remove supernatant. Cell pellet was flash-freezed by immersing the tube bottom in liquid nitrogen for 1 min before briefly pressure equalization to prevent tube cracking. Samples were sealed and stored at −80°C until further processing.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Transfer the sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube using 600 μL of methanol-water (V:V = 1:1) containing mixed internal standards.</p><p>Add 600 μL of chloroform, followed by ice-bath ultrasonication for cell disruption (1620 W, 10 min, with cycles of 6 s on and 4 s off).</p><p>Perform ultrasonic extraction in an ice-water bath for 10 min, then allow the mixture to stand overnight at 4°C.</p><p>Centrifuge for 10 min (12,000 rpm, 4°C), collect 450 μL of the lower phase, and transfer it to an LC MS vial for solvent evaporation.</p><p>Reconstitute the dried lipid residue in the LC MS vial with 300 μL of isopropanol-methanol (V:V = 1:1) by vortexing for 30 s and ice-water bath ultrasonication for 3 min, then transfer the solution into a 1.5 mL EP tube.</p><p>Centrifuge for 10 min (12,000 rpm, 4°C), take 150 μL of the supernatant, and load it into an LC MS vial with an insert for LC MS analysis.</p><p>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of extraction solvent from all individual samples, with each QC volume matching that of a single sample.</p><p>Note:&nbsp;All extraction reagents were pre-cooled to 4°C before use</p>	<p>The analytical instrument used in this experiment was a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class plus/Thermo QE liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry system.</p><p>Column temperature: 55°C;</p><p>Mobile phase A: acetonitrile : water = 6 : 4 (v/v, containing 10 mM ammonium acetate);</p><p>Mobile phase B: isopropanol : acetonitrile = 9 : 1 (v/v, containing 10 mM ammonium acetate);</p><p>Flow rate: 0.26 mL/min;</p><p>Injection volume: 3 μL.</p>	<p>Ion source: HESI; the sample mass spectrometry signals were acquired in both positive and negative ion scanning modes.</p><p>Data acquisition mode: DDA</p><p>Scanning method: Full MS/dd-MS2 (Top 10)</p>	<p>Peak Extraction:&nbsp;Lipid Search software was used to read the raw data exported from the Q Exactive LC-MS/MS system, extracting MS information and the exact mass of precursor ions.</p><p>Identification:&nbsp;Based on the precursor ions and multistage mass spectrometry data from each individual sample, lipid molecular structures and their adduct patterns in positive and negative ion modes were identified.</p><p>Peak Alignment:&nbsp;The search results for each independent sample were aligned within a specified retention time window, and the results were consolidated into a single report to generate the raw data matrix.</p>	<p>The LipidSearch lipidomics database, developed by Thermo Scientific for Q Exactive series high-resolution mass spectrometers, is one of the mainstream databases in lipidomics research. It includes over 8 major classes, 300 subclasses, and 1.7 million lipid molecules along with their predicted fragment ion spectra. Through different lipid identification algorithms-such as product ion, precursor ion, and neutral loss scans—the database enables systematic and reliable lipid identification.</p>

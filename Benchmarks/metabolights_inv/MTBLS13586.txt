Study Title	Metabolomics Study On Bacterial Cellulose Biosynthesis In Komagataeibacter hansenii HDM1-3
Study Description	<p>To investigate the metabolic shift mechanism of Komagataeibacter hansenii(K. hansenii)HDM1-3 from the bacterial growth phase to the bacterial cellulose (BC) synthesis phase,untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed on K. hansenii&nbsp;HDM1-3 cultures at the early fermentation stage(18h) and late fermentation stage(36h).Pathway enrichment analysis identified six significantly enriched key metabolic pathways:Starch and sucrose metabolism,Glycerophospholipid metabolism&nbsp;and Galactose metabolism.Differential metabolite screening revealed a total of 211&nbsp;differential metabolites. Compared to 18h,92&nbsp;metabolites were upregulated and 119&nbsp;metabolites were downregulated in 36h.Further screening identified significant abundance differences between 18h and 36h for the following metabolites:UDP-glucose, ADP-glucose&nbsp;andMelibiose.These metabolites serve as potential biomarkers distinguishing different fermentation phases.The research enriches the metabolomic understanding of K. hansenii&nbsp;and provides a theoretical reference for enhancing BC biosynthesis yield in this bacterium in future studies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The strain&nbsp;K. hansenii&nbsp;HDM1-3 (CCTCC No.: M2010332) was isolated from rotten kiwi fruit.</p><p>Seed culture medium:&nbsp;Sucrose 2percent, yeast extract 0.3percent, peptone 0.5percent, KH2PO4 0.2percent, MgSO4 0.015percent, natural pH.</p><p>Liquid fermentation medium:&nbsp;Glucose 5percent, yeast extract 0.5percent, peptone 0.5percent, citric acid 0.1percent, K2HPO4 0.1percent, Na2HPO4 0.2percent, MgSO4 0.025percent, pH 5.8 .</p><p>Chemicals of analytical grade:&nbsp;Peptone (BR grade), yeast extract, glucose, sucrose, K2HPO4, KH2PO4, MgSO4, NaOH, and Na2HPO4.</p><p>Chemicals of chromatographic grade:&nbsp;Methanol, acetonitrile, formic acid, propanol, and water.</p>	<p>The activated strain was inoculated into the culture medium and incubated with shaking at 28 °C and 150 r·min-1 for 24 h. Subsequently, the seed culture was transferred at a 7% inoculum size into the fermentation medium and cultured statically at a constant temperature of 28 °C. Samples were taken at 18 h and 36 h of fermentation, respectively, to determine the residual sugar content, pH, bacterial cellulose (BC) yield, and optical density (OD) of the culture broth.</p>	<p>The sample was analyzed using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm) with the column temperature set at 40 °C and an injection volume of 3 μL. Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water and 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), while mobile phase B was composed of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol, and 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid).</p>	<p>The mass scan range was set from 70 to 1050 m/z, with positive ion spray voltage at 3.5 kV and negative ion spray voltage at –3 kV. The sheath gas flow was 50 arb, auxiliary heating gas flow was 13 arb, and the ion source heating temperature was 450 °C.</p>	<p>The raw data obtained from LC MS analysis were subjected to data preprocessing via the bioinformatics cloud analysis platform&nbsp;of Shanghai Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. .</p>	<p>The preprocessed data were subjected to differential metabolite analysis and multivariate statistics using the ropls package (version 1.6.2). Differential metabolites were screened based on the criteria of VIP &gt; 1, P &lt; 0.05, and FC &gt; 2 or FC &lt; 0.5. Enrichment analysis of metabolic pathways was performed by comparing the results with the KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) database using the scipy package (version 1.0.0) in Python, with a significance threshold of P &lt; 0.05. Finally, KEGG metabolic pathway data were obtained using the software KEGG Pathway (Release 2017-05-01) .</p>

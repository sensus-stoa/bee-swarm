Study Title	Influence of Culture Conditions on Metabolite Diversity in Epichloë sinensis Strains
Study Description	<p>Epichloë sinensis, associated with Festuca sinensis, exhibits&nbsp;rich morphological,&nbsp;genetic and functional diversity&nbsp;across different strains. To comprehensively evaluate the categories and difference in the secondary metabolites&nbsp;of different E. sinensis&nbsp;strains and thereby reveal the functional diversity, the present study analyzed the metabolites of mycelia collected from potato dextrose agar (PDA) and potato dextrose broth (PDB) cultures&nbsp;and fermentation broths of three different E. sinensis&nbsp;strains. These strains&nbsp;(ID1, 2, and 84C) were isolated from F. sinensis&nbsp;seeds collected from different locations. These three strains of E. sinensis&nbsp;produced a total of 1029 compounds categorized into&nbsp;14 major classes&nbsp;in the mycelia and fermentation which&nbsp;included a wide range of compound structure and types, revealing E. sinensis&nbsp;possesses abundant metabolites. The different strains shared the similar types of metabolites which only varied in concentrations&nbsp;of&nbsp;some metabolites,&nbsp;indicating the similarity in metabolic function. However, types, quantity and concentrations of secondary metabolites varied in&nbsp;different conditions, suggesting the metabolites changed&nbsp;with environmental variations, as 135 unique compounds were only detected in mycelia from PDA (solid medium), while 205 exclusive metabolites were identified in PDB fermentation broth (liquid environment). Comparing metabolites of hyphae in PDA and PDB, there were a total of 100 different compounds. Comparing metabolites of hyphae in PDB and fermentation broth, there were 136 different compounds. These metabolites were mainly involved in the citrate cycle&nbsp;(TCA cycle) and alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolism.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Three E.&nbsp;sinensis&nbsp;strains, 1, 2 and 84C, were isolated from F. sinensis seeds collected from Xiahe, Gansu Province (102°31'30'N, 35°12'0'E, strain 1), Qinghai Yushu (97°10'15.4'N, 32°49'38'E, strain 2), and Qinghai Ping'an&nbsp;(102°05.116N, 36°17.723E, strain 84C), respectively. The corresponding biological replicates for each group were 4 cases.&nbsp;The strains were stored at 4°C in the Microbial Germplasm Resource Bank of the Center for Grassland Microbiome of Lanzhou University.</p><p>E.&nbsp;sinensis&nbsp;was grown on a sterilized PDA medium at 25℃ for four weeks to produce hypha. A 6-mm diameter puncher was then taken at the edge of the colony and inoculated into potato dextrose broth&nbsp;(PDB)&nbsp;and PDA media. Liquid culture was carried out using 250 mL conical flasks, containing 100 mL each of PDB medium. The three E.&nbsp;sinensis&nbsp;strains were inoculated and replicated 20 times per strain. The E.&nbsp;sinensis&nbsp;strains were shaken on an intelligent shaker at 25℃ and 145 rpm.</p><p>Four samples were randomly collected from fungal endophytes&nbsp;mycelia cultured in PDB medium, PDB fermentation broth, and fungal endophytes&nbsp;mycelia cultured in PDA medium (Liu et al.&nbsp;2004). The fungal endophytes hyphae and fermentation broth&nbsp;was collected&nbsp;as described by Luo and Tian et al. (2021). These samples were preserved on dry ice and subsequently transported to Biotree Biomedical Technology (Biotree Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China) for LC-MS and GC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Weigh 500 mg sample into a 2 mL EP tube and add steel ball.&nbsp;1000 μL extract (methanol: acetonitrile: water = 2:2:1) containing internal ribonucleol was added.&nbsp;35 Hz grinding treatment for 4 min, ultrasonic in ice water bath for 5 min (repeat the step 3 times).&nbsp;Refrigerate at -40℃&nbsp;for one hour&nbsp;.&nbsp;Centrifuge the sample at 4℃&nbsp;at 12000 rpm for 15 min.&nbsp;Carefully remove 900 μL supernatant into 2 mL EP tube and blow dry.&nbsp;Add 90 μL methanol: acetonitrile: water = 2:2:1 for resolution.&nbsp;Centrifuge the sample at 4℃&nbsp;at 12000 rpm for 15 min.&nbsp;Carefully remove 50 μL supernatant from each sample, take 20 μL from mixed QC.&nbsp;Dry extract in a vacuum concentrator.</p><p>After evaporation in a vacuum concentrator, 80&nbsp;μL of Methoxyamination&nbsp;hydrochloride (20mg/mL&nbsp;in pyridine) was added and then incubated at 80 ℃ for 30 min, then derivatized by 100 μL of BSTFA regent (1% TMCS,&nbsp;v/v) at 40 ℃ for 1.5 h. Gradually cooling samples to room temperature, 8 μL of FAMEs (in chloroform) was added to QC sample. All samples were then analyzed by gas chromatograph coupled with a time-of-flight mass spectrometer&nbsp;(GC-TOF-MS).</p>	<p>GC-TOF-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph coupled with a time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The system utilized a DB-5MS capillary column. 1 μL aliquot of sample was injected in split&nbsp;(10:1). Helium was used as the carrier gas, the front inlet purge flow was 3&nbsp;mL min−1, and the gas flow rate through the column was 1&nbsp;mL min−1. The initial temperature was kept at 50&nbsp;°C for 1&nbsp;min, then raised to 310 °C at a rate of 10&nbsp;°C min−1, then kept for 8 min at 310&nbsp;°C. The injection, transfer line, and ion source temperatures were 280, 280and 250&nbsp;°C, respectively. The energy was -70&nbsp;eV in electron impact mode. The mass spectrometry data were acquired in full-scan mode with the m/z range of 50-500 at a rate of 12.5 spectra per second after a solvent delay of 6.25 min.</p>	<p>GC-TOF-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph coupled with a time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The system utilized a DB-5MS capillary column. 1 μL aliquot of sample was injected in split&nbsp;(10:1). Helium was used as the carrier gas, the front inlet purge flow was 3&nbsp;mL min−1, and the gas flow rate through the column was 1&nbsp;mL min−1. The initial temperature was kept at 50&nbsp;°C for 1&nbsp;min, then raised to 310 °C at a rate of 10&nbsp;°C min−1, then kept for 8 min at 310&nbsp;°C. The injection, transfer line, and ion source temperatures were 280, 280and 250&nbsp;°C, respectively. The energy was -70&nbsp;eV in electron impact mode. The mass spectrometry data were acquired in full-scan mode with the m/z range of 50-500 at a rate of 12.5 spectra per second after a solvent delay of 6.25 min.</p>	<p>Raw data analysis, including peak extraction, baseline adjustment, deconvolution, alignment and integration, was finished with Chroma TOF (V 4.3x, LECO) software and LECO-Fiehn Rtx5 database was used for metabolite identification by matching the mass spectrum and retention index. Finally, the peaks detected in less than half of QC samples or RSD&gt;30% in QC samples was removed.</p>	<p>The metabolite data were log2-&nbsp;transformed (log2) to improve normality and mean centering before OPLS-DA analysis&nbsp;by&nbsp;SIMCA-16.0&nbsp;software&nbsp;(Umetricus AB, Sweden). The variable importance in the projection (VIP) ≥ 1 in the OPLS-DA model and the absolute Log2FC (fold change) ≥ 1 were set for screening differential metabolites. Those having a p-value of t-test 0.05 and VIP ≥1 were deemed to be different between the two groups. Venn diagrams were employed to illustrate the number of divergent metabolites. An enrichment analysis of differential metabolites was conducted using the KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/pathway.html).&nbsp;KEGG&nbsp;enrichment analysis&nbsp;was performed using the Metware Cloud, a free online platform for data analysis (https://cloud.metware.cn).</p>

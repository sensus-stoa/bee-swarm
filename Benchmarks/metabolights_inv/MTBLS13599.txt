Study Title	Gut Microbiota Mediates Maternal PM2.5 Exposure-induced Neurodevelopmental Disorder in offspring via Disturbing the Glutamate Metabolism: Evidence from Multiomics Analysis
Study Description	<p> While accumulating evidence links maternal PM2.5 exposure to offspring neurodevelopmental impairments, yet the underlying biological pathways require further elucidation. To address this, female ICR mice are exposed to filtered air (FA) or concentrated ambient particulate matter (CAP) for three months before conception and 16 days during gestation. Behavioral tests indicate that declined memory function, anxiety-like behavior and social deficits accompanied by synaptic impairments are induced in offspring by maternal PM2.5 exposure in a gender-dependent manner. Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) from PM2.5-exposed dams to pseudo-germ-free recipients successfully remodels the neurodevelopmental deficits in offspring, confirming the key mediating role of maternal gut microbiota. Multi-omics analysis shows that maternal PM2.5 exposure causes dysbiosis of gut microbiota in both mother and offspring, disturbs glutamate metabolism and glutamatergic synapase-related pathways. Further in vivo and in vitro experiments indicate that these effects may be induced via ERK/CREB signaling pathway. Additionally, dietary glutamine supplementation effectively ameliorates behavioral deficits and synaptic dysfunction in offspring. In conclusion, this study reveals that maternal PM2.5 exposure can disrupt offspring neurodevelopment via the gut microbiota-glutamate-brain axis and glutamine supplementation may serve as a potential effective intervention strategy.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Serum samples for metabolomic analysis were collected from the offspring at 11 weeks of age. Briefly, blood was obtained via retro-orbital puncture under appropriate anesthesia. The whole blood was allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes and then centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C to separate the serum. The supernatant (serum) was carefully aliquoted into sterile tubes to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles and immediately stored at -80°C until subsequent metabolomic profiling.</p>	<p>Serum samples were thawed on ice. Automated sample preparation was performed using an Eppendorf epMotion Workstation. Briefly, 25 μL of plasma was aliquoted into a 96-well plate, followed by the addition of 120 μL of ice-cold methanol containing internal standards. After vortexing for 5 minutes, the plate was centrifuged at 4000g for 30 minutes. Then, 30 μL of supernatant was transferred to a new plate and derivatized with 20 μL of freshly prepared derivative reagents at 30°C for 60 min. The derivatized samples were diluted with 330 μL of ice-cold 50% methanol, stored at -20°C for 20 minutes, and centrifuged again at 4000g (4°C) for 30 minutes. Finally, 135 μL of the resulting supernatant was combined with 10 μL of internal standards in a new plate for LC-MS analysis.</p><p>A set of quality control samples was included: internal standards were added to all samples; pooled QC samples were created from aliquots of all study samples; reagent blank samples (solvents only) were processed to monitor contamination; and a calibration series consisting of a blank, a zero, and seven concentration points was used for quantification. These QC samples were injected at regular intervals throughout the analytical sequence.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC system (Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). Chromatographic separation was achieved on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (1.7 µm, 2.1 x 100 mm) equipped with a VanGuard pre-column (1.7 µm, 2.1 x 5 mm) (Waters Corp.). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C and the sample manager temperature at 10°C. The mobile phases consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) a mixture of acetonitrile and isopropanol (70:30). The gradient elution was programmed as follows: 0-1 min (5% B), 1-11 min (5-78% B), 11-13.5 min (78-95% B), 13.5-14 min (95-100% B), 14-16 min (100% B), 16-16.1 min (100-5% B), and 16.1-18 min (5% B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the injection volume was 5.0 µL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was performed using a Xevo TQ-S tandem mass spectrometer (Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). The instrument was equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source and data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes. The key source parameters were set as follows: capillary voltage at 1.5 kV for ESI+ and 2.0 kV for ESI-, source temperature at 150°C, and desolvation temperature at 550°C. The desolvation gas flow rate was 1000 L/hr.</p>	<p>The raw data files generated by the UPLC-MS/MS system were processed using TMBQ software (v1.0, Metabo-Profile, Shanghai, China). This proprietary software was used for peak integration, calibration, and quantitation of each metabolite. Subsequently, statistical analyses—including PCA, OPLS-DA, univariate analysis, and pathway analysis—were performed using the self-developed iMAP platform (v1.0, Metabo-Profile, Shanghai, China).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and quantitation were achieved by comparing the experimental data to a library of commercially available reference standards. The targeted metabolites were definitively identified based on the precise match of both the chromatographic retention time and the mass spectral fragmentation pattern (MS/MS) to their respective reference standards. Quantitation was performed by constructing a calibration curve for each metabolite using a series of standard samples of known concentration, following the principle of y = ax + b, where instrument response (y) is plotted against analyte concentration (x).</p>

Study Title	Free fatty acid generation via Rhein-induced LDL-C endocytosis accelerates biofilm infected diabetic wound healing
Study Description	<p>Diabetic foot infection (DFI) significantly increases risks of hospitalization and amputation,&nbsp;with biofilm infections caused by methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and&nbsp;carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) being key drivers of treatment failure. Here, we&nbsp;analyze MRSA/CRE biofilm-infected ulcer tissues in patients using proteomics and metabolomics.&nbsp;Compared to non-healing cases, healed tissues show activation of the low-density lipoprotein&nbsp;receptor (LDLR)-mediated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) endocytosis and increase&nbsp;free fatty acids production, which we hypothesize a potential countermeasure against biofilms.&nbsp;However, free fatty acids’ oxidative instability limits their therapeutic use. Building on our findings&nbsp;demonstrating Rhein’s potent anti-MRSA/CRE activity and its synergy with Ag⁺, we develop&nbsp;an injectable hydrogel for sustained co-delivery of both agents. This formulation outperforms Ag⁺&nbsp;monotherapy, accelerating wound closure in biofilm-infected diabetic wounds. Mechanistically,&nbsp;Rhein binds to LDLR, upregulates its expression, and enhances LDL-C endocytosis pathway in&nbsp;vascular endothelial cells to generate more free fatty acids. Among these, eicosapentaenoic acid&nbsp;(EPA) is identified as the most critical free fatty acids promoting healing, as it inhibits biofilm&nbsp;formation and synergizes with Ag⁺ to exert antibacterial and pro-healing effects. Collectively, this&nbsp;study identifies endogenous host lipid metabolites as “metabolic antibiotics” and establishes a&nbsp;synergistic therapeutic strategy that concurrently suppresses biofilm formation, inhibits planktonic&nbsp;bacteria, alleviates inflammation, and promotes tissue repair in drug-resistant biofilm infected&nbsp;diabetic wounds.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>we enrolled 45 inpatients with biofilm-infected DFU caused by MRSA and CRE from the Department of Vascular Surgery at Yueyang Hospital between May 2023 and September 2024. Ulcer tissues were collected post-debridement. Patients were stratified into healed (DFI-healers) and non-healed (DFI-non-healers) groups after 3-month follow-up; 36 participants completed the study. Additionally, discarded foot skin tissues were collected from 10 diabetic outpatients without ulcers (DM-non-ulcers) after foot tumor resection or orthopedic surgery.&nbsp;</p><p>Patients meeting the following criteria were enrolled, aged ≥ 40 and ≤ 90 years; diabetes duration &gt; 10 years; foot ulcer duration ≥ 4 weeks; ulcer area 6 ~ 20 cm²; Wagner grade 2 ~ 4; MRSA and CRE biofilm-confirmed infections by bacterial culture and antibacterial susceptibility testing; scheduled for surgical debridement at our institution.&nbsp;DM-non-ulcers were defined as aged ≥ 40 and ≤ 90 years; type 2 diabetes without foot ulcer history;&nbsp;absence of chronic comorbidities or prior foot surgeries; scheduled for resection of benign skin&nbsp;lesions (e.g., lipomas, nevi) or orthopedic foot procedures.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The sample stored at -80 °C refrigerator was thawed on ice.

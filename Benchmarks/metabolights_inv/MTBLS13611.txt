Study Title	Free fatty acid generation via Rhein-induced LDL-C endocytosis accelerates biofilm infected diabetic wound healing
Study Description	<p>Diabetic foot infection (DFI) significantly increases risks of hospitalization and amputation, with biofilm infections caused by methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) being key drivers of treatment failure. Here, we analyze MRSA/CRE biofilm-infected ulcer tissues in patients using proteomics and metabolomics. Compared to non-healing cases, healed tissues show activation of the low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR)-mediated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) endocytosis and increase free fatty acids production, which we hypothesize a potential countermeasure against biofilms. However, free fatty acids’ oxidative instability limits their therapeutic use. Building on our findings demonstrating Rhein’s potent anti-MRSA/CRE activity and its synergy with Ag⁺, we develop an injectable hydrogel for sustained co-delivery of both agents. This formulation outperforms Ag⁺ monotherapy, accelerating wound closure in biofilm-infected diabetic wounds. Mechanistically, Rhein binds to LDLR, upregulates its expression, and enhances LDL-C endocytosis pathway in vascular endothelial cells to generate more free fatty acids. Among these, eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) is identified as the most critical free fatty acids promoting healing, as it inhibits biofilm formation and synergizes with Ag⁺ to exert antibacterial and pro-healing effects. Collectively, this study identifies endogenous host lipid metabolites as “metabolic antibiotics” and establishes a synergistic therapeutic strategy that concurrently suppresses biofilm formation, inhibits planktonic bacteria, alleviates inflammation, and promotes tissue repair in drug-resistant biofilm infected diabetic wounds.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Lipidomic sequencing analysis was performed on skin wound tissue samples from C57BL/6J infection model mice treated with Treat group and Vehicle group.</p>	<p> 1) Remove the sample from the -80 ° C freezer and thaw it on ice;</p><p> 2) After thawing the sample, weigh 20mg into the corresponding 2mL centrifuge tube;</p><p> 3) Add a steel ball with tweezers, homogenize with a ball mill (30HZ) for 20 seconds, centrifuge at 3000r/min for 30 seconds under 4C conditions, and centrifuge the sample to the bottom of the tube (the homogenization time can be appropriately increased according to the actual homogenization situation);</p><p> 4) Add 1mL of internal standard lipid extraction solution (methyl tert butyl ether: methanol=3:1, V/V), vortex for 15 minutes; </p><p> 5) Add 200 μL of water, vortex for 1 minute, centrifuge at 12000r/min for 10 minutes under 4C conditions; </p><p> 6) Suck 200 μL of upper clear liquid into the corresponding numbered centrifuge tube and concentrate until completely dry;</p><p> 7) Add 200 μL of lipid complex solution (acetonitrile: isopropanol=1:1, V/V), vortex for 3 minutes, centrifuge at 12000r/min for 3 minutes, and transfer the supernatant for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>1) Chromatography column: Thermo Accucore™ C30 column (2.6 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm i.d.);</p><p>2) Mobile phase: Phase A, acetonitrile/water (60/40, v/v) (containing 0.1% formic acid, 10 mmol/L ammonium formate); Phase B, acetonitrile/isopropanol (10/90, v/v) (containing 0.1% formic acid, 10 mmol/L ammonium formate);</p><p>3) Gradient elution programme: 0 min: A/B 80:20 (V/V); 2 min: 70:30 (V/V); 4 min: 40:60 (V/V); 9 min: 5:95 (V/V); 17.3 min: 5:95 (V/V); 17.5 min: 15:85 (V/V); 14 min: 10:90 (v/v), 15 min: 80:20 (v/v), 20 min: 80:20 (v/v);</p><p>4) Flow rate: 0.35 mL/min; Column temperature: 45 °C; Injection volume: 2 μL</p>	<p>Electrospray Ionisation (ESI) source temperature: 500°C. Mass spectrometer voltage: 5500V in positive ion mode, -4500V in negative ion mode. Ion source gas1 (GS1): 45psi, gas2 (GS2): 55psi, curtain gas (CUR): 35psi. Within the triple quadrupole, each ion pair is scanned and detected according to optimised declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE).</p>	<p>The raw MS data (wiff.scan files) were converted to MzML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert before importing into freely available XCMS software.</p>	<p>Based on the self-built MWDB (metware database), qualitative analysis is performed using the retention time (RT) and parent-daughter ion pair information of the detected substances. Lipid quantification is achieved through multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) analysis utilising a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. In MRM mode, the quadrupole first screens precursor ions (parent ions) of target compounds, filtering out ions corresponding to other molecular weights to preliminarily exclude interference. After collision-induced ionisation in the collision cell, precursor ions fragment into numerous fragment ions. These fragments are then filtered by the triple quadrupole to select a specific target fragment ion, eliminating non-target ion interference for enhanced quantitative precision and reproducibility. Following acquisition of lipid mass spectrometry data from diverse samples, peak area integration is performed for all chromatographic peaks. Quantitative analysis is conducted using the internal standard method.</p>

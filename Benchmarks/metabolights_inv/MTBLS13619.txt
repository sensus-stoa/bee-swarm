Study Identifier	MTBLS13619
Study Title	Untargeted metabolomic profiling of Quercus glauca leaves under drought stress and brassinosteroid treatment
Study Description	<p>This study presents an untargeted LC-MS-based metabolomics dataset generated from leaves of Quercus glauca subjected to drought stress and exogenous brassinosteroid treatment. Leaf samples were collected at multiple time points under control, drought, and drought plus brassinosteroid conditions to capture dynamic metabolic responses. Metabolites were extracted and analyzed using reverse-phase UPLC coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry in both positive and negative ion modes. This dataset provides a comprehensive resource for investigating metabolic reprogramming associated with drought stress and brassinosteroid-mediated stress mitigation in woody plant species.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Leaf samples of Quercus glauca were collected from seedlings subjected to control, drought stress, and drought plus brassinosteroid treatments. Drought stress was imposed using polyethylene glycol (PEG-6000), and brassinosteroid was applied as a foliar spray at the selected effective concentration. Leaf tissues were harvested at multiple time points after treatment initiation to capture dynamic metabolic responses. For each treatment and time point, independent biological replicates were collected. Immediately after sampling, leaf materials were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>For solid samples, 50 mg of each sample was weighed and extracted with 1000 microliters of extraction solvent containing internal standards at a ratio of 1000 to 2. The extraction solvent consisted of methanol acetonitrile and water mixed at a volume ratio of 2 to 2 to 1 with an internal standard concentration of 2 mg per liter. Samples were vortex mixed for 30 seconds, followed by bead beating at 45 Hz for 10 minutes and ultrasonic extraction for 10 minutes in an ice water bath. For liquid samples, 100 microliters of sample was mixed with 500 microliters of extraction solvent containing internal standards at a ratio of 1000 to 2. The extraction solvent consisted of methanol and acetonitrile mixed at a volume ratio of 1 to 1 with an internal standard concentration of 2 mg per liter. Samples were vortex mixed for 30 seconds and subjected to ultrasonic extraction for 10 minutes in an ice water bath. For both solid and liquid samples, the extracts were incubated at minus 20 degrees C for 1 hour and then centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 degrees C. A volume of 500 microliters of the supernatant was carefully transferred to a clean tube and dried under vacuum. The dried extracts were reconstituted in 160 microliters of extraction solvent consisting of acetonitrile and water mixed at a volume ratio of 1 to 1, followed by vortex mixing for 30 seconds and ultrasonic treatment for 10 minutes in an ice water bath. After centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 degrees C, 120 microliters of the supernatant was transferred to a 2 mL vial for analysis. Quality control samples were prepared by pooling 10 microliters from each sample and analyzed together with the study samples.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ultra performance liquid chromatography system equipped with an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 micrometer, 2.1 by 100 mm, Waters). The column temperature was maintained at 40 degrees C and the autosampler temperature was set to 4 degrees C. The flow rate was 0.4 mL per minute and the injection volume was 2 microliters. Mass spectrometric analysis was carried out on a Xevo G2 XS QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source and operated in both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Xevo G2 XS QTOF mass spectrometer manufactured by Waters and equipped with an electrospray ionization source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes. The capillary voltage was set to 2000 V in positive ion mode and minus 1500 V in negative ion mode. The source temperature was set to 120 degrees C and the desolvation temperature was set to 500 degrees C. Full scan data were acquired over an m z range of 50 to 1200. Data acquisition was performed in MSe mode.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data files were processed using Progenesis QI software (Nonlinear Dynamics, Waters). The software was used for peak detection, retention time alignment, and peak area extraction across all samples. Metabolic features were normalized within the software prior to downstream statistical analysis. Processed data matrices were then exported for subsequent multivariate and univariate statistical analyses.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed based on accurate mass information and MS MS fragmentation patterns obtained from high resolution mass spectrometry data. Metabolic features detected after data processing were annotated by matching precursor m z values and MS MS spectra against public metabolite databases. Candidate metabolites were assigned according to mass accuracy and spectral similarity.</p>

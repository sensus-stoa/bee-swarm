Study Title	Limosilactobacillus reuteri with indole-3-lactic acid-producing capacity protects against atherosclerosis by fortifying the gut-vascular barrier
Study Description	<p>Introduction: The gut microbiota and its metabolites are implicated in atherosclerosis, but the specific effector molecules and their pathophysiological significance remain unclear.</p><p>Objectives: This study aimed to identify key atheroprotective microbial metabolites and to elucidate the mechanism by which they confer protection.</p><p>Methods: We combined clinical correlation analysis in atherosclerosis patients with interventional studies in ApoE⁻/⁻ mice supplemented with the high ILA-producing probiotic strain Limosilactobacillus reuteri Lr9. Mechanistic insights were gained through integrated vascular proteomics, colon transcriptomics, gut microbiomics, metabolome analysis, and validation in a Caco-2/HUVEC co-culture model of the gut-vascular barrier (GVB).</p><p>Results: Serum levels of indole-3-lactic acid (ILA) were significantly reduced in patients and inversely correlated with systemic LPS. Lr9 supplementation attenuated aortic plaque formation, reduced serum LPS, and restored GVB integrity in mice. Multi-omics analysis revealed that Lr9 suppressed pro-inflammatory signaling, upregulated tight junction and autophagy pathways, and remodeled the gut ecosystem by suppressing LPS-producing taxa while enriching beneficial bacteria and protective unsaturated fatty acids. Both Lr9 and its effector ILA directly enhanced barrier function in the GVB model.</p><p>Conclusion: Our findings establish ILA as a definitive mediator of atheroprotection and identify the GVB as a critical, therapeutically actionable target. We position L. reuteri Lr9 as a promising next-generation probiotic that acts via the gut-vascular axis to ameliorate disease.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Colonic contents were collected from mice with different treatments after euthanasia, and stored at -80℃.</p>	<p>Colonic contents were resuspended and homogenized in 200 µL of ultrapure water, followed by the addition of an equal volume of the methanol/acetonitrile solution. All mixtures were vortexed for 30 seconds and incubated at -20°C for 1 hour to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 15,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C, the supernatant was collected and concentrated under vacuum at 40°C. The resulting dry residues were reconstituted in 200 µL of methanol/water (1:5, v/v) and centrifuged again at 15,000 rpm for 10 minutes. The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane and transferred to autosampler vials for LC-MS/MS analysis. A quality control (QC) sample was prepared by pooling 5 µL from each sample. The resuspended solvent was used as blank.</p>	<p>Analysis was performed using a Vanquish™ MD HPLC system coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with a Hypersil GOLD™ VANQUISH™ C18 UHPLC column (1.9 µm, 100 × 2.1 mm, Thermo Scientific). The sample volume injected was 2 µL. The solvent system consisted of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid (mobile phage A) and acetonitrile/isopropanol (1:1, v/v) (mobile phage B) delivered at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–0.5 min, 2% B; 0.5–10.0 min, 2–50% B; 10.0–14.0 min, 50-98% B; 14.0–16.0 min, 98% B; 16.0–16.1 min, 98-2% B; 16.1–18.0 min, 2% B. The column temperature was maintained at 35°C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>An Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) was used. Mass spectrometry was conducted in full scan with both polarity and heated electrospray ionization mode with a resolution of 120,000. The scan range (m/z) was 70-1050. HESI parameters were set as follows: spray voltage, 3 kV; capillary temperature, 300°C; sheath gas, 35 arb; auxiliary gas, 15 arb.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed with Compound Discoverer 3.4 (Thermo Scientific) for peak alignment, picking, and quantification.</p>	<p>&nbsp;Metabolite identification was achieved by matching against the mzCloud, mzVault, and ChemSpider databases. Compounds with an mzCloud MS/MS spectral match score ≥ 70 were considered confidently identified. Differential metabolites were selected based on a variable importance in projection (VIP) score &gt; 1, an adjusted p-value &lt; 0.05, and an absolute log2(fold-change) ≥ 1.</p>

Study Title	Synergistic Effects of High-Fiber Diet and N-Carbamylglutamate on Sow Reproductive Performance via Uterine Microbiota-PI3K/Akt Modulation
Study Description	<p> Uterine microbiome is crucial for embryo implantation and pregnancy by creating a favorable environment. While both high fiber diet and N-Carbamylglutamate (NCG) have been individually demonstrated to enhance reproductive efficiency in sows, their potential synergistic effects and the involvement of uterine microbial mechanisms remain largely unexplored. In this study, we employed a multi-omics approach to investigate the synergistic impact of a high-fiber diet combined with 0.05% NCG on reproductive performance in Landrace × Yorkshire sows. The study revealed that high fiber with NCG (H+N) significantly increased FSH activity and T-AOC content in serum. Metagenomic analysis indicated an increased abundance of Clostridium disporicumin the uterine microbiota, which was positively correlated with elevated levels of the metabolite hydroxylysine. Subsequent transcriptomic profiling revealed activation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, which was associated with increased FSH activity and T-AOC content. Overall, these findings demonstrated that the supplementation of a high-fiber diet with NCG enhances sow reproductive efficiency by modulating the uterine microbiota (specifically Clostridium disporicum), increasing hydroxylysine production, and activating the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, thereby improving hormonal activity and antioxidant capacity.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>400 healthy sows (Landrace x Yorkshire hybrid) with identical parity (second or third) were selected from Hunan Longhua Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Technology Co., Ltd. The sows were randomly assigned to four dietary treatment groups (n = 100 per group) as follows: low-fiber group (LF; 3.73% crude fiber); low-fiber + NCG group (L+N; 3.73% crude fiber + 0.05% NCG); high-fiber group (HF; 7.46% crude fiber); high-fiber + NCG group (H+N; 7.46% crude fiber + 0.05% NCG). The experimental was initiated on the day of breeding and continued throughout the entire gestation period of 114 days. Six sows per treatment group were randomly chosen for euthanasia, and cervix samples were collected.</p>	<p>1.Weigh 50 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>2.Add 200 μL of pre-cooled water, add 2 steel beads, put into a high-throughput tissue grinder, 55 Hz, grind</p><p>for 60 s, repeat this step once;</p><p>3.Add 800 μL methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v);</p><p>4.Place in ultrasonic cleaning machine for 30 min;</p><p>5.Freeze in a -20℃ refrigerator for 30 min;</p><p>6.Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, take 800 μL of the supernatant and concentrate to dryness</p><p>under vacuum;</p><p>7.Add 150 μL 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine) to dissolve and vortex for 30</p><p>seconds;</p><p>8.Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22 μm filter membrane,</p><p>and add the filtrate to the test bottle;</p><p>9.Take 10-20 μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample for evaluating instrument stability</p><p>and data reliability.</p>	<p>An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used with a flow rate of</p><p>0.4 mL/min, a column temperature of 40°C, an autosampler temperature of 8°C, and an injection volume of</p><p>2 μL.</p>	<p>Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in</p><p>positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source,</p><p>spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325℃, auxiliary</p><p>gas temperature 300℃, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT</p><p>100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s,</p><p>secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto.</p>	<p>The .raw format data was imported into the commercial software Compound Discoverer™ 3.3 (version</p><p>3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithm of</p><p>the software, peak extraction, alignment, correction and other operations were performed. The unique peak</p><p>quality rating calculation and filter greatly reduced the interference of background peaks and low-quality</p><p>peaks. The peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the missing values of the undetected peaks were filled based on the software Fill Gaps algorithm, and the Sum total peak</p><p>area was normalized.</p>	<p>The identification of metabolites was based on self-built library PSNGM Database,</p><p>mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB</p><p>(https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/) and NIST_2020_MSMS spectral library. The</p><p>MS1 mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>

Study Title	Integrated lipidomic and transcriptomic analysis uncovers the lipid dynamic changes in Camellia weiningensis during seed development
Study Description	<p>Oil-tea <em>Camellia</em> is a promising woody oil crop that has been widely used as a cooking oil, lubricant, and cosmetics. However, there is a lack of dominant varieties suitable for high-altitude cold mountainous regions. <em>Camellia weiningensis</em> (<em>C. weiningensis</em>) is the only high-altitude species of the Camellia genus incorporated into Camellia oil production in China, characterized by high oil content and superior quality. However, this species has garnered limited attention and its lipid biosynthesis mechanism remains unexplored. In this study, lipidomic and transcriptomic analyses were conducted across different developmental stages of C. weiningensis. The oil content of C. weiningensis seeds increased continuously during seed development and was rich in unsaturated fatty acids, particularly oleic acid. A total of 1352 lipid molecules were identified, among which triacylglycerol exhibited the highest abundance and diversity. Furthermore, diacylglycerol, phosphatidic acid, and phosphatidylcholine exhibited high relative contents, with an abundance of unsaturated fatty acids especially oleic acid, predominantly located at the sn-2 position. RNA sequencing of developing seeds revealed enhanced expression of genes associated with fatty acid synthesis. Co-expression analysis identified several key structural genes (ACC, SAD, FAD2, DGAT1, PDAT1, and Ole) and transcription factors (MYBs, ERFs, WRKYs, and GRFs) were involved in fatty acid synthesis. These findings provide insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying fatty acid biosynthesis in C. weiningensis seeds, which will be beneficial for increasing the yield of oil-tea Camellia and for breeding superior varieties adapted to cold mountainous areas.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples used in this study were collected from the Guizhou University Oil-Tea Camellia experimental field at an altitude of 2200 m, located in Weining County, Guizhou, China. Nine healthy C. weiningensis trees (three trees per biological replicate) with consistent growth and fruit-bearing capacity under normal cultivation conditions were selected. Fruits were collected at 120, 134, 148, 162, and 176 days of pollination (DAP) according to the characteristics of oil accumulation, denoted as S1, S2, S3, S4, and S5, respectively. Thirty fruits at each stage were selected from different positions on each tree without any obvious spots or diseases.</p>	<p>Total lipids were extracted from the samples using a methyl-tert-butyl ether (MTBE)-based protocol following a method described by Dong et al. with minor modifications. Briefly, fresh samples (50 mg) were ground into powder at -10℃ (6 min, 50 Hz) with 280 μL extract (methanol: water = 2:5) and 400 μL MTBE. Subsequently, the mixture was subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5 ℃, 40 KHz), followed by standing at -20 ℃ for 30 min and centrifugation for 15 min (13000 g, 4℃). A 350 μL aliquot of the supernatant was collected and blow-dried with nitrogen. Subsequently, 100 μL reconstituted solution (isopropyl alcohol: acetonitrile =1:1) was added, vortex-mixed for 30 s, extracted using low-temperature ultrasonication for 5 min (5℃, 40KHz), centrifuged for 10 min (13000g, 4℃), and the supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS analysis. In addition, equal volumes of each sample were mixed as quality control (QC) samples to monitor the stability of the analysis. Isotope-labeled internal standards were added exclusively to QC samples to monitor the stability and reproducibility of the entire analytical process—from extraction to instrumental analysis—throughout the batch sequence.</p>	<p>The lipids in the samples were determined using UPLC–MS/MS. The UPLC system conditions was configured with a Thermo Accucore C30 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 2.6 μm), the flow rate: 0.4 mL/min, the injection volume: 5 μL, and the column temperature: 40℃. Mobile phase A consisted of 50% acetonitrile–water containing formic acid (0.1%) and ammonium acetate (10 mmol/L), and mobile phase B was acetonitrile–isopropanol–water (10:88:2, v/v) containing formic acid (0.02%) and ammonium acetate (2 mmol/L). Gradient elution was performed as follows: 65% ~ 40% A (0 ~ 4 min); 40% to 15% A (4 ~ 12 min); 15% to 0% A (15 ~ 17 min); 0% to 65% A (17 ~ 20 min).</p>	<p>The Q-Exactive mass spectrometer was employed to acquire mass spectrometric data in positive and negative modes under the following MS conditions: the scan range was 200-2000 m/z, and the sheath gas and auxiliary gas flow rates were 60 psi and 20 psi, respectively. The ionspray voltage floating was +3000 V (ESI+) and -3000 V (ESI-). The normalized collision energy was 20-40-60 V in rolling mode for MS/MS.</p>	<p>The raw data files generated by UHPLC–MS/MS were processed using LipidSearch 4.2 software to perform peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area integration.</p>	<p>Data were matched with public databases (LipidBLAST, LipidMaps, and HMDB) to obtain accurate qualitative results. The quantitative results were normalized using the total peak area, yielding the final relative quantitative results of the metabolites.</p>

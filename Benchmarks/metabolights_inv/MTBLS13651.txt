Study Title	Silage-derived flavonoids reprogram rumen microbiota to improve milk fat synthesis via rumen-mammary gland axis
Study Description	<p>Inoculation with homofermentative lactic acid bacteria (LAB) effectively enhances the silage quality of forages. Moreover, feeding such LAB-inoculated silage modulates rumen microbiota composition and metabolites, thereby improving ruminant production performance. Nevertheless, the specific mechanism through which LAB inoculants regulate the silage–rumen–mammary gland axis remains unclear.</p><p>Inoculation with homofermentative Lactiplantibacillus plantarum BX62 improved the alfalfa silage quality. Dairy goats fed the BX62 group silage showed significantly higher milk fat content compared to the control group (no inoculation) (P &lt; 0.05). Integrated analysis of silage microbial metabolomics and experimental validation revealed a significant increase in flavonoid content in the BX62 silage. This was attributed to microbial community restructuring and secretion of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes), which facilitated plant cell wall degradation and flavonoid release. Rumen metagenomic assembly and binning indicated that feeding flavonoid-rich BX62 silage induced the proliferation of flavonoid-degrading microbes and reshaped the rumen microbiota, which resulted in the upregulation of CAZymes and energy metabolic pathways (e.g., ko00620 Pyruvate metabolism), and enhanced fiber degradation and volatile fatty acid (VFA) production in the rumen. Consequently, acetate-dependent milk fat synthesis was promoted in BX62 group goats as showed by the elevated expressions of acetyl-CoA carboxylase 1 (ACC1), fatty acid synthase (FASN) and acyl-CoA synthetase short-chain family member 2 (ACSS2) in mammary gland. Moreover, four milk fat-positively correlated bacteria species (Eggerthellaceae bacterium, Clostridioides difficile, Candidatus Limivicinus sp., and Collinsella aerofaciens) harboring flavonoid-degrading genes proliferated with elevated flavonoid concentrations in the rumen. In vitro trial further confirmed flavonoid degradation capability in both C. difficile and A. equolifaciens (family Eggerthellaceae), and dose-dependent growth promotion in A. equolifaciens. These results demonstrate that silage-derived flavonoids drive rumen microbiome remodeling and promote mammary lipogenesis through a silage-rumen microbiota-mammary gland triad mechanism.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Sample collection was performed immediately after rumen fistulation. Fresh rumen fluid samples were filtered through four layers of sterile gauze to remove feed residues, aliquoted into cryopreservation tubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until extraction to minimize metabolite degradation.</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction was optimized for target metabolites (flavonoids): rumen fluid samples were mixed with ammonium acetate buffer (favorable for the extraction and stabilization of flavonoids), vortexed for 2 min, and centrifuged at 15,000 × g and 4 °C for 15 min. The supernatant was filtered through 0.22 μm filters and subjected to UHPLC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was carried out on a Thermo Fisher Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System with a Split Sampler HT autosampler. The separation was achieved using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Accucore Vanquish C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.6 μm) protected by a matching Accucore Vanquish C18 VanGuard precolumn (2.1 mm × 5 mm, 1.6 μm). The column temperature was set at 40 °C, and the autosampler was maintained at 4 °C. The mobile phase was composed of 0.1% formic acid in ultrapure water (phase A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (phase B). A modified gradient elution program was optimized for target flavonoids: 0–5 min, 5% B; 5–8 min, linear gradient from 5% to 30% B; 8–12 min, linear gradient from 30% to 95% B; 12–14 min, 95% B; 14–14.1 min, linear gradient from 95% to 5% B; 14.1–18 min, 5% B. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL to ensure high sensitivity for target analytes.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted on a Thermo Fisher Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 120 Mass Spectrometer coupled with a HESI-II ion source, running in negative ESI mode (optimized for flavonoids, which are prone to form negative ions). The scan m/z range was optimized to 150–600 m/z (suitable for the molecular weight range of most flavonoids), with a resolution of 30,000 at m/z 200 to balance sensitivity and quantitative accuracy. Parallel reaction monitoring (PRM) mode was employed for targeted quantification: specific precursor ions of target flavonoids were selected by the quadrupole, and their characteristic fragment ions were monitored in the Orbitrap analyzer. The normalized collision energy (NCE) was individually optimized for each target flavonoid (typically 28–40 eV, suitable for the fragmentation of flavonoid structures) to maximize fragment ion intensity. The spray voltage was 2.5 kV (negative mode), capillary temperature was 300 °C, sheath gas flow rate was 40 arb, and auxiliary gas flow rate was 8 arb.</p>	<p>Data processing was performed using triple quadrupole mass spectrometry quantitative analysis software; external standard curves were established for each target flavonoid, and peak area integration was conducted to achieve absolute quantification.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was confirmed by comparing the retention time and characteristic ion pairs of samples with those of standard reference substances, ensuring high specificity and accuracy of targeted detection.</p>

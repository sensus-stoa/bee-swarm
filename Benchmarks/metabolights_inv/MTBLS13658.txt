Study Title	Platycodin D attenuates alcohol-associated fatty liver disease by suppressing TLR4–NF-κB and activating PPAR signaling
Study Description	<p>Alcohol-associated fatty liver disease (AFLD) is driven by ethanol-induced metabolic dysregulation and hepatic inflammation, with gut dysbiosis and endotoxin-triggered innate immune activation implicated in disease progression. Platycodin D (PD), a triterpenoid saponin from Platycodon grandiflorum, has been reported to have antioxidant and immunomodulatory activities, but its efficacy and mechanisms in AFLD remain unclear. We hypothesized that PD alleviates AFLD by suppressing TLR4/NF-κB signaling, activating PPAR pathways, and remodeling gut microbiota and hepatic metabolites.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Non-targeted metabolomics was conducted to characterize metabolic alterations associated with ethanol exposure and PD intervention. Liver tissue (sample mass per replicate: 50 mg) was extracted using methanol:</p><p><br></p>	<p>acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) containing internal standards (2 mg/L). Samples were vortexed, ultrasonicated for 10 min in an ice-water bath, incubated at 20 °C for 1 h for protein precipitation, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. Supernatants were collected and dried in a vacuum concentrator, then reconstituted in acetonitrile: water (1:1, v/v), ultrasonicated for 10 min, and centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant (120 µL) was filtered through a 0.22 µm membrane and transferred to vials. Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing 10 µL aliquots from each sample.</p>	<p>UHPLC-QTOF-MS analysis was performed on a Waters UPLC Acquity I-Class PLUS system coupled to a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF using an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm). Injection volume was 1 µL; mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water, and mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile; flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. Mass spectrometry was performed using electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative ion modes.

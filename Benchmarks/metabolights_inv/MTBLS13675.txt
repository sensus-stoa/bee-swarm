Study Description	<p>Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) represents the most common chronic liver disease worldwide. Caloric restriction (CR) is a major dietary intervention known to reshape the gut microbiota; however, the implications of these microbial changes for MASLD pathophysiology are not yet fully elucidated.&nbsp;We propose a new caloric restriction intervention which ameliorates MASLD by shaping the gut microbiome that elevates 5-HIAA levels, thereby activating the hepatic PXR receptor.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All experimental mice were obtained from Experiments Center of the Air Force Medical University (Shannxi, China) and acclimatized for one week prior to experimentation. The animals were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility with no more than four mice per cage, under controlled conditions: a 12 h light/dark cycle, room temperature of 22 ± 2 °C, and humidity of 50 ± 5%. Food and tap water were provided ad libitum. Body weight and food intake were recorded weekly throughout the study. At the end of the experimental period, mice were euthanized, and tissue samples were collected, weighed, and processed for subsequent analysis. Tissues designated for morphological examination were fixed in formalin, while those intended for molecular analysis were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Blood samples were kept at 4 °C overnight, and serum was isolated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C.</p>	<p>The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water: acetonitrile = 2: 98, v/v) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile). The flow rate was maintained at 0.40 mL/min, the column temperature was set to 40 °C, and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis was performed on a UHPLC-Q Exactive system (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd.</p>	<p>The UHPLC system was coupled to a Q Exactive mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative modes. The optimized ESI parameters were as follows: ion source temperature, 400 °C; sheath gas flow rate, 40 arbitrary units (arb); auxiliary gas flow rate, 10 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF), 2800 V in negative mode and 3500 V in positive mode; normalized collision energy, stepped 20–40–60 eV for MS/MS fragmentation. Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode, scanning over a mass range of m/z 70–1050.</p>	<p>Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, and other preprocessing steps, ultimately yielding a data matrix containing information such as retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. Subsequently, feature peak search and identification were performed using the software by matching MS and MS/MS spectral information against metabolic databases. The mass error for MS was set to less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on MS/MS matching scores. The primary databases used included mainstream public databases such as http://www.hmdb.ca/ and https://metlin.scripps.edu/, as well as an in-house database.</p>

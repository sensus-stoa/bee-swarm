Study Title	Quercetin alleviates ETEC K88-induced host injury by driving the circulation and fluctuation of AHL-like quorum-sensing signaling molecules in the blood–liver–colon system
Study Description	<p>Abstract:</p><p>Background: Quercetin is a flavonoid with various probiotic properties, demonstrating anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative, anti-cancer, and anti-obesity effects in humans and animals. However, quercetin’s effect on enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli (ETEC) K88-induced host body injury mediated by bacterial quorum sensing remains insufficiently explored.</p><p>Results: Quercetin supplementation alleviated ETEC K88-induced body damage in weaned piglets, changed the microbial composition of the host intestinal tract, enriched the main probiotic Lactobacillus amylovorus, and inhibited microbial functions, such as virulence factor secretion. In addition, it influenced changes in AHL-type quorum-sensing signaling molecules in the host circulation. The signaling pathway alleviated ETEC K88-induced host intestinal barrier and inflammatory damage.</p><p>Conclusion: This study provides a theoretical framework for further understanding and exploring the use of quorum sensing as a medium to alleviate human and animal diseases caused by pathogenic bacteria and other external adverse factors.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>On the morning following completion of the challenge trial, weanling piglets were euthanized. Colonic contents were collected from piglets in each group into 2 mL cryotubes. These samples were immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for subsequent non-targeted metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Non-targeted metabolomics analysis of the colonic contents was performed by Parsono Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The test used Thermo’s high-resolution LC-MS platform to perform non-targeted metabolomics detection on the samples and employed a new generation of commercial software Compound DiscovererTM 3.3, combined with various MS map libraries, for qualitative and quantitative analysis of metabolites. Metabolite extraction: A 50 mg sample was weighed in a 2 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 500 µL pre-cooled methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine). Two steel balls were included, and the mixture was vortexed for 30 s. The sample was subsequently ground at 55 Hz for 60 s using a high-throughput tissue grinder; the procedure was repeated once. The tube was then placed in an ultrasonic cleaner for 10 min, and stored in a refrigerator at 20 ° C for 30 min. Afterward, the sample was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm filter membrane. The filtrate was added to the detection bottle, and 10–20 μL of the sample filtrate was mixed into a QC sample to evaluate the stability of the instrument and the reliability of the data.</p>	<p>For chromatographic analysis, ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 chromatographic column (100Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used. The procedure included a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min, column temperature of 40 , an automatic sampler of 8 , and an injection volume of 2 μL. Mass spectrometry: Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometry data in positive and negative ion modes under the control of the Xcalibur software (version 4.7, Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p>	<p>The analysis was conducted using the HESI source with a spray voltage of 3.5/3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325 , auxiliary gas temperature 300 , primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100–1000 m/z, AGC target set to standard, and maximum injection time (Max IT) of 100 ms. The first four ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, with a dynamic exclusion time of 8 s, secondary resolution at 15,000, HCD collision energy of 30%, AGC target set to standard, and Max IT set to auto.</p>	<p>Metabolite database search: The raw data were imported into the commercial software Compound DiscovererTM 3.3 (version 3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Peak extraction, alignment, correction, and other procedures were performed based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithms of the software.</p>	<p>The unique peak rating calculation and filter significantly reduced the interference of background and low-quality peaks. The undetected peaks in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the undetected peaks were filled with missing values based on the Fill Gaps algorithm software, and the total peak area of the sum was normalized. Metabolites were identified based on the company’s self-built library: mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/), and NIST _ 2020 _ MSMS library. The MS1 mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm, and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>

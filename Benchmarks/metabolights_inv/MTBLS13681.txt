Study Title	Variety-Specific Polyphenol Accumulation and Antioxidant Activity Differences in Apple Fruits
Study Description	<p>Oxidative stress is a key inducer of chronic diseases, and apple polyphenols exhibit significant antioxidant potential. This study systematically analyzed polyphenol accumulation characteristics and antioxidant activity of four apple cultivars (Qincui, Jiuyue Qiji, Orin, Ruixue) using a multi-level system of traditional biochemistry, cell experiments, and untargeted metabolomics. Results showed Ruixue had the highest soluble solids, soluble sugar, total phenol contents, and DPPH scavenging activity, while Jiuyue Qiji was rich in total flavonoids. Apple polyphenols alleviated D-gal-induced BV2 cell senescence, with Ruixue and Jiuyue Qiji showing the strongest protective effects. Metabolomics identified 207 polyphenols, and 87 core active polyphenols significantly correlated with antioxidant activity were screened. Ruixue exhibited distinct polyphenol enrichment. This study clarifies cultivar-specific polyphenol accumulation and core active components, providing theoretical basis for high-antioxidant apple breeding and functional product development.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fruit pulp tissue was ground into powder in liquid nitrogen.</p><p>Sample differences: Significant variations exist in polyphenol accumulation, nutritional components (soluble solids, soluble sugar), and antioxidant activity among the four apple cultivars. Ruixue has the highest total phenol content and DPPH scavenging activity; Jiuyue Qiji is richest in total flavonoids; Qincui has the highest ascorbic acid content; Orin shows moderate performance in most indicators.</p><p>Controlled variables: Uniform cultivation environment (standardized planting base in Ludian, Zhaotong, with the same soil type and climatic conditions), consistent tree age (6-year-old), identical fruit ripeness (stable peel color and qualified soluble solid content), and uniform experimental treatment methods (e.g., polyphenol extraction and metabolomic detection conditions). The only variable is the genetic background of the apple cultivars.</p>	<p> The four tested apple cultivars (QC, JYQJ, OR,&nbsp;RX) were all collected from Ludian County, Zhaotong City, Yunnan Province (27°21′N, 103°42′E). The region has an average annual temperature of 13.6℃, an annual precipitation of 738.2 mm, and the soil type is yellow soil. For each cultivar, 3 6-year-old plants with consistent growth potential and no pests or diseases were selected. From each plant, 30 fruits with consistent maturity (stable peel color and soluble solid content meeting the cultivar maturity standard) were collected from the east, south, west, and north directions, and 4 biological replicates were set for each cultivar. Within 2 hours after harvest, the fruits were brought back to the laboratory, and the fruit stems and peels were immediately removed. The pulp tissue was cut into 2 cm³ pieces, quick-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in an ultra-low temperature refrigerator at -80℃ for subsequent traditional biochemical index analysis and metabolomic detection.</p>	<p>Chromatography was performed on a Thermo Fisher Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with a Hypersil Gold C18 column (100×2.1 mm, 1.9 μm). The column temperature was 40℃, and the flow rate was 0.2 mL/min; mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in ultrapure water, and mobile phase B was methanol (LC-MS grade). Gradient elution was carried out as follows: 2% B from 0 to 1.5 min, linearly increased to 85% B from 1.5 to 3 min, maintained at 100% B from 3 to 10 min, and returned to 2% B from 10.1 to 12 min for equilibration; the injection volume was 2 μL, and all reagents were filtered through a 0.22 μm filter membrane.</p>	<p> Mass spectrometry was performed on a Thermo Fisher Q Exactive™ HF mass spectrometer with ESI source for simultaneous positive and negative ion scanning (m/z 100-1500); the spray voltage was 3.5 kV, the sheath gas flow rate was 35 psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10 L/min, the ion transfer tube temperature was 320℃, the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 350℃, and the secondary scanning was in data-dependent mode.</p>	<p> The raw data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard, and peak extraction, quantification, and alignment were performed using XCMS, with the first sample used for peak area calibration; metabolites were identified by combining 10 ppm mass deviation, adduct ion information, and secondary spectrum database comparison. After removing background ions with blank samples, the relative peak area was obtained by normalization according to the formula (sample original quantitative value/(sum of sample metabolite quantification/sum of first sample metabolite quantification)). Metabolites were annotated using KEGG, HMDB, and LIPIDMaps databases. After total normalization, removing variables with RSD&gt;30% in QC samples, and log10 transformation, PCA and OPLS-DA analyses were performed using the R package 'ropls' (v1.6.2). Differential metabolites were screened based on VIP&gt;1 (OPLS-DA) and p&lt;0.05 (Student’s t-test), and finally, polyphenolic compounds (phenolic acids, flavonoids, etc.) were selected and compounds with CV&gt;30% were excluded for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>Statistical analysis and plotting were performed using R 4.5.2 software: total metabolic heatmaps and flavonoid HCA heatmaps were plotted using ggplot2 and ComplexHeatmap packages (metabolite contents were standardized by z-score); PCA was performed using the factoextra package and loading plots and score plots were drawn; Venn diagrams were drawn using Venny 2.1; differential flavonoid compounds were screened with the criteria of |log2FC|≥1 and p&lt;0.05 (independent sample t-test); Pearson correlation analysis was performed using the corrplot package (significant correlation criteria: p&lt;0.05 for significant, p&lt;0.01 for extremely significant). All indicators were set with 4 technical replicates, and the results were expressed as mean ± SD.</p>

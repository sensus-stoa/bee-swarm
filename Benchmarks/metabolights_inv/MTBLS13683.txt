Study Title	Accumulation Characteristics of Flavonoids and Contribution to Antioxidant Capacity in Wild and Cultivated Kiwifruits
Study Description	<p>This study aimed to clarify flavonoids accumulation characteristics and their contribution to antioxidant capacity in wild type (WT) kiwifruit (Actinidia deliciosa) and three cultivated varieties (Xuxiang, XX; Yazui, YZ; Hongyang, HY). Integrating untargeted metabolomics and traditional biochemical analysis, we compared morphological, biochemical, and metabolic differences, analyzed flavonoid profiles, and screened core flavonoids linked to antioxidant activity. Results showed cultivated varieties exhibited superior fruit shape, sweetness (TSS/SS), total phenols, flavonoids, and DPPH scavenging activity (HY highest). Flavonoid metabolic profiles differed significantly: WT enriched stress-resistant flavonoids (e.g., Leucocyanidin), while cultivated varieties showed specialized accumulation (e.g., flavonol glycosides in HY). Fourteen core flavonoids (e.g., Kuwanon D, Hyperoside) strongly correlated with antioxidant activity were identified. This study reveals domestication’s impact on kiwifruit metabolism, providing theoretical support for directed breeding of high-nutrition varieties and wild germplasm utilization.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fruit pulp tissue was ground into powder in liquid nitrogen.</p><p>Sample differences: WT: Wild green-fleshed kiwifruit, irregular fruit shape, hard epidermal trichomes, low sweetness and antioxidant-related metabolite content.</p><p>XX: Cultivated green-fleshed kiwifruit, regular long cylindrical shape, soft down, moderate sweetness and flavonoid content.</p><p>YZ: Cultivated yellow-fleshed kiwifruit, regular elliptical shape, soft down, highest sweetness (TSS/SS), moderate flavonoid content.</p><p>HY: Cultivated red-core yellow-fleshed kiwifruit, regular short cylindrical shape, soft down, highest total phenol, total flavonoid content, and DPPH scavenging activity.</p>	<p> The test materials included wild kiwifruit (WT) and three main cultivated varieties (XX, YZ, HY), which were collected from Daguan County, Zhaotong City, Yunnan Province (27°36′N, 103°43′E). Plants with consistent growth environment, robust tree vigor, and no pests or diseases were selected. For each variety, 4 biological replicates were set, and 30 fruits with consistent maturity were collected from the middle of the crown of each plant. Fruits were transported back to the laboratory within 2 hours after harvesting. After removing the peel, the pulp tissue was cut into 1 cm³ pieces, quick-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in an ultra-low temperature refrigerator at -80 °C for subsequent traditional biochemical index analysis and metabolome detection.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>	<p>Chromatographic detection was performed using a Thermo Fisher Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with a Hypersil Gold C18 column (100×2.1 mm, 1.9 μm). The column temperature was 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.2 mL/min. Mobile phase A was ultrapure water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was methanol (LC-MS Grade). The gradient elution program was as follows: 2% B was maintained for 0~1.5 min, linearly increased to 85% B within 1.5~3 min, increased to 100% B within 3~10 min and maintained until 10.1 min, then decreased back to 2% B within 10.1~12 min for equilibration. The injection volume was 2 μL, and all reagents were filtered through a 0.22 μm filter membrane.</p>	<p> Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Thermo Fisher Q Exactive™ HF mass spectrometer with ESI source scanning in both positive and negative ion modes, and the scanning range was m/z 100-1500. The spray voltage was 3.5 kV, the sheath gas flow rate was 35 psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10 L/min, the ion transfer tube temperature was 320 °C, the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 350 °C, and the secondary scanning adopted the data-dependent acquisition mode.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>	<p>Statistical analysis and graphing were performed using R 4.5.2 software: the ggplot2 package and ComplexHeatmap package were used to visualize the total metabolic heatmap and flavonoid HCA heatmap, with metabolite contents processed by z-score standardization; the factoextra package was used for PCA to draw loading plots and score plots; Venny 2.1 software was used to draw Venn diagrams; differential flavonoid compounds were screened with the criteria of |log2 fold change (FC)|≥1 and p&lt;0.05 (independent sample t-test); Pearson correlation analysis was performed using the corrplot package, with significance levels set as p&lt;0.05 (significant) and p&lt;0.01 (extremely significant). All indicators were set with 4 technical replicates, and the results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD).</p>	<p>The raw data files were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard. Peak extraction and quantification were performed using XCMS, and peaks were aligned among different samples based on parameters such as retention time and mass-to-charge ratio. Peak area calibration was performed using the first sample to improve quantification accuracy. Then, metabolite identification was carried out by comparing with high-quality secondary spectrum databases based on set parameters including 10 ppm mass deviation and adduct ions. Subsequently, background ions were removed using blank samples, and the original quantitative results were standardized according to the formula: sample original quantitative value/(sum of sample metabolite quantitative values/sum of first sample metabolite quantitative values) to obtain relative peak areas. The identified metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html),&nbsp;HMDB database (https://hmdb.ca/metabolites),&nbsp;and LIPIDMaps database (http://www.lipidmaps.org/).&nbsp;The mass spectral peak response intensity was corrected by the sum normalization method, and variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt;30% in QC samples were eliminated, followed by log10 transformation. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) were performed using the R package 'ropls' (v1.6.2). Significantly differential metabolites were screened based on the Variable Importance in Projection (VIP&gt;1) from the OPLS-DA model and Student’s t-test results (<em>p</em>&lt;0.05). According to the chemical structure and KEGG pathway annotation results, compounds with CV&gt;30% were eliminated, and flavonoid compounds were selected for subsequent analysis.</p>

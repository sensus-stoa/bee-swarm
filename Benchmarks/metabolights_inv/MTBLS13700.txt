Study Description	<p>Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is a complex cardiometabolic disorder closely associated with systemic metabolic dysregulation. In this study, plasma samples were collected from C57BL/6J mice subjected to a high-fat diet–induced HFpEF model, with or without intervention, and analyzed using untargeted metabolomics to characterize disease-associated metabolic alterations. This dataset aims to provide insights into plasma metabolic signatures and mechanisms underlying HFpEF.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Plasma samples were collected from C57BL/6J mice subjected to control or high-fat diet–induced heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) conditions. Whole blood was collected at the experimental endpoint, and plasma was obtained by centrifugation according to standard procedures. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Plasma metabolites were extracted using methanol-based protein precipitation with internal standards. Briefly, 50 μL plasma was mixed with extraction solvent containing internal standards, vortexed thoroughly, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected, dried or directly reconstituted depending on polarity, and subjected to LC–MS/MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of all samples.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) systems equipped with reversed-phase and HILIC columns. For reversed-phase analysis, a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) was used. For polar metabolites, a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH HILIC column (1.0 × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) was employed. Mobile phases consisted of water and organic solvents with formic acid or ammonium salts, using gradient elution at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. Column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and injection volumes ranged from 2 to 5 μL depending on acquisition mode.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using AB SCIEX TripleTOF 6600 and QTRAP 6500 mass spectrometers equipped with electrospray ionization (ESI) sources operating in both positive and negative ion modes. Typical source parameters included ion spray voltages of 5000 V (positive) and −4000 V (negative), source temperature of 450–550 °C, and optimized gas settings. Data were acquired using full scan and MS/MS modes to enable metabolite identification and quantification.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were processed using Analyst and MultiQuant software. Peak detection, integration, and correction were performed, followed by normalization and quality control filtering. Metabolites with coefficient of variation (CV) ≤ 0.3 in QC samples were retained for downstream analysis. Multivariate statistical analyses including PCA and OPLS-DA were conducted using appropriate statistical software.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed based on accurate mass, retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation patterns by matching against an in-house metabolite database (MWDB) and public databases including HMDB, KEGG, and METLIN. Metabolites were annotated using a combination of spectral matching and database-assisted identification strategies.</p>

Study Title	Response Mechanisms of Hepatopancreatic Metabolism and Gut Microbiota in Chinese Mitten Crab (Eriocheir sinensis) Under Chronic High-pH Stress
Study Description	<p>This study subjected Eriocheir sinensis to chronic high-pH stress (control: pH=8; experimental groups: pH=9 and pH=10) and integrated hepatopancreas transcriptomic/metabolomic analyses with intestinal microbiota 16S rRNA sequencing to explore its adaptive mechanisms. The results revealed distinct adaptive strategies across stress phases: under short-term (15 days) high-pH stress, E. sinensis maintained somatic homeostasis mainly via rapid regulation of carbohydrate, lipid, protein metabolism, and energy allocation. In contrast, long-term (30 days) stress drove a strategic shift toward accelerated lipid catabolism, enhanced energy metabolism, and activated immune-related pathways. Additionally, high-pH stress significantly altered the intestinal microbiota community structure, marked by increased abundances of Proteobacteria and Pseudomonas, indicating a potential risk of microbial dysbiosis. Collectively, these findings elucidate the physiological adaptation mechanisms of E. sinensis to high-pH environments and lay a theoretical foundation for improving the sustainability of its aquaculture under extreme pH conditions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples were collected at 15 days post-challenge (15 dpc) and 30 days post-challenge (30 dpc) of pH stress treatment. The treatment groups were abbreviated as follows: tne pH=8 group at 15 dpc (A8), pH=9 group at 15 dpc (A9), pH=10 group at 15 dpc (A10); pH=8 group at 30 dpc (B8), pH=9 group at 30 dpc (B9), and pH=10 group at 30 dpc (B10). During sampling, juvenile crabs were randomly selected from each replicate of the pH gradient treatment groups. After anesthesia on ice, the crabs were dissected, and their hepatopancreas and intestinal tissues were collected into 2 mL cryopreservation tubes, which were immediately placed in liquid nitrogen. Specifically, tissues from 4 individuals per group were used for transcriptomic analysis, 6 individuals for metabolomic analysis, and 4 individuals for intestinal microbiota analysis. All samples were analyzed as independent biological replicates without pooling. Different individuals were used for each assay due to platform requirements, but all originated from the same experimental batch under identical conditions.Tissues from the remaining individuals were quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and then transferred to a -80℃ refrigerator for long-term storage.</p>	<p>Approximately 30 ± 5 mg of hepatopancreas tissue was weighed and processed with grinding beads and an extraction solvent for 6 minutes under cryogenic conditions. The samples were then subjected to low-temperature ultrasonication at 5 °C and 40 kHz for 30 minutes, incubated at –20 °C for 30 minutes, and centrifuged at 13,000 g at 4 °C for 15 minutes. The supernatant was collected for analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites were profiled using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with Triple Time-of-Flight mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Triple TOF) system. Chromatographic separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column with mobile phase A (95% water + 5% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid) and mobile phase B (47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water, 0.1% formic acid) at a flow rate of 0.40 mL/min. The injection volume was 10 μL, and the column temperature was 45 °C. </p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was conducted with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source in both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>Raw data were imported into Progenesis QI v3.0 software and matched against standard and in-house databases.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass accuracy was controlled within 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified by combining MS and MS/MS information. Data were normalized and subjected to quality control. Variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% were removed, and the remaining data were log-transformed to correct for heteroscedasticity.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS13730
Study Title	Natural photosynthetic system for restoring homeostasis of animal organelle interaction network
Study Description	<p>Disruption of organelle interactions due to metabolic stress is a crucial factor in the pathological processes of many degenerative diseases. Current treatments usually focus on individual organelles while ignoring the role of organelle interactions in cell damage caused by cascade reactions. There is an urgent need to develop a systematic and effective method to restore homeostasis of the organelle interaction network. Compared with animal cells, the participation of chloroplasts enables plant cells to show defensive adaptation under stress. Therefore, delivering plant-derived photosynthetic systems into animal cells may help to establish a more stable organelle interaction network. In this study, plant-derived photosynthetic systems effectively restored homeostasis of the interaction network of animal organelles. Specifically, plant-derived nanothylakoid units provided energy to animal cells, regulated intracellular Ca2+&nbsp;homeostasis, increased endoplasmic reticulum (ER) lipid unsaturation and global membrane fluidity, reduced abnormal contact between mitochondria and ER, and&nbsp;alleviated mitochondrial dysfunction. By combining implantable light-emitting diodes with wireless charging, we expanded photosynthesis therapy, enabling smarter and more effective treatments for deeper tissues. This study provides a proof-of-concept for disease treatment based on the regulation of organelle interaction networks by natural photosynthetic systems and establishes a novel therapeutic approach for treating deep tissues.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>On September 20, 2025, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) was isolated from rat nucleus pulposus cells using an endoplasmic reticulum isolation kit (Sigma). Samples were stored at -80°C before transport for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>Lipid extraction was adapted and modified from previous publication (J Lipid Res. 2008 May;49(5):1137–1146.). Briefly, 400 μl of cold 80% MeOH (−80℃) and 20 μl of Lipidomics ISTD mix (SPH 17:1, Sphingosine-1-Phosphate d17:1, CAR 16:0-d3, LPC 13:0, LPE 13:0, Cer d18:1/12:0, SM d18:1/12:0, CE 9:0, Cholesterol-d7, DG 15:0-15:0, TG 17:0-17:0-17:0, PC 13:0-13:0, PE 17:0-17:0, 14:0 CL) were added to each plate. ER samples were transferred to a 10 ml glass Pyrex tube. Extraction was performed with 960μL of MTBE and vortexed for 1min. The phase separation was induced by adding 240μl water. After centrifugation at 1000 x g for 10min, the upper phase was moved to a clean glass Pyrex tube and evaporated to dryness under nitrogen. The residue was resuspended in 100 μl of MTBE/MeOH (1:3 vol ratio). Three microliter injections were used for LC-MS analysis with lipidomics method.</p>	<p>Lipids were separated using an Accucore C18 HPLC column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 2.6 μm particle size) (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA) on a Vanquish Flex UHPLC equipped with a refrigerated autosampler (10 ℃), and the column heater was set to 50 ℃. Solvent A was 1/1 acetonitrile/water with 10 mM ammonium formate. Solvent B was of 10/88/2 acetonitrile/isopropanol/water with 10 mM ammonium formate. Flow rate was 0.4 ml min−1. Flow gradient conditions were as follows: 0~0.5min, 20% B;4 min, 70% B; 10 min, 99% B;12 min, 99 %B; 12.1 min, 20% B; 15min, 20% B.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using a TSQ Altis Plus mass spectrometer operated in both positive and negative ion mode with dynamic scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM). The following mass spectrometer conditions were used: spray voltage, 3500V (positive), 3000V (negative); sheath gas, 50; aux gas, 10; sweep gas, 1; ion transfer tube temp, 325 ℃; vaporizer temp, 325 ℃.</p>	<p>Quantification of lipid species was determined by comparison to the relevant internal standard using Skyline 22.2.0527.</p>	<p>Based on the LIPID MAPS online lipid database (https://www.lipidmaps.org/) and the LION (Lipid Ontology) database (http://lipidontology.com/), all detected lipids were annotated, functionally defined, and classified.</p>

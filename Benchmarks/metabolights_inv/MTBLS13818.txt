Study Description	<p>This study not only identified an efficient host-homologous antioxidant probiotic, PGM326, but also systematically elucidated its mechanism of exerting antioxidant function by synthesizing BA to promote OXPHOS in intestinal epithelial cells, thereby enhancing cell differentiation. This provides a systematic solution for developing host-homologous, mechanism-defined antioxidant microecological feed additives.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Throughout the entire analytical process, samples were maintained at 4 °C in the autosampler. Separation was performed on a SHIMADZU-LC30 ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The injection volume was 6 µL, the column temperature was set at 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was applied as follows: 0–2 min, 0% B; 2–6 min, 0% to 48% B; 6–10 min, 48% to 100% B; 10–12 min, 100% B; 12–12.1 min, 100% to 0% B; and 12.1–15 min, 0% B.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using the MSDIAL software for peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction.</p>	<p>The samples were thawed at 4 °C and vortexed thoroughly. An aliquot of 600 μL from each sample was transferred to a microcentrifuge tube and dried under vacuum in a high-speed centrifugal concentrator. Then, 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol-water (4:1, v/v) was added, followed by mixing and sonication in an ice bath for 20 min. After standing at -20 °C for 1 h, the mixture was centrifuged at 16,000 × g and 4 °C for 20 min. The resulting supernatant was collected and concentrated to dryness again under vacuum. For LC–MS analysis, the residue was reconstituted in 100 μL of pre-chilled methanol-water (1:1, v/v), centrifuged at 20,000 × g and 4 °C for 15 min, and an appropriate volume of the supernatant was injected for analysis.</p>	<p>Each sample was analyzed in both positive (+) and negative (-) ion modes using electrospray ionization (ESI). After UPLC separation, mass spectrometry was performed on a Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with a HESI ion source. The ionization parameters were set as follows: spray voltage: 3.8 kV (+) and 3.2 kV (-); capillary temperature: 320 °C; sheath gas: 30 arb; aux gas: 5 arb; probe heater temperature: 350 °C; S-lens RF level: 50. Mass spectrometry data were acquired under the following settings: acquisition time: 15 min; full scan range: m/z 75–1050; MS1 resolution: 70,000 at m/z 200; AGC target: 3e6; maximum ion injection time (IT) for MS1: 100 ms. For MS/MS analysis, the top 10 most intense precursor ions from each full scan were selected for fragmentation. The MS2 settings were: resolution: 17,500 at m/z 200; AGC target: 1e5; maximum IT: 50 ms; activation type: HCD; isolation window: 2 m/z; normalized collision energy (stepped): 20, 30, 40.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching exact mass (mass tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS spectra (mass tolerance &lt; 0.01 Da) against public databases including HMDB, MassBank, and GNPS, as well as an in-house Bepu Metabolite Library (BP-DB).</p>	<p>Strain PGM326, a porcine-derived antioxidant probiotic, was purified and cultured for more than three generations. The bacterial cells were then centrifuged at 4 °C (10000 × g, 5 min) to collect the strain PGM326-CFS (cell-free supernatant), which was subsequently sent to BiotechBio Corporation for metabolomic analysis.</p><p><br></p>

Study Title	Dysregulated high-density lipoprotein and low-density lipoprotein subfractions increase metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease risk: A study of patients across body mass index categories
Study Description	<p><strong>Background: </strong>To investigate biomarker differences among patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) across body mass index (BMI) types, we analyzed clinical data from 2,013 subjects and serum samples from 402 patients. The clinical characteristics and lipoprotein subclass profiles were evaluated.</p><p><strong>Methods:</strong> Participants were grouped based on BMI into overweight MASLD (113 participants), overweight controls (107 participants), lean MASLD (83 participants), and lean controls (99 participants). Serum samples from each group underwent nuclear magnetic resonance-based metabolomic analyses, and clinical and omics data were compared between the lean and overweight MASLD groups and paired control cohorts.</p><p><strong>Results:</strong> Our study demonstrated distinct omics characteristics for lean MASLD compared with their overweight equivalents. Metabolomic analysis of the serum from the four groups identified six lipoprotein subclasses with significant diagnostic accuracy (area under the curve (AUC) &gt; 0.7), unique to lean individuals with MASLD. In contrast, overweight patients with MASLD had 13 unique lipoprotein subclasses that exhibited a high diagnostic value. These lipoproteins correlate with clinical parameters, such as uric acid, urea, creatinine, eosinophils, blood glucose, and alanine aminotransferase.</p><p><strong>Conclusion: </strong>Lean and overweight patients with MASLD display unique lipoprotein omics characteristics in an Asian population, primarily involving high-density lipoprotein (HDL) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) subcomponents, suggesting their potential as effective biomarkers.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Raw Free Induction Decay (FID) signals were processed using the instrument's native Topspin 4.1.1 software (Bruker Biospin GmbH). Fourier Transformation was applied to convert the time-domain FIDs into frequency-domain spectra.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Spectral Preprocessing</strong></p><p>The following steps were performed within the same software: 1.Phase Correction:&nbsp;All spectra were manually or automatically phase-corrected to achieve symmetrical peak shapes. 2.Baseline Correction:&nbsp;Baseline correction was applied using polynomial fitting or other algorithms to remove low-frequency noise interference. 3.Chemical Shift Referencing:&nbsp;All spectra were referenced by setting the TMSP-d4 internal standard signal to 0.0 ppm. 4.Integration and Quantification:&nbsp;The concentrations of 110 lipoprotein subfractions and 31 small-molecule metabolites were quantified using the dedicated quantification algorithm/database included in the Bruker IVDr Standard Operating Procedure. This process involved automated integration of signals in specific chemical shift regions of the spectra. The resulting concentration data were exported in tabular format for subsequent statistical analysis.</p>	<p>This study employed a direct injection method for NMR analysis, requiring no complex metabolite extraction. Prior to analysis, frozen serum samples were thawed slowly on ice.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Quality Control – 5 mm NMR Tubes</strong></p><p>Quality Control (QC) ensures spectral quality, as well as consistent, comparable, and accurate results within specified limits of precision. Based on good QC performance, the NMR data generated can be correctly quantified and analyzed for further interpretation and decision-making. QC was implemented on a daily basis. The QC procedure consisted of the following steps - <strong>MeOD</strong>: Calibrate the temperature at 310 K and activate temperature correction. Check if the calibrated temperature is still valid. The actual temperature difference of the calibrated samples must not exceed ± 0.1 K. <strong>Sucrose</strong>: At 310 K: Perform O1 calibration, basic shim, water suppression test, and stability test. <strong>QuantRefC</strong>: At 310 K: Perform QuantRefC calibration for plasma and H2O + D2O at 310 K. QC was conducted at SampleJet positions 1, 2, and 3. The dates in the QC form were updated, and the standard samples were submitted for testing. Sample preparation proceeded only after QC passed.</p>	<p><strong>R</strong><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>eference Spectral Library Matching</strong></p><p>Metabolite identification was primarily based on the following approach.&nbsp;Identification of metabolites from NMR spectra relied on matching against a&nbsp;built-in reference spectral library. This study strictly adhered to the&nbsp;Bruker IVDr (In Vitro Diagnostics Research) Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), which incorporates a validated library containing NMR signature profiles for a large number of known metabolites and lipoprotein subfractions.</p>	<p><strong>Plasma/Serum Sample Preparation</strong></p><p>Carefully thaw the samples, which have been stored at -80 °C, at room temperature (30 minutes for ≤ 2 mL volume sample). Pipette 400 µL of Bruker plasma buffer into a Cryovial or Eppendorf container (of 1.5 mL or 2 mL volume). Add 400 µL of plasma/serum. Shake the mixture gently for 1 minute (do not use the Vortex mixer). Transfer 600 µL of well mixed sample into a 5 mm 7” NMR tube or a 5 mm&nbsp;SampleJet rack tube.</p>	<p>Serum samples were collected on a <strong>600 MHz Avance NEO NMR spectrometer</strong> (Bruker Biospin GmbH, Rheinstetten, Germany) equipped with a BBI probe and a Samplejet autosampler with the temperature set at 4 °C. Prior to sample collection, the instrument was first subjected to quality control experiments (B.I. BioBank QC) in order to ensure consistent spectral quality. For each sample, solvent presaturation 1D 1H experiments were performed at 310 K using the NOESY pulse sequence, 32 scans were performed, totaling 98,304 data points covering a spectral width of 17,857 Hz. Corrections for baseline and phase were performed in Topspin 4.1.1 after data acquisition was completed.</p>	<p>All participants were recruited from Putuo Hospital, Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Shanghai, China). Samples were collected between March 2019 and June 2024. All blood samples were collected in the&nbsp;morning after a fasting period of at least 12 h.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Handling and Storage Procedures:</strong></p><p>1.Venous blood was collected into coagulation-promoting tubes. 2.Tubes were left at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow complete clotting. 3.Serum was separated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 °C. 4.The supernatant serum was immediately aliquoted into sterile cryovials. 5.Aliquoted serum samples were stored at -80 °C for long-term preservation until metabolomic analysis. 6.To ensure analysis quality, all samples underwent only&nbsp;one freeze-thaw cycle&nbsp;prior to analysis.</p>

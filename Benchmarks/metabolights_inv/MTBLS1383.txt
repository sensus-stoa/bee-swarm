Study Identifier	MTBLS1383
Study Title	Endoplasmic reticulum-derived bodies enable a single-cell chemical defense in Brassicaceae plants
Study Description	Brassicaceae plants have a dual-cell type of chemical defense against herbivory. Here we show a novel single-cell defense involving endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-derived organelles (ER bodies) and the vacuoles. We identify various glucosinolates as endogenous substrates of the ER-body β-glucosidases BGLU23 and BGLU21. Woodlice strongly prefer to eat seedlings of bglu23 bglu21 or a glucosinolate-deficient mutant over wild-type seedlings, confirming that the β-glucosidases have a role in chemical defense: production of toxic compounds upon organellar damage. Deficiency of the Brassicaceae-specific protein NAI2 prevents ER-body formation, which results in a loss of BGLU23 and a loss of resistance to woodlice. Hence, NAI2 that interacts with BGLU23 is essential for sequestering BGLU23 in ER bodies and preventing its degradation. Artificial expression of NAI2 and BGLU23 in non-Brassicaceae plants results in the formation of ER bodies, indicating that acquisition of NAI2 by Brassicaceae plants is a key step in developing their single-cell defense system.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Seeds of Arabidopsis thaliana wild type (Col-0) and 'bglu23-1 bglu21-1' mutant were surface sterilized by 70% (v/v) ethanol, washed with absolute ethanol, and then dried with a vacuum. Seeds were sown in an area (1 cm^2) on vermiculite covered with nylon mesh sheet. Seedlings were germinated at 22 °C under 18 h light/6 h dark for 7 days. Aerial parts of seedlings were harvested and then homogenized with liquid nitrogen. The seedling homogenates were analyzed before and after incubation at 26 ˚C for 30 min to allow any β-glucosidases present to react with their substrates.</p>	<p>The experiment was outsourced to Kazusa DNA Research Institute (LC-MS Basic Analysis, <a href='http://www.biosupport.kazusa.or.jp/sub_center3/index.php/lcms-basic/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.biosupport.kazusa.or.jp/sub_center3/index.php/lcms-basic/</a>).</p><p><br></p><p>Briefly, between 100-150 mg of sample was place in 2 ml plastic tubes. An extraction solution [0.1% (v/v) formic acid and 80% (v/v) MeOH] of 500 µl to 100 mg of sample was added. After a zirconium bead was added, metabolites were extracted using a TissueLyser (25 freq, 2 min) followed by sonification (2 min). Debris was spun down by 15000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and then filtered. Next was spin column conditioning with the following conditions: i) 100 µl MeOH, 5000 x g for 2 min at 4 °C; ii) 100 µl water, 5000 x g for 2 min at 4 °C; and iii) 200 µl extraction solution, 5000 x g for 2 min at 4 °C. The resultant extract was filtered theough a PTEE membrane (pore size 0.2 µm). The samples were diluted x 10 or x 100 with extraction solution (subjected as 'lipid removed' samples).</p>	<p>Metabolite separation was carried out on a Agilent 1200 series instrument coupled to a Thermo Scientific LTQ Orbitrap XL mass spectrometer. 5 µl of sample was injected into a TSKgel ODS-100V 100 Å (5 µm, 3 mm x 50 mm; Tosoh Bioscience) column with a TSKgel ODS-100V Guard Cartridge (5 µm, 2 mm x 10 mm; Tosoh Bioscience) (Part no. 21841) column at 40 °C. Buffer A/B: 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in water/0.1% (v/v) formic acid in acetonitrile. The flow rate was 0.4 ml/min and the temperature gradient pattern (time, B％) was as follows: 0 min, 3%; 15 min, 97%; 20 min: 97%; 20.1 min, 3%; 25.0 min, 3%.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were collected on a Thermo Scientific LTQ Orbitrap XL mass spectrometer fitted with a HESIII ionisation source in negative-ion mode. Scan range was 100 - 1500 m/z at a resolution of 60,000 amu in centroid mode with the Orbitrap detector. Heater Temp: 400 °C; Nitrogen Sheeth Gas Flow rate: 50; Aux Gas Flow rate: 10; Spray Voltage: 3.5 kV; Capillary Temp: 300 °C; Spray position (x,y,z): 0,1,C; Lock mass on/off: on; Lock mass (m/z): 391.284286. MS/MS data was collected with the Ion Trap detector with a Coliision Energy of 35%.</p>	<p>The obtained raw data was statistically analyzed with R software to find out the differentially accumulated chemicals in each sample. The MS intensities were transformed to log2 values.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified with PowerGet<strong>[1]</strong>. Glucosinolates were also manually searched with the signature of MSMS spectrum in m/z = 259<strong>[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Sakurai N, Ara T, Enomoto M, Motegi T, Morishita Y, Kurabayashi A, Iijima Y, Ogata Y, Nakajima D, Suzuki H, Shibata D.

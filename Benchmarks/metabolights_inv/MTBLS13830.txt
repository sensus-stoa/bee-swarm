Study Title	Metabolomic profiling of Sophora flavescens under different tissue types using LC–MS-based untargeted metabolomics
Study Description	<p>Sophora flavescens (Kushen) is a traditional medicinal plant widely used in East Asia for its anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antitumor properties. Its pharmacological activities are mainly attributed to diverse secondary metabolites, including alkaloids, flavonoids, and phenolic compounds. However, the metabolic differences between distinct tissues remain incompletely characterized.</p><p>In this study, an untargeted metabolomics approach based on liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) was employed to comprehensively profile the metabolic composition of Sophora flavescens. Root and stem tissues were collected and analyzed to investigate tissue-specific metabolic variations. Metabolite extraction was performed using organic solvent-based protocols, followed by high-resolution LC–MS analysis in both positive and negative ionization modes.</p><p>Multivariate statistical analyses, including principal component analysis (PCA) and differential abundance analysis, were applied to evaluate metabolic differences between tissues. Differentially accumulated metabolites were further annotated and mapped to KEGG pathways to elucidate the biological processes and metabolic pathways underlying tissue-specific metabolite distribution.</p><p>This dataset provides a valuable resource for understanding the metabolic basis of bioactive compound accumulation in Sophora flavescens and supports further studies on medicinal plant metabolomics, functional metabolism, and natural product discovery.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a reverse-phase liquid chromatography system equipped with a C18 column. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (water with 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid). A gradient elution program was applied at a constant flow rate. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 2–5 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data files were converted into open formats and processed using standard metabolomics data processing pipelines. Peak detection, retention time alignment, and peak normalization were performed to generate a data matrix of mass features and corresponding intensities. Quality control samples were used to assess analytical stability and data reproducibility.</p>	<p>Approximately 100 mg of frozen plant tissue was ground into a fine powder under liquid nitrogen. Metabolites were extracted using cold methanol–water solution (70:30, v/v). Samples were vortexed thoroughly, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 30 min, and incubated at −20 °C for 1 h to enhance extraction efficiency. After centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected for LC–MS analysis. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal aliquots from each extract.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a high-resolution mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ion modes. Data were acquired over an m/z range of 50–1,500. Source parameters were optimized to ensure stable ionization and mass accuracy. Full-scan MS data were collected for untargeted metabolomic profiling.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed by comparing accurate mass, retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation patterns against public metabolite databases, including HMDB, KEGG, and MassBank. Putative identifications were assigned according to established metabolomics reporting standards.</p>	<p>Five-year-old Sophora flavescens plants were cultivated under standard field conditions. Stem tissues were collected from approximately 1 cm above the root–shoot junction, while root tissues were sampled from the main taproot at approximately 3 cm below the soil surface. All samples were harvested at the same developmental stage, immediately rinsed with distilled water to remove surface contaminants, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until metabolite extraction. Three independent biological replicates were collected for each tissue type.</p><p><br></p>

Study Title	Effects and mechanisms of blueberry extract on improving age-related sperm motility in mice by alleviating inflammatory responses
Study Description	<p>Aging leads to progressive decline in male fertility, characterized by structural degeneration of the seminiferous epithelium and oxidative imbalance. Objective: This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Blueberry Extract (BBE) in ameliorating reproductive aging and to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms involving cell fate determination. Methods: Aging male ICR mice (8 months old) were treated with varying doses of BBE. Testicular histology, sperm quality, and expression of key germ cell markers were analyzed. Results: BBE administration significantly alleviated body weight loss, reduced seminiferous tubule vacuolization, and improved sperm motility parameters in aging mice. Immunofluorescence and Western Blot analyses revealed that BBE successfully reconstituted the germline population, as evidenced by the upregulation of DDX4. Notably, a dose-dependent divergence in cellular fate was observed: high-dose BBE significantly promoted the self-renewal of PLZF+ spermatogonial stem cells (SSCs), whereas low/medium doses preferentially upregulated STRA8 to drive meiotic differentiation. This cell fate remodeling was associated with a 'U-shaped' regulation of the cell cycle regulator CCND1 and enhanced SIRT1 expression. Conclusion: BBE mitigates age-related spermatogenic failure by orchestrating a delicate balance between SSC self-renewal and differentiation via the SIRT1 pathway. These results position BBE as a promising functional food ingredient for preserving male reproductive health.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>1. Column: Separation was carried out on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm).</p><p>2. Mobile Phase:</p><p>Solvent A: 0.1% Formic acid in Water.</p><p>Solvent B: Acetonitrile containing 0.1% Formic acid.</p><p>3. Gradient Elution: The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. The column temperature was 40°C, and the autosampler was 8°C. The injection volume was 2 µL. The elution gradient was as follows:</p><p>0-1 min: 5% B</p><p>1-7 min: 5% to 95% B</p><p>7-8 min: 95% B</p><p>8-8.1 min: 95% to 5% B</p><p>8.1-12 min: 5% B (Equilibration)</p>	<p>The raw data files (.raw) were imported into Compound Discoverer™ 3.3 software (version 3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA) for processing.</p><p>1. Peak Picking &amp; Alignment: The workflow utilized the software's advanced peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithms to perform peak extraction, alignment, and correction.</p><p>2. Filtering: Background and low-quality peaks were minimized using a unique peak rating calculation and filter. Peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered out.</p><p>3. Gap Filling &amp; Normalization: Missing values for undetected peaks were filled using the Fill Gaps algorithm. The data was then normalized using Sum total peak area normalization.</p><p><br></p>	<p>1. Homogenization: 50 mg of sample was weighed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. 200 μL of pre-cooled water and 2 steel beads were added. The samples were homogenized using a high-throughput tissue grinder at 55 Hz for 60 s, and this step was repeated once.</p><p>2. Extraction: 800 μL of methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) was added to the homogenate. The mixture was sonicated in an ultrasonic cleaning machine for 30 min and then incubated at -20°C for 30 min to precipitate proteins.</p><p>3. Concentration: The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min. 800 μL of the supernatant was collected and concentrated to dryness under vacuum.</p><p>4. Reconstitution: The dried residue was reconstituted in 150 μL of 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine as internal standard) and vortexed for 30 seconds.</p><p>5. Filtration: The mixture was centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min. The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm filter membrane and transferred to the test bottle for LC-MS analysis. QC samples were prepared by mixing 10-20 μL of each sample filtrate.</p>	<p>Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source, spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325°C, auxiliary gas temperature 300°C, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT 100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s, secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto.</p><p>All formal samples and QC sample were loaded into the equipment according to the above-mentioned chromatography and mass spectrometry methods. Before the formal injection, QC sample were injected 2-4 times to balance the system. During the injection process, one QC sample was injected for every 5-10 samples for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p>Databases Used:</p><p>mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/)</p><p>LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/)</p><p>HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/)</p><p>MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/)</p><p>NIST_2020_MSMS library</p><p>2. Identification Parameters:</p><p>MS1 Mass Tolerance: 15 ppm</p><p>MS2 Match Factor Threshold: 50</p>	<p>All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Agricultural University.</p><p>1. Animal Housing and Treatment: Male ICR mice (aged 9 months) were purchased from Henan Skbess Biological Technology Co., Ltd. The mice were housed under standard conditions with a 12h light/12h dark cycle and free access to water. Although the overall experiment included four groups, this metabolomics study focused on two specific groups: the Control group and the Blueberry Extract group. Mice in the treatment group received intragastric administration of blueberry extract (200 mg/kg) daily for 4 weeks, while the control group received the vehicle.</p><p>2. Sample Preparation: At the end of the 4-week treatment period, mice were fasted for 12 hours and then sacrificed. Testis tissues were rapidly dissected and washed with cold saline to remove residual blood.</p><p>3. Storage: The collected testis samples were immediately quenched in liquid nitrogen to stop metabolic activity and were subsequently stored at -80°C until metabolomics analysis.</p>

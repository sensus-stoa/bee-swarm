Study Identifier	MTBLS13838
Study Title	Ceramide Disrupts TM9SF2-PGK1 Axis to Redirect PD-L1 Trafficking and Enhance Antitumor Immunity
Study Description	<p>The PD-1/PD-L1 axis represents a cornerstone of cancer immunotherapy, yet the dynamic shuttling of PD-L1 between endosomal recycling and lysosomal degradation routes limits durable responses. Using a CRISPR screen targeting glycosphingolipid metabolism, we identify TM9SF2 as a key regulator of PD-L1 levels. TM9SF2 orchestrates a dual mechanism: it recruits PGK1 to promote PD-L1 recycling to the plasma membrane while dismantling the HIP1R-mediated lysosomal degradation pathway. Genetic or pharmacological disruption of the TM9SF2-PGK1 complex depletes PD-L1 levels and boosts antitumor immunity. Further, the endogenous ceramide species Cer(d18:1/26:0) destabilizes this complex, triggering PD-L1 lysosomal destruction and potentiating antitumor immunity. These findings delineate a ceramide-gated sorting mechanism within the endosomal network, revealing a druggable metabolic switch to disrupt immune evasion and amplify checkpoint blockade efficacy.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>1. Sample Origin and Experimental Groups</p><p>Species: Human (Homo sapiens).</p><p>Sample Type: Cell lines.</p><p>Study Groups: The study involved 12 samples divided into two groups:</p><p>NC (Control Group): 6 biological replicates (NC_1 to NC_6).</p><p>shTM9SF2 (Experimental/Treatment Group): 6 biological replicates (shTM9SF2_1 to shTM9SF2_6).</p><p><br></p><p>2. Relevant Treatment</p><p>Genetic Manipulation: The experimental group underwent knockdown or modification of the TM9SF2 gene (indicated by the 'shTM9SF2' nomenclature, typically referring to short hairpin RNA-mediated silencing).</p><p><br></p><p>3. Collection and Storage Procedure</p><p>While the specific time points of collection are not explicitly listed in the report, the following handling and storage protocols were documented:</p><p>Storage: Samples were stored in a $-80^{\circ}C$ refrigerator prior to extraction.</p><p>Handling: During the extraction process, samples were thawed on ice, and all subsequent operations were strictly performed on ice to maintain metabolite stability.</p><p><br></p><p>4. Sample Extraction Protocol (Preparation)</p><p>Thawing: Samples were removed from $-80^{\circ}C$ and thawed on ice.</p><p>Extraction: 1 mL of lipid extraction solution (Methyl tert-butyl ether: Methanol = 3:1, V/V) containing internal standards was added, followed by vortexing for 15 minutes.</p><p>Phase Separation: 200 µL of water was added. The mixture was vortexed for 1 minute and then centrifuged at 12,000 r/min for 10 minutes at $4^{\circ}C$.</p><p>Concentration: 500 µL of the upper layer (supernatant) was transferred to a new tube and concentrated until completely dry.</p><p>Reconstitution: The residue was reconstituted in 200 µL of lipid reconstitution solution (Acetonitrile: Isopropanol = 1:1, V/V), vortexed for 3 minutes, and centrifuged at 12,000 r/min for 3 minutes.</p><p>Analysis: The resulting supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The sample extracts were analyzed using an LC-ESI-MS/MS system. The analytical conditions were as follows, UPLC: column, Thermo Accucore™ C30; solvent system, A: acetonitrile/water (60/40,V/V, 0.1% formic acid, 10 mmol/L ammonium formate), B: acetonitrile/isopropanol (10/90 V/V, 0.1% formic acid, 10 mmol/L ammonium formate); flow rate, 0.35 ml/min; temperature, 45 °C; Injection volume: 2μl. The effluent was alternatively connected to an ESI-triple quadrupole-linear ion trap (QTRAP)-MS.</p>	<p>Process mass spectrometry data using software Analyst 1.6.3</p>	<p>Take out the sample from the -80 °C refrigerator and thaw it on ice. Add 1mL of the extraction solvent (MTBE: MeOH =3:1, v/v) containing internal standard mixture. After whirling the mixture for 15 min, 200 μL of ultrapure water was added. Vortex for 1 min and centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. 500 μL of the upper organic layer was collected and evaporated using a vacuum concentrator. The dry extract was dissolved in 200 μL reconstituted solution (ACN: IPA=1:1, v/v) to LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>LIT and triple quadrupole (QQQ) scans were acquired on a triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spectrometer (QTRAP), QTRAP® LC-MS/MS System, equipped with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, operating in positive and negative ion mode and controlled by Analyst 1.6.3 software (Sciex). The ESI source operation parameters were as follows: ion source, turbo spray; source temperature 500 °C; ion source gas 1 (GS1), gas 2 (GS2), curtain gas (CUR) were set at 45, 55, and 35 psi, respectively; the collision gas (CAD) was medium. Instrument tuning and mass calibration were performed with 10 and 100 μmol/L polypropylene glycol solutions in QQQ and LIT modes, respectively. QQQ scans were acquired as MRM experiments with collision gas (nitrogen) set to 5 psi. DP and CE for individual MRM transitions was done with further DP and CE optimization. A specific set of MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the metabolites eluted within this period.</p>	<p>Based on a self-built database, qualitative analysis is conducted according to the retention time (RT) and ion pair information of the detected substances.</p><p>Lipid quantification is achieved through analysis using the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode of a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. In the MRM mode, the quadrupole first screens precursor ions (parent ions) of target substances, eliminating ions corresponding to other molecular weight substances to initially exclude interference. The precursor ions undergo ionization induction in the collision chamber and fragment into many fragment ions. These fragment ions are then filtered and selected by the triple quadrupole to obtain a desired characteristic fragment ion, eliminating interference from non-target ions, resulting in more precise quantification and better reproducibility. After obtaining lipid mass spectrometry analysis data from different samples, the peak area integration of all substance mass spectrometry peaks is performed, and the mass spectrometry peak appearance of the same lipid in different samples is corrected by peak area integration.</p>

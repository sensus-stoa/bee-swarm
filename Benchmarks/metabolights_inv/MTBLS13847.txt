Study Title	Effects of Ultra-fine Pulverized Mulberry Leaf Combined with Multi-enzyme Preparations on Lipid Metabolism in Serum, Liver, Muscle and Intestine of Grass Carp
Study Description	<p>Grass carp are prone to excessive fat deposition under intensive aquaculture conditions, which impairs their health and reduces the economic benefit of farmed carp. We investigated the effects of dietary ultrafine pulverized mulberry leaf on lipid metabolism in grass carp, using phenotypic indicators and multi-omics approaches. A total of 480 grass carp (8.45 ± 0.17 g) were randomly divided into three groups (4 replicates per group, 40 fish per replicate). After 24 h of fasting, the fish were fed a basal diet (CK) and two experimental diets supplemented with either 6% or 12% ultrafine pulverized mulberry leaf and multi-enzyme preparations for 60 d. The results showed that serum triglyceride (TG) content decreased with increasing mulberry leaf supplementation, whereas intestinal lipase activity increased in a dose-dependent manner. Compared with the CK group, the mulberry leaf groups reduced the levels of serum total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), while decreasing the activity of glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) and superoxide dismutase (SOD). Malondialdehyde (MDA) content increased in the 6% group, whereas both total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and MDA content decreased significantly in the 12% group. The mulberry leaf groups showed increased dorsal muscle pH and reduced centrifugal loss and ether extract (EE) content. The 12% group had lower Opto value and crude protein (CP) content than the 6% group and the lowest muscle shear force. In terms of the dorsal muscle fatty acid composition, compared with the CK group, contents of unsaturated fatty acid (UFA), polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA), ω-3 PUFA, and ω-6 PUFA decreased in the 6% group but increased in the 12% group. Meanwhile, monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA) showed the opposite trend; the 6% group had the lowest saturated fatty acid (SFA) content. The mulberry leaf groups showed downregulated expression in the liver of genes involved in glycolysis (gk, pfk, pk) and lipogenesis (fas, acc), and all genes except pk showed a dose-dependent decrease with increasing mulberry leaf supplementation. Intestinal omics analysis revealed that the lysine degradation pathway is a key pathway regulating lipid metabolism in ultrafine pulverized mulberry leaves. The reduced levels of multiple differential metabolites and the enhanced microbial metabolic functions (especially amino acid and lipid metabolism) were closely associated with the inhibition of lipid metabolism in grass carp. This study indicates that ultrafine pulverized mulberry leaves effectively inhibit lipogenesis and reduce lipid deposition in grass carp by regulating the gut microbiota-metabolite-liver axis, providing a theoretical basis for the development of functional feed comprising mulberry leaf. A supplementation level of 12% ultrafine pulverized mulberry leaves is recommended.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>An ACQUITY UPLCHSST3column (100Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min, a column temperature of 40°C, an autosampler temperature of 8°C, and an injection volume of 2 μL.</p><p>Positive and negative mode mobile phase: mobile phase A is 0.1% formic acid water, mobile phase B is acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and the elution gradient is as follows: Time(0, 1, 7, 8, 8.1, 12 min)and corresponding B % (5, 5, 95, 95, 5, 5 %).</p>	<p>The .raw format data was imported into the commercial software Compound Discoverer™ 3.3 (version 3.3.2.31, Thermo, Waltham, USA). Based on the new peak detection and peak quality scoring algorithm of the software, peak extraction, alignment, correction and other operations were performed. The unique peak quality rating calculation and filter greatly reduced the interference of background peaks and low-quality peaks. The peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were filtered, and the missing values of the undetected peaks were filled based on the software Fill Gaps algorithm, and the Sum total peak area was normalized. </p>	<p>1.Weigh 50 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube;</p><p>2.Add 200 μL of pre-cooled water, add 2 steel beads, put into a high-throughput tissue grinder, 55 Hz, grind</p><p>for 60 s, repeat this step once;</p><p>3.Add 800 μL methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v);</p><p>4.Place in ultrasonic cleaning machine for 30 min;</p><p>5.Freeze in a-20℃ refrigerator for 30 min;</p><p>6.Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, take 800 μL of the supernatant and concentrate to dryness</p><p>under vacuum;</p><p>7.Add 150 μL 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine) to dissolve and vortex for 30</p><p>seconds;</p><p>8.Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, filter the supernatant through a 0.22 μm filter membrane,</p><p>and add the filtrate to the test bottle;</p><p>9.Take 10-20 μL of each sample filtrate and mix them into a QC sample for evaluating instrument stability</p><p>and data reliability.</p>	<p>Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used to collect DDA mass spectrometric data in positive and negative ion modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version: 4.7, Thermo). HESI source, spray voltage 3.5 kV/-3.0 kV, sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 15 arb, capillary temperature 325℃, auxiliary gas temperature 300℃, primary resolution 60,000, scan range 100-1000 m/z, AGC Target Standard, Max IT 100 ms, the top 4 ions were screened for secondary fragmentation, dynamic exclusion time was 8 s,</p><p>secondary resolution 15,000, HCD collision energy 30%, AGC Target Standard, Max IT Auto.</p><p>All formal samples and QC sample were loaded into the equipment according to the above-mentioned chromatography and mass spectrometry methods. Before the formal injection, QC sample were injected 2-4 times to balance the system. During the injection process, one QC sample was injected for every 5-10 samples for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p>The identification of metabolites was based on self-built library PSNGM Database, mzCloud online library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/) and NIST_2020_MSMS spectral library. The MS1mass tolerance was set to 15 ppm and the MS2 Match Factor Threshold was set to 50.</p>	<p>Grass carp were purchased from the Fuyuan Fry Farm in Guangzhou City, Guangdong Province. The feeding trial was conducted in ponds at the Modern Fishery Science and Technology Park in Foshan City using net cages (1.35 × 1.35 × 2.45 m). After 14 days of acclimation feeding and 24 h of fasting, a total of 480 grass carp (8.45 ± 0.17 g) were randomly assigned to three treatment groups: feed supplemented with 0%, 6%, and 12% ultra-fine pulverized mulberry leaf. Each treatment consisted of four replicates with 40 fish per replicate. The fish were reared for 60 consecutive days and fed twice daily at 8:30–9:00 and 17:00–17:30 (daily feeding rate: 2.00–5.00% of body weight, to apparent satiation). During the trial, the culture water was aerated 24 h, with water temperature maintained at 27.00 ± 2.00 °C, dissolved oxygen &gt; 5.00 mg/L, pH 7.50–8.50, and ammonia nitrogen &lt; 0.20 mg/L. The photoperiod followed the natural light cycle in Foshan, Guangdong, China. At the end of the rearing period, the fish were fasted for 24 h and the final body weight was measured to obtain the average final body weight. In each replicate, six fish with body weights close to the average were anesthetized (MS-222 solution, 100 mg/L). Three of the bled fish were used for tissue sampling, mid-hindgut samples were collected, pooled, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C until analysis. Each treatment comprised four replicates.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a UHPLC system (Q Exactive HF-X, Thermo Scientific) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm) maintained at 40°C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid, delivered at a flow rate of 0.40 mL/min using a gradient elution program. The injection volume was 4 µL.</p>	<p>Raw data files were processed using Progenesis QI software. The procedures included baseline correction, peak picking, peak alignment across all samples, and peak area integration to generate a feature matrix.</p>	<p>Exactly 50 mg of each sample was homogenized in 400 µL of a cold methanol:water mixture (4:1, v/v). The homogenate was centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was carefully collected, filtered through a 0.22 µm membrane, and transferred into an injection vial for UHPLC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was conducted on a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer with an electrospray ionization source. Data were acquired in both positive ionization (spray voltage: +3,500 V) and negative ionization (spray voltage: -3,200 V) modes. The full scan covered a mass-to-charge (m/z) range from 84 to 1250.</p>	<p>Putative metabolite identification was achieved by matching the acquired MS/MS spectra against the reference spectra in the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB). Differential metabolites were further annotated and subjected to KEGG pathway enrichment analysis using the&nbsp;scipy.stats&nbsp;package in Python.</p>	<p>Intestinal content samples were collected and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. All samples were subsequently stored at -80°C until further analysis.</p>

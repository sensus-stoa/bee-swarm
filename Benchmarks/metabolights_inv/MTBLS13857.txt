Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD, AB SCIEX) equipped with both reversed-phase and HILIC columns. For reversed-phase analysis, a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) was used with mobile phases consisting of water (0.1% formic acid) and acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid) under a gradient elution. The flow rate was set to 0.4 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μL for targeted and 5 μL for untargeted analyses.</p><p>For HILIC separation, a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH HILIC column (1.7 μm, 1.0 mm × 100 mm) was used with ammonium formate–based mobile phases adjusted to alkaline pH. Gradient elution was applied at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min with the column temperature maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS/MS data were processed using Analyst and MultiQuant software (AB SCIEX) for peak detection, integration, and alignment. Metabolite peak areas were normalized and missing values were imputed using one-fifth of the minimum detected value for each metabolite. Quality control filtering was applied by retaining metabolites with a coefficient of variation (CV) &lt; 0.3 in QC samples, generating the final quantitative dataset used for downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen samples were thawed on ice and approximately 20 mg (±1 mg) of each sample was weighed into centrifuge tubes. Metabolites were extracted using 400 μL of 70% methanol containing internal standards, followed by vortexing, grinding, incubation at 20 °C, and centrifugation at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was collected for LC–MS/MS analysis, while pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal aliquots of all extracts. Solvent blanks and QC samples were included throughout the analytical sequence to monitor background noise, carryover, and system stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was carried out using AB SCIEX TripleTOF 6600 and QTRAP 6500 systems equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes, with ion spray voltages of 5000 V (positive) and 4000 to 4500 V (negative), and source temperatures ranging from 450–550 °C. Nitrogen was used as curtain gas, nebulizer gas, and auxiliary gas with optimized pressures according to the manufacturer’s recommendations.</p><p>For targeted metabolomics, multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode was applied on the QTRAP 6500 platform, while untargeted profiling was performed using information-dependent acquisition (IDA) on the TripleTOF 6600 system. The mass scan range and collision energies were optimized for metabolite coverage and quantitative reproducibility.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching accurate mass, retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation patterns against the Metware Database (MWDB), a self-built metabolite database. Additional annotation was supported using integrated public databases including HMDB, METLIN, and KEGG, as well as AI-assisted prediction tools and MetDNA algorithms. Only metabolites meeting strict matching criteria across spectral similarity and retention behavior were retained for qualitative annotation and quantitative analysis.</p>	<p>The EFM single-strain culture was propagated through three successive passages in TSB medium. Following cultivation, samples were centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and the resulting supernatant was carefully collected. Supernatants were immediately stored at 80 °C until further processing to minimize metabolic degradation. All samples were handled on ice throughout the procedure to preserve metabolic stability and limit enzymatic activity prior to extraction.</p>

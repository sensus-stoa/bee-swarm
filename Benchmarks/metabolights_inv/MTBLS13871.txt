Study Title	Integrative analysis of metabolome and transcriptome reveals new insights into formation and regulation of major components of G. leucocontextum and G. lucidum
Study Description	<p>Ganoderma is a class of medicinal fungi with diverse medicinal effects, of which triterpenoids are one of the main pharmacologically active substances. In this study, sporocarps of two Ganoderma species (G. leucocontextum, a newly discovered species, and G. lucidum, a traditional medicinal species) at five different developmental stages were used as test materials for joint transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. Metabolomic analysis via Ultra-High Performance Liquid Chromatography Tandem Mass Spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) identified a total of 514 differential metabolites (screened by VIP&nbsp;1 and P &lt; 0.05). Among these, triterpenoids were the primary class of differentially abundant metabolites between the two species: G. leucocontextum consistently contained higher levels of ganoleucoin during development, while G. lucidum maintained higher levels of ganoderic acid. Transcriptomic analysis yielded 227.61 Gb of clean data (Q30 base percentage &gt; 93%), with 228,593 significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified across the five developmental stages. Most genes in the triterpenoid synthesis pathway (including several Ganoderma-specific CYP450 family genes) were more highly expressed in G. lucidum. RT-qPCR validation of 6 key DEGs confirmed that their expression trends were consistent with transcriptomic data. Additionally, 6 putative transcription factors (belonging to HSF and GNAT families) potentially involved in Ganoderma triterpene metabolism were identified. Our findings provide datasets on dynamic gene expression and differential metabolites during the development of the two Ganoderma species, laying a foundation for subsequent in-depth research.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>1. Instrument and Column Details</p><p>UPLC Instrument: ExionLC AD UPLC system (Sciex, https://sciex.com.cn/)</p><p>Mass Spectrometry Instrument: Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) system (Sciex, https://sciex.com.cn/)</p><p>Column: Agilent SB-C18 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm), manufactured by Agilent Technologies.</p><p>2. Mobile Phase Composition</p><p>Mobile Phase A: Ultra-pure water containing 0.1% formic acid</p><p>Mobile Phase B: Acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid</p><p>3. Gradient Elution Program</p><p>The gradient elution procedure was carried out as follows:</p><p>0.00 min: Initial composition with 5% mobile phase B</p><p>9.00 min: Linear gradient to increase mobile phase B proportion to 95%</p><p>9.00–10.00 min: Maintain mobile phase B proportion at 95% for 1 min</p><p>10.00–11.10 min: Decrease mobile phase B proportion back to 5%</p><p>11.10–14.00 min: Maintain mobile phase B proportion at 5% for column re-equilibration</p><p>4. Chromatographic Operating Settings</p><p>Flow Rate: 0.35 mL/min</p><p>Column Temperature: 40 °C</p><p>Injection Volume: 2 μL</p>	<p>Raw UPLC-MS/MS data were preprocessed with Analyst 1.6.3 software. The data were imported into MultiQuant for chromatographic peak extraction, integration and correction, with peak area indicating metabolite relative content. QC assessment included TIC overlap analysis in positive and negative ion modes and CV analysis, where over 75% of metabolites had CV &lt; 0.3 and over 85% had CV &lt; 0.5. Isotope signals, adduct ion signals and redundant fragment ion signals were removed to ensure data accuracy.</p><p>Metabolite identification was based on the self-constructed MetWare Database (MWDB) by matching secondary mass spectrometry spectra, retention time and characteristic ion pairs. Quantification was performed via Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode, with relative content determined by comparing calibrated peak areas of characteristic ion pairs across samples.</p><p>Two normalization methods were applied for statistical analysis. Unit Variance Scaling (UV) was used for Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Hierarchical Cluster Analysis (HCA). Log2 transformation combined with mean centering was adopted for Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) to reduce data skewness and enhance model reliability.</p>	<p>Freeze-drying: Samples were dried in a freeze dryer (Scientz-100F).</p><p>Grinding: Dried samples were ground into powder (MM400, Retsch; 30 Hz, 1.5 min).</p><p>Extraction: 50 mg powder was mixed with 1200 μL pre-cooled (-20 °C) 70% methanol aqueous internal standard solution; the ratio was maintained for samples &lt; 50 mg.</p><p>Vortexing: Vortexed for 30 s every 30 min, 6 cycles in total.</p><p>Centrifugation: Centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 3 min; the supernatant was collected.</p><p>Filtration: The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm microporous membrane and stored in injection vials for UPLC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Instrument DetailsTandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) system (Sciex, https://sciex.com.cn/) was coupled with UPLC for metabolite detection.</p><p>Ion Source and Ionisation Mode</p><p>Ion Source: Electrospray Ionization (ESI)</p><p>Ionisation Mode: Both positive (P) and negative (N) ion modes were applied</p><p>Ion Spray Voltage: 5500 V for positive mode; -4500 V for negative mode</p><p>Key Operating Parameters</p><p>Source Temperature: 550 °C</p><p>Source Gases: Ion Source Gas I (GSI) = 50 psi; Ion Source Gas II (GSII) = 60 psi; Curtain Gas (CUR) = 25 psi</p><p>Collision-Activated Dissociation (CAD): High</p><p>Scan Parameters</p><p>Scan Mode: Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM)</p><p>Collision Gas: Nitrogen (medium pressure)</p><p>Parameter Optimization: Declustering Potential (DP) and Collision Energy (CE) were optimized for each MRM ion pair to ensure accurate metabolite detection</p><p>Specific MRM Transitions: A set of target MRM transitions were monitored according to the metabolites eluted during different time periods</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and annotation were implemented based on an ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) platform, combined with self-constructed and public databases, following standardized data processing pipelines.</p><p>The primary reference database for metabolite identification was the self-constructed MetWare Database (MWDB). Metabolite qualification was achieved by matching the secondary mass spectrometry spectra, retention time, and characteristic ion pairs of samples with the corresponding entries in MWDB. During the process, interference signals were eliminated, including isotope signals, adduct ion signals containing K+, Na+ and NH4+, and fragment ion signals derived from larger molecular weight metabolites, to ensure the accuracy of identification results.</p><p>The core identification pipeline relied on the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode of triple quadrupole mass spectrometry. First, the precursor ions of target metabolites were screened by the first quadrupole to exclude interference from other ions. Then, the precursor ions were fragmented in the collision cell, and the characteristic fragment ions were filtered by the third quadrupole for targeted detection. The declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE) parameters for each MRM ion pair were optimized to improve the specificity and sensitivity of metabolite detection. After acquiring the mass spectrometry data, chromatographic peak extraction, integration and correction were performed to ensure consistent peak detection across different samples.</p>	<p>After 20 days of growth on sorghum medium at 25, mycelia were inoculated onto the substrate packed in heat-sealed cultivation bags with microfilter windows and cultured in the dark at 25 C for 1 month. The sorghum-based medium was specifically designed to provide optimal nutritional supplementation for Ganoderma growth, while concurrently establishing a porous matrix structure with superior moisture retention properties. For fruiting body growth, the bags were cultured in a room with 10 h of illumination and 30 min of ventilation at 26 .</p>

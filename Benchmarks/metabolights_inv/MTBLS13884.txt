Study Protocol Description	<p>Metabolite separation was performed using a UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with a reversed-phase C18 column. The detailed chromatography parameters are shown as follows:</p><p>Chromatography Instrument: UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)</p><p>Autosampler model: Thermo Fisher UltiMate 3000 Autosampler</p><p>Column model: Thermo Scientific Hypersil GOLD™ C18 Column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm)</p><p>Guard column: Thermo Scientific Hypersil GOLD™ C18 Guard Column (10 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm)</p><p>The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 5 μL. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (0.1% formic acid in ultrapure water) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile), with a gradient elution program: 0-2 min, 5% B; 2-15 min, 5%-95% B; 15-18 min, 95% B; 18-18.1 min, 95%-5% B; 18.1-22 min, 5% B. The flow rate was set at 0.3 mL/min throughout the separation process.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were processed using a standard workflow with Thermo Xcalibur software (version 4.3) and Compound Discoverer software (version 3.1). Briefly, the data were subjected to peak detection, peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area quantification. The internal standard was used for normalization to reduce systematic errors. The data were then converted to a matrix format for subsequent multivariate statistical analysis and univariate statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen bacterial pellets were thawed on ice, and 500 μL of pre-cooled methanol-water solution (v/v = 8:2) was added as the extraction solvent. The samples were vortexed vigorously for 30 seconds to fully resuspend the pellets, then sonicated on ice for 15 minutes (power: 200 W, interval: 3 seconds) to promote metabolite release. After sonication, the samples were incubated at -20 °C for 30 minutes to precipitate proteins, followed by centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully transferred to a new centrifuge tube, dried under nitrogen flow, and redissolved in 100 μL of methanol-water solution (v/v = 1:1) for UHPLC-MS/MS analysis. The undissolved precipitates were discarded after centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 5 minutes.</p>	<p>After chromatographic separation, metabolites were detected using a high-resolution LC–MS/MS system coupled with an ESI source (Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The detailed mass spectrometry parameters are shown as follows:</p><p>Scan m/z range: 50-1000 m/z</p><p>Instrument: Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)</p><p>Ion source: Electrospray ionization (ESI) source</p><p>Mass analyzer: Orbitrap mass analyzer</p><p>The ESI source was operated in both positive and negative ion modes. The spray voltage was set to 3.5 kV (positive ion mode) and 3.0 kV (negative ion mode), and the capillary temperature was 320 °C. The sheath gas flow rate was 40 arb, and the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10 arb. The resolution for full scan was 70,000, and the resolution for MS/MS scan was 17,500. The collision energy was set at 20, 40, and 60 eV for stepped collision dissociation.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by database matching based on MS/MS spectra. The MS/MS spectra of detected metabolites were matched against the publicly available metabolite databases, including Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Metlin database. The identification criteria were set as follows: the mass error between the measured m/z and the theoretical m/z was less than 5 ppm, and the MS/MS spectral similarity was greater than 80% compared with the database entries. Only metabolites that met the above criteria were considered reliably identified</p>	<p>Helicobacter pylori cultures in the logarithmic growth phase were collected by centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4 °C. The bacterial pellets were gently washed twice with pre-cooled phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) to remove residual culture medium, and then immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until metabolite extraction to avoid metabolite degradation</p>

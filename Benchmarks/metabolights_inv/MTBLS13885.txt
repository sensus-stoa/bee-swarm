Study Identifier	MTBLS13885
Study Title	Multiomic analysis reveals that polyamines alter G. vaginalis-induced cervicovaginal epithelial cell dysfunction
Study Description	<p>An anaerobe-dominant,&nbsp;<em>Lactobacillus</em>-deplete cervicovaginal microbiome is associated with adverse reproductive outcomes.&nbsp;<em>Gardnerella vaginalis</em>, a cervicovaginal anaerobe, alters cervicovaginal epithelial cell function, resulting in immune activation and barrier breakdown. Host-microbial mechanisms inducing this epithelial dysfunction remain unknown. We show microbe-specific alterations in cervicovaginal epithelial cell metabolite profiles where&nbsp;<em>G. vaginalis</em>, but not&nbsp;<em>Lactobacillus crispatus</em>, increases polyamine biosynthesis. Pretreatment with polyamines (putrescine, spermidine and spermine) globally shifts&nbsp;<em>G. vaginalis</em>-induced transcriptomic profiles. Alterations in enzyme transcripts responsible for polyamine synthesis and catabolism provide evidence that&nbsp;<em>G. vaginalis</em>&nbsp;modifies polyamine biosynthesis. Polyamine-mediated transcriptomic changes include genes related to bacterial defense, inflammation, and epigenetic processes. Polyamines mitigate&nbsp;<em>G. vaginalis</em>-induced inflammatory responses through reduction of cytokines/chemokines and matrix metalloproteinases.&nbsp;In vitro&nbsp;transcriptional signatures positively correlated to existing human datasets. The ability of cervicovaginal metabolites to alter microbe-mediated changes in epithelial cell function suggests that metabolite-microbe interactions are critical mediators of epithelial defense against a&nbsp;<em>Lactobacillus</em>-deplete microbiota.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human cervicovaginal epithelial cell lines (ectocervical Ect/E6E7, endocervical End1/E6E7, and vaginal VK2/E6E7; ATCC) were cultured under standard conditions and exposed to live bacterial strains and/or polyamines. Cells were plated at 1.5 × 10^5 cells per well in antibiotic-free keratinocyte serum-free medium. Cells were pretreated with polyamines or vehicle control for 4 hours prior to bacterial exposure. Live <em>Lactobacillus crispatus</em> or <em>Gardnerella vaginalis</em> were added at 10^7 CFU per well and incubated for an additional 24 hours. Cell culture supernatants were collected after 24 hours for metabolomics analysis and stored at −80 °C prior to shipment.</p>	<p>Metabolite separation was performed using ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC). Samples were analyzed using reverse-phase (RP) chromatography with conditions optimized for broad metabolite coverage. Multiple chromatographic methods were employed, including reverse-phase separations compatible with positive ion electrospray ionization. Detailed chromatographic conditions (column chemistry, mobile phase composition, gradients, flow rates, and temperatures) followed Metabolon’s validated global metabolomics platform.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were extracted, peak-identified, and quality-control processed using proprietary software developed by Metabolon, Inc. Peaks were quantified using area-under-the-curve measurements. Data were log10-transformed prior to downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Cell culture supernatants were processed by Metabolon, Inc. using a global untargeted metabolomics workflow. Proteins were removed by methanol precipitation. The resulting extract was divided into multiple fractions to enable complementary chromatographic and ionization conditions. Control samples included pooled sample matrix, extracted water samples (process blanks), and a mixture of quality control standards spiked into each sample to monitor process variability and instrument performance.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed using tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) with electrospray ionization (ESI). Samples were analyzed in positive ion mode for this assay. Mass spectra were acquired across a broad m/z range suitable for untargeted metabolomics. Instrument parameters were optimized by the vendor as part of a standardized platform to ensure reproducible detection of chemically diverse metabolites.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by comparison to a curated Metabolon reference library of purified chemical standards. Identification was based on three criteria: retention index, accurate mass match (±10 ppm), and MS/MS fragmentation pattern matching (forward and reverse spectral scores). Only compounds meeting all identification criteria were assigned biochemical identities.</p>

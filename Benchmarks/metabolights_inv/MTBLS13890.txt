Study Identifier	MTBLS13890
Study Title	Dietary Glutamine Supplementation Mitigates Age-Associated Cardiac Decline by Restricting H3K27me3
Study Description	<p>Epigenetic alterations are central drivers of cardiovascular aging, with histone modifications regulating gene expression through chromatin remodeling and changes in DNA accessibility. However, the mechanisms underlying these epigenetic shifts in the aging heart remain poorly defined. Here, we identify an age-associated accumulation of the repressive histone mark H3K27me3 in the myocardium of mice and humans, implicating this modification in myocardial aging. Elevated H3K27me3 was associated with impaired glutamine metabolism, driven by reduced expression of the amino acid transporter SLC1A5. Clinically, low circulating glutamine levels correlated with increased heart failure incidence, and genetic variants in SLC1A5 were linked to heightened cardiovascular disease risk. Mechanistically, elevated myocardial H3K27me3 suppressed cardiomyocyte autophagy and induced metabolic reprogramming, thereby promoting age-related myocardial dysfunction. Dietary glutamine supplementation in aged mice reduced H3K27me3 accumulation and improved myocardial function. Collectively, these findings identify a novel epigenetic mechanism underlying cardiac aging and highlight the glutamine–SLC1A5 axis as a potential therapeutic target for preserving myocardial function with age.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mice we fed control diet or diet supplimented with glutamine (2-months) were culled at 83 weeks of aged. Cardiac tissue was extracted, snap frozen and stored in liquid nitrogen.</p>	<p>The dried polar metabolite fractions were reconstituted in acetonitrile/water (v/v 3/2) and analyzed using a 1290 Infinity II ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system coupled to a 6546 quadrupole time-of-flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies). Samples were separated on a Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z column (2.1 × 100 mm, 2.7 μm).</p>	<p>Chromatograms were time aligned to a control sample prior to metabolite identification</p>	<p>About 30 mg of pulverised mouse young and aged cardiac tissue samples were lyophilised overnight at -55°C using a freeze-dryer. Metabolite extraction was performed following a modified Le Belle et al. method, utilising a methanol/chloroform/water extraction protocol. The extraction procedure was carried out in two stages:</p><p><br></p><p>Initial Extraction: 600 μL of methanol/chloroform (2:1; v/v) was added to the tissue sample. The mixture was subjected to sonication in a water bath at 4°C for 1 hr, with three 8-min pulse sonication cycles. Following centrifugation, the supernatant was retained.</p><p><br></p><p>Secondary Extraction: The remaining tissue pellet underwent further extraction with 200 μL of water and 400 μL of methanol. This mixture was sonicated for an additional hour in the water bath, again with three 8-min sonication cycles.</p><p>The supernatants from both extractions were combined and concentrated using a SpeedVac vacuum centrifuge overnight (Thermofisher Scientific). The dried extract was reconstituted in a chloroform:methanol:water mixture (1:3:3, v/v/v). The polar metabolite fraction (upper phase) was isolated by centrifugation at 16,000xG at 4°C. This fraction was then dried and stored at -80°C pending liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were separated on a Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z column (2.1 × 100 mm, 2.7 μm) and analyzed in negative ionization mode using water with 10 mM ammonium acetate (solvent A) and acetonitrile with 10 mM ammonium acetate (solvent B), both solvents containing 5 μM InfinityLab deactivator additive (Agilent Technologies). The elution gradient used was as follows: isocratic step at 95% B for 2 min, 95% to 65% B in 12 min, maintained at 65% B for 3 min, then returned to initial conditions over 1 min, and then the column was equilibrated at initial conditions for 8 min. The flow rate was 0.25 mL/min; injection volume was 5 μL, and the column oven was maintained at 30 °C. The 6546 Q-TOF was operated at 2 GHz extended dynamic range with a mass acquisition range from 0 to 1700 m/z. The drying and sheath gas temperature was set at 250 °C and 380 °C, respectively, with the nebulizer pressure at 45 psi and voltage 3000. The fragmentor voltage was set at 115 V. </p>	<p>Feature annotation and metabolite identification were based on accurate mass and standard retention times with a tolerance of ±5 ppm and ±0.5 min, respectively, and performed with MassHunter Profinder (version 10.0.2, Agilent Technologies) using an in-house curated metabolite library based on metabolite standards (Sigma-Aldrich). Metabolite levels are represented as ion peak height normalized to protein concentration. Data visualization and statistical analyses were performed using MetaboAnalyst v5.0.</p>

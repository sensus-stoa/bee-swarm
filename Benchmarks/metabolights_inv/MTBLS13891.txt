Study Title	Time-Restricted Feeding Orchestrates Astrocytic Metabolic Reprogramming via the Wnt Pathway to Alleviate Sevoflurane-Induced Neural Injury
Study Description	<p>Perioperative neurocognitive disorders (PND) pose a significant challenge for aging surgical patients, with sevoflurane anesthesia being an identified risk factor. Time-restricted feeding (TRF), a dietary regimen, shows promise in mitigating age-related cognitive decline, but its efficacy and mechanism against sevoflurane-induced PND remain unclear. Using the aging-accelerated mouse model (SAMP8) exposed to sevoflurane, we found that TRF improved cognitive performance and synaptic integrity, as evidenced by increased levels of PSD-95 and synaptophysin. Mechanistically, TRF activated the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, driving a beneficial metabolic reprogramming in hippocampal astrocytes characterized by enhanced ketogenesis and fatty acid oxidation. This was associated with reduced neuroinflammation and oxidative stress. The neuroprotective effects of TRF were abolished by Wnt inhibition and enhanced by Wnt activation. Our study identifies TRF as a non-pharmacological strategy that counteracts PND by linking Wnt-mediated astrocyte metabolic reprogramming to improved neuronal function.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II LC system (Agilent Technologies, USA), which is compatible with the subsequent mass spectrometric detection and widely used in metabolomics analysis. The system was equipped with an Agilent 1290 Infinity II Multisampler autosampler to ensure accurate and reproducible sample injection. Separation of metabolites was achieved on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, USA), which exhibits excellent separation performance for polar and semi-polar metabolites. A matching Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 VanGuard pre-column (5 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, USA) was used to protect the analytical column from contamination and extend its service life. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (phase A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (phase B), with a gradient elution program optimized for comprehensive metabolite coverage, and the flow rate was set at 0.3 mL/min</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were converted into mzXML format using the MSConvert tool embedded in the Proteowizard software package (v3.0.8789). Subsequent data preprocessing, including peak detection, peak filtering, peak alignment, and peak integration, was performed using the XCMS package in R software with the following optimized parameters: bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, and method = 'centWave'. The resulting data matrix was normalized using the quantile normalization method to eliminate systematic variations between samples, and then subjected to missing value imputation (k-nearest neighbor method) and noise reduction to obtain a reliable metabolite quantification list for subsequent statistical analysis</p>	<p>Frozen hippocampal tissues (50 mg per sample) were homogenized with 500 μL of pre-chilled methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v) containing L-2-chlorophenylalanine as the internal standard. The homogenates were vortexed vigorously for 30 s, ultrasonicated on ice for 15 min to facilitate metabolite release, and then incubated at -20°C for 1 h to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 15 min, the supernatants were carefully collected, dried under nitrogen gas, reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v), and filtered through a 0.22 μm polyethersulfone membrane to remove impurities before LC-MS detection</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was carried out on a Thermo Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), which combines high resolution and high sensitivity, suitable for untargeted metabolomics analysis of hippocampal tissues. The spectrometer was equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ion modes to cover a wide range of metabolites with different polarities. The scan m/z range was set to 50–1000 m/z to ensure the detection of most small-molecule metabolites (e.g., ketones, fatty acids, amino acids) involved in astrocyte metabolic reprogramming. The mass analyzer was an Orbitrap mass analyzer, which provides high mass accuracy (≤5 ppm) for accurate metabolite identification</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was conducted by matching the accurate mass (mass error ≤5 ppm), retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation patterns of the detected peaks against public metabolomics databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Metlin Database, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Database. Additionally, the internal standard was used to correct the retention time and peak intensity to improve the accuracy of metabolite identification. Only metabolites with a matching score ≥80 (out of 100) and consistent fragmentation patterns with reference standards were considered reliably identified. The identified metabolites were further annotated and mapped to KEGG pathways to explore their roles in astrocyte metabolic reprogramming and neuroprotection.</p>	<p>Hippocampal tissues were collected from aging-accelerated SAMP8 mice exposed to sevoflurane anesthesia with or without time-restricted feeding (TRF) intervention. After sacrifice, mice were quickly decapitated, and the hippocampus was dissected on ice, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen to preserve metabolic profiles, and stored at -80°C until metabolite extraction and subsequent LC-MS analysis</p>

Study Title	Postoperative Exercise Modulating Gut Microbiota and Associated Metabolites to Facilitate Bone-Tendon Healing in Rotator Cuff Tears
Study Description	<p>Background: Rotator cuff tear (RCT) is a prevalent shoulder injury, and postoperative exercise is considered to facilitate RCT healing. However, the effect of postoperative exercise on RCT healing through gut microbiota and metabolites remains unclear. </p><p>Method: The control (con) group (n=10) underwent sham surgeries, while the model (mod) and postoperative exercise (sport) groups (each n=10) underwent supraspinatus tendon repair surgeries, and the supraspinatus tendon-humerus complex, fecal, and serum samples were collected. </p><p>Results: Synchrotron radiation micro-CT and histological studies indicated that postoperative exercise markedly reduced bone loss and promoted new bone formation at tendon-bone sites. Gut microbiome profiling revealed that exercise significantly reshaped microbial composition, with increased p__Parabacteroides and g__Dubosiella, and decreased g__Lachnoclostridium and g__Allobaculum in the sport group compared with the model group. PICRUSt prediction showed that the HIF-1 signaling pathway and β-Alanine metabolism were notably enriched following exercise. Network correlation analysis suggested that g__Dubosiella functioned as a central taxon within the microbial community. Metabolomics suggested that exercise altered metabolite abundances like Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg, impacting pathways like Gap junction. Moreover, metabolomics further identified β-Alanine metabolism as the key overlapping pathway connecting microbial alterations with serum metabolic changes, with β-alanyl-L-arginine as a representative metabolite. Seven microbe–metabolite pairs showed significant correlations. Among them, g__Parabacteroides exhibited strong associations with multiple metabolites, including cycloheptyl derivatives, and demonstrated predictive value for tendon–bone healing (AUC = 0.88). </p><p>Conclusion: Postoperative exercise following RCT repair surgery modulates gut microbiota and metabolites, potentially enhancing RCT healing. This finding provides insights into integrating postoperative exercise into RCT recovery strategies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Based on the experimental report, the analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system coupled to a Q-Exactive HF-X hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm internal diameter, 1.8 µm particle size). The mobile phase consisted of (A) 95% water + 5% acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid, and (B) 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water with 0.1% formic acid. The column temperature was maintained at 40°C, and the injection volume was 3 µL. Mass spectrometric detection was conducted using electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative ion modes, with a scan range of m/z 70–1050. Key MS parameters included a spray voltage of ±3500 V, a heater temperature of 425°C, a capillary temperature of 325°C, a sheath gas flow rate of 50 arbitrary units, an auxiliary gas flow rate of 13 arbitrary units, and resolutions of 60,000 for full MS and 7,500 for MS/MS acquisition.</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (version 3.0, Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). The workflow included baseline filtering, peak detection, retention time alignment, and peak integration to generate a data matrix containing retention time, m/z, and peak intensity information. Metabolite identification was performed by matching acquired MS and MS/MS spectra against public databases such as HMDB and Metlin , with a mass tolerance of &lt;10 ppm and evaluation of fragmentation pattern scores.</p>	<p>For serum metabolomic analysis, we start by taking 100 μL of the serum sample and adding 300 μL of pre-cooled chromatographic-grade methanol; we then vortex the mixture at 1500 rpm for 1 minute to mix it thoroughly. Next, the mixture is incubated at -20°C for 2 hours to precipitate proteins, followed by centrifugation at 4000 rpm (at 4°C) for 20 minutes. We transfer the resulting supernatant to a new tube and blow it dry using nitrogen. After that, we reconstitute the residue with 100 μL of a 50% methanol aqueous solution, vortex for 1 minute, and centrifuge again at 12000 rpm (4°C) for 10 minutes. The final supernatant is then transferred to an autosampler vial for the subsequent LC-MS analysis.</p><p>For quality control during detection, we prepared several types of control samples. Pooled Quality Control (QC) samples are made by mixing 5 μL of each serum sample across all groups; we process this pooled sample using the same extraction method as above, and inject it every 10 test samples to monitor instrument stability. A solvent blank is prepared by replacing serum with 100 μL of ultrapure water, processed through the identical extraction procedure to eliminate background interference from reagents or the environment.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system coupled with a Q-Exactive HF-X hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. The ion source was electrospray ionization (ESI), operated in both positive and negative ionization modes. The full scan mass range was set from m/z 70 to 1050. Key mass spectrometric parameters included a heater temperature of 425 °C, a capillary temperature of 325 °C, a spray voltage of ±3500 V, a sheath gas flow rate of 50 arbitrary units, and an auxiliary gas flow rate of 13 arbitrary units. The mass resolution was 60,000 for full MS and 7,500 for data-dependent MS/MS acquisition.</p>	<p>Raw LC MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (version 3.0, Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). The pipeline included baseline filtering, peak detection, retention-time alignment, and peak integration to generate a data matrix of retention time, m/z, and peak intensity. Metabolite identification was performed by matching acquired MS and MS/MS spectra against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB,&nbsp;http://www.hmdb.ca/) and the METLIN database (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as an in-house spectral library. Matching criteria included a mass error &lt; 10 ppm and evaluation of MS/MS fragmentation scores.</p>	<p>Origin of samples and relevant treatment: Samples were derived from 30 male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old, 22-24 g), randomly divided into three groups (n=10 each): control (con), rotator cuff tear model (mod), and postoperative exercise (sport) groups. The con group received sham surgery (only surgical incisions without substantial intervention) and free cage movement. The mod group underwent left supraspinatus tendon (SST) repair surgery to establish rotator cuff tear model and free cage movement postoperatively. The sport group underwent the same SST repair surgery, followed by 1-week preoperative acclimatization training (motorized treadmill, 10 m/min, 20 min/day) and postoperative aerobic exercise intervention (starting on postoperative day 7, horizontal treadmill, 10 m/min, 20 min/day, 5 days/week for 4 weeks). Mice in con and mod groups were exposed to the treadmill room and stationary treadmill for the same duration as sport group to control confounding factors.</p><p>Time points: Samples were collected 24 hours after the final treadmill-related operation (exercise or environmental exposure) of all groups.</p>

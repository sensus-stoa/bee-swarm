Study Identifier	MTBLS1391
Study Title	Characterization of Cabernet Sauvignon Wines by Untargeted HS-SPME GC-QTOF-MS
Study Description	Untargeted metabolomics approaches are emerging as powerful tools for the quality evaluation and authenticity of food and beverages and have been applied to wine science. However, most fail to report the method validation, quality assurance and/or quality control applied, as well as the assessment through the metabolomics-methodology pipeline. Knowledge of Mexican viticulture, enology and wine science remains scarce, thus untargeted metabolomics approaches arise as a suitable tool. The aim of this study is to validate an untargeted HS-SPME-GC-qTOF/MS method, with attention to data processing to characterize Cabernet Sauvignon wines from two vineyards and two vintages. Validation parameters for targeted methods are applied in conjunction with the development of a recursive analysis of data. The combination of some parameters for targeted studies (repeatability and reproducibility &lt; 20% RSD; linearity &gt; 0.99; retention-time reproducibility &lt; 0.5% RSD; match-identification factor &lt; 2.0% RSD) with recursive analysis of data (101 entities detected) warrants that both chromatographic and spectrometry-processing data were under control and provided high-quality results, which in turn differentiate wine samples according to site and vintage. It also shows potential biomarkers that can be identified. This is a step forward in the pursuit of Mexican wine characterization that could be used as an authentication tool.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Head-space solid phase micro-extraction (HS-SPME) with 50/30 µm DVB/CAR/PDMS Stable Flex Supelco fiber. A total of 3 g of NaCl were added to 10 mL of sample in a 20 mL amber vial sealed with an aluminum cap and an 18 mm blue PTFE/silicone septum. Samples were conditioned at 40 °C for 5 min, and extracted for 30 min. Fiber were conditioned before extraction at 250 °C for 10 min and post-desorption for 5 min. For injection, fiber desorption was at 250 °C for 10 min.</p>	<p>Chromatography was preformed on a GC 7890B (Agilent Technologies) PAL3 Autosampler with splitless injection using a DB-WAX (30 m x 250 µm; 0.25 µm, Agilent) high polarity column. Flow rate 1.0 mL/min (RT lock 2-Undecanone at 36.158 min). Oven ramp: 40 °C for 5 min, 3 °C/min to 180 °C, 30 °C/min to 220 °C for 10 min. Total run 63 min. Post-run 2 min at 220 °C.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was preformed on a qTOF-MS 7200 (Agilent Technologies) using electron ionization with the following parameters: source temperature, 230 °C; electron energy, 70 eV; acquisition rate, 2.5 spectra/s; acquisition time, 400 ms/spectrum; mass range: 30-400 amu; solvent delay: 3 min.</p>	<p>Calibration curves, LOD and LOQ determinations were performed in MassHunter WorkStation Quantitative Analysis B. Data mining, deconvolution and alignment were performed in MassHunter WorkStation Unknowns Analysis, deconvolution by SureMass with RT window of 300, no height filters were applied, a 5 SNR (signal to noise ratio), extraction window of 10 ppm, threshold of 25% in the component shape, 4 ion peaks minimum for the extraction and maximum 10 ion peaks to store.</p>	<p>Compound identification were performed in MassHunter Workstation Unknowns Analysis with the Accurate Mass Flavors Database<strong>[1]</strong> and NIST 17. The identification method included spectral search with a minimum match factor of 70, performing an exact mass comparison, starting at 30 m/z, with accuracy &lt; 20 ppm. Putative identified compounds were assigned when the ion peaks, mass fragmentation spectrum and IR matched the component from the libraries.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Baunmann et al., 2013: ASMS poster MP685</p>	<p>Cabernet Sauvignon wines from Bodegas de Santo Tomas (Ensenada, B.C., Mexico) vintages 2017 and 2018 from 2 different vineyards, La Changa and Los Dolores, were collected, bottled and stored at room temperature until processing. For quality control (QC) purposes a subset of samples from Santo Tomás’ wines were pooled (vintages 2015, 2016 and 2017), aliquoted and stored at -50 °C until needed.</p>

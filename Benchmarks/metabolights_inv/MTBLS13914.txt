Study Description	<p>Dietary regimen exerts a dominant influence in reshaping&nbsp;the&nbsp;gut microbiota structure and microbial metabolite types and levels in <em>S.&nbsp;chuatsi</em>, subsequently&nbsp;affecting host physiological functions like&nbsp;immunity,&nbsp;and has a more pronounced effect than environmental factors.&nbsp;These findings offer new insights into the dietary-microbiota-host interaction network, and fill the gap in the understanding of the significant role that diet plays in contributing to the gut microbiota.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Four hours after the last feeding, eight similar-sized fish&nbsp;were randomly selected from each&nbsp;group&nbsp;and each location. Weight gain rate and feed conversion ratio were calculated using the previous formula. Sampled fish were anesthetized on ice, dissected on sterile trays, and intestines were removed. After&nbsp;rinsing the intestines with sterile PBS,&nbsp;intestinal contents were collected. Both the contents and tissues were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80℃.&nbsp;The animal experimental procedures&nbsp;were approved by the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethical Reviewing Committee of Shanghai Fisheries Research Institute (SFRI-2025-002).</p>	<p>&nbsp;A 400 μL supernatant was taken for UHPLC-MS/MS analysis (HSS T3 column, 100 mm x 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (phase A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol (1:1, v/v) (phase B). QC&nbsp;samples were prepared by pooling equal-volume metabolites from all samples and&nbsp;inserted in&nbsp;the instrumental analysis sequence to monitor process reproducibility.</p>	<p>The pretreatment of LC/MS raw data was performed by Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) software, and a three-dimensional data matrix in CSV format was exported. The information in this three-dimensional matrix included: sample information, metabolite name and mass spectral response intensity. Internal standard peaks, as well as any known false positive peaks (including noise, column bleed, and derivatized reagent peaks), were removed from the data matrix, deredundant and peak pooled.</p>	<p>Intestinal&nbsp;contents weighing 50 mg were&nbsp;measured,&nbsp;and the metabolites were extracted using a&nbsp;500 μL&nbsp;extraction solvent.&nbsp;After grinding&nbsp;and ultrasonication (35 Hz,&nbsp;4 min) steps, then standing still for 1 hour, centrifuge to obtain the supernatant (4°C,&nbsp;12,000 rpm, 15 min).</p>	<p>The UPLC system was coupled to a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive Mass Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. Data acquisition used positive and negative ion scanning modes (70-1050&nbsp;m/z,&nbsp;Positive/Negative ion spray voltage: 3500V/-3000&nbsp;V, ion source temperature 450°C). Raw LC-MS data were imported into Progenesis QI for baseline filtering, peak identification, and generating data matrix. MS and MS/MS spectra were matched with public and in-house databases for metabolite identification. The post-identification data matrix was uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform</p>	<p>At the same time, the metabolites were identified by searching database, and the main databases were the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin ( https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and Majorbio Database.</p>

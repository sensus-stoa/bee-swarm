Study Title	Zhaqu Compound Improves Glucose and Lipid Metabolism in T2DM with MASLD by Modulating Gut Microbiota and PPARγ
Study Description	<p>Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) with metabolic syndrome-associated steatohepatitis (MASLD) is a complex metabolic comorbidity characterized by hyperglycemia, pancreatic β-cell dysfunction, and hepatic lipid metabolism disorders, in which gut microbiota dysbiosis and abnormal PPARγ signaling play pivotal roles. Zhaqu Compound (ZQC), a traditional Chinese herbal formula, has demonstrated clinical efficacy in improving glucose and lipid metabolism, though its mechanisms remain unclear. In this study, db/db mice with T2DM-MASLD were treated for 12 weeks with ZQC, metformin, or saline, and metabolic outcomes were evaluated via blood glucose, lipid profiles, hepatic lipid accumulation, hepatocyte ultrastructure, gut microbiota (16S rRNA), non-targeted metabolomics, and liver transcriptomics. In high-glucose + palmitate-induced HepG2 cells, ZQC-containing serum and the PPARγ agonist GW1929 were applied, and Western blot was used to assess key gluconeogenic and lipogenic proteins. LC-MS, network pharmacology, and molecular docking identified major ZQC components and their potential interactions with PPARγ. ZQC improved glycemia, insulin resistance, and lipid profiles, and alleviated hepatic steatosis. It increased beneficial gut bacteria (Bacteroidetes, Lactobacillales) and partially restored Lachnospiraceae and Muribaculaceae, modulated amino acid metabolism and AGE-RAGE/mTOR pathways, and activated hepatic PPARγ signaling while regulating fatty acid and arachidonic acid metabolism. In HepG2 cells, ZQC reduced lipid droplet accumulation, lowered triglyceride and total cholesterol levels, enhanced glucose uptake, downregulated key gluconeogenic and lipogenic proteins, and moderately decreased PPARγ and its downstream transporters (CD36, FABP4). N-phenethylhexadecanamide, palmitic acid, and isoflavone likely mediate these effects. Collectively, ZQC improves glucose and lipid metabolism in T2DM with MASLD by modulating gut microbiota, moderately regulating PPARγ signaling, and enhancing β-cell function, providing mechanistic insight into its therapeutic potential.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male Lepr-/-(db/db) mice (7 weeks of age, approximately 40 g), a widely used model of type 2 diabetes mellitus that spontaneously develops obesity and metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), were used in this study. Age-matched male Lepr+/-(db/m) mice (approximately 20 g) served as controls. All animals were housed under standardized specific pathogen-free conditions with controlled temperature and humidity and a 12 h light/dark cycle, with free access to standard chow and water.</p><p>After one week of acclimation, db/m mice were assigned to the control group (CON, n = 8), while db/db mice were randomly divided into the model group (MOD), ZQC treatment group (ZQC), and metformin treatment group (MET) (n = 8 per group). ZQC was administered by oral gavage at a dose of 5.0 g/kg/day, and metformin was administered at 183 mg/kg/day. Mice in the MOD group received an equivalent volume of 0.9% NaCl. All treatments were continued for 12 weeks, during which body weight, food intake, and water consumption were monitored weekly.</p><p>Fresh fecal samples were collected from individual mice at the end of the experimental period. Samples were obtained immediately after spontaneous defecation to avoid contamination, transferred into sterile tubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until metabolomic analysis. No freeze–thaw cycles were allowed prior to sample processing.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed on an Agilent 7890B-5977B gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) system equipped with an Agilent J&amp;W Scientific DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Folsom, CA, USA). High-purity helium (≥99.999%) was used as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. The injector temperature was set at 260 ℃, with an injection volume of 1 μL in splitless mode and a solvent delay of 6.2 min. The oven temperature was programmed as follows: initial temperature of 60 ℃ held for 0.5 min, ramped to 125 ℃ at 8 ℃/min, then to 210 ℃ at 8 ℃/min, further to 270 ℃ at 15 ℃/min, and finally to 305 ℃ at 20 ℃/min, where it was held for 5 min.</p>	<p>In this study, raw data (.D format) acquired in GC_MS full scan mode were converted to ABF (Analysis Base File) format using AnalysisBaseFileConverter software for data format adaptation to enable rapid data retrieval. The converted data could be directly imported into subsequent analytical software for qualitative and quantitative analysis of metabolites.</p>	<p>Samples were weighed into centrifuge tubes containing two steel beads, and 400 μL of methanol_water (4:1, v/v) with mixed internal standards was added. After pre_cooling at −40 ℃ for 2 min, the mixture was ground at 45 Hz for 2 min, followed by ultrasonic extraction in an ice_water bath for 30 min. The samples were then stored at −40 ℃ overnight. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm and 4 ℃ for 10 min, 120 μL of supernatant was transferred to a glass derivatization vial and evaporated to dryness under vacuum.</p><p>For derivatization, 80 μL of 15 mg/mL methoxylamine hydrochloride in pyridine was added, and oximation was performed at 37 ℃ for 60 min in a shaking incubator. Then, 50 μL of BSTFA derivatization reagent, 20 μL of n_hexane, and 10 μL of internal standard mixture (C8/C9/C10/C12/C14/C16/C18/C20/C22/C24 in chloroform) were added successively. The derivatization was carried out at 70 ℃ for 60 min, followed by equilibration at room temperature for 30 min prior to GC_MS analysis.</p><p>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all sample extracts.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry analysis was performed using the mass spectrometer coupled with an Agilent 7890B-5977B gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) system. An electron impact (EI) ion source was employed in positive ionization mode with an electron energy of 70 eV. The temperature parameters were set as follows: ion source temperature 230 ℃, quadrupole temperature 150 ℃. Full scan (SCAN) mode was used with a mass scan range of m/z 50–500.</p>	<p>Raw data (.D format) acquired in GC_MS full scan mode were converted to ABF format using AnalysisBaseFileConverter software. The converted data were imported into MS_DIAL software for preprocessing, including peak detection, deconvolution, and peak alignment. Chromatographic peaks were extracted and background noise was removed. Metabolite identification and quantification were performed by similarity matching based on retention time, fragment ion mass spectra, and corresponding databases. A raw data matrix containing sample information, retention time, mass_to_charge ratio, and peak area was finally exported.</p><p>For qualitative analysis, common metabolites were identified by matching against the OE Biotech self_developed LuMet_GC 5.0 database (containing &gt;2500 GC_MS detectable metabolites with m/z 85-650, covering lipids, amino acids, and other compound classes, with HMDB and KEGG identifiers included). For volatile compounds and headspace GC_MS experiments of similar substances, the NIST database was used for identification.</p>

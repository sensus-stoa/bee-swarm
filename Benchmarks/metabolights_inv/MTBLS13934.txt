Study Identifier	MTBLS13934
Study Title	Integrated transcriptome-metabolome analysis reveals attenuated combined toxicity: Antagonistic interactions of three thermal processing contaminants in murine kidney-urine systems
Study Description	<p>To investigate the toxicological effects after individual or combined sub-chronical exposure to oxidized triacylglycerols, aldehydes, and 3-MCPDE formed during thermal processing of high-fat foods, this study implemented transcriptome-metabolome analysis of renal tissues in Kunming mice, supplemented with urine metabolomics study. Transcriptomic analysis highlighted perturbations in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, while metabolomic and integrated analyses both identified disruptions in glycerophospholipid metabolism. Urine metabolomic results further corroborated renal dysfunction, with altered tryptophan and purine metabolism. Results revealed that the exposure of three toxicants induced multi-organ metabolic network encompassing the kidney, liver, and even nervous system. Toxic effects were less pronounced when the three toxicants were exposed in combination while compared to their individual effects, suggesting the necessity for further investigation in the potential antagonistic mechanisms. Results provide critical insights into the co-exposure toxicity and interaction mechanisms of thermally induced toxicants in high-fat foods, offering toxicological foundations for establishing safety regulations.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) coupled with a Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo Fisher, Germany) in both ESI positive (+) and negative () ion modes. The chromatographic column used was a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B), with a gradient program of 012 min, 1100% B, and 1213 min, held at 100% B. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, the injection volume was 1.0μL, and the column temperature was 40 °C .</p>	<p>Raw chromatographic and MS data of metabolites were obtained using Xcalibur 3.0 software and processed for peak picking, alignment, and normalization using Progenesis QI v2.3 (Waters Co., Milford, MA). Metabolites in the four experimental groups were identified through the online Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, website: https://hmdb.ca/).</p>	<p>Based on the standardized metabolomic sample extraction protocol established by the university instrumental analysis center, accurately weighted 50 mg of kidney samples were grinded for 3 min in a 300 µL extractant of methanol: acetonitrile: water = 2:2:1 and vortexed. The samples were kept at -20&nbsp;for 1 h, then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C and obtained the supernatants. Another one aliquot of 200 μL of extractant was added into centrifugal sediment for repeating the extraction. Following combination of supernatant parts in two extractions and dryness using a centrifugal concentrator, add 100 μL of methanol-water solvent (methanol/water = 3/7) for dissolution, vortex samples for 1 min, centrifuge for 10 min at 12,000 rpm at 4 , and transfer 80 μL of the supernatant to vials with the inserted tubes for analyses. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equivalent volumes (10 µL) of all samples. Urine sample processing followed a protocol similar to that used for kidney tissue: 100 µL of urine was treated with 100 µL extractant (methanol/acetonitrile = 1/1). Subsequent procedures and QC sample preparation were identical to the kidney tissue workflow.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry conditions were as follows: spray voltage, 2.8 kV (negative mode) and 3.2 kV (positive mode); scan range, 671000 amu; and capillary</p><p>temperature, 320 °C.</p>	<p>All unique components of the chromatogram were identified and, where possible, matched to the online Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, website: https://hmdb.ca/), which supports mice biomarker annotation since such annotation relies on matching physicochemical properties (not species specificity), and mammalian metabolic pathways (e.g., lipid metabolism) are highly conserved with core biomarkers sharing identical chemical structures between mice and humans (Wishart et al., 2007).</p>	<p>At the 90th day of feeding, urine samples were collected through metabolic cages. After anesthesia, all mice were euthanized via CO2 asphyxiation combined with cervical dislocation to minimize animal suffering and ensure rapid and humane termination. This procedure complies with experimental animal ethical guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and kidneys were carefully collected and stored at -80 °C for further analyses. The animal study was approved by IACUC of Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Approval Number: A2023111), and strictly complied with the “Guidelines for the Use and Management of Laboratory Animals” issued by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) of the USA and the relevant regulations of the ARRIVE Guidelines 2.0 (https://arriveguidelines.org) to improve the standardization of experimental design.</p><p><br></p>

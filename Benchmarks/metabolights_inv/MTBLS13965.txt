Study Title	Mild cold exposure mitigates ischemic heart injury through indole-3-acetamide–AHR-mediated SMAD2 proteolysis
Study Description	<p>Ambient temperature profoundly influences organismal metabolism,&nbsp;physiology,&nbsp;and&nbsp;disease. While cold exposure is a well-recognized risk factor for&nbsp;the onset&nbsp;of myocardial infarction (MI), its impact on the&nbsp;injured heart&nbsp;after MI remains&nbsp;unexplored. Here,&nbsp;we demonstrate&nbsp;that a mild cold exposure (MCE) regimen&nbsp;at 16oC improved&nbsp;mice systolic function post-MI compared to the controls kept at room temperature. Integrative multi-omics analyses revealed a critical link between a cold-inducible tryptophan&nbsp;metabolite indole-3-acetamide (I3AM) and&nbsp;cardiac fibrosis in post-MI hearts. MCE elevated I3AM&nbsp;levels&nbsp;in&nbsp;serum and heart&nbsp;tissue&nbsp;and&nbsp;upregulated gut indole-related metabolic&nbsp;pathways; which was necessary to&nbsp;attenuate cardiac fibrotic remodeling by&nbsp;inhibiting SMAD2/3 signaling and fibroblast&nbsp;activation. Mechanistically,&nbsp;I3AM acts as a ligand for the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR). I3AM-bound AHR promoted ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of SMAD2. Treatment with&nbsp;I3AM recapitulated the cardioprotective effects of MCE, while&nbsp;genetic or pharmacological inhibition&nbsp;of AHR&nbsp;abolished&nbsp;the benefits&nbsp;of both MCE and I3AM in MI mice. In patients with and without heart failure, serum I3AM levels correlated positively with cardiac function,&nbsp;underscoring&nbsp;its&nbsp;clinical relevance. Our findings unveil a previously unrecognized&nbsp;metabolic-proteolytic axis&nbsp;triggered by&nbsp;MCE and identify the&nbsp;temperature responsive I3AM-AHR-SMAD&nbsp;pathway as a&nbsp;potential therapeutic target for ischemic hearts.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cardiac tissue was homogenized in 200 μL of ultrapure water, and metabolites were extracted by adding 800 μL of a methanol/acetonitrile mixture (1:1, v/v). After centrifugation (14,000 × g, 20 minutes, 4°C), the supernatant was collected, lyophilized, and reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). The sample was centrifuged again (14,000 × g, 15 minutes, 4°C), and the final supernatant was subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Heart tissues were homogenized in 200 μL of ultrapure water, and metabolites were extracted by adding 800 μL of methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). After centrifugation (14,000 g, 20 min, 4°C), the supernatants were collected, lyophilized and reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). The samples were centrifuged again (14,000, 15 min, 4°C) and the final supernatants were subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>All samples were for two LC/MS methods. One aliquot was analyzed using positive ion </p><p>conditions and was eluted from T3 column (Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column 1.8 </p><p>µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm) using 0.1 % formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1 % formic acid </p><p>in acetonitrile as solvent B in the following gradient: 5 to 20 % in 1 min, increased to 99 % </p><p>in the following 2 mins and held for 1.5 min, then come back to 5 % mobile phase B witnin </p><p>0.1 min, held for 1.4 min. The analytical conditions were as follows, column temperature, </p><p>40 °C; flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; injection volume, 4 μL; Another aliquot was using negative ion </p><p>conditions and was the same as the elution gradient of positive mode.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were converted from the original format to mzML format using ProteoWizard. Peak picking, alignment, and retention time correction were then performed using the XCMS software. Peaks with a missing rate greater than 50% across samples in any group were filtered out. For missing values, a combined imputation method was applied: if the proportion of missing values was greater than 50%, they were replaced with 1/5 of the minimum peak area; if the proportion was less than 50%, they were imputed using the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) method. Finally, the peak areas were corrected using the Support Vector Regression (SVR) method.</p>	<p>The sample stored at -80 °C refrigerator was thawed on ice. The thawed sample was </p><p>homogenized by a grinder (30 HZ) for 20 s. A 400 μL solution (Methanol : Water = 7:3, V/V) </p><p>containing internal standard was added in to 20 mg grinded sample, and shaked at 1500 </p><p>rpm for 5 min. After placing on ice for 15 min, the sample was centrifuged at 12000 rpm for </p><p>10 min (4 °C). A 300 μL of supernatant was collected and placed in -20 °C for 30 min. The </p><p>sample was then centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 3 min (4 °C). A 200 μL aliquots of </p><p>supernatant were transferred for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>All the methods alternated between full scan MS and data dependent MSn scans using </p><p>dynamic exclusion. MS analyses were carried out using electrospray ionization in the </p><p>positive ion mode and negative ion mode using full scan analysis over m/z 75-1000 at </p><p>35000 resolution. Additional MS settings are: ion spray voltage, 3.5 KV or 3.2 KV in positive </p><p>or negative modes, respevtively; Sheath gas (Arb), 30; Aux gas, 5; Ion transfer tube </p><p>temperature, 320 °C; Vaporizer temperature, 300 °C; Collision energy, 30,40,50 V; Signal </p><p>Intensity Threshold, 1.00E+06 cps; Top N vs Top speed, 10; Exclusion duration, 3s.</p>	<p>The corrected and filtered peaks were identified by searching against an in-house database, integrated public databases, and predictive databases. Subsequently, metabolites with an identification composite score above 0.5 and a coefficient of variation (CV) value below 0.3 in QC samples were extracted. Peaks identified in both positive and negative ionization modes were then merged, retaining the substance entry with the highest qualitative level and the smallest CV value. The final consolidated data was saved in the file all_sample_data.xlsx.</p>

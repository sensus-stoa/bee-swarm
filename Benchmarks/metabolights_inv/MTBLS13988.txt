Study Description	<p>Cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome is a complex systemic disorder. In this study, we developed a urea-activated nanoparticle (T-PAAD NP) for targeted delivery of Empagliflozin to the renal S1 segment. Untargeted GC-MS-based metabolomics was performed to characterize metabolic reprogramming in kidney tissues of a CKM mouse model.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Kidney tissues were rapidly harvested from CKM model and control mice after euthanasia. Samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until extraction to ensure metabolic stability</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on an Agilent 7890A GC system. Helium was used as the carrier gas at a constant flow of 0.56 mL/min. The injection volume was 2 µL in splitless mode with the inlet temperature at 250°C. The oven program started at 60°C (1 min), then increased to 320°C at 10°C/min and held for 5 min. The total run time was 32 minutes.</p>	<p>Raw GC-MS data were acquired using MSD Chemstation software (E.02.02, Agilent). The data files were imported into AMDIS for mass spectral deconvolution and peak area extraction, with a peak area threshold set at 3000 and a relative abundance threshold of 1%. Post-deconvolution, only metabolic features present in more than 80% of the samples were retained to eliminate noise from column bleed or derivatization reagents. Finally, peak areas were normalized to the internal standard (D27-Myristate acid) for subsequent multivariate statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Extraction: Tissue samples were homogenized in a cold methanol:chloroform:water (5:2:2, v/v/v) extraction solvent at a 1:10 mass-to-volume ratio, with D27-Myristate acid added as an internal standard. After vortexing and centrifugation at 12,000 g (4°C, 10 min), 300 µL of supernatant was transferred and completely dried using a vacuum freeze dryer.</p><p>Derivatization: The dried residues were first methoximated with 100 µL of methoxyamine hydrochloride in pyridine (10 mg/mL) at 37°C for 1 h. Subsequently, 100 µL of BSTFA (containing 1% TMCS) was added for silylation at 60°C for 1 h. Samples were then equilibrated to room temperature before GC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>MS detection was performed using an Agilent 5975C MSD in Full Scan mode. The electron impact ionization energy was 70 eV. The ion source temperature was set to 230°C and the quadrupole to 150°C. The transfer line temperature was maintained at 320°C. The solvent delay was 6.20 minutes.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by searching against the Agilent Fiehn GC/MS Metabolomics Retention Time Locking (RTL) Library. Identification reliability was further enhanced by locking the absolute retention time using the internal standard D27-Myristate acid. For unidentified features, mass spectra were compared with the NIST 11 library to provide putative annotations where possible.</p>

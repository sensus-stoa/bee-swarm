Study Title	Mechanistic profiling of Bifemetstrobin against the tea anthracnose pathogen and candidate evaluation of an amine oxidase target
Study Description	<p>Tea anthracnose caused by Glomerella cingulata threatens yield and quality, while resistance to legacy fungicides highlights the need for new modes of action. We characterized the antifungal activity and mechanism of bifemetstrobin against G. cingulata&nbsp;by combining efficacy assays with cell biology and integrated multi omics. Bifemetstrobin inhibited mycelial growth (EC50&nbsp;= 5.04 μg/mL) and spore germination and provided up to approximately 80% curative suppression on detached tea leaves. Microscopy revealed dose dependent hyphal and conidial deformation and mitochondrial injury. Consistently, bifemetstrobin collapsed mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), reduced intracellular ATP, and increased membrane leakage with depletion of reducing sugars and soluble proteins. Transcriptome, data-independent acquisition (DIA) proteomics and metabolome data converged on reprogramming of carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism, membrane processes, and redox homeostasis, with DNA replication signatures emerging at the higher dose. Cross-layer mapping prioritized an amine oxidase (AO) as a hub. Bifemetstrobin bound AO with micromolar affinity (microscale thermophoresis/isothermal titration calorimetry), supported by docking and molecular dynamics, and outperformed azoxystrobin in binding. Pairing bifemetstrobin with pyrrolnitrin yielded strong in vitro synergy and improved leaf protection. Across additional phytopathogens, bifemetstrobin retained activity in vitro (EC50&nbsp;range 1.19–27.38 μg/mL), outperforming azoxystrobin on the tea anthracnose strain. These findings nominate AO as a high confidence candidate supported by binding assays and link target engagement to mitochondrial failure and carbon-nitrogen reprogramming, supporting bifemetstrobin (alone or in rational mixtures) as a promising candidate for tea anthracnose management.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Scientific UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Parameter Value[Chromatography Instrument]: Thermo Scientific UltiMate 3000 HPLC) equipped with an autosampler (Parameter Value[Autosampler model]: Thermo Scientific UltiMate 3000 Autosampler). A Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (Parameter Value[Column model]: Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3, 100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) was used for metabolite separation, which is suitable for non-targeted metabolomics due to its excellent separation performance for polar and non-polar metabolites. A matching guard column (Parameter Value[Guard column]: Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 Guard Column, 20 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) was installed to protect the analytical column and improve detection stability. </p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using PeakView 2.2 software (SCIEX) for peak extraction, peak alignment, deconvolution, and normalization to obtain a matrix of metabolite peaks with retention time, m/z value, and peak intensity. The processed data were then subjected to multivariate statistical analysis (including principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA)) to screen for differential metabolites between the EC30 treatment group and the CK group.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction, the fungal samples were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen to quench metabolic activity, ground into fine powder, and extracted with a mixture of methanol:water (4:1, v/v) under ice bath conditions; the extract was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm organic membrane for subsequent LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite detection was carried out using a TripleTOF 6600+ high-resolution tandem quadrupole-time-of-flight mass spectrometer (SCIEX, Massachusetts, USA) coupled with the HPLC system. The key mass spectrometry parameters were set as follows: Parameter Value[Instrument]: SCIEX TripleTOF 6600+; Parameter Value[Scan m/z range]: 50-1500 m/z; Parameter Value[Ion source]: Electrospray Ionization (ESI); Parameter Value[Mass analyzer]: Quadrupole-Time of Flight (Q-TOF).</p>	<p>For metabolite identification, the accurate mass of each peak (mass error &lt; 10 ppm) was matched with the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Metlin, and KEGG databases; the MS/MS fragmentation patterns were further compared with the database spectra to confirm the structure of metabolites. Only metabolites with a matching score ≥80 and a false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05 were considered reliably identified.</p>	<p>Samples were collected from mycelia of Glomerella cingulata treated with bifemetstrobin at EC30 dose for 24 hours, with 6 biological replicates in the treatment group (EC30) and 6 biological replicates in the control group (CK)</p>

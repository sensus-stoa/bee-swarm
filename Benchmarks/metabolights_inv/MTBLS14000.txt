Study Identifier	MTBLS14000
Study Title	Gut microbiota-derived deoxycholic acid promotes pancreatic regeneration following acute pancreatitis via Reg3a signaling pathway
Study Description	<p>Recovery from acute pancreatitis (AP) involves complex biological processes, and the mechanisms governing pancreatic repair remain unclear. In a clinical cohort during the recovery phase of AP, impaired pancreatic recovery was associated with intestinal dysbiosis, depletion of the microbial bile salt hydrolase gene (bsh), and reduced levels of the secondary bile acid deoxycholic acid (DCA). Consistently, in mouse models, depletion or reshaping of the gut microbiota markedly altered the course of pancreatic repair, whereas supplementation of mice with BSH-overexpressing strains or direct administration of DCA accelerated recovery. Mechanistic investigations further indicated that DCA activates the nuclear receptor pathway of PXR/RXR, subsequently enhancing transcription of the regeneration-associated gene Reg3α. Elevated Reg3α expression further upregulated Ptf1a, a critical factor maintaining acinar cell identity, thereby inhibiting aberrant ADM and facilitating recovery of damaged acinar cell function. Together, these findings identify a gut microbiota related signaling axis associated with pancreatic repair after AP.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on an ExionLC AD system using a Waters BEH C18 column (150 × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm) at 50°C. The mobile phases were 0.1% formic acid in water and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min with a 5 μL injection volume.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed in AB Sciex OS software using automatic peak recognition and integration with manual inspection. Metabolite concentrations were calculated from analyte-to-internal-standard peak area ratios using linear regression calibration curves.</p>	<p>Samples were extracted with methanol-based solvent containing isotope-labeled internal standards. After homogenization or vortexing, sonication, precipitation, centrifugation, and where required nitrogen drying and reconstitution, the resulting supernatants were subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Targeted bile acid analysis was performed on an AB SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ mass spectrometer using electrospray ionization in negative mode. The main source parameters were CUR 35, CAD medium, IS -4500 V, TEM 550°C, GS1 50, and GS2 50.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified and quantified using a targeted bile acid panel containing 47 authentic standards together with isotope-labeled internal standards. Identification was based on predefined MRM transitions and standard-based LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mouse pancreas, serum, and fecal samples were collected for targeted bile acid metabolomics analysis. Samples were processed and stored under low-temperature conditions before extraction and LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>

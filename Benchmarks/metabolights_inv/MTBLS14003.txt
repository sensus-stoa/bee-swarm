Study Identifier	MTBLS14003
Study Title	Metabolome of aluminum-tolerant phosphate-solubilizing fungi
Study Description	<p>We constructed a bacterial fungal synthetic community and found that it has stronger phosphorus solubilization ability than a single bacterium or fungus. Among them, fungi mainly play a role in phosphorus solubilization, while bacteria can promote fungal phosphorus solubilization. Through metabolomics, the mechanism of fungal phosphorus solubilization is analyzed to further explore whether fungi utilize bacterial metabolites to promote phosphorus solubilization. It was found that fungi mainly utilize organic acids to dissolve phosphorus, while bacterial metabolite whey acid can be utilized by fungi to promote their ability to dissolve phosphorus.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic conditions: 3 μL of the sample was separated on an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm, Waters, USA) and then subjected to mass spectrometry detection. Mobile phase A is 95% water+5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B is 47.5% acetonitrile+47.5% isopropanol+5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid). The flow rate is 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature is 40℃.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The raw data was imported into the metabolomics processing software ProgenesQI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, etc. are used to obtain the final results including retention time, mass to charge ratio, and peak intensity. A data matrix of degree and other information. </p>	<p>The supernatant (100 μL) was added with 400 μL extraction solution (methanol: water=4:1) containing 0.02 mg mL-1 of internal standard (L-2-chloroalanine). The mixture was ultrasonically extracted for 30 min (5℃, 40 KHz), centrifuged for 15 min (4℃, 13000 g), blow-dried with nitrogen, and then dissolved with 100 μL solution (acetonitrile: water=1:1). The extraction was centrifuged for 10 min, and the supernatant was transferred to an injection vial with an inner tube for UPLC-MS (UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X, Thermos, USA) analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry conditions: The sample mass spectrometry signal is collected using positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scanning range of 70-1050 m/z. The sheath gas flow rate is 50psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate is 13 psi, the auxiliary gas heating temperature is 425℃, the positive mode ion spray voltage is set to 3500 V, the negative mode ion spray voltage is set to -3500 V, the ion transfer tube temperature is 325℃, and the normalized collision energy is 20-40-60V cyclic collision energy. The resolution of primary mass spectrometry is 60000, and the resolution of secondary mass spectrometry is 7500. Data is collected using DDA mode.</p>	<p>Afterwards, the software was used for feature peak search and identification, and the MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry information was compared with the representative data. Match with the Xie database, set the MS mass error to less than 10 ppm, and identify metabolites based on the secondary mass spectrometry matching score.</p>	<p>Fungi <em>T. fuscoviridis</em><strong> </strong>were cultured in PKO medium, and the supernatant was collected to conduct metabolomics analysis.</p>

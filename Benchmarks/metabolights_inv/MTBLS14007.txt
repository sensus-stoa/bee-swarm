Study Title	Digital Phenomics and Dual-Fluid Metabolomics for Precision Stratification of Diabetic Kidney Disease: A Multicenter Cohort Study
Study Description	<p>We conducted a prospective-retrospective two-center cohort study involving 364 participants. The Discovery Cohort (n=282) enrolled from two centers consisted of healthy controls (n=80), patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) without kidney disease (n=102), and patients with confirmed DKD (n=100). An independent External Validation Cohort (n=82) was established to assess model generalizability. We developed a standardized 'Digital Urine' analysis pipeline using computer vision algorithms to quantify chromatic and foam stability phenotypes. Concurrently, dual-fluid metabolomics (untargeted LC-MS/MS and targeted 1H-NMR) was performed on serum and urine to map metabolic flux. The primary outcome was the construction and validation of a multi-modal disease classifier. The secondary outcome was the identification of metabolite flux ratios reflecting renal tubular transporter function.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The LC analysis was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).</p><p>Chromatography was carried out with an ACQUITY UPLC ® HSS T3 (2.1×100 mm, 1.8 µm)</p><p>(Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The column maintained at 40 ℃. The flow rate and injection volume</p><p>were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively. For LC-ESI (+)-MS analysis, the mobile phases</p><p>consisted of (B2) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (v/v) and (A2) 0.1% formic acid in water (v/v).</p><p>Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0~1 min, 10% B2; 1~5 min, 10%~98% B2;</p><p>5~6.5 min, 98% B2; 6.5~6.6 min, 98%~10% B2; 6.6~8 min, 10% B2. For LC-ESI (-)-MS analysis,</p><p>the analytes was carried out with (B3) acetonitrile and (A3) ammonium formate (5mM).</p><p>Separation was conducted under the following gradient: 0~1 min, 10% B3; 1~5 min, 10%~98% B3;</p><p>5~6.5 min, 98% B3; 6.5~6.6 min, 98%~10% B3; 6.6~8 min, 10% B3</p>	<p> The raw data were firstly converted to mzXML format by MSConvert in ProteoWizard&nbsp;&nbsp;software&nbsp;package&nbsp;(v3.0.8789)[1]&nbsp;and processed using R XCMS(v3.12.0) for feature detection[2], retention time correction and alignment. Key parameters settings were set as follows: ppm=15, peakwidth=c(5, 30), mzdiff=0.01, method=centWave. The batch effect was then eliminated by correcting the data based on QC samples. Metabolites with RSD &gt; 30% in QC samples were filtered and then used for subsequent data analysis.</p>	<p>1. Thaw the experimental sample at 4℃, vortex the sample for 1 min after thawing, and mix</p><p>evenly;</p><p>2. Accurately transfer an appropriate amount of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, add 100μL</p><p>2-Amino-3-(2-chloro-phenyl)-propionic acid (4 ppm) solution prepared with 80% methanol water</p><p>and vortex for 1 min;</p><p>3. Centrifuge for 10 min at 12,000 rpm and 4℃,filter the supernatant by 0.22 μm membrane and</p><p>transfer into the detection bottle for LC-MS detection.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection of metabolites was performed on Orbitrap Exploris 120 (Thermo</p><p>Fisher Scientific, USA) with ESI ion source. Simultaneous MS1 and MS/MS (Full MS-ddMS2 mode,</p><p>data-dependent MS/MS) acquisition was used. The parameters were as follows: sheath gas</p><p>pressure, 40 arb; aux gas flow, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV and -2.50 kV for ESI(+) and ESI(-),</p><p>respectively; capillary temperature, 325 ℃; MS1 range, m/z 100-1000; MS1 resolving power,</p><p>60000 FWHM; number of data dependant scans per cycle, 4; MS/MS resolving power, 15000 FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30%; dynamic exclusion time, automatic</p>	<p> The metabolites were identified by accuracy mass and MS/MS data which were matched with HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca)[8], massbank (http://www.massbank.jp/)[9], KEGG (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/)[12], LipidMaps (http://www.lipidmaps.org)[10], mzcloud (https://www.mzcloud.org)[11]&nbsp;and the metabolite database. The molecular weight of metabolites was determined according to the m/z (mass-to-charge ratio) of parent ions in MS data. Molecular formula was predicted by ppm (parts per million) and adduct ion, and then matched with the database. At the same time, the MS/MS data from quantitative table of MS/MS data, were matched with the fragment ions and other information of each metabolite in the database, so as to realize the MS/MS identification of metabolites.</p>	<p>Urine samples were collected from the subjects during their physical examination period. Morning mid-stream urine (30 mL) was uniformly collected. Prior to collection, the researchers informed the subjects that they were required to fast for 8-12 hours, and their dietary intake and medication usage from the previous day were recorded in detail. Subjects were excluded if they had consumed any foods or medications within 24 hours before sampling that could potentially affect the color of the urine.</p><p>Blood samples were collected from the subjects in the early morning on the day of their physical examination. All subjects were required to fast for 8-12 hours before blood collection to minimize the impact of dietary factors on blood metabolite levels. Venous blood (5 mL) was collected by professionally trained medical personnel using disposable sterile vacuum blood collection tubes. Strict aseptic procedures were followed during blood collection to avoid hemolysis and exogenous contamination.</p>

Study Title	A gut-associated Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group lineage modulates host metabolic pathways and enhances feed efficiency in ducks
Study Description	<p>Amidst the mismatch between domestic feed supply and demand in China, along with the competition for food between humans and animals, optimizing feed efficiency has become crucial for industry growth. Nevertheless, the biological processes driving variations in feed efficiency remain unexplored. This research integrated analyses of production performance, gut microbiota, and host gene expression in ducks with varying feed efficiencies to investigate the regulatory mechanisms focused on intestinal metabolism.In this study, compared to LFE ducks, HFE ducks showed improved growth performance, including higher ADG, FE, and muscle deposition, alongside a reduced F/G and lower AFP, with no difference in ADFI. Further investigation revealed that the jejunum of HFE ducks exhibits a higher VH/CD ratio, as well as a general enhancement of the activity of key digestive enzymes, such as trypsin and lipase. In the jejunal contents, although overall microbial diversity remained unchanged, HFE ducks exhibited a marked increase in jejunal Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group. Their abundances strongly correlated with host metabolites involved in amino acid and energy metabolism, suggesting a pivotal role in mediating feed efficiency. In the jejunal mucosa, transcriptomic analysis combined with RT-qPCR verification revealed coordinated upregulation of genes (e.g., PHGDH, PSAT1, SHMT2, PKM) driving the serine synthesis pathway, one-carbon metabolism, and glycolysis, indicating metabolic reprogramming. This metabolic state was accompanied by an enrichment of metabolites supporting protein synthesis and lipid catabolism. These host changes were strongly correlated with the enriched Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group.The enhanced feed efficiency in HFE ducks was linked to enrichment of the gut-associated Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group lineage in the jejunum, co-occurring with host metabolic reprogramming toward serine synthesis, glycolysis, and anabolic metabolism. These coordinated microbial and host alterations collectively underpinned the growth phenotype, providing novel insights into feed efficiency mediated by host-microbial metabolic interactions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Scientific™ Accucore™ 150 amide column (2.6 µm, 100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d.). The mobile phase consisted of ultrapure water containing 10 mM ammonium acetate (A) and acetonitrile (B) at a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C and the injection volume was 2 μL. The gradient elution program was set as follows: 0–1 min, 5% A and 95% B; 6–7.5 min, 50% A and 50% B; 7.6–10 min, 5% A and 95% B (V/V).</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were processed using Analyst 1.6.3 and MultiQuant 3.0.3 software. Peak integration and correction were conducted based on retention time and peak shape of reference standards. Before multivariate statistical analysis, metabolite intensities were normalized by unit variance scaling (UV scaling).</p>	<p>The samples were thawed on ice, and all subsequent procedures were performed on ice. A 50 mg aliquot of sample was weighed into a centrifuge tube, and 500 μL of methanol solution containing 10 μL of internal standard working solution at a concentration of 500 ng/mL was added. After vortex mixing for 3 min, the mixture was allowed to stand at -20 °C for 30 min. Following centrifugation at 12000 r/min for 10 min at 4 °C, 250 μL of the supernatant was transferred into a new centrifuge tube. After another centrifugation, 150 μL of the supernatant was transferred into an autosampler vial and stored temporarily at -20 °C.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was performed on a Q Trap 6500 system equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The ion source temperature was set at 550 °C, the ion spray voltage was 5500 V, and the curtain gas (CUR) was 35 psi. Each ion transition was monitored using the optimized declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using the MetWare Database (MWDB) established with authentic standards, combined with retention time and MRM transition matching on a Q Trap 6500 system. Quantification was achieved in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode with optimized declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE) for each metabolite. Calibration curves were constructed with serial standard dilutions; sample concentrations were calculated via linear regression and converted to final contents based on sample weight and extraction volume.</p>	<p>A group of ten samples of intestinal mucosal samples of the jejunum was collected, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80°C for subsequent assays.</p>

Study Title	Multi-Omics Analysis Reveals Alcohol exposure affects Ferroptosis pathway in Brain
Study Description	<p>Alcohol-related brain damage (ARBD) constitutes a major global public health issue and is often comorbid with neuropsychiatric disorders such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases. Ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death characterized by iron dependency and lipid peroxidation, has been implicated in the pathogenesis of various neurodegenerative disorders. However, the role and underlying mechanisms of ferroptosis in mediating ARBD and its associated neuropsychiatric dysfunctions remain largely unexplored. This study aimed to investigate whether chronic alcohol exposure induces neurodegenerative-like behavioral phenotypes in mice and to identify associated molecular alterations using a multi-omics approach. Behavioral assessments demonstrated that chronic alcohol exposure led to impairments in memory, exploratory activity, and motor coordination. Histopathological analysis revealed significant neuronal damage in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. Integrated proteomic and metabolomic profiling identified a significant enrichment of pathways related to ferroptosis and neurodegenerative diseases in alcohol-exposed mice.&nbsp;Additionally, biochemical assays further confirmed alcohol induced disruption of iron homeostasis, elevated reactive oxygen species, and disruption of lipid peroxidation-key features consistent with ferroptosis activation. Taken together, these findings indicate that chronic alcohol exposure induces behavioral deficits and neuronal injury in mice, and is associated with molecular and biochemical changes indicative of ferroptosis.&nbsp;This multi-omics evidence suggests ferroptosis as a plausible contributing pathway in alcohol-related brain damage, offering new directions for understanding its pathophysiology.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Throughout the analysis, samples were placed in an autosampler maintained at 4 °C. Chromatographic separation was performed using a SHIMADZU-LC30 UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The injection volume was 16 μL, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 0.1% formic acid in water and (B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–2 min, 0% B; 2–6 min, linear increase from 0% to 48% B; 6–10 min, linear increase from 48% to 100% B; 10–12 min, maintained at 100% B; 12–12.1 min, linear decrease from 100% to 0% B; and 12.1–15 min, maintained at 0% B.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using MS-DIAL software for peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction. From the extracted data, ion peaks with more than 50% missing values within any group were excluded from subsequent statistical analysis. Positive and negative ion mode data were separately normalized by total peak area, then integrated for pattern recognition using Python software. Following Unit Variance (UV) scaling preprocessing, the data were subjected to downstream analysis.</p>	<p>Brain tissue samples were thawed at 4 °C, and 50 mg of tissue from each sample was weighed and placed into a microcentrifuge tube. A volume of 400 μL of pre-cooled methanol: water solution (4:1, v/v) was added. The mixture was homogenized under low-temperature conditions using a tissue grinder, followed by sonication in an ice bath for 20 minutes. Samples were then incubated at room temperature (20 °C) for 1 hour and centrifuged at 16,000 × g at 4 °C for 20 minutes. A portion of the resulting supernatant was collected for subsequent metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Each sample was analyzed using electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive (+) and negative (-) ion modes. Following UPLC separation, mass spectrometric analysis was performed on a Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. The ionization parameters were set as follows: spray voltage, 3.8 kV (positive mode) and 3.2 kV (negative mode); capillary temperature, 320 °C; sheath gas flow rate, 30 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow rate, 5 arbitrary units; probe heater temperature, 350 °C; and S-Lens RF level, 50. The mass spectrometry acquisition settings were as follows: acquisition time, 15 min; precursor ion scan range, m/z 75-1050; MS1 resolution, 70,000 at m/z 200; AGC target, 3e6; maximum injection time, 100 ms. MS/MS analysis was performed using data-dependent acquisition, where the top 10 most abundant precursor ions from each full scan were selected for fragmentation. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 17,500 at m/z 200, with an AGC target of 1e5, maximum injection time of 50 ms, and an isolation window of 2 m/z. Higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) was employed with stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 30, and 40 eV.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was achieved by matching accurate mass (mass tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS spectra (mass tolerance &lt; 0.01 Da) against public databases including HMDB, MassBank, and GNPS, as well as the in-house Bioprofile Metabolite Database (BP-DB).</p>	<p>Adult male C57BL/6J mice (8-9 weeks, 22-25g, n=30) were obtained from Shandong Pengyue Laboratory Animal Science and Technology Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China). Mice were randomly assigned to either a control group or an alcohol-fed group, with 15 animals in each group. A pair-feeding protocol was employed using the Lieber-DeCarli isocaloric liquid diet (BioServ, Frenchtown, NJ, USA). The control group received an ethanol-free diet (0% of total calories from ethanol), while the alcohol-fed group received a diet in which 36% of total caloric content was derived from ethanol. Mice were continuously fed for 8 weeks (56 days). Beginning in week 6, mice in the alcohol exposure group additionally received intragastric gavage of ethanol twice weekly at a dosage of 5 g/kg. The ethanol gavage solution was prepared by mixing 6.6 mL of 95% ethanol with 13.4 mL of distilled water, yielding a final ethanol concentration of 31.5% (v/v).After successful 8-week ARBD model establishment, 7 mice from each group were randomly selected and sacrificed, followed by immediate whole-brain dissection. The samples were stored in a -80°C freezer. Among these, six samples from each group were submitted for metabolomic and proteomic analyses, while the remaining sample was reserved as a backup.</p>

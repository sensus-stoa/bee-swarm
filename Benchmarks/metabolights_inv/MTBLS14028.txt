Study Title	Transcriptomic and Metabolomic Joint Analysis to Explore the Molecular Mechanisms of Different Resistant Poplar Varieties in Response to the Feeding Damage of Anoplophora glabripennis
Study Description	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='863c1d7e-ae90-480b-b7f9-9254a87224c3'>In China, Asian longhorned beetle (ALB) infestations severely threaten poplar resources, the primary species used for shelterbelt afforestation. Two fast-growing poplar hybrids, Populus alba × (P. alba × P. glandulosa) (QB3) and P. nigra × P. deltoids (Ta), exhibit contrasting ALB resistance: QB3 is resistant, while Ta is highly susceptible. To elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms, we examined transcriptome and metabolome changes in QB3 and Ta after ALB feeding. Transcriptomic analysis revealed significantly differential expressions of MKK9, EBF1, and PRB1 genes between QB3 and Ta under both control and infested conditions, with enrichment in the plant MAPK signaling pathway. Following insect herbivory, QB3 upregulated LOX3.1, ACX3, AOS3, PED1, and JMT genes, enriched in α-linolenic acid metabolism. Metabolomic profiling showed significant variations in α-linolenic acid abundance. After ALB feeding, QB3 exhibited a marked increase in α-linolenic acid content compared to Ta, while γ-linolenic acid levels remained unchanged. Both genotypes showed elevated jasmonoyl-isoleucine (JA-Ile) concentrations, with QB3 displaying extremely significant increases versus moderately significant increases in Ta. These results offer insights into poplar tree responses to ALB feeding damage.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='26df13fb-2551-4480-b18c-31c7b1f99cec'>The samples were separated using an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC Ultra High Performance Liquid Chromatography (UHPLC) system with a HILIC column. The column temperature was set at 25°C, the flow rate was 0.5 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The mobile phase composition was as follows: A: water + 25 mM ammonium acetate + 25 mM ammonia water, B: acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0 - 0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5 - 7 min, B linearly changed from 95% to 65%; 7 - 8 min, B linearly changed from 65% to 40%; 8 - 9 min, B maintained at 40%; 9 - 9.1 min, B linearly changed from 40% to 95%; 9.1 - 12 min, B maintained at 95%. Throughout the analysis, the samples were kept at 4°C in an autosampler. To avoid the influence of instrument detection signal fluctuations, the samples were analyzed continuously in a random order. QC samples were inserted into the sample queue to monitor and evaluate the stability of the system and the reliability of the experimental data.</p>	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='ac774d85-3286-41e0-a7de-e571b16dabc2'>The original data was converted into the .MzML format using ProteoWizard, and then peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction were performed using the XCMS program. The data extracted by XCMS was first subjected to metabolite structure identification, data preprocessing (missing value filtering: deleting ion peaks with missing values &gt; 50%; missing value filling: KNN filling: data filtering: filtering features with RSD &gt; 50%), and finally data analysis was conducted. The XCMS software parameter settings are as follows: For peak picking, centWave m/z =10 ppm, peakwidth = c (10, 60), prefilter = c (10, 100). For peak grouping, bw = 5,</p><p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='ac774d85-3286-41e0-a7de-e571b16dabc2'>mzwid = 0.025, minfrac = 0.5 were employed. The identification of metabolites was accomplished by comparing the compounds with the standard high-precision m/z values (&lt;10 ppm) and MS/MS spectra in the internal database.</p>	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='a6f785d0-89ec-4a18-a65b-689b1d8daf9a'>Samples were taken for liquid nitrogen grinding. Approximately 100 mg of each sample was weighed and 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol-acetonitrile-water solution (v/v, 2:2:1) was added. The samples were vortexed and subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic treatment for 30 minutes each, twice. They were then left to stand at -20°C for 60 minutes, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 g and 4°C for 20 minutes. The supernatant (equivalent to 50 mg of the sample) was taken, vacuum dried, and for mass spectrometry analysis, 100 μL of acetonitrile-water solution (acetonitrile: water = 1:1, v/v) was added to re-dissolve it. The mixture was vortexed and centrifuged at 14,000 g and 4°C for 15 minutes. The supernatant was taken for injection analysis. Quality control samples (QC) were prepared by mixing equal volumes of the samples to be tested. They were used to determine the instrument status before injection and to balance the chromatography-mass spectrometry system, as well as to evaluate the system stability throughout the entire experimental process.</p>	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='a2c70e3b-f2c0-49e5-bc48-057ad6f60462'>The AB TripleTOF 6600 mass spectrometer was used to collect the primary and secondary mass spectra of the samples. The ESI source conditions after UHPLC chromatographic separation were as follows: nebulizer gas, auxiliary heating gas 1 (Gas1): 60, auxiliary heating gas 2 (Gas2): 60, curtain gas (CUR): 30 psi, ion source temperature: 600°C, spray voltage (ISVF): ±5500 V (positive and negative modes); the primary mass-to-charge ratio detection range: 60-1000 Da, secondary fragment ion mass-to-charge ratio detection range: 25-1000 Da, primary mass spectrometry scan accumulation time: 0.20 s/spectrum, secondary mass spectrometry scan accumulation time: 0.05 s/spectrum; the secondary mass spectrometry was obtained using a data-dependent acquisition mode (IDA), and a peak intensity value screening mode was adopted. The declustering potential (DP): ±60 V (positive and negative modes), collision energy: 35 ± 15 eV, and the IDA settings were as follows: dynamic exclusion of isotopic ion range: 4 Da, and 10 fragment spectra were collected for each scan.</p>	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='8f3dac3d-e8a7-4713-ab4e-0215394c1ad0'> KEGG database analysis was employed for pathway enrichment. MetPA analysis was conducted using differential metabolite names and abundance data from comparative groups against the KEGG background, with betweenness centrality calculations determining pathway positions within metabolic regulatory networks. Significant enrichment of metabolites in specific pathways was evaluated via the Hypergeometric test. Integration of betweenness centrality and Hypergeometric test results identified key metabolic pathways involving differential metabolites in comparative groups.</p>	<p dy-tran-visited='youdaollm' yd-root-id='aab1f080-2cb9-40a4-9726-5f5f17437f96'>After a 24-hour feeding period, the ALB (Anoplophora glabripennis) adults were quickly removed,&nbsp;the phloem tissues from the feeding sites on the trunks were collected. Control phloem tissues were taken from the same height and position. The control group without ALB feeding of QB3 and Ta was set as QCK and TCK, respectively. The treatment group with ALB feeding of QB3 and Ta was set as QALB and TALB, respectively. The bark samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for subsequent analysis.</p>

Study Title	Ni-MOFs as a Simple and Effective Nanomedicine for ROS-Mediated Antibacterial Wound Healing via a Themoelectrocatalytic Process
Study Description	<p>Thermoelectrocatalytic nickel-based metal-organic frameworks (Ni-MOFs) autonomously generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) via human body-ambient temperature differences. They exhibit potent antibacterial activity against wound-infecting pathogens, and effectively promote infected skin wound healing without systemic toxicity, showcasing great potential for antibiotic-resistant bacterial infection treatment.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was the Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Exploris240 system with ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-tandem Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). Chromatographic conditions: The chromatographic column was an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, USA). Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water + 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B was 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid). The injection volume was 3 μL, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software ProgenesisQI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, ultimately yielding a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity information.</p>	<p>1. Transfer all samples to 2 mL centrifuge tubes and add a 6 mm diameter grinding bead; 2. Add 250 µL of extraction buffer (methanol:water = 4:1 (v:v)) containing four internal standards (L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL), etc.); 3. Grind frozen tissue in a tissue grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz); 4. Perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz); 5. Store samples at-20°C for 30 min; 6. Centrifuge for 15 min (13,000 g, 4°C), transfer the supernatant to a sample vial with an insert, and analyze on the instrument; 7. Additionally, aliquot 20 µL of supernatant from each sample, mix them, and use as quality control samples.</p>	<p>The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was the Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Exploris240 system with ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-tandem Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). The mass spectrometry was performed by electrospray ionization with positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>Subsequently, the software was employed for feature peak search and library identification, matching MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry data with metabolic databases. The MS mass error was set to less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on the matching scores from the secondary mass spectrometry. The primary databases included mainstream public databases such as http://www.hmdb.ca/ and https://metlin.scripps.edu/, as well as self-built databases.</p>	<p>Freeze-dried samples, S.aureus&nbsp;was co-incubated with Ni-MOF in TSB medium at 37 ℃&nbsp;with shaking at 230 rpm for 3 hours. After incubation, bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 5 min at room temperature. The collected bacterial pellets were weighed, and only samples with a wet weight of ≥ 50 mg were retained for metabolomics analysis.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14057
Study Title	SIRT5 alleviate cardiomyocyte senescence in radiation-induced heart disease via desuccinylation of HADHB
Study Description	<p>SIRT5 deficiency caused widespread protein hypersuccinylation in cardiac tissue. Succinylation proteomic analysis revealed HADHB as the key SIRT5 target, with lysine 292 (K292) being the dominant succinylation site. Functional studies demonstrated that K292 succinylation impairs HADHB-mediated fatty acid β-oxidation, ultimately promoting cardiomyocyte senescence. These findings reveal a critical regulatory axis involving SIRT5-HADHB succinylation in the pathogenesis of radiation-induced cardiomyopathy, providing new insights into potential therapeutic strategies.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, the flow rate was set at 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 4 μL.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard. Peak detection, alignment, and retention time correction were subsequently performed using XCMS. Peaks with a missing rate greater than 50% across samples in each group were removed. Missing values were then imputed using a combination of KNN imputation and one-fifth of the minimum value. Specifically, when the proportion of missing values exceeded 50%, the missing values were filled with one-fifth of the minimum value; when the missing rate was less than 50%, missing values were imputed using the KNN method. Peak areas were further corrected using the support vector regression (SVR) method. The corrected and filtered peaks were subjected to metabolite identification by searching against an in-house laboratory database and integrating public databases, including Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, https://hmdb.ca/</p><p>) and KEGG (https://www.kegg.jp/). Finally, metabolites with a comprehensive identification score greater than 0.5 and a coefficient of variation (CV) of QC samples less than 0.3 were retained. Data from positive and negative ion modes were then merged, keeping the metabolites with the highest identification level and the lowest CV value, resulting in the final dataset stored in the file all_sample_data.xlsx.</p>	<p>Add 500 μL of 80% methanol–water extraction solution containing internal standards and vortex for 2 min. Place the centrifuge tube containing the sample into liquid nitrogen for rapid freezing for 5 min, then remove and thaw on ice for 5 min. Avoid direct hand contact with the sample tube to prevent sudden temperature differences that may cause the tube cap to burst open and result in sample loss. Vortex for 2 min to mix thoroughly. Repeat the cycle of liquid nitrogen freezing, thawing, and vortexing three times. Centrifuge the samples at 12,000 r/min for 10 min at 4 °C. Transfer 200 μL of the supernatant and pass it through a protein precipitation plate for subsequent instrumental analysis. The prepared injection solution should be stored at −20 °C before analysis.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ion modes. The spray voltage was set to 3500 V in ESI+ mode and 3200 V in ESI− mode. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set to 30 Arb and 5 Arb, respectively. The ion transfer tube temperature and vaporizer temperature were maintained at 320 °C and 300 °C, respectively. The MS1 scan range was m/z 75–1000 with a resolution of 35,000 and an AGC target of 1 × 1000000. The MS2 scan range was also m/z 75–1000, with a resolution of 17,500 and an AGC target of 2 × 100000. The normalized collision energies (CE) were set to 30, 40, and 50. The signal intensity threshold was 1 × 100000 cps, and the Top N value was set to 10 precursor ions. The dynamic exclusion duration was 3 s.</p>	<p>The composition of metabolites exhibits sample specificity, meaning that different types of samples contain distinct categories and proportions of metabolites. In addition, metabolite composition can change under different treatments or biological processes. By analyzing the proportional composition of metabolites, the overall distribution of major metabolite classes within samples can be systematically evaluated.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Human cell samples were freeze-dried in whole tubes to preserve cellular components and maintain sample stability during storage and transport.</p>

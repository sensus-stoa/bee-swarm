Study Identifier	MTBLS14069
Study Title	Study on the symbiont preparation of Typha orientalis and polyphosphate bacteria for remediating black-odor water
Study Description	<p>Currently, significant progress has been made in the management of black-odor water in China. However, some of the water bodies remain at risk of reverting to black and odorous conditions. The mechanisms for rebuilding and sustaining a healthy aquatic ecosystem after the remediation of black-odor water are still unclear. Therefore, this study focuses on the technical approach of 'functional biological screening - biosynthesis of symbiotic organisms - evaluation of remediation efficiency. Using cattail (Typha orientalis) and phosphate-solubilizing bacteria (Pseudomonas aeruginosa) as target organisms, this study optimized the composition and concentration of biological substrate materials to develop a composite ligand with functional organisms at its core. This ligand can transform and remove phosphates from black and odorous water bodies while ensuring the establishment and growth of aquatic plant communities. Our research provides technical support for the ecological restoration of black and odorous water bodies. </p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><em>Typha orientalis</em> seedlings were harvested at 120 days after germination. The plant were cut and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen to stop metabolic activity.</p>	<p>Separation was performed using a Reverse Phase C18 column (e.g., ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3). The mobile phase consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile.</p>	<p>Raw data files (.raw/.d) were converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard msConvert. The data processing was performed using [Software Name, e.g., Compound Discoverer 3.3 / XCMS / MZmine 2]. The workflow included peak picking, retention time alignment, and peak grouping. Noise filtering was applied with a signal-to-noise ratio threshold of 3. Missing values were imputed using the k-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm. Data normalization was performed using total ion current (TIC) to minimize analytical variation.</p>	<p>100 mg of frozen tissue was ground and extracted with 1 mL of 80% methanol (pre-cooled at -20 °C). Samples were vortexed and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Data acquisition was performed using an <strong>AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600+</strong> mass spectrometer. The instrument was operated in <strong>positive [and negative]</strong> electrospray ionization mode. The scan range was set from <strong>100 to 1000 m/z</strong>. The source temperature was maintained at <strong>550°C</strong>, and the ion spray voltage was set at <strong>5500 V</strong> (positive mode). The curtain gas was set at <strong>35 psi</strong>. For MS/MS analysis, the collision energy was set at <strong>35 ± 15 eV</strong> for information-dependent acquisition (IDA).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching experimental MS/MS spectra against reference databases, including HMDB (Human Metabolome Database), KEGG, and an in-house library. The precursor mass tolerance was set to 5 ppm, and the product ion tolerance was 10 ppm. Compounds were annotated based on accurate mass, isotopic patterns, and MS2 fragmentation patterns. Identification levels were assigned according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) guidelines, with Level 2 (putatively annotated compounds) being the primary focus for the untargeted analysis.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14092
Study Title	Trichoderma-based fertilizer enhances quality of Elymus breviaristatus silage via microbial and metabolic modulation
Study Description	<p> Elymus breviaristatus is an important alpine forage, there is limited information regarding its potential use as silage and how fertilizer treatments affect the ensiling process in this forage crop. Here, we investigated how organic fertilizer (M), nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium fertilizer (NPK), and Trichoderma harzianum fertilizer (B) affect E. breviaristatus silage quality (30 days and 60 days ensiling) via microbiome and metabolome analyses. Before ensiling, plant height and chlorophyll content increased by 29.93% and 39.72%, respectively, in the B group. After 60 days of ensiling, the M group had reduced crude protein and elevated butyric acid, the NPK group had higher ammonia nitrogen and butyric acid, and the B group had increased crude protein and lactic acid. These quality shifts correlated with microbial and metabolic changes. In the M group, alpha-linolenic acid metabolism was downregulated and Alternaria enriched, while the NPK group had enhanced flavone biosynthesis and a reduced level of Lactiplantibacillus. The B group had enhanced glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, and displayed the most complex microbial networks along with increased levels of Lactiplantibacillus and Aspergillus. Overall, these results demonstrate that Trichoderma-based fertilization enhances silage quality by promoting accumulation of beneficial microbes and increasing flux through specific metabolic pathways, potentially offering a sustainable strategy for alpine forage improvement.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>After centrifugation (10,000 g, 10 min), metabolites were absorbed using a CNWBOND Carbon-GCB SPE Cartridge (250 mg, 3 mL; ANPEL Laboratory Technologies Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China) and filtered (SCAA-104, 0.22 μm pore size). Using an LC-ESI-MS/MS system (Shimadzu UFLC system, Kyoto, Japan; Applied Biosystems 4500 QTRAP MS, Foster City, California, USA) for detection of the extracts. The chromatographic conditions were as follows. Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (2.1*100 mm), water/acetonitrile gradient, flow rate 0.4 ml/min, temperature 40 °C and injection volume 5 μL. The effluent was introduced into a triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spectrometer (QTRAP; Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source for detection. All mass spectrometric analyses in triple quadrupole (QQQ) scan mode were performed using QTRAP system. MRM transitions were monitored for each metabolite based on their elution times, with further optimization of declustering potential (DP) and collision energy (CE).</p>	<p>We employed the false discovery rate (FDR) to correct the P-values for multiple testing in metabolomics analysis. Pseudotargeted metabolomics data results were integrated and analyzed using SIMCA14.1. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed for all treatment groups, followed by the orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) model and permutation test for the pairwise comparison analysis, and then variable importance in the project (VIP) value of the pairwise comparison was outputted. Then, the fold change (FC) value and P-value of metabolic data were calculated, and the metabolites, which were consistent with FC &gt; 2 or &lt; 0.5, P-value &lt; 0.05 and VIP&gt;1, were selected and namely for the differential metabolites.</p>	<p>The pseudotargeted metabolome integrated the extensive scope of non-targeted metabolome with the high accuracy of targeted metabolome. Metabolites were extracted from silage samples for 60 days (Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China). The chemical identities of metabolites were confirmed using the self-built MWDB (Metware Database) and a widely targeted UPLC-MS/MS platform. Qualitative analysis was performed based on precursor ion pair, secondary spectrum, and retention time (RT).

Study Title	Clostridium butyricum ameliorates meat quality in coccidiosis-affected sheep by rescuing thiamine diphosphate-mediated glucose metabolism
Study Description	<p>Sheep coccidiosis, caused by Eimeria&nbsp;spp., is widely studied for inducing diarrhea, but its impact on mutton quality remains poorly understood. Here, we systematically investigated the&nbsp;effects of Eimeria&nbsp;infection on meat&nbsp;quality and the interventing role of Clostridium butyricum&nbsp;(C. butyricum), alongside underlying mechanisms. Results showed that C. butyricum&nbsp;intervention (CB-IF group) significantly improved muscle pH and water-holding capacity, and&nbsp;textural properties (shear force, hardness;&nbsp;all p&nbsp;&lt;&nbsp;0.05). Eimeria&nbsp;infection&nbsp;reduced&nbsp;serum pro-inflammatory factors (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, and TNF-α)&nbsp;and&nbsp;the GSH/GSSG ratio, accompanied by&nbsp;an elevation&nbsp;in the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10.&nbsp;Meanwhile,&nbsp;local immunity (IL-17&nbsp;and&nbsp;IL-18) and catalase activity in the Longissimus dorsi&nbsp;were suppressed, and these impairments were&nbsp;partially reversed by C. butyricum&nbsp;intervention. Integrative metabolomics and transcriptomics revealed that&nbsp;Eimeria&nbsp;spp. infection (IF group) downregulated&nbsp;thiamine transporter protein SLC19A1, reducing HET-P and thiamine diphosphate (TDP)&nbsp;levels in Longissimus dorsi, thereby disrupting glucose metabolism pathways, including the pentose phosphate pathway, fructose and mannose metabolism, galactose metabolism. The joint analysis indicated&nbsp;that Longissimus dorsi&nbsp;muscle compensated by upregulating glycosylation-related genes&nbsp;(GMPPB&nbsp;and&nbsp;TSTA3) and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation genes&nbsp;(NDUFA3, NDUFA11, NDUFB11, and COX17), leading to enhanced&nbsp;oxidative stress. Notably, C. butyricum&nbsp;intervention&nbsp;significantly upregulated SLC19A1&nbsp;expression, restored TDP/HET-P levels, rebalanced&nbsp;energy metabolism, and improved meat qualtiy (e.g.,&nbsp;elevated 24h pH and&nbsp;redness [a*]). This study&nbsp;reveals&nbsp;a novel mechanism through which thiamine diphosphate-mediated glucose metabolism modulates&nbsp;meat quality&nbsp;in sheep infected with Eimeria&nbsp;spp, thereby laying&nbsp;a theoretical foundation&nbsp;for formulating&nbsp;targeted nutritional strategies via probiotic&nbsp;intervention.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>On days 0, 21, and 40, 5 mL&nbsp;jugular&nbsp;blood samples were&nbsp;collected&nbsp;and prefed&nbsp;into procoagulant tubes. The samples were gently inverted and mixed and subsequently&nbsp;allowed to stand before centrifugation&nbsp;at 3000×g for 10 minutes. The serum was then collected and preserved&nbsp;at -80 °C for further analysis. After&nbsp;40&nbsp;days, the lambs were fasted for 12&nbsp;h and euthanized&nbsp;via jugular exsanguination, and the abdominal cavity was dissected&nbsp;to remove all internal organs.&nbsp;Muscle samples (2 g)&nbsp;from the longissimus dorsi (LT) muscle (between the 10th and 13th ribs on the left side of the corpse) were&nbsp;stored in EP tubes on dry ice for transcriptome and nontarget metabolome sequencing&nbsp;(Mavis Biotechnology). An extra 5 g of muscle sample was procured in liquid nitrogen for subsequent laboratory analysis. Approximately 500 g of muscle sample was stored at 4 °C for the evaluation of meat quality.</p>	<p>All samples were for two LC/MS methods. One aliquot was analyzed using positive ion conditions and was eluted from T3 column (Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm) using 0.1 % formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1 % formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B in the following gradient: 5 to 20 % in 2 min, increased to 60 % in the following 3 mins, increased to 99 % in 1 min and held for 1.5 min, then come back to 5 % mobile phase B witnin 0.1 min, held for 2.4 min. The analytical conditions were as follows, column temperature, 40 °C; flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; injection volume, 4 μL; Another aliquot was using negative ion conditions and was the same as the elution gradient of positive mode.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The raw data of the mass spectrometer was converted into mzML format by ProteoWizard, and peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using XCMS program. Filter the peaks with a missing rate&gt;50% in each group of samples, fill in the blank values with KNN, and use SVR method to correct the peak area. The peaks after calibration and screening were identified for metabolites by searching the laboratory's self built database, integrating public databases, prediction databases, and metDNA methods. Finally, extract substances with a comprehensive identification score of 0.5 or above and a QC sample CV value less than 0.3, and then merge the positive and negative modes (retaining the substance with the highest qualitative level and the smallest CV value) to obtain the final data.</p>	<p>The sample stored at -80 °C refrigerator was thawed on ice. The sample was grinded with liquid nitrogen. A 400 μL solution (Methanol : Water = 7:3, V/V) containing internal standard was added into 20 mg grinded sample, and shaked at 1500 rpm for 5 min. After placing on ice for 15 min. the sample was centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 10 min (4 °C). A 300 μL of supernatant was collected and placed in -20 °C for 30 min. The sample was then centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 3 min (4 °C). A 200 μL aliquots of supernatant were transferred for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>All the methods alternated between full scan MS and data dependent MSn scans using dynamic exclusion. MS analyses were carried out using electrospray ionization in the positive ion mode and negative ion mode using full scan analysis over m/z 75-1000 at 35000 resolution. Additional MS settings are: ion spray voltage, 3.5 KV or 3.2 KV in positive or negative modes, respevtively; Sheath gas (Arb), 30; Aux gas, 5; Ion transfer tube temperature, 320 °C; Vaporizer temperature, 300 °C; Collision energy, 30,40,50 V; Signal Intensity Threshold, 1*e6 cps; Top N vs Top speed, 10; Exclusion duration, 3s.</p>	<p>The raw data of the mass spectrometer was converted into mzML format by ProteoWizard, and peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using XCMS program.

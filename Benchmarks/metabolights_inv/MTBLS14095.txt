Study Identifier	MTBLS14095
Study Title	Effects of Guanxinning tablet on the gut microbiota and bile acid metabolism in mice with hyperlipidemia
Study Description	<p>Introduction:&nbsp;Guanxinning&nbsp;tablet (GXNT), a traditional Chinese medicine preparation, has been found to improve lipid metabolism in patients with cardiovascular disease. However, the underlying mechanisms are still poorly understood.&nbsp;This study aims to determine whether the gut microbiota and bile acid&nbsp;(BA)&nbsp;metabolism are involved in the mechanisms by which GXNT ameliorates hyperlipidemia.</p><p>Methods: The chemical composition of GXNT&nbsp;was characterized using&nbsp;UPLC-Q-TOF/MS.&nbsp;A mouse hyperlipidemia model&nbsp;was established through&nbsp;high-fat diet (HFD), and GXNT&nbsp;or simvastatin was administered by gavage for 6 weeks. The impact of GXNT on hyperlipidemia was assessed by measuring markers&nbsp;related to lipid metabolism, liver injury and inflammation. Furthermore, 16S rDNA sequencing, targeted metabolomics, immunohistochemistry, molecular docking&nbsp;and western blot were used to investigate the underlying mechanisms.</p><p>Results: GXNT treatment reduced blood lipid levels, improved liver injury, and mitigated hepatic inflammation in HFD-fed mice. GXNT also ameliorated the dysfunction of the intestinal barrier by upregulating the expression of zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), occludin and claudin-1. Importantly, GXNT remodeled the gut microbiota in mice with hyperlipidemia, manifested by an increase in the abundance of Bacteroidota and Rikenellaceae_RC9_gut_group, as well as a decrease in the abundance of Desulfovibrio, Monoglobus, and Streptococcus. In addition, GXNT intervention altered the composition of fecal BAs and regulated&nbsp;BA metabolism by regulating the hepatic farnesoid X receptor (FXR)/small heterodimer partner (SHP) axis and intestinal FXR/fibroblast growth factor 15 (FGF15) axis.</p><p>Conclusions: GXNT improved hyperlipidemia in HFD-fed mice,&nbsp;which was associated with the gut microbiota and BA metabolism. Our results&nbsp;provide a theoretical basis for the application of GXNT.</p>

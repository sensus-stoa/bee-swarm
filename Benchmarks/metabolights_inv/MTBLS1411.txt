Study Identifier	MTBLS1411
Study Title	Hfq Regulates Efflux Pump Expression and Purine Metabolic Pathway to Increase the Trimethoprim Resistance in Aeromonas veronii
Study Description	<p>Aeromonas veronii (A. veronii) is a zoonotic pathogen. It causes clinically a variety of diseases such as dysentery, bacteremia, and meningitis. And it also brings huge losses to aquaculture. A. veronii has been documented as a multiple antibiotic resistant bacterium. Hfq (host factor for RNA bacteriophage Qβ replication) plays vital regulation roles in a variety of cell programs. The deletion of hfq in A. veronii showed an increased sensitivity to the trimethoprim, accompanying by the down regulation of the efflux pump related genes acrA and acrB in transcriptomic data. Coherently, the complementation of acrA and acrB in ∆hfq recovered the trimethoprim resistance. Besides, the productions of adenosine and guanosine were revealed to augment in ∆hfq in metabonomic data. Both wild type and ∆hfq conferred more sensitive to trimethoprim after appending 1 mM adenosine or guanosine to M9 medium. These results demonstrated that Hfq mediated trimethoprim resistance by elevating efflux pump expression and degrading adenosine, and guanosine metabolites. Collectively, Hfq is a potential target to tackle trimethoprim resistance in A. veronii infection.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Aeromonas veronii bacteria were cultured for 20 hr then lysed. The sample was transferred into a EP tube in 1 ml ice cold extraction solvent (2:2:1 methanol/acetonitrile/water).</p>	<p>The extracts were homogenized in a ball mill for 4 min at 45 Hz, and subjected to ultrasonic treatments for 5 min incubated in ice water. After homogenization for 3 times, followed by incubation at -20 °C for 1 hr to precipitate the proteins, then centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was transferred into EP tubes and dried in a vacuum concentrator. The extraction solvent (1:1 acetonitrile/water) was then added for reconstitution. After vortexing for 30 s and ultrasonication for 10 min at 4 °C in a water bath, the reconstituted extracts were spun down for 15 min at 12000 rpm, 4 °C, and 60 μl of the supernatant was transferred into a fresh 2 ml LC/MS glass vial. For pooled QC samples, 10 µl supernatant was combined from each sample.</p>	<p>UPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (1290, Agilent Technologies) with a UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 μm 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters) coupled to TripleTOF 6600 (Q-TOF, AB Sciex). The mobile phase consisted of 25 mM NH4OAc and 25 mM NH4OH in water pH 9.75 (A) and acetonitrile (B) with elution gradient as follows: 0 min, 95% B; 7 min, 65% B; 9 min, 40% B; 9.1 min, 95% B; 12 min, 95% B, which was delivered at 0.5 ml/min. The injection volume was 2 μl.</p>	<p>The Triple TOF mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on an information-dependent basis (IDA) during an LC/MS experiment. In this mode, the acquisition software (Analyst TF 1.7, AB Sciex) continuously evaluates the full scan survey MS data as it collects and triggers the acquisition of MS/MS spectra depending on preselected criteria. In each cycle, 12 precursor ions whose intensity greater than 100 were chosen for fragmentation at collision energy (CE) of 30 V (15 MS/MS events with product ion accumulation time of 50 ms each). ESI source conditions were set as following: ion source gas 1 at 60 psi, ion source gas 2 at 60 psi, curtain gas at 35 psi, source temperature 650 °C, Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF) 5000 V or -4000 V in positive or negative modes respectively.</p>	<p>MS raw data (.wiff) files were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed by R package XCMS (version 3.2). The preprocessing results generated a data matrix that consisted of the retention time (RT), massto-charge ratio (m/z) values, and peak intensity.</p>	<p>R package CAMERA was used for peak annotation after XCMS data processing. An in-house MS2 database was applied for metabolite identification.</p>

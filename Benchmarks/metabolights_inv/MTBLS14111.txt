Study Title	Characterization Epstein-Barr Virus-Associated Encephalitis via Identification of Metabolomic Biomarkers
Study Description	<p>Objective:&nbsp;Fulminant Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) infection may lead to fatal outcomes in patients with EBV-associated encephalitis (EBVAE). To investigate potential&nbsp;biomarkers in EBVAE, we utilized an untargeted metabolomic methodology to identify disease-associated biomarkers in patients with EBVAE.&nbsp;</p><p>Methods:&nbsp;Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) from 15 patients with EBVAE and 6 control subjects with functional neurological disorders were collected and analyzed utilizing untargeted metabolomics. Metabolites were annotated, clustered, and subjected to statistical analysis.</p><p>Results:&nbsp;Significant metabolomic alterations were observed between EBVAE patients and controls:&nbsp;164&nbsp;CSF metabolites were identified to be upregulated and&nbsp;115&nbsp;downregulated in patients with EBVAE. Among those, serotonin and tryptophan were markedly elevated in patients with EBVAE. Interestingly, 6 patients tested positive for glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) antibodies in CSF met the criteria for GFAP-astrocytopathy (GFAP-A). Astrocytic&nbsp;monoamine derivative methylphenyl ethylene glycol was significantly elevated in GFAP-A subgroup compared with GFAP Ab-negative group. 6 out of 15 patients with EBVAE progressed to acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM). Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) was found to be negatively correlated with Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores (r = -0.858, p &lt; 0.001) in the ADEM subgroup.</p><p>Conclusion:&nbsp;Our study first characterizes the metabolomic profile of patients with EBVAE, including those with EBV positive GFAP-A or ADEM.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC system equipped with an Agilent 1290 Infinity II autosampler, using two complementary columns: an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 reverse-phase column (both 2.1 × 150 mm, 1.7 μm), each protected by a matching guard column. The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C, with mobile phases composed of acetonitrile, water, ammonium acetate, and formic acid, optimized for broad-spectrum metabolite detection in cerebrospinal fluid.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were imported into commercial metabolomics software (e.g., Compound Discoverer 3.3, MS-DIAL, or XCMS Online) for peak detection, alignment, noise filtering, and normalization. Internal standards were used for quality control and signal correction, and only features with consistent detection across QC samples were retained for further analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen CSF samples were thawed on ice and centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris. A 200 μL aliquot of supernatant was mixed with 800 μL of pre-cooled methanol (containing internal standards) to precipitate proteins, followed by vortexing and centrifugation at 12,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was dried under vacuum, reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile-water (9:1, v/v), and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane before LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was carried out using a Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to the UHPLC system, equipped with a Heated Electrospray Ionization (HESI-II) source operating in both positive and negative ion modes. The quadrupole-Orbitrap hybrid mass analyzer was set to scan over an m/z range of 70–1050 for full MS acquisition and 50–1050 for data-dependent MS/MS acquisition, enabling high-resolution accurate mass measurement and comprehensive metabolite fragmentation analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching accurate mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against public databases (HMDB, Metlin, MassBank, and GNPS) and a CSF-specific metabolite library, with a mass tolerance of ≤5 ppm and MS/MS similarity score ≥0.7. Isotopes and adducts were annotated using CAMERA or similar tools, and metabolites were classified based on their chemical class and biological function for downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Organism: Homo sapiens (Human)</p><p>Sample type: Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)</p><p>Treatment groups: Antiviral drugs treatment; No treatment (control)</p><p>Collection method: CSF samples were collected via lumbar puncture at the L4–L5 intervertebral space using a 22G spinal needle. Approximately 10 mL of CSF was collected from each subject and immediately stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>

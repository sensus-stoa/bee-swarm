Study Title	Targeting secreted MDK-mediated tryptophan/kynurenine metabolism enhances DLL3 CAR T therapy for SCLC
Study Description	<p>Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) exhibits profound immunometabolic suppression that impairs antigen presentation and constrains immunotherapy efficacy. Through integrated multi-omics analyses of primary human SCLC tumors, we identified midkine (MDK) as a dominant tumor-secreted driver of immune evasion. Mechanistically, MDK activates STAT3 to induce indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), driving tryptophan-kynurenine metabolic reprogramming. This axis suppresses myeloid antigen presentation, reduces HLA class I expression, and impairs CD8+ T cell function, establishing a immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). We engineered DLL3-targeted CAR T cells secreting anti-MDK scFv. These dual-functional CAR T cells neutralize MDK within the TME, restore antigen presentation, alleviate metabolic suppression, and reinvigorate CD8+&nbsp;T cell responses. In SCLC models, they exhibited markedly enhanced antitumor activity, improved metabolic fitness, and durable immune activation without systemic toxicity. Collectively, our findings identify MDK as a key immunometabolic regulator and support sMDK-DLL3 CAR T cells as a targeted immunotherapy for SCLC.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chromatographic separation was conducted on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system equipped with a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon autosampler, using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 analytical column coupled with a matching Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 guard column. The column temperature was stably controlled at 40℃, with a mobile phase flow rate of 0.4 mL/min and an injection volume of 2 μL. A gradient elution program was adopted, with mobile phase A being 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution and mobile phase B being 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile solution, optimized to achieve efficient separation of complex small molecule metabolites in both cell and tumor tissue samples. The autosampler temperature was maintained at 4℃ to preserve metabolite stability, and samples were tested in randomized order with QC samples interspersed to ensure data reliability.</p>	<p>Raw UHPLC-MS/MS data were first subjected to format conversion using ProteoWizard software, then imported into professional metabolomics data processing software (Compound Discoverer 3.3 or XCMS Online) for standardized processing. The processing steps included peak detection, peak alignment, retention time correction, noise filtering, and background impurity removal. Metabolite peak area quantification was conducted, and missing values were filtered and supplemented reasonably. Data normalization was performed based on QC samples and internal standards, and only metabolite features with good repeatability (CV &lt; 30% in QC samples) were retained for subsequent statistical analysis to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the dataset.</p>	<p>All samples were extracted following a standardized pre-cooled methanol-acetonitrile aqueous solution method, optimized for both cell precipitates and animal tumor tissues to ensure efficient and comprehensive release of intracellular metabolites, avoiding metabolite degradation and loss during operation.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Frozen cell precipitates and tumor tissue samples were kept on ice throughout the process to maintain low temperature. Tissue samples were first fully ground into fine powder under liquid nitrogen protection, while cell samples were directly subjected to lysis and extraction without grinding. A certain volume of pre-cooled extraction solution (methanol: acetonitrile: water = 2:2:1, v/v/v) was added to all samples, followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction to fully break cells and release metabolites. After extraction, samples were centrifuged at high speed and low temperature to precipitate protein and cell debris, the supernatant was collected and dried with a vacuum concentrator. The dried metabolites were re-dissolved with a reconstitution solution, centrifuged again to remove insoluble impurities, and the final supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm hydrophilic PTFE filter, prepared for UHPLC-MS/MS detection. QC samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of all experimental sample extracts, inserted periodically during sample detection to evaluate system stability and data repeatability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap mass spectrometer coupled with the Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system, equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI-II) source that runs in alternating positive and negative ion modes to cover a wide range of polar and non-polar metabolites. The mass analyzer is a high-resolution quadrupole-Orbitrap hybrid system, with a full-scan m/z range set to 70 - 1050 for both primary MS scanning and data-dependent secondary MS/MS fragmentation acquisition. High-resolution scanning mode was applied to ensure accurate mass-to-charge ratio measurement and reliable metabolite structure inference, with optimized ionization parameters to enhance detection sensitivity.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was carried out by combining accurate mass matching and secondary mass spectrometry fragmentation spectrum comparison. The accurate mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) deviation was controlled within ± 5 ppm, and the obtained MS/MS spectra were matched with mainstream public metabolomics databases including HMDB (Human Metabolome Database), Metlin, MassBank, and a self-built tumor metabolomics database. Annotation of isotopes, adducts and fragment ions was completed using relevant annotation tools, and only metabolites with high matching score and reliable spectral similarity were identified and annotated. The identified metabolites were classified according to their chemical categories and metabolic pathways, providing a solid foundation for downstream differential metabolite screening and biological function analysis.</p>	<p>Human SCLC Cell Line Samples: Two human SCLC cell lines, NCI-H146 and SHP77, were used in this study. Each cell line was divided into MDK knockdown group and negative control group, cultured under standard cell culture conditions suitable for SCLC cell lines. After reaching the predetermined growth status, cells were digested, collected, centrifuged to discard culture supernatant, and washed gently with pre-cooled PBS buffer to remove residual medium and impurities. The obtained clean cell precipitates were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80℃ until metabolite extraction.</p><p>Mouse Spontaneous SCLC Tumor Tissue Samples: Genetically engineered RPM mice were used to construct a spontaneous small cell lung cancer model. After successful model establishment, lung-specific MDK knockdown treatment and negative control treatment were performed. After the experimental endpoint, mice were euthanized, and the lung tumor tissues were rapidly dissected and collected. Visible normal tissues and necrotic parts were removed, tissues were rinsed with pre-cooled PBS to remove blood contamination, then quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80℃ awaiting subsequent analysis.</p>

Study Title	Transcriptome and metabolome reveal the mechanism of FaLEC2 regulating lipid metabolism in strawberry
Study Description	<p>Lipids are vital cellular components with structural, storage, signaling, and defensive functions, and lipid metabolism is critical for strawberry quality. However, the regulatory mechanism underlying lipid metabolism during strawberry development and ripening remains unclear. Although LEAFY COTYLEDON 2 (LEC2) regulates lipid metabolism, its function in strawberry is unknown. This study explored the role of FaLEC2 in strawberry. </p>

Study Protocol Description	<p> XD-CT1 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD-CT2 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD-CT3 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD1 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD2 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD3 Rna&nbsp;Interference XD4 Rna&nbsp;Interference OE-CT1 Overexpression OE-CT2 Overexpression OE-CT3 Overexpression OE1 Overexpression OE2 Overexpression OE3 Overexpression </p>	<p>The resulting filtrate was analyzed using an ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography mass spectrometer (LC-30A, Shimadzu, Japan) fitted with an ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters, UK).</p><p>All samples were analyzed using two LC/MS methods. An aliquot of each sample was analyzed under positive ion mode with gradient elution on a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B), with the following gradient: 5-20% B over 2 min, increased to 60% B over 3 min, then to 99% B over 1 min and held for 1.5 min, followed by a return to 5% B within 0.1 min and re-equilibration for 2.4 min. The column temperature was 40 °C, the flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 4 μL. A second aliquot was analyzed under negative ion mode using the same chromatographic conditions.</p>	<p>Raw MS data were converted from .wiff to mzXML using ProteoWizard (v3.0). Peak picking, alignment, and retention time correction were performed with XCMS in R. Features with &gt;50% missing values were filtered, and remaining missing values were imputed using KNN.</p>	<p>We crushed the fruit into a powder in liquid nitrogen, weighed out 100 mg of the powder, and extracted it with 1.2 mL of 70% methanol. The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes. Following centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, the supernatant was filtered.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a TripleTOF 6600 system (Q-TOF, AB Sciex, Concord, ON, Canada) equipped with an electrospray ionization source operating in information-dependent acquisition mode controlled by Analyst TF 1.7.1 Software (Sciex, Concord, ON, Canada). Source parameters were: ion source gas 1 and 2 at 50 psi, curtain gas at 25 psi, temperature at 550 °C, declustering potential at ±60 V, and ion spray voltage floating at 5000 V (positive) or -4000 V (negative). TOF MS scans covered m/z 50-1000 with an accumulation time of 200 ms. Product ion scans covered m/z 25-1000 with an accumulation time of 40 ms, collision energy at ±30 V with a spread of 15, and resolution set to UNIT. Up to 18 candidate ions per cycle were monitored with an intensity threshold of 100 cps, mass tolerance of 50 ppm, and isotope exclusion within 4 Da.</p>	<p>We identified and annotated metabolites in collaboration with Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd., using their self-built database of standards and public databases. Only features meeting stringent criteria were retained: identification score &gt;0.5 and QC relative standard deviation (RSD) &lt;0.5. Features from positive and negative modes were merged, retaining the entry with the highest identification confidence and lowest RSD for each metabolite. </p>

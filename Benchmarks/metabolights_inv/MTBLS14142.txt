Study Identifier	MTBLS14142
Study Title	Systems toxicology of a tire-wear pollutant: Multi-omics evidence for bioenergetic reprogramming in freshwater clams
Study Description	<p>The increasing release of tire-derived transformation products into aquatic ecosystems has raised significant environmental concerns, particularly regarding 6PPD-quinone (6PPDQ), a highly toxic compound formed from a widely used tire antioxidant. Despite growing attention, its chronic effects and underlying mechanisms in benthic invertebrates remain poorly understood. This study investigates the systemic impacts of environmentally relevant concentrations of 6PPDQ on the freshwater bivalve Corbicula fluminea using an integrated multi-omics and physiological framework. A 21-day exposure experiment was conducted at concentrations of 4, 8, and 16 ug L-1 alongside a control. Microbial community dynamics were assessed through 16S rRNA and ITS2 sequencing, while host responses were evaluated using RNA sequencing and untargeted LC-MS metabolomics. Co-occurrence network analysis enabled integration of microbiome, transcriptome, and metabolome datasets, revealing key interaction networks and functional pathways. Additionally, oxidative stress biomarkers and histopathological analyses provided evidence of physiological and tissue-level damage. Overall, this study offers a comprehensive systems-level understanding of 6PPDQ toxicity, contributing to improved ecological risk assessment of emerging tire-derived pollutants in freshwater environments.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 28 biological samples were collected and subjected to non-targeted metabolomics analysis using an LC-QTOF platform. The samples were prepared according to standardized metabolomics workflows to ensure consistency and reliability for downstream qualitative and quantitative metabolite profiling.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was conducted using a Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system. The separation conditions were optimized to achieve high-resolution metabolite profiling, enabling efficient separation of complex metabolite mixtures prior to mass spectrometry detection.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were processed using MassLynx V4.2 and Progenesis QI software for peak detection, alignment, and normalization. Data quality was evaluated using quality control (QC) samples, correlation analysis, and principal component analysis (PCA). Log-based transformation and multivariate statistical methods were applied to improve data robustness and reduce variability.</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction was performed by adding an appropriate extraction solvent to the samples, followed by grinding and sonication to enhance metabolite release. After centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and subjected to vacuum drying. The dried extracts were then reconstituted in a suitable solvent prior to instrumental analysis, ensuring optimal metabolite recovery and stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF high-resolution mass spectrometer. Data acquisition was carried out under optimized conditions to detect metabolites in high sensitivity and accuracy. Both qualitative and quantitative metabolite information was obtained using advanced acquisition modes suitable for non-targeted metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using Progenesis QI by matching spectral data against multiple databases, including METLIN, HMDB, and self-built libraries. Identification was further supported by MS/MS fragmentation pattern analysis. A total of 7,806 peaks were detected, of which 1,478 metabolites were successfully annotated. Functional interpretation of metabolites was conducted through pathway analysis using KEGG and related metabolomics databases.</p>

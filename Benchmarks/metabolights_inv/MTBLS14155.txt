Study Identifier	MTBLS14155
Study Title	Aqueous Artemisia argyi extract mitigates acute lung injury in association with coordinated alterations in gut microbiota, metabolic homeostasis, and pulmonary inflammatory gene expression
Study Description	his study investigated the metabolic alterations in colonic contents associated with aqueous extract of Artemisia argyi (AEAA) intervention in a lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced acute lung injury (ALI) mouse model. Male C57BL/6 mice were randomly divided into control, LPS, and AEAA treatment groups with low-, medium-, and high-dose administration.</p><p>AEAA was administered by oral gavage for 28 consecutive days. On day 29, ALI was induced by intratracheal instillation of LPS (5 mg/kg) in the LPS and treatment groups, while control mice received sterile saline. Colonic content samples were collected 8 hours after LPS administration.</p><p>Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed using liquid chromatography¨Cmass spectrometry (LC-MS). Quality control (QC) samples were included to ensure analytical stability. The metabolomic profiles of colonic contents were analyzed to explore gut metabolic alterations and potential mechanisms underlying the protective effects of AEAA in acute lung injury.</p><p>This dataset provides raw LC-MS data of colonic content metabolomics and can be used to investigate gut-related metabolic pathways involved in inflammation and natural product intervention.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male C57BL/6 mice were acclimatized for 7 days and then randomly assigned to five groups: control, LPS, low-dose AEAA, mid-dose AEAA, and high-dose AEAA. AEAA was administered via drinking water for 28 consecutive days. On day 29, acute lung injury was induced in the LPS and AEAA-treated groups by intratracheal instillation of lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 5 mg/kg), while control mice received an equivalent volume of sterile saline. Eight hours after LPS administration, mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal overdose of sodium pentobarbital (150 mg/kg), followed by cervical dislocation to ensure death. Colonic contents were collected aseptically immediately after euthanasia, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at ?80 ¡ãC until metabolomics analysis. Representative samples from each experimental group were selected for metabolomic profiling .</p>	<p>LC separation was performed on a UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system equipped with an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm). The injection volume was 3 μL. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A, water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, and solvent B, acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were imported into Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment to generate a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. Metabolite annotation was performed by matching MS and MS/MS data against public databases including HMDB and METLIN, together with an in-house database. The data matrix was further processed by removing variables with more than 20% missing values within at least one group according to the 80% rule, imputing missing values using the minimum observed value, performing total ion normalization, removing variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% in QC samples, and applying log10 transformation prior to downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Colonic content samples were processed as solid samples for metabolite extraction. Briefly, 100 mg of sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube containing one 6 mm grinding bead. Then, 800 μL extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing internal standards was added. Samples were homogenized for 6 min at -10 °C and 50 Hz using a tissue grinder, followed by ultrasonic extraction for 30 min at 5 °C and 40 kHz. The samples were then incubated at -20 °C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was transferred to LC-MS vials with inserts for instrumental analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all extracted samples and were injected regularly during the analytical run to monitor analytical reproducibility.</p>	<p>LC separation was performed on a UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system equipped with an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm). The injection volume was 3 μL. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A, water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, and solvent B, acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching MS and MS/MS spectral information against public metabolite databases, including HMDB and METLIN, together with an in-house spectral database, following feature extraction and alignment in Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Metabolite annotations were generated in an untargeted and exploratory manner and were not considered targeted quantitative confirmation.</p>

Study Title	Aqueous Artemisia argyi extract mitigates acute lung injury in association with coordinated alterations in gut microbiota, metabolic homeostasis, and pulmonary inflammatory gene expression
Study Description	Background: Artemisia argyi is a traditional medicinal herb with established anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties. Its aqueous extract (AEAA), enriched in water-soluble bioactive constituents, exhibits favorable safety and bioavailability; however, its potential protective effects against acute lung injury (ALI) and its associations with systemic immunometabolic regulation remain incompletely understood. Methods: An LPS-induced ALI mouse model was established following 28 days of AEAA pretreatment. Lung histopathology, pulmonary edema, and inflammatory cytokines were evaluated. Integrated multi-omics analyses—including gut microbiota profiling, untargeted metabolomics of colonic contents and serum, and lung transcriptomics—were performed to characterize treatment-associated microbial, metabolic, and transcriptional alterations. Results: AEAA pretreatment dose-dependently alleviated lung injury, reduced pulmonary edema, and suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines while restoring anti-inflammatory IL-10 levels. AEAA treatment was associated with partial reversal of LPS-induced gut dysbiosis, characterized by reduced abundance of inflammation-associated taxa and enrichment of beneficial genera, particularly Akkermansia and Lactobacillus. Metabolomic analyses revealed treatment-associated normalization of intestinal and systemic metabolic disturbances, including increased homeostasis-related metabolites and reduced inflammation-associated metabolites. Lung transcriptomic profiling suggested attenuation of LPS-associated transcriptional signatures related to NF-κB, MAPK, Toll-like receptor, and PI3K–AKT signaling pathways. Cross-omics integration further revealed coordinated associations among microbial shifts, metabolic remodeling, and pulmonary inflammatory gene expression. Conclusion: These findings suggest that aqueous Artemisia argyi extract is associated with mitigation of LPS-induced acute lung injury, accompanied by coordinated alterations in gut microbiota composition, host metabolic profiles, and pulmonary inflammatory gene expression. Although causal relationships were not established, this integrated multi-omics analysis provides a systems-level, hypothesis-generating framework supporting the potential of AEAA as a multi-target botanical candidate for ALI.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male C57BL/6 mice were acclimatized for 7 days and then randomly assigned to five groups: control, LPS, low-dose AEAA, mid-dose AEAA, and high-dose AEAA. AEAA was administered via drinking water for 28 consecutive days. On day 29, acute lung injury was induced in the LPS and AEAA-treated groups by intratracheal instillation of lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 5 mg/kg), while control mice received an equivalent volume of sterile saline. Eight hours after LPS administration, mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal overdose of sodium pentobarbital (150 mg/kg), followed by cervical dislocation to ensure death. Blood was collected aseptically immediately after euthanasia, allowed to clot, and centrifuged to obtain serum. Serum samples were aliquoted, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C until LC-MS metabolomics analysis. Representative serum samples from each experimental group were selected for untargeted metabolomic profiling.</p>	<p>LC separation was performed on a UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, the flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the injection volume was 5 μL. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A, 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (2:98, v/v), and solvent B, 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. A suitable gradient elution program was applied according to the instrument method for untargeted metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were imported into Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline correction, peak picking, peak integration, retention time alignment, and generation of a three-dimensional data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and ion intensity information. Metabolite annotation was performed by matching MS and MS/MS spectra against public databases including HMDB and METLIN, together with an in-house spectral database. Internal standard peaks and known false-positive signals, including background noise and column bleed, were removed before statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Serum samples were processed as liquid samples for metabolite extraction. Briefly, 100 μL of serum was transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and mixed with 800 μL extraction solvent (acetonitrile:methanol = 1:1, v/v) containing internal standards. Samples were vortexed for 30 s and subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min at 5 °C and 40 kHz to facilitate protein precipitation and metabolite extraction. The mixture was then incubated at -20 °C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and evaporated to dryness under nitrogen, reconstituted in 100 μL acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v), sonicated for 5 min at 5 °C, and centrifuged again at 13,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C. The final supernatant was transferred to LC-MS vials for instrumental analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all serum extracts and were injected at regular intervals during the analytical sequence to monitor system stability and analytical reproducibility.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was carried out using a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operated in both positive and negative ion modes. The source temperature was set at 425 °C, sheath gas flow at 50 arb, and auxiliary gas flow at 13 arb. The ion-spray voltage floating was set to -3500 V in negative mode and 3500 V in positive mode. MS/MS acquisition was performed using data-dependent acquisition (DDA), with normalized collision energies of 20-40-60 V. Data were acquired over an m/z range of 70-1050.</p>	<p>The resulting data matrix was further processed on the Majorbio cloud platform. Metabolic features detected in at least 80% of samples in any group were retained. Missing values were imputed using the minimum observed value, and signal intensities were normalized to the sum. Variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% in QC samples were excluded, and the retained data were log10-transformed before downstream analyses. Principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were performed using the R package ropls, and differential metabolites were screened using VIP &gt; 1 and p &lt; 0.05. Pathway enrichment analysis of differential metabolites was performed based on the KEGG database. Metabolite annotations generated in this workflow were derived from untargeted database matching and should be interpreted as putative metabolite identifications rather than targeted quantitative confirmation.</p>

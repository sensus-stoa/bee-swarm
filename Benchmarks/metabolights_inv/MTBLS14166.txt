Study Title	F-actin dynamics couples sphingolipid metabolism to epithelial barrier integrity in chronic colitis
Study Description	Intestinal barrier dysfunction is a hallmark of gastrointestinal disorders, including inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). In IBD, the disruption of the gut barrier causes liquid loss and persistent immune response. Understanding the cellular events underlying epithelial barrier disruption during chronic inflammation is essential for targeting increased intestinal permeability. Filamentous actin (F-actin) destabilization accompanied by metabolic dysfunction has been previously reported in the Muc2 knockout colitis model; however, the mechanistic contribution of these defects to chronic intestinal barrier loss remains unclear. Here, we identify impaired cytoskeleton dynamics as a critical driver of intestinal barrier dysfunction in chronic colitis. An imbalance between polymeric and monomeric actin disrupts the epithelial barrier in both 3D organoid system and in vivo. Actin and associated factors were identified as primary interactors of claudin-3, and this interaction was reduced under chronic inflammatory conditions in vivo. Further analysis revealed ceramide metabolism as a potential metabolic regulator of actin dynamics and barrier integrity during chronic inflammation. Consistently, intestinal samples from IBD patients showed concurrent disruption of tight and adherens junctions and reduced F-actin levels. Together, these findings reveal F-actin dynamics as one of the key mechanisms of barrier dysfunction in IBD and highlight ceramide metabolism as a potential therapeutic target in IBD.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Sample Description:</p><p>Crypts isolated from the colons of mice from the following groups:</p><p>1-5 C57Bl/6 control</p><p>6-10 Muc2-/- experiment (mutation + microflora)</p>	<p>Chromatographic-Mass Spectrometric Analysis</p><p>Materials and buffers:</p><p>Water (purified on a Milli-Q Integral 3 system), acetonitrile (Panreac), isopropanol (Panreac), ammonium acetate (Sigma), and formic acid (Merck).</p><p>A Waters Aquity UPLC system (Thermo Scientific) was used for analysis, coupled in series with a Q-Exactive HF-X mass spectrometric detector (Thermo Scientific) with an electrospray ionization source. Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH C8, 1.7 μm, 2.1 x 100 mm column with a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH C8 VanGuard 1.7 μm, 2.1 x 5 mm precolumn. Mobile phase composition and chromatographic analysis conditions:</p><p>Mobile phase A – 10 mM ammonium acetate in MilliQ water with 0.1% formic acid; mobile phase B – 10 mM ammonium acetate in a mixture of 70% acetonitrile/30% isopropanol with 0.1% formic acid. Sample volume: 1 µL. Column temperature: 60°C. Gradient elution mode at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. </p>	<p>The resulting RAW files were converted to MGF format using MSconvert (ProteoWizard 3.0). Spectra of the groups were analyzed using Progenesis LC-MS (Nonlinear Dynamics). Statistical comparison of the sample groups was performed using PCA (principal component analysis).</p>	<p>Liquid-liquid extraction for LC-MS/MS </p><p>1. Sample dilution buffer was added to the weighed samples at a ratio of 1 ml of the mixture per 1 mg of the substance.</p><p>2. Sonication was performed for 5 min in an ultrasonic bath with the addition of ice.</p><p>3. Centrifugation was performed for 5 min at 10°C at 13,000 g. The supernatants were transferred to clean tubes.</p><p>4. The resulting supernatants of the Akk and Con groups were transferred to chromatographic vials (50 µl each) for analysis. Sample 14 was diluted 10-fold with sample dilution buffer, and 50 µl of the resulting supernatant was transferred to a chromatographic vial.</p>	<p>The following parameters were set for mass spectrometric analysis: emitter voltage of 4.5 kV, capillary temperature of 320°C. Panoramic scanning was performed in positive ion mode in the mass range from 100 m/z to 1200 m/z, at a resolution of 70,000 in the time interval from 0 to 95 minutes of analysis. For tandem scanning, the resolution was set to 17,500 in the mass range from 50 m/z to the upper limit, which was determined automatically based on the precursor mass. Precursor ion isolation was performed in a window of ±1.2 m/z. The maximum number of ions allowed for isolation in MS2 mode was set to no more than 10, while the cutoff limit for precursor selection for tandem analysis was set to 5*104 units, and the normalized collision energy (NCE) was changed in steps: NCE = 20, NCE = 35, NCE = 50, with averaging of the obtained fragment spectra from the corresponding precursor ion. For tandem scanning, only ions with z = 1+ were taken into account. The maximum accumulation time for precursor ions and for fragment ions was 50 ms. The AGC value for precursors and fragment ions was set to 1*106 and 2*105, respectively. All measured precursors were dynamically excluded from tandem MS/MS analysis for 8 s. The total analysis duration was 120 min.</p>	<p>Metabolites with annotations in HMDB (Human Metabolome Database) and Lipid Maps databases were filtered out of total metabolomics data obtained by LC-MS/MS, and analyzed using Metaboanalyst 5.0 online tool [66]. The data were normalized by median, log10-transformed and Pareto scaling was applied. Then Fold Change analysis, t-test and PCA were performed. Additionally, KEGG Pathway Analysis [67] on normalized by median and log10-transformed data was conducted. For significantly different annotated metabolites and differentially expressing genes (FDR &gt; 0.05, Fold Change ≥ 2) Pathway Joint Analysis was performed.</p>

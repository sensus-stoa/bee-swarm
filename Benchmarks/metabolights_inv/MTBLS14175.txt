Study Identifier	MTBLS14175
Study Title	Integrated metagenomic binning and metabolomic profiling reveal a microbial-bile acid feedback loop in neonatal calf diarrhea_2
Study Description	A metagenomic binning analysis was performed on fecal samples from 50 healthy calves and 50 diarrheic calves to comprehensively investigate the association between microbial dysbiosis and calf diarrhea. By integrating shotgun metagenomics, untargeted metabolomics, targeted bile acid metabolomics, and qPCR assays, this study demonstrates that microbial dysbiosis and bile acid perturbations form a self-reinforcing vicious cycle that drives the onset and persistence of calf diarrhea.

Study Protocol Description	<p>At 14 days of age, six calves per group were randomly selected and humanely euthanized in accordance with the AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals by intravenous overdose of sodium pentobarbital administered by a licensed farm veterinarian, followed by collection of intestinal and liver samples. The remaining calves were maintained until 28 days of age for growth performance monitoring.&nbsp;Following euthanasia, the abdominal cavity was opened under aseptic conditions. The esophagus and rectum were ligated to prevent cross-contamination, and standardized intestinal segments were collected from the jejunum, ileum, and colon according to established anatomical landmarks.&nbsp;Each segment was divided into three portions: one portion was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for histomorphological analysis, whereas the other portion was rinsed with sterile ice-cold PBS, the mucosa was scraped off, and then transferred into RNase-free cryogenic tubes and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. The remaining portion of contents was collected and placed in cryogenic tubes. All samples were stored at -80 °C for subsequent analyses.</p>	<p>The chromatographic analysis was performed on an LC-ESI-MS/MS system equipped with an UHPLC ExionLC™ AD (Sciex, https://sciex.com.cn/). The analytical conditions were as follows:</p><p>Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm);</p><p>Mobile phase: Phase A was ultra-pure water containing 0.01% acetic acid and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate, and Phase B was acetonitrile containing 0.01% acetic acid;</p><p>Gradient elution program: the proportion of Phase B was increased from 5% to 40% within 1 min, then raised to 50% within the next 6 min, further increased to 75% within the subsequent 5 min, then elevated to 95% within 2 min, maintained at 95% for 2 min for washing, and finally ramped back to 5% at 16–17.5 min;</p><p>Other parameters: flow rate was set at 0.35 mL/min, column temperature was 40°C, and injection volume was 3 μL.</p>	<p>Data acquisition of mass spectrometric signals was completed using Analyst 1.6.3 software (Sciex). The raw detection data were processed and quantified by MultiQuant 3.0.3 software (Sciex), which was used for the quantitative analysis of all detected metabolites based on the MRM data and internal standard method.</p>	<p>Samples (20 mg) were first grinded with a ball mill, and then extracted with 495 μL of methanol. Subsequently, 5 μL of internal standard (IS) mixed solution (10 μg/mL) was added into the extract for quantitative analysis. The samples were placed at -20°C for 10 min to precipitate proteins, followed by centrifugation at 12000 r/min and 4°C for 10 min. After centrifugation, the supernatant was passed through a Protein Precipitation Plate for subsequent LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was conducted on a QTRAP® 6500+ LC-MS/MS System (Sciex, https://sciex.com.cn/) (also referred to as Applied Biosystems 6500 Triple Quadrupole) equipped with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, and the system was controlled by Analyst 1.6.3 software (Sciex). The mass spectrometry conditions were optimized as follows:</p><p>Ionization mode: negative ion mode (ESI-);</p><p>ESI source operation parameters: source temperature 550 °C, ion spray voltage (IS) -4500 V, curtain gas (CUR) 35 psi;</p><p>Detection mode: scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) was adopted for the analysis of bile acids;</p><p>Parameter optimization: the declustering potentials (DP) and collision energies (CE) of the mass spectrometer for individual MRM transitions were further optimized, and a specific set of MRM transitions was monitored for each time period according to the metabolites eluted in the corresponding period;</p><p>Scanning mode: linear ion trap (LIT) and triple quadrupole (QQQ) scans were acquired for detection.</p>	<p>Bile acids were selected as the target metabolites for identification and quantitative analysis. Metabolite identification was achieved by scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) based on the specific MRM transitions of each bile acid. A specific set of MRM transitions was dynamically monitored for different elution time periods according to the metabolites eluted in the corresponding period, combined with the optimized mass spectrometric parameters (declustering potentials (DP) and collision energies (CE)) for individual MRM transitions to ensure the specificity of metabolite detection. The retention time of the target metabolites and the matching degree of MRM ion pairs were used as the core basis for metabolite identification.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14181
Study Title	Metabolomic Profiling of Non-target Metabolites in Streptomyces sp. LZZY-S40
Study Description	This study aimed to characterize the non-targeted metabolite profile of Streptomyces sp. LZZY-S40 to explore its potential for producing bioactive secondary metabolites. The strain LZZY-S40 was cultivated on ISP 3 agar plates at 28°C for 7 days. Spores were harvested and inoculated into 250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing 50 mL of seed medium (pH 7.2–7.4) for 48 hours at 28°C with shaking at 250 rpm. The seed culture (8% v/v) was then transferred into fermentation medium and incubated under the same conditions for 7 days. After fermentation, the culture broth was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes to separate the supernatant from the mycelial biomass. The supernatant was extracted three times with ethyl acetate, and the mycelial pellet was extracted with methanol. Organic extracts were concentrated under reduced pressure at temperatures below 40°C. The combined crude extracts were analyzed by untargeted liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS). Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes across an m/z range of 50-1200, with chromatographic separation achieved using a gradient elution program at a flow rate of 400 μL/min and a column temperature of 40°C.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Strain Streptomyces sp. LZZY-S40 was cultured in optimized fermentation broth at suitable temperature and shaking speed for adequate incubation.</p>	<p>Untargeted metabolic profiling was performed using a Thermo UltiMate 3000 ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a matched UltiMate 3000 autosampler. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 analytical column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) coupled with a Waters ACQUITY HSS T3 guard pre-column (2.1 mm × 5 mm, 1.8 μm) to protect the main column and ensure stable separation of microbial secondary metabolites from Streptomyces sp. LZZY-S40.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data in original .d format were converted into standard mzML format for unified analysis. Subsequent data preprocessing including peak picking, retention time alignment, baseline correction, noise filtering and peak area normalization was completed using open-source software (MS-DIAL, XCMS) and commercial metabolomics workstation. Systematic correction based on QC sample signals was applied to eliminate retention time drift and intensity deviation of the detection system.</p>	<p>Fermentation cultures were harvested, centrifuged to separate supernatant and mycelium, mixed with high-purity organic extraction solvent (methanol/water or acetonitrile/methanol system) for metabolite extraction, subjected to ultrasonic ice-bath extraction and vortex oscillation, followed by high-speed centrifugation; the resulting supernatant was filtered through 0.22 μm organic filter membrane. Quality control (QC) sample was prepared by pooling equal volumes of all experimental samples to monitor system stability and data reproducibility throughout detection.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was conducted on a Thermo Q Exactive Orbitrap LC-MS/MS platform with a HESI-II heated electrospray ionization source in both positive and negative ionization modes. The full scan m/z range was set from 50 to 1500 to cover low-molecular-weight bioactive secondary metabolites, and an Orbitrap high-resolution mass analyzer was adopted to acquire high-precision mass spectral data for reliable metabolite preliminary identification.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation and identification were realized by matching accurate molecular mass, MS/MS fragment patterns and retention time information against public databases including HMDB, METLIN, LipidMaps and self-built Streptomyces secondary metabolite database. Finally, the non-targeted metabolite profile of Streptomyces sp. LZZY-S40 was characterized to screen differential and bioactive secondary metabolites and evaluate its metabolic potential for bioactive compound biosynthesis.</p>

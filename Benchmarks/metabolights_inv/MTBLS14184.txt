Study Identifier	MTBLS14184
Study Title	Functional targeting of PTTG1 in pericytes restores vascular integrity in diabetic retina
Study Description	Pericyte loss is an early and critical event in the pathogenesis of diabetic retinopathy (DR), yet the molecular mechanisms underlying pericyte dysfunction remain incompletely understood. Using single-cell RNA sequencing, we generated a retinal cellular overview comprising 37,982 cells from diabetic and non-diabetic mice. We identified a previously unrecognized pericyte subpopulation defined by high expression of pituitary tumor transforming gene 1 (PTTG1), which was enriched in diabetic retina. Functional studies demonstrated that CRISPR/Cas9- or siRNA-mediated silencing of PTTG1 restored pericyte stability and barrier-supporting function under high glucose stress. In vivo, PTTG1 silencing via viral or pericyte-specific AAV delivery improved retinal vascular integrity and reduced retinal vascular dysfunction in diabetic mice. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling revealed that PTTG1 silencing reprogrammed metabolism, modulating glycolytic flux and attenuating oxidative stress.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Origin of samples:</strong> Human retinal pericytes were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS under standard incubator conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2).</p><p><strong>Relevant treatment and time points: </strong>The cells were divided into two experimental groups (n = 6 per group): a vegative control (NC) group and a PTTG1 knockdown group. The knockdown group was transfected with PTTG1 siRNA for 48 hours to achieve targeted gene silencing.</p><p><strong>Collection and storage procedure: </strong>At the end of the specific time point, the cell culture medium was completely removed. To rapidly quench cellular metabolism and remove residual medium, the cells were rinsed twice with 1 mL of ice-cold PBS. The cells were then immediately subjected to metabolite extraction. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all biological samples.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatograph (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 µm). The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (aqueous phase containing 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia) and solvent B (acetonitrile). The autosampler temperature was maintained at 4 °C, and the injection volume was 2 µL.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data (RAW files) were first converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard software. Subsequent data processing, including peak recognition, feature extraction, peak alignment, and integration, was performed using a customized R-based pipeline incorporating the XCMS package. </p>	<p><strong>Sample Extraction:</strong> &gt; The collected samples were treated with 400 µL of extraction solution (methanol:acetonitrile:water = 2:2:1, v/v). The mixture was subjected to three freeze-thaw cycles (1 min in liquid nitrogen followed by 10 min at 37 °C) and then sonicated on ice for 10 min. The extracts were incubated at -40 °C for 1 h to precipitate proteins and then centrifuged for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were carefully transferred to autosampler vials for LC-MS analysis.</p><p><strong>Control and QC Samples Preparation: </strong>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of the supernatants from all individual biological samples. The pooled QC samples were processed identically to the biological samples and injected at regular intervals throughout the analytical run to monitor instrument stability and data reproducibility.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on a Q Exactive HFX mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source, operated in both positive and negative ionization modes. The source parameters were optimized with a capillary temperature of 350 °C, a sheath gas flow rate of 30 Arb, and an aux gas flow rate of 25 Arb. The spray voltage was maintained at 3.6 kV for positive mode and -3.2 kV for negative mode. For the Orbitrap mass analyzer, the Full MS resolution was set at 60,000 and the MS/MS (dd-MS2) resolution at 7,500. Fragmentation was executed using stepped normalized collision energy (NCE) at 10/30/60 to ensure comprehensive MS2 spectral coverage.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using Compound Discoverer software by matching high-resolution mass spectrometry features against mzCloud, mzVault, and MassList databases. The identification process integrated experimental MS/MS fragmentation patterns and collision energies with reference library entries to ensure accurate spectral matching.</p>

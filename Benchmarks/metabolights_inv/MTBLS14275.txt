Study Title	Integrative Peptidomics and Deep Learning Identifies Endogenous Antimicrobial Peptides in Periprosthetic Joint Infection
Study Description	Background: Periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) remains a major clinical challenge due to biofilm formation and antimicrobial resistance. This study used an integrated clinical peptidomics and deep learning approach to explore the biochemical features of the infected joint microenvironment, aiming modelsto identify endogenous antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) with translational potential. Results: Untargeted metabolomics revealed pronounced metabolic reprogramming in PJI patients, with enrichment of tryptophan, bile acid, and purine pathways, reflecting an intensified host immune-inflammatory response and heightened oxidative stress. Peptidomic analysis identified 435 DEPs, from which seven candidates were prioritized and synthesized via deep learning prediction. In vitro assays confirmed that c_AMPS15, c_AMPS16, and c_AMPS17 exhibited potent bactericidal activity against Staphylococcus aureus, while c_AMPS15 also showed efficacy against Pseudomonas aeruginosa. These peptides demonstrated superior biosafety profiles with minimal hemolysis and effectively inhibited bacterial biofilm maturation on prosthetic surfaces. In vivo, these peptides significantly reduced S. aureus burdens, while c_AMPS15 retained antimicrobial activity against P. aeruginosa and attenuated local inflammatory response. Conclusion: This integrative peptidomics and deep learning framework identified host-derived AMPs as promising therapeutic candidates for PJI, highlighting c_AMPS15 as a lead molecule with broad-spectrum and antibiofilm activity.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Serum samples were collected from patients at the Department of Orthopedics, Xi'an Honghui Hospital. The study cohort consisted of an PJI group (patients who underwent revision arthroplasty for periprosthetic joint infection) and a control group (patients without joint infection). The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Xi'an Honghui Hospital, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to sample collection.</p>	<p>Liquid chromatography was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm). The mobile phase A was an aqueous solution containing 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia hydroxide (pH adjusted to 9.75), and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The injection volume was 2 μL and the autosampler was maintained at 4 °C.</p>	<p>The raw MS data files (.raw) were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard. Data processing was subsequently performed using an in-house R-based pipeline built on the XCMS package for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. Metabolite identification was achieved by leveraging the internal R package in conjunction with the BiotreeDB (V3.0) database for spectral matching and annotation.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 100 μL of serum samples by adding 400 μL of a pre-cooled extraction solution consisting of Methanol and Acetonitrile (1:1, v/v), which contained deuterated internal standards. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and sonicated for 10 min in a 4 °C water bath. To ensure complete protein precipitation, the samples were incubated at -40 °C for 1 h. Subsequently, the mixtures were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm (13,800 × g) for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was transferred to a fresh glass vial for LC-MS/MS analysis. A Quality Control (QC) sample was prepared by pooling equal aliquots of the supernatant from all individual samples to monitor system stability.</p>	<p>An Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was operated in information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode controlled by Xcalibur software. The ESI source parameters were: sheath gas flow rate 50 Arb, auxiliary gas flow rate 15 Arb, and capillary temperature 320 °C. The spray voltage was set at 3.8 kV in positive mode and -3.4 kV in negative mode. MS/MS spectra were acquired with a resolution of 15,000 and collision energy settings of SNCE 20/30/40. Full MS resolution was 60,000.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching the experimental MS/MS spectra and accurate mass against reference standards and spectral libraries. An in-house R package and the BiotreeDB (V3.0), which contains authentic standards and public database records (HMDB), were utilized for annotation. The identification confidence was prioritized based on MS/MS spectral similarity and precursor mass tolerance. </p>

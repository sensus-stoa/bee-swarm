Study Title	Serum LC-MS metabolomics profiling during musk secretion in captive forest musk deer (Moschus berezovskii)
Study Description	This study investigates serum metabolomic changes associated with musk secretion in captive male forest musk deer (Moschus berezovskii). Paired serum samples were collected before secretion and during secretion and analyzed using LC-MS in both positive and negative ion modes. The study includes biological samples and pooled quality-control samples and aims to characterize hormone-related metabolic remodeling during the musk secretion period.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Paired serum samples were collected from captive adult male forest musk deer (Moschus berezovskii) at two physiological stages: pre-secretion and secretion. Animals were maintained at the Zhiyang Tianbao Forest Musk Deer Artificial Breeding Base under comparable husbandry conditions. Blood samples were collected by a licensed veterinarian under gentle physical restraint without anesthesia. Approximately 5 mL of blood was drawn from the lateral saphenous vein after skin disinfection with 70% ethanol and collected into plain tubes. Samples were kept on ice and centrifuged at 600 x g for 10 min at 4 C. Serum was aliquoted into sterile cryovials and stored at -80 C until metabolomics analysis. Serum samples were thawed once before extraction. Pooled quality-control (QC) samples were prepared separately for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class PLUS system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 um, 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters). The injection volume was 1 uL and the flow rate was 0.4 mL min-1. The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B). The assay was conducted under reverse-phase LC conditions. Positive- and negative-ion datasets were acquired as separate assays using the same chromatographic platform and column configuration.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed in Progenesis QI (version 3.0) for peak detection, retention-time alignment, and signal normalization. Positive- and negative-ion datasets were processed separately. Quality-control performance was assessed during the analytical workflow, and only features with acceptable QC behavior were retained for downstream analysis. The resulting feature tables were used for metabolite annotation and statistical analysis.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction, 100 uL of serum was mixed with 500 uL of cold methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) containing 2-chloro-L-phenylalanine (20 mg L-1) as an internal standard. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, sonicated on ice for 10 min, incubated at -20 C for 1 h for protein precipitation, and centrifuged at approximately 9,600 x g for 15 min at 4 C. A total of 500 uL of supernatant was vacuum-dried and reconstituted in 160 uL acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v). The reconstituted extract was vortexed and sonicated again, cleared by centrifugation, and 120 uL was transferred to LC-MS vials for analysis. Pooled QC samples were prepared by mixing 10 uL aliquots from each serum sample and were injected regularly throughout the analytical run.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data for the positive-ion assay were acquired on a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source operating in positive ion mode. Data were collected using MSe acquisition with a low collision energy of 2 V and a high collision energy ramp of 10-40 V. Spectra were acquired over an m/z range of 50-1200 with an acquisition time of 0.2 s per spectrum. The mass analyzer was quadrupole time-of-flight.</p><p>Mass spectrometry data for the negative-ion assay were acquired on a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source operating in negative ion mode. Data were collected using MSe acquisition with a low collision energy of 2 V and a high collision energy ramp of 10-40 V. Spectra were acquired over an m/z range of 50-1200 with an acquisition time of 0.2 s per spectrum. The mass analyzer was quadrupole time-of-flight.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using Progenesis QI (version 3.0) by matching detected features against METLIN, KEGG, HMDB, LIPID MAPS, and an in-house spectral library. Precursor and fragment mass tolerances were set to 100 ppm and 50 ppm, respectively. Only features supported by acceptable isotopic and fragment evidence and showing acceptable QC performance were retained. Annotated metabolites were subsequently used for downstream interpretation, including focused reporting of hormone-related metabolites relevant to the study.</p>

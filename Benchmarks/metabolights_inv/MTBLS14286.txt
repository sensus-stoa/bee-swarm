STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS14286
Study Title	SCFA quantification in the feces of amitriptyline treated mice
Study Description	Amitriptyline, a tricyclic antidepressant commonly prescribed for depression and various other disorders, has been reported to possess both anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. Paradoxically, clinical evidence suggests that patients with depressive disorders receiving pharmacological treatment exhibit an increased susceptibility to infections, though the mechanisms behind this increased susceptibility remain unclear. This study investigates the impact of amitriptyline on intestinal homeostasis and vulnerability to enteropathogenic infection in a murine model. Oral administration of amitriptyline was found to significantly reduce gut microbial diversity and alter microbiota composition under both homeostatic and infectious conditions, as determined by 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Importantly, there was a marked depletion of butyrate-producing bacteria, accompanied by decreased fecal butyrate levels. Additionally, amitriptyline treatment led to a reduction in colonic regulatory T cells and cytokine production in specific pathogen-free mice, but not in germ-free animals.These results indicate that amitriptyline directly modulates host–microbiota interactions, and suggest that disruption of the gut microbiota may underlie the increased susceptibility to infection. Overall, this study underscores the critical role of the gut microbiome in shaping host responses to pharmacological interventions and identifies it as a potential therapeutic target to mitigate infection risks associated with amitriptyline use.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from mice, weighted, and stored at −80 °C until analysis to preserve metabolite integrity. Approximately 5 mg of frozen fecal material was used for subsequent extraction.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an Agilent 1260 Infinity HPLC system equipped with a Poroshell 120 EC-C18 column (3.0 × 150 mm, 2.7 µm) and a matching guard column.</p><p>The mobile phases consisted of:</p><p>Solvent A: Water + 0.1% formic acid</p><p>Solvent B: Acetonitrile + 0.1% formic acid</p><p>Gradient elution was applied from 60% B to 100% B over 10 minutes at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min. The total run time, including column re-equilibration, was 20 minutes.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were processed using MassHunter Quantitative Analysis software (version 10.1, Agilent Technologies).</p>	<p>Approximately 5 mg of fecal material was mixed with 750 µL ethanol and 10 µL of an ethanolic internal standard mixture containing 100 µM each d3-acetate (Sigma-Aldrich), d5-propionate and d5-butyrate (both from CDN Isotopes). Samples were homogenized using a Bead Ruptor 12 (Omni International), followed by 30 minutes of ultrasonic treatment. Subsequently, samples were centrifuged at 9,500 × g at 4 °C for 15 minutes. The supernatants were collected and evaporated to dryness using a a SpeedVac vacuum concentrator (Thermo Fisher Scientific). On the day of analysis, SCFA were derivatized using 2-diphenylacetyl-1,3-indandione-1-hydrazone (DAIH) and the catalyst 1-ethyl-3-(3-(dimethylamino)propyl)carbodiimide (EDC) (both from Sigma-Aldrich). The dry residues were dissolved in 75 µl of water and 25 µl of ethanol. This was followed by the addition of 50 µl of DAIH (0.6 mg/ml in acetonitrile/ethanol 80:20 v:v) and 50 µl of EDC (250 mM in ethanol containing 3% pyridine). The reaction was then carried out for 15 min at 80°C, followed by one hour of cooling. Dilutions in ethanol (1:10, v:v) were prepared from the reaction mixtures and subjected to HPLC-MS/MS</p>	<p>Detection was performed using an Agilent 6490 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source operating in negative ion mode (ESI−).</p><p>Analytes were quantified using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) with the following transitions (qualifier ions in parentheses):</p><p>Acetate: m/z 395.1 -&gt; 201.0 (159.0)</p><p>d3-Acetate: m/z 398.2 -&gt; 204.0 (159.0)</p><p>Propionate: m/z 409.2 -&gt; 215.0 (159.0)</p><p>d5-Propionate: m/z 414.2 -&gt; 219.9 (159.0)</p><p>Butyrate: m/z 423.2 -&gt; 229.1 (159.0)</p><p>d5-Butyrate: m/z 428.2 -&gt; 234.2 (159.0)</p>	<p>Peak integration and SCFA quantification in relation to their internal standards was performed with MassHunter Quantitative Analysis software (version 10.1, Agilent Technologies).</p>

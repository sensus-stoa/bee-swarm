Study Title	Multiomics analysis of residual feed intake regulation in calves by intestinal microbiota-mediated epigenetic modifications
Study Description	Feed efficiency is a critical trait affecting the economic profitability and environmental sustainability of livestock production. Residual Feed Intake (RFI), as a key metric for feed efficiency, effectively reflects differences in energy utilization for maintenance and production. This study aims to employ metabolomics to deeply investigate the differences in gut metabolic function between calves with distinct RFI phenotypes.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from calves with high and low residual feed intake. Approximately 2 g of fresh feces were obtained using a sterile sampling spoon and placed into 5 mL centrifuge tubes. The samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Thermo Fisher Scientific Q Exactive HF-X system.&nbsp;A 3 μL aliquot of the sample was separated on an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) prior to mass spectrometry detection. Mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (95/5, v/v) with 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B was acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5/47.5/5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was maintained at 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was set at 40.</p>	<p>Following instrumental analysis, the LC-MS raw data were imported into the Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, resulting in a data matrix comprising retention time, m/z, and peak intensity. Simultaneously, the MS and MS/MS spectral information was matched against public metabolomics databases, including HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as the Majorbio in-house library, to obtain metabolite identification.</p><p>The searched data matrix was uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform (cloud.majorbio.com) for further analysis. First, the data matrix underwent preprocessing as follows: Missing values were filtered using the 80% rule, meaning variables with non-zero values in at least 80% of the samples within one group were retained. Subsequently, missing values were imputed using the minimum value from the original matrix. To mitigate errors arising from sample preparation and instrument instability, the response intensity of the mass spectrometry peaks was normalized using the sum normalization method, yielding a normalized data matrix. Variables with a relative standard deviation (RSD) in QC samples exceeding 30% were removed, and the data were log10 transformed to obtain the final data matrix used for subsequent analysis.</p><p>Second, the processed data matrix was subjected to Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) using the ropls package (Version 1.6.2) in R software. Model stability was evaluated using 7-fold cross-validation. Significantly differential metabolites were selected based on the Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) derived from the OPLS-DA model and Student’s t-test p-values; metabolites with VIP &gt; 1 and p &lt; 0.05 were defined as significantly differential.</p>	<p>Weigh 100 mg of solid sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube and add a 6 mm grinding bead. Add 800 μL of extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing four internal standards (e.g., L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL) for metabolite extraction. Grind the sample solution in a frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5, 40 kHz). Place the samples at -20 for 30 min, then centrifuge for 15 min (4, 13000 g). Transfer the supernatant to autosampler vials with inserts for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes with a scan range of m/z 70–1050. The ion spray voltage was set to 3.5 kV for positive mode and -3.0 kV for negative mode. The curtain gas was maintained at 50 psi, the auxiliary gas at 13 psi, and the source temperature at 450. A rolling collision energy of 20–40–60 V was applied.</p>	<p>Differential metabolites were annotated to metabolic pathways using the KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/pathway.html). Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the Python scipy.stats package, and the biological pathways most associated with the experimental treatment were identified via Fisher's exact test.</p>

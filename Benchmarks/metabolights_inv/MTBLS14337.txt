Study Title	Integrated Metabolomics and Gut Microbiota Analysis to Elucidate the Underlying Mechanisms of Xuanfei Heji Against Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease
Study Description	<p>Xuanfei Heji (XFHJ) is a hospital-formulated herbal preparation and has demonstrated significant clinical efficacy in managing chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). However, its systemic pharmacological mechanisms and the interplay between host metabolism and microbial communities remain to be fully elucidated. The chemical compounds of XFHJ were analyzed using Q Exactive HF-X system. A rat model of COPD was established via intratracheal lipopolysaccharide instillation combined with cigarette smoke exposure. The therapeutic effects of XFHJ were evaluated by monitoring pulmonary function parameters, lung histopathology, and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α). Serum non-targeted metabolomics and fecal 16S rRNA sequencing were employed to identify differential metabolites and microbial signatures. Spearman's rank correlation was performed to integrate multi-omics data. A total of 57 chemical compounds were identified in XFHJ. XFHJ intervention significantly improved pulmonary function and attenuated lung pathological damage. Elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines were markedly downregulated. Metabolomic analysis revealed that XFHJ primarily modulated tryptophan and glycerophospholipid metabolism. Specifically, XFHJ restored indole-3-carbinol levels and upregulated microbiota-derived metabolites, including indole-3-propionic acid, indoleacrylic acid, and N-hydroxytryptamine. 16S sequencing showed that XFHJ restored microbial diversity and enriched beneficial genera such as Roseburia, unclassified_f_Lachnospiraceae, and specific Clostridia lineages, while reducing the abundance of pathogenic Klebsiella. Spearman analysis highlighted significant positive correlations between bioactive indole derivatives and the enriched Clostridia and Roseburia taxa. Collectively, these findings suggest that XFHJ alleviates COPD-related pathological and inflammatory changes in rats, potentially in association with the regulation of gut microbiota and glycerophospholipid- and tryptophan-related metabolic disturbances.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Rats were anesthetized with isoflurane, and blood samples were drawn from the abdominal aorta. After allowing the blood samples to stand at room temperature for 40 min, we centrifuged them at 4000 r/min and 4°C for 10 min. The separated serum was aliquoted and stored at -80°C.</p>	<p>The mobile phases consisted of (A) 95% water and 5% acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid, and (B) 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol, and 5% water containing 0.1% formic acid. In positive ion mode, the gradient program was as follows: 0–3 min, 0%–20% B; 3–4.5 min, 20%–35% B; 4.5–5 min, 35%–100% B; 5–6.3 min, 100% B; 6.3–6.4 min, 100%–0% B; 6.4–8 min, 0% B. In negative ion mode, the gradient was: 0–1.5 min, 0%–5% B; 1.5–2 min, 5%–10% B; 2–4.5 min, 10%–30% B; 4.5–5 min, 30%–100% B; 5–6.3 min, 100% B; 6.3–6.4 min, 100%–0% B; 6.4–8 min, 0% B. The injection volume was 3 μL, the flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>The raw metabolic features were subjected to a series of preprocessing steps. (1) Missing value filtration: Metabolic features with missing values exceeding 20% within every experimental group were deemed unreliable and removed; only features exhibiting a missing rate ≤ 20% in at least one experimental group were retained. (2) Missing value imputation: For the retained features, missing values were imputed using a half-minimum value approach, where the imputed value was set to one-half of the minimum detected value within the corresponding group. (3) Normalization: Data were normalized using an internal standard (IS) normalization method. 2-Chloro-L-phenylalanine was employed as the internal standard for both positive and negative ionization modes, respectively. (4) Quality control (QC) filtering: Only metabolic features with a relative standard deviation (RSD) ≤ 30% in the pooled QC samples were retained for further analysis. (5) Data transformation: The quantitative data obtained after the aforementioned preprocessing were subjected to log10 transformation to approximate a normal distribution for subsequent statistical modeling.</p>	<p>We accurately aliquoted an appropriate volume of serum and added 400 μL of cold methanol solution (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v). After vortex mixing, we performed ultrasonic extraction on ice three times, with each cycle lasting 10 min. The samples were then incubated at -20°C for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 × g and 4°C for 15 min. The resulting supernatant was collected for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were ionized by electrospray ionization (ESI), and mass spectral signals were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes over a mass scan range of m/z 70-1050. The ion source parameters were as follows: sheath gas flow rate, 60 arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 20 arb; ion source heater temperature, 350 °C; capillary temperature, 320 °C; spray voltage, +3400 V in positive ion mode and -3000 V in negative ion mode; S-lens RF level, 70. Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Full-scan MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 60,000, and MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000. Higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) fragmentation was performed using normalized collision energies of 20%, 40%, and 60%.</p>	<p>Raw data were imported into Progenesis QI v3.0 software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. Feature annotation was performed by matching the MS and MS/MS spectra against metabolic databases. The mass tolerance for MS matching was set to &lt; 10 ppm, and metabolite identification was based on the MS/MS matching score. The primary databases utilized included major public repositories such as HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as an in-house database.</p>

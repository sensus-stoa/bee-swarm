Study Title	Chilling-induced restructuring of seed-borne endophytes enhances early cold tolerance and microbiome assembly in common vetch
Study Description	Chilling stress constitutes a major abiotic constraint on plant growth and productivity. Although plants mobilize well-characterized physiological and transcriptional acclimation mechanisms, the integrative roles of root-associated microbiomes and root exudate chemistry in chilling tolerance, particularly in leguminous systems, remain incompletely resolved. Results In Vicia sativa, chilling induced transient physiological adjustments and system-wide transcriptomic reprogramming, with pronounced enrichment of genes associated with MAPK-mediated stress signaling and hormone transduction pathways. The endophytic bacterial community underwent marked restructuring, resulting in a root-enriched assemblage of chilling-associated taxa, notably Pseudomonas and members of the Allorhizobium–Neorhizobium–Pararhizobium–Rhizobium (ANPR) clade. Two chilling-enriched endophytes isolated from this system—Pseudomonas endophytica R9 and Rhizobium sp. R17—displayed complementary plant growth-promoting (PGP) traits. Co-inoculation with this consortium most effectively mitigated chilling-induced growth suppression, enhanced photosynthetic performance, and recalibrated phytohormone profiles, increasing jasmonic acid (JA) and abscisic acid (ABA) while reducing salicylic acid (SA) and cytokinin (CTK) concentrations. Inoculation further reshaped rhizosphere–endosphere microbiome assembly, promoted microbial recruitment from soil into root tissues, and increased ecological network complexity. Root exudate metabolomics revealed selective enrichment of antimicrobial metabolites whose abundance correlated with microbiome restructuring and suggested activation of a JA-mediated induced systemic resistance (ISR) pathway. This study demonstrates that chilling-enriched endophytes contribute to chilling tolerance in V. sativa through a coordinated mechanism involving root exudate reprogramming, microbiome reassembly, and activation of a JA-centered ISR pathway. The combined Pseudomonas–Rhizobium consortium described here represents a tractable microbial strategy for enhancing resilience in forage legumes under chilling stress.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Roots were gently cleaned of soil as described above and immersed in 30 mL of deionized water in sterile 50 mL centrifuge tubes. The roots remained attached to the plants to ensure the integrity of the root system and to mimic the natural exudate conditions. The tubes were then incubated for 8 h under controlled environmental conditions used for growth (4 °C, 16 h light/8 h dark, and 65% relative humidity). Exudate solutions were then collected, freeze-dried, weighed (≥20 mg), and stored in 1.5-mL tubes at −80 °C prior to analysis.</p>	<p>The UHPLC-MS/MS system used for metabolomics analysis consisted of a Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-performance liquid chromatography system coupled with a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high-resolution mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was performed on an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm) purchased from Waters.</p><p><br></p><p>Positive ion mode:Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water;Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile.</p><p><br></p><p>Negative ion mode:Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water;Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile.</p><p><br></p><p>The injection volume was 1 μL.</p>	<p>Raw data acquired using MassLynx V4.2 were processed using Progenesis QI software for peak picking, peak alignment, and other data processing procedures.</p>	<p>1.Pipette 100 μL of sample, add 500 μL of extraction solution (methanol:acetonitrile = 1:1, v/v; internal standard concentration = 2 mg/L) containing an internal standard at a ratio of 1000:2, and vortex-mix for 30 s.</p><p><br></p><p>2.Sonicate for 10 min in an ice-water bath.</p><p><br></p><p>3.Allow the sample to stand at −20 °C for 1 h.</p><p><br></p><p>4.Centrifuge the sample at 12000 rpm and 4 °C for 15 min.</p><p><br></p><p>5.Carefully transfer 500 μL of the supernatant to an Eppendorf tube.</p><p><br></p><p>6.Dry the extract in a vacuum concentrator.</p><p><br></p><p>7.Reconstitute the dried metabolites with 160 μL of extraction solution (acetonitrile:water = 1:1, v/v).</p><p><br></p><p>8.Vortex for 30 s and sonicate in an ice-water bath for 10 min.</p><p><br></p><p>9.Centrifuge the sample at 12000 rpm and 4 °C for 15 min.</p><p><br></p><p>10.Carefully transfer 120 μL of the supernatant to a 2 mL autosampler vial. A quality control (QC) sample is prepared by mixing 10 μL aliquots from each individual sample for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>The Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high-resolution mass spectrometer was operated in MSe mode under the control of MassLynx V4.2 software (Waters) for the acquisition of both primary and secondary mass spectral data. In each data acquisition cycle, dual-channel data collection was performed simultaneously at low collision energy and high collision energy. The low collision energy was set at 2 V, the high collision energy range was 10–40 V, and the scanning frequency was 0.2 seconds per mass spectrum. Parameters of the ESI ion source were as follows: capillary voltage: 2000 V (positive ion mode) or −1500 V (negative ion mode); cone voltage: 30 V; ion source temperature: 150 °C; desolvation temperature: 500 °C; purge gas flow rate: 50 L/h; desolvation gas flow rate: 800 L/h.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed based on the online METLIN database integrated in Progenesis QI and a self-built database from Biomarker Technologies. Theoretical fragment identification was also conducted, with mass deviations all within 100 ppm.</p>

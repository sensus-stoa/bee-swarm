Study Identifier	MTBLS14341
Study Title	Saccharomyces boulardii alleviates acute pancreatitis by producing inosine that activates the A2AR/CREB signaling axis
Study Description	<p>This study investigates the protective role of Saccharomyces boulardii (Sb) in a cerulein-induced mouse model of acute pancreatitis (AP). Sb administration significantly reduced pancreatic injury, inflammation, and apoptosis. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses revealed that Sb produces inosine, which is the key functional metabolite. Inosine protected against AP by activating the A2AR/cAMP/PKA/CREB signaling pathway in pancreatic acinar cells, independent of the gut microbiota. The ISN1 gene in Sb was identified as critical for inosine synthesis. These findings highlight Sb-derived inosine as a potential therapeutic agent for AP.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from C57BL/6 mice with acute pancreatitis (AP) and corresponding control mice. All samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until further processing, with no additional treatment prior to storage.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class plus system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). The mobile phase consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile. The column temperature was maintained at 45°C, the flow rate was 0.35 mL/min, and the injection volume was 4 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using MZmine for peak picking, alignment, and feature detection. Metabolite annotation was performed using HMDB and METLIN databases, followed by normalization and statistical analysis using MetaboAnalyst.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 100 μL of each fecal sample using 400 μL of ice-cold methanol:acetonitrile (2:1, v/v) containing mixed internal standards (4 μg/mL). The mixture was vortexed for 1 min, ultrasonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min, and incubated at -40°C overnight. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C, 150 μL of the supernatant was collected, filtered through a 0.22 μm organic syringe filter, and transferred to LC vials. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all study sample extracts. All extraction reagents were pre-cooled at -20°C before use.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on a Thermo Q Exactive HF (QE HF) high-resolution mass spectrometer coupled to the Waters UPLC system. Data acquisition parameters were set according to standard protocols for metabolomics profiling, including heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source in positive and negative modes.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and annotation were performed using a multi-step workflow. Raw LC-MS features from both positive (POS) and negative (NEG) ionization modes were detected and aligned using MZmine. Accurate mass matching was performed against public databases including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and METLIN, with a mass tolerance of 5 ppm. Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) spectra were matched against reference libraries for enhanced confidence. All annotations were classified according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) confidence levels.</p>

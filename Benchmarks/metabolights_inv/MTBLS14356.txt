Study Identifier	MTBLS14356
Study Title	Integrated Analysis of Gut Microbiota, Serum Metabolomics, and Proteomics Reveals Novel Associations With Clinical Symptoms in Patients With Cerebral Infarction
Study Description	Cerebral infarction (CI) is a major cause of adult disability and mortality worldwide. Mounting evidence supports the critical role of the gut–brain axis in cerebrovascular disease progression. This study aimed to characterize the alterations in gut microbiota, serum metabolome, and serum proteome in patients with CI, and to identify multi-omics signatures associated with clinical symptoms. A total of 20 CI patients and 20 healthy controls (HC) were enrolled. Fecal microbiota was profiled using 16S rRNA gene high-throughput sequencing. Serum metabolomics and proteomics were analyzed using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC–MS/MS) and data-independent acquisition (DIA) proteomics, respectively. Spearman correlation and multi-omics integration were applied to explore the associations among microbiota, metabolites, proteins, and clinical indicators.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human fecal samples were collected from subjects assigned to two groups, namely the CI group and the control group. All samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen after collection and stored at −80 °C until further pretreatment and metabolomic analysis, avoiding repeated freeze-thaw cycles to maintain metabolite stability.</p>	<p>Parameter Value[Chromatography Instrument]: Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography systemParameter Value[Autosampler model]: Thermostatted autosampler compatible with UHPLCParameter Value[Column model]: Waters HSS T3 chromatographic columnParameter Value[Guard column]: Waters HSS T3 guard column</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were imported and processed using Progenesis QI software. Peak detection, alignment, noise reduction, and peak area integration were performed systematically to generate a matrix of metabolite features with corresponding peak intensities. Data normalization and quality control filtering were conducted to ensure reliability for subsequent statistical and identification analysis.</p>	<p>Fecal samples were thawed on ice, and an appropriate aliquot was mixed with ice-cold methanol-water solution for metabolite extraction. The mixture was vortexed vigorously, sonicated in an ice-water bath, and centrifuged at low temperature to precipitate proteins and debris. The supernatant was collected, filtered through a microporous membrane, and transferred to chromatographic vials for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>Parameter Value[Scan m/z range]: 50–1500 m/zParameter Value[Instrument]: Triple TOF 5600 systemParameter Value[Ion source]: Electrospray ionization (ESI) sourceParameter Value[Mass analyzer]: Quadrupole time-of-flight (Q-TOF) mass analyzer</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed by matching experimental mass spectral data, accurate mass measurements, and fragmentation patterns against reference databases including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and the Metlin database. Metabolites were identified at level 2 or above according to metabolomics reporting standards.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Bacterial samples (Vibrio harveyi) were cultured under standard laboratory conditions. The treatment group was exposed to polysaccharide, while the control group received no treatment. After incubation, cells were collected by centrifugation, washed with sterile saline, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system equipped with a C18 column. The mobile phase consisted of water with 0.1% formic acid (A) and acetonitrile (B), using a gradient elution program. The flow rate was set at 0.3 mL/min, column temperature at 40 °C, and injection volume at 2 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using standard software for peak detection, alignment, and normalization. The resulting data matrix was subjected to multivariate statistical analysis, including PCA and OPLS-DA.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted by adding cold methanol/acetonitrile/water (2:2:1, v/v/v) to the samples, followed by vortexing and sonication. After incubation at −20 °C, samples were centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected for LC-MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a high-resolution mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. The scan range was m/z 50–1000, with typical source parameters set according to standard operation conditions.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by comparing mass spectra and retention times with public databases such as HMDB, METLIN, and KEGG. Annotation was based on accurate mass and MS/MS fragmentation patterns.</p>

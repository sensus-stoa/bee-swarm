Study Title	Role and Mechanism of SERPINA1/PLG/TGFB1-Induced Adipose Fibroblast Activation in Dorsal Cervical Adipose Accumulation
Study Description	This untargeted metabolomics study investigates metabolic differences in human adipose tissue between Group A (n=7) and Group B (n=7). Samples were analyzed using LC-MS/MS in both positive and negative ion modes on a Thermo Q-Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer. A total of 2,305 metabolites were detected, of which 2,032 were identified at MS2 level. Differential metabolites were screened using VIP > 1 (from OPLS-DA) and P-value < 0.05 (Student's t-test). A total of 145 significant differential metabolites were identified, with 73 up-regulated and 72 down-regulated in Group A relative to Group B. KEGG pathway enrichment, HMDB annotation, and MSEA were performed to characterize the functional implications of these metabolic changes. Raw data files (mzML format) and processed data tables are provided.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human adipose tissue samples were surgically obtained as discarded pathological specimens. Specimens were immediately stored at 80°C until processing. For extraction, samples were thawed on ice prior to homogenization. Samples were collected in 2025. No specific clinical information beyond group assignment was recorded.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Scientific, Massachusetts, USA) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 0.1% formic acid in water and (B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The flow rate was set at 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 4 μL. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–2 min, 5–20% B; 2–5 min, 20–60% B; 5–6 min, 60–99% B; 6–7.5 min, 99% B; 7.5–10 min, 5% B. The same chromatographic conditions were applied for both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data (.raw) were converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard. Peak detection, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using XCMS. Features with &gt;50% missing values in any group were filtered out. Missing values were imputed using KNN imputation or, if &gt;50% missing, replaced with one-fifth of the minimum value. Peak areas were corrected using support vector regression (SVR). Metabolite identification was carried out by searching against an in-house database, public databases, predicted libraries, and the metDNA method. Only metabolites with an identification score 0.5 and a QC sample coefficient of variation (CV) &lt;0.3 were retained. Finally, positive and negative ion mode data were merged, keeping the metabolite with the highest identification level and the smallest CV for each compound. The processed data are provided in the file all_sample_data.xlsx.</p>	<p>Approximately 20 mg of frozen adipose tissue was weighed and homogenized with a steel ball using a grinder (30 Hz, 20 s). After centrifugation (3,000 rpm, 30 s, 4°C), 400 μL of 70% methanol in water containing internal standards was added, vortexed (1,500 rpm, 5 min), and placed on ice for 15 min. The mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. Then 300 μL of supernatant was collected and kept at 20°C for 30 min, followed by a second centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 3 min at 4°C. Finally, 200 μL of supernatant was transferred to an LC-via for analysis.</p><p>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of all sample extracts and were injected every 10 analytical runs to monitor instrument stability and reproducibility. Internal standards were added to all samples during extraction.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Massachusetts, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. Full scan MS spectra were collected over a mass range of m/z 75–1000 at a resolution of 35,000 (FWHM). Data-dependent MS/MS spectra (MS2) were acquired at a resolution of 17,500 with stepped collision energies of 30, 40, and 50 V. The signal intensity threshold was set at 1e6 cps, and the top 10 precursors were selected per scan cycle with a dynamic exclusion duration of 3 seconds.</p><p><br></p><p>Key source parameters for positive ion mode: spray voltage 3500 V; for negative ion mode: spray voltage 3200 V. Common parameters for both modes: sheath gas flow rate 30 arb, auxiliary gas flow rate 5 arb, ion transfer tube temperature 320°C, vaporizer temperature 300°C.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by searching the acquired MS/MS spectra against an in-house database, public databases (including HMDB, KEGG, and METLIN), predicted libraries, and using the metDNA method. The identification criteria required a minimum identification score of 0.5. For each metabolite, the identification level was assigned based on available spectral matching. Only metabolites with a QC sample coefficient of variation (CV) &lt;0.3 were retained after identification. Positive and negative ion mode data were merged, keeping the metabolite with the highest identification level and the smallest CV for each compound. A total of 2,305 metabolites were detected, of which 2,032 were identified at MS2 level</p>

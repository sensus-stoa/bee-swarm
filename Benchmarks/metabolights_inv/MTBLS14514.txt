Study Title	The mechanism of vascular injury caused by polystyrene microplastics: A molecular toxicology study involving ferroptosis and metabolomics
Study Description	<p>Background: Polystyrene microplastics (PS-MPs) are degradation products of plastics that are prevalent in the environment. Recent studies have established a significant correlation between exposure to PS-MPs and the incidence of cardiovascular diseases (CVD). Objective: This study aims to investigate the specific mechanisms underlying the vascular toxicity effects of exposure to environmental polystyrene microplastics (PS-MPs) in mice.Conclusion: The findings of our research indicate that exposure to PS-MPs leads to vascular toxicity, which is marked by injury and dysfunction of the vascular endothelial. This phenomenon is potentially driven by oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and metabolic disturbances. Importantly, administration of Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) significantly alleviates vascular endothelial injury and reduces vascular remodeling, thereby improving vascular endothelial dysfunction.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Use scissors to remove the hair from the chest area of the mouse. Prepare a 1 mL syringe in advance and insert it into the 2-3rd intercostal space of the mouse to draw the blood from the heart. Draw the blood slowly. After drawing the blood, transfer it to a centrifuge tube. Let it stand at room temperature for 30 minutes and then centrifuge. Use a pipette to take the supernatant and transfer it to a new centrifuge tube. Store the separated serum in a -80℃ refrigerator for subsequent testing.</p>	<p>The analytical instrument platform used in this LC-MS analysis is the UHPLC-Exploris240 system from Thermo Fisher, which is a high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with Fourier transform mass spectrometry.</p><p>The chromatographic column is ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3, 100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d, 1.8 μm, Waters, Milford, USA). The mobile phase A is 95% water + 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and the mobile phase B is 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid). The injection volume is 3 μL, and the column temperature is 40°C.</p>	<p>Before conducting the statistical analysis, a series of preprocessing steps need to be carried out on the original data. The raw data is imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis Ql (Waters Corporation, Miford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. Then, data preprocessing is performed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>Take the mouse serum, accurately measure the sample volume, then add 400 µL of cold methanol solution (methanol:water = 4:1) to the sample. After performing operations such as homogenization, mixing, and ultrasonic extraction, let it stand for 30 minutes and then centrifuge to obtain the supernatant.</p>	<p>The samples were subjected to electrospray ionization, and mass spectrometric signals were collected using both positive and negative ion scanning modes.The manufacturer of UHPLC liquid chromatography system is Thermo Scientific, and the model of this system is Vanquish Horizon. The manufacturer of mass spectrometer is also Thermo Scientific, and the model of this mass spectrometer is Exploris240.Scan type(m/z) is 70-1050</p>	<p>The raw data was imported into the metabolomics processing software ProgenesisQI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, etc. Eventually, a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity and other information was obtained. Subsequently, this software was used for feature peak library search and identification. The MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry information was matched with the metabolic database, with the MS mass error set to less than 10 ppm. At the same time, metabolites were identified based on the secondary mass spectrometry matching score. The main databases included mainstream public databases such as htp://www.hmdb.ca/ and https://metlin.scripps.edu/, as well as self-built data.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14545
Study Title	Targeted activation of USP5 ameliorates MASLD
Study Description	To explore the regulatory mechanism of USP5 in the progression of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), wild-type and USP5-overexpressing mice were subjected to a high-fat diet (HFD) to establish an obese and fatty liver model. Hepatic tissues were harvested for untargeted/targeted metabolomic analysis. The differential metabolites and enriched metabolic pathways were screened to clarify the key endogenous metabolites mediated by USP5, thereby revealing the underlying molecular metabolic mechanism by which USP5 modulates the occurrence and development of MASLD.

Study Protocol Description	<p>USP5-floxed and CKI mice were fed with a high-fat diet. Liver tissues were collected for untargeted metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2865'>The LC-MS analysis was performed on an UHPLC-Triple TOF 6600 system manufactured by ABSCIEX.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2865'>Chromatographic conditions: Separation was carried out using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, USA). Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water and 5% acetonitrile supplemented with 0.1% formic acid. Mobile phase B was composed of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol and 5% water containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was set at 0.40 mL/min, the injection volume was 10 μL, and the column temperature was maintained at 45 °C.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2865'>Mass spectrometric conditions: Electrospray ionization (ESI) was adopted, and mass spectral data were acquired in both positive and negative ion scanning modes.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='3046'>Raw data were imported into metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA).Subsequent procedures including baseline filtering, peak identification, peak integration, retention time correction and peak alignment were performed.Finally, a data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity was obtained.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>1. Accurately weigh 50 ± 5 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and place one 6 mm diameter grinding bead into the tube.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>2. Add 400 μL extraction solution consisting of methanol and water at a volume ratio of 4:1 (v:v), which contains four internal standards including L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL).</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>3. Homogenize the samples using a frozen tissue grinder at 50 Hz and −10 °C for 6 min.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>4. Perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction at 5 °C and 40 KHz for 30 min.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>5. Place the samples at −20 °C for 30 min for static treatment.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>6. Centrifuge the mixture at 13000 g and 4 °C for 15 min. Transfer the supernatant into injection vials with inner inserts for subsequent instrumental analysis.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2808'>7. Furthermore, take 20 μL supernatant from each individual sample and mix them evenly to prepare quality control (QC) samples.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2943'>Scan range (m/z): 50–1200Ion Source Gas 1 (psi): 50Auxiliary heating gas: 4Ion Source Gas 2 (psi): 50Curtain Gas (psi): 35Source Temperature (℃): 500Positive ion spray voltage (V): 5500Negative ion spray voltage (V): −4500Interface heater: OnDeclustering Potential (V): 80Collision Energy (eV): 40±20</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='3123'>Subsequently, characteristic peaks were identified via database searching using ProgenesisQIv3.0. The MS and MS/MS spectral information was matched against metabolite databases, with the MS mass error set to less than 10 ppm. Metabolites were further annotated based on MS/MS matching scores.The main databases included public databases such as the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB, http://www.hmdb.ca/), METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as an in-house database.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14587
Study Title	Effects of biodegradable Film Mulching on Yield, Grain Quality, and Soil Metabolic Characteristics of Water-Saving and Drought-Resistant Rice
Study Description	This study investigates the metabolic changes in paddy soil subjected to three different rice cultivation treatments: non-flooded unmulched cultivation (UM), non-flooded biodegradable film mulching cultivation (BM), and traditional flooding cultivation (TF). Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed on rhizosphere soil samples collected at harvest stage using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) in both positive and negative ionization modes. Raw data were acquired on a SCIEX mass spectrometer (wiff format). Soil metabolites were annotated using the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and KEGG pathways. This study is part of a larger investigation into the effects of biodegradable film mulching on yield, grain quality, and soil metabolic characteristics of water-saving and drought-resistant rice (variety Hanyou 73).

Study Protocol Description	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Rhizosphere soil samples were collected from a pot experiment conducted in a mobile greenhouse in 2023. Three rice cultivation treatments were applied: traditional flooding cultivation (TF), non-flooded biodegradable film mulching cultivation (BM), and non-flooded unmulched cultivation (UM). Soil samples were collected at the crop harvest stage from the 0-15 cm soil layer using a soil drill. For each treatment, soil from five pots was pooled into one composite sample, which was then divided into three independent biological replicates. A total of 9 soil samples (3 treatments × 3 replicates) were collected. Additionally, pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal aliquots of all soil samples. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Shimadzu Nexera X2 LC-30AD UHPLC system. The column used was a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm), maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0-2 min, 0% B; 2-6 min, 0-48% B; 6-10 min, 48-100% B; 10-12 min, 100% B; 12-12.1 min, 100-0% B; 12.1-15 min, 0% B for re-equilibration. The injection volume was 2 µL.</p>	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Raw MS data (`.wiff` files) were processed using MS-DIAL software (version 4.0) for peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction. The processing parameters were set as follows: mass tolerance: 0.01 Da for MS1 and 0.025 Da for MS2; retention time tolerance: 0.05 min; minimum peak height: 1000 amplitude; mass slice width: 0.1 Da; smoothing method: linear weighted moving average with 2 scans; retention time factor: 0.5; peak identification was based on the MS-DIAL built-in library and public databases. Metabolites were identified by accurate mass (mass tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS data (mass tolerance &lt; 0.02 Da) matching against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), MassBank, and an in-house metabolite standard library.</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'><br></p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>For extracted ion features, only variables with more than 50% of non-zero measurement values in at least one experimental group were retained. Data were then normalized by sum of peak intensities and Pareto-scaled (mean-centered and divided by the square root of standard deviation) prior to multivariate analysis.</p>	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Soil metabolites were extracted from frozen soil samples. Approximately 200 mg of soil was accurately weighed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. Then, 1 mL of pre-cooled extraction solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water = 2:2:1, v/v/v) was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 seconds and then sonicated in an ice-water bath for 1 hour. The mixture was then incubated at -20 °C for 1 hour, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully transferred to a new tube and evaporated to dryness under vacuum. The dried residue was reconstituted in 200 µL of 50% acetonitrile (or methanol:water = 1:1, v/v) by vortexing and sonication, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C. The final supernatant was transferred to an autosampler vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'><br></p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>For quality control (QC) samples, an equal aliquot (10 µL) of each extracted soil sample was pooled and mixed thoroughly. The pooled QC sample was then aliquoted and processed in the same manner as individual samples. The QC sample was injected periodically throughout the analytical run (every 5-10 injections) to monitor instrument stability and data reproducibility.</p>	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Mass spectrometry was performed using an AB Sciex Triple-TOF 5600 mass spectrometer (SCIEX, USA) coupled with the UHPLC system. Electrospray ionization (ESI) was used as the ion source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes separately. Full MS scans were acquired over the m/z range of 70-1050 Da.</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'><br></p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>The source parameters were set as follows:</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- Spray voltage: 3.8 kV (positive mode) / 3.2 kV (negative mode)</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- Capillary temperature: 320 °C</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- Sheath gas (nitrogen) flow: 30 arb</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- Auxiliary gas flow: 5 arb</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- Probe heater temperature: 350 °C</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>- S-Lens RF level: 50</p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'><br></p><p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>The mass analyzer was a time-of-flight (TOF) mass analyzer. The resolution was set to 70,000 at m/z 200 for full MS scans. For MS/MS acquisitions, the resolution was set to 17,500 at m/z 200. The maximum injection time was 100 ms for MS and 50 ms for MS/MS. The isolation window for MS/MS was set to 2 m/z, and stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 30, and 40 were used for fragmentation.</p>	<p style='text-shadow: rgb(74, 74, 74) 0px 0px 0px !important;'>Metabolite identification was performed using MS-DIAL software (version 4.0). Metabolites were annotated by matching accurate mass (mass tolerance &lt; 10 ppm) and MS/MS fragmentation patterns (mass tolerance &lt; 0.02 Da) against multiple reference databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), MassBank, and an in-house metabolite standard library. The identification criteria required a mass error within ±10 ppm and MS/MS spectral similarity score above 70% (dot product). For metabolites without MS/MS confirmation, putative annotations were assigned based on accurate mass and isotopic pattern matching. The identified metabolites were further classified using HMDB superclass and class ontologies.</p>

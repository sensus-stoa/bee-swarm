Study Identifier	MTBLS14614
Study Title	Identification of a specialized lipid barrier for Drosophila metamorphosis (MS (MTBLS))
Study Description	<p>Many terrestrial insects transition from humid to dry environments at metamorphosis yet how they avoid dehydration is unclear. In <em>Drosophila melanogaster</em>, we identify a lipid barrier to water loss in the pupa that is anatomically distinct from that of the adult. It comprises unisex hydrocarbons that are longer and less volatile than their sex-specific adult counterparts. These hyper-long hydrocarbons are produced by larval oenocytes and stored in the fat body for subsequent transport to the cuticle at pupariation. A fatty acid elongase, <em>EloHL</em>, is required for hyper-long hydrocarbon biosynthesis and for a functional barrier to dehydration during early metamorphosis. Hyper-long barrier hydrocarbons are widespread across the <em>Drosophila</em> genus and typically transition to shorter sex-specific versions during adult maturation. In the desert species <em>D. mojavensis</em>, however, adults retain hyper-long hydrocarbons. This study reveals how insect lipid barriers are tuned to environmental variations in aridity and temperature during development and evolution.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>For analysis of surface hydrocarbons, five larvae or pupae were collected from the side of the vial with a paintbrush, washed with PBS, dried on tissue paper, and weighed in analysis groups on a microbalance (Sartorius Lab Instruments, Sartorius Cubis MSE3.6P-000-DM). Adults were anesthetized with CO2 and weighed on a microscale. </p>	<p>Splitless injection of 1 µl (injection temperature 310°C) onto a 30 m + 10 m × 0.25 mm BPX5 column (SGE) was used, with helium as the carrier gas. The initial oven temperature was 100°C (1 min), followed by a temperature gradient to 350°C at 10°C/min with an optional hold time of 25 minutes used for internal and whole animal hydrocarbon samples.</p>	<p>&nbsp;Peak areas were first normalized to an internal standard (octadecane) spiked into the sample at the time of metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Pooled individuals were washed in 50 µl hexane with 0.1 mM octadecane (internal standard) in a GC vial (Agilent Technologies 5182-0715) for 2 min by agitating the solution. The hexane extract was then transferred to a new GC vial and capped. For analysis of internal hydrocarbons, five (or 10 for the data in Figure S3C) larvae, pupae or adults were first processed as above to remove external surface hydrocarbons. For analysis of specific internal tissues, fat body, inverted carcass (with all internal tissues and the head removed), and remaining internal organs (gut, Malpighian tubules, crop) referred to as gut were dissected from 10 larvae in phosphate buffered saline (PBS). Larval cuticles (puparia) were dissected from ~30 individuals after head eversion, at ~24 hr APF. Pooled tissues or whole animals were transferred to a metal Precellys tube with 500 µl hexane with 0.01 mM octadecane (internal standard) and homogenized using the Precellys Evolution tissue homogenizer. Samples were homogenized for four rounds of 25 seconds at 6000 rpm, transferred to a 1.5 ml microcentrifuge tube, and centrifuged for 10 min at 14,000 rpm. The supernatant was then transferred to a GC vial and the hexane allowed to evaporate overnight in a fume hood. Samples were then reconstituted in 50 µl hexane and the GC vials capped.</p>	<p>High temperature gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (HT-GC-MS) utilized an Agilent 7890B-7000C GC–MS system in EI mode, positive polarity with a mass range of 50 - 550.</p>	<p>Compounds were identified by referencing commercial standards when available, or by retention time and fragmentation pattern when standards were unavailable.</p>

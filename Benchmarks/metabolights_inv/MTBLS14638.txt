Study Protocol Description	<p>B-ALL cells were obtained from the patients who were provided informed consent in accordance with the guidelines of the local ethics committee. The cells were treated by imatinib overnight, following collection for LC-MS.</p><p><br></p><p>p190+ BAF3 cells were stably transfected with shRNA for ASS1. p190+ BAF3 of WT or ASS1 Knockdown were intravanously injected into tail vein of Six- to eight-week-old BALB/c-nu mice. 2 weeks later, p190+ BAF3 cells were collected from PBMC of the mouse for further LC-MS.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Cells were collected, and metabolites were extracted with cold 80% (v/v) methanol via thorough mixing. The extracts were centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 10 min, and the supernatant was evaporated to dryness under vacuum using a SpeedVac evaporator. To globally profile cellular metabolites, two complementary LC–MS/MS methods were employed in this study.</p><p>The first method primarily targeted organic acids (e.g., tricarboxylic acid [TCA] cycle intermediates and nucleotides) and was performed on a TSQ Quantiva Triple Quadrupole mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with positive/negative ion switching for the analysis of 377 metabolites. Consequently, some metabolites were detected in the results of both analytical approaches. Mobile phase A was prepared by adding 2.376 mL of tributylamine and 0.858 mL of acetic acid to high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)-grade water, which was then brought to a final volume of 1 L with additional HPLC-grade water. Mobile phase B was HPLC-grade methanol. A Synergi Hydro-RP C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, Phenomenex, USA) was used for polar metabolite separation at a column temperature of 35℃. Ion transitions were optimized using chemical standards, and metabolites were identified via Tracefinder 3.2 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) based on retention times. Chromatographic areas were used for relative quantitation.</p><p>The second method focused on amine-containing metabolites, including amino acids and their derivatives, and was conducted using a Q Exactive HFX orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, CA, USA) in positive ion mode. A BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters, USA) was utilized for separation at a column temperature of 35℃. Mobile phase A was prepared by dissolving 0.63 g of ammonium formate in 50 mL of HPLC-grade water, followed by the addition of 950 mL of HPLC-grade acetonitrile and 1 mL of formic acid. Mobile phase B was prepared by dissolving 0.63 g of ammonium formate in 500 mL of HPLC-grade water, then adding 500 mL of HPLC-grade acetonitrile and 1 mL of formic acid. Separation was initiated with 1% mobile phase B at a flow rate of 300 μl/min. Metabolite identification was performed using Tracefinder 3.2 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, CA, USA) with an in-house library containing MS/MS spectra of over 1500 endogenous metabolites. A mass tolerance of 10 ppm and 15 ppm was applied for precursor and fragment ion searches, respectively. Metabolites were assigned based on fragment matching with MS/MS spectra in the library. Two levels of identification were achieved: one with MS/MS confirmation, and the other based solely on the accurate masses of precursor ions. Chromatographic peak areas were used for relative quantitation, with a maximum retention time (RT) shift of 0.25 min allowed for peak alignment.</p>	<p>The acquired MS data pretreatments including peak picking, peak grouping, retention time correction, second peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts was performed using XCMS software. LC-MS raw data files were converted into mzXML format and then processed by the XCMS, CAMERA and metaXtoolbox implemented with the R software</p>	<p>cells were collected and metabolites were extracted with cold 80% methanol (v/v) by thorough mixing. Extracts were centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 10 min and the supernatant was evaporated to dryness under vacuum in a SpeedVac evaporator.</p>	<p>1) TSQ Quantiva Triple Quadrupole mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with positive/negative ion switching for the analysis of 377 metabolites. A BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters, USA) was utilized for separation at a column temperature of 35℃. Mobile phase B was prepared by dissolving 0.63 g of ammonium formate in 500 mL of HPLC-grade water, then adding 500 mL of HPLC-grade acetonitrile and 1 mL of formic acid.

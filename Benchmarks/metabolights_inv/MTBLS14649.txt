Study Identifier	MTBLS14649
Study Title	An alkaline-extracted low-molecular-weight heteroglucan from Ganoderma lucidum enhances immunity by modulating the gut microbiota
Study Description	<p><em>Ganoderma</em> lucidum polysaccharides exhibit various biological activities, but conventional extraction is biased toward high-molecular-weight polymers. This study aimed to explore the structure and bioactivity of low-molecular-weight polysaccharides from <em>Ganoderma</em> <em>lucidum</em>, referred to as AGLP1. A low-molecular-weight heteroglucan from <em>Ganoderma lucidum</em> was purified by alkaline extraction, membrane filtration and column chromatography. Structural characterization indicated that AGLP1, with an approximate molecular weight of 9.0 kDa, is a heteroglucan mainly composed of a 1,3-linked glucopyranosyl backbone with 1,6-linked glucopyranosyl side chains. In vivo investigations showed that AGLP1 enhanced the spleen index, macrophage phagocytic activity and proliferation of T and B lymphocytes. These immunomodulatory effects were gut microbiota-dependent, as they were reduced in microbiota-depleted mice after antibiotic treatment. Mechanistically, AGLP1 reshaped the gut microbiota and metabolite profile by enriching potentially beneficial bacteria such as <em>Ruminococcaceae</em> and <em>Eubacterium brachy group</em>, as well as associated metabolites including decanoic acid, stearic acid and pimelic acid. In vitro fermentation using a human fecal model showed that AGLP1 also increased the abundances of <em>Ruminococcaceae</em> and <em>Eubacterium brachy group</em>. These findings indicate that AGLP1 has immunomodulatory and microbiota-reshaping effects and may have potential as a functional food ingredient.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples (100 μL) were placed in the EP tubes and resuspended with prechilled 80% methanol by well vortex. Then the samples were incubated on ice for 5 min and centrifuged at 15,000 g, 4°C for 20 min. Some of supernatant was diluted to final concentration containing 53% methanol by LC-MS grade water.The samples were subsequently transferred to a fresh Eppendorf tube and then were centrifuged at 15000 g, 4°C for 20 min.</p>	<p>UHPLC separation was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC system with a Hypersil Gold column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.9 um). A 12-min reverse-phase gradient was used at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The mobile phases were 0.1% formic acid in water and methanol.</p>	<p>UHPLC-MS/MS data were processed using XCMS for peak alignment, peak picking and quantitation. Missing values were filtered and imputed, background ions were removed according to blank samples, and relative peak areas were normalized. Compounds with a coefficient of variation greater than 30% in quality control samples were filtered out.</p>	<p>Cecal content samples were extracted with prechilled 80% methanol. The samples were vortexed, incubated on ice and centrifuged. The supernatant was diluted with LC-MS grade water to a final methanol concentration of 53%, centrifuged again and used for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed using a Thermo Fisher Q Exactive HF or Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer operated with electrospray ionization in positive and negative ion modes. The scan range was m/z 100-1500.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by matching adduct ions and accurate mass information with a mass deviation threshold of 10 ppm against the in-house high-quality secondary spectrum database NovoMetDB. Metabolites were further annotated using KEGG, HMDB and LIPIDMaps databases.</p>

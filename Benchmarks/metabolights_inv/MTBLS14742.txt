Study Identifier	MTBLS14742
Study Title	LC-MS based metabolomics analysis of mouse fecal metabolites reveals microbiota-derived products that impair colonic stem cell regeneration in Lamtor5+/- mice
Study Description	This study aimed to investigate the metabolic alterations in mouse fecal samples and identify microbiota-derived products responsible for impaired colonic stem cell regeneration in Lamtor5+/- mice. A total of four experimental groups were included: wild-type (WT) mice treated with PBS (control), Lamtor5+/- mice treated with PBS, WT mice treated with DSS, and Lamtor5+/- mice treated with DSS. Fecal samples were collected and analyzed using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) in both positive and negative ion modes. Untargeted metabolomics data preprocessing included peak filtering, missing value imputation, normalization, and log transformation. Differential metabolites were identified and annotated using KEGG and HMDB databases. The results provide insights into how the absence of the H gene alters gut microbiota-derived metabolites and subsequently reduces colonic stem cell regeneration capacity.

Study Protocol Description	<p>WT mice or Lamtor5+/- mice were randomly divided into two groups: control group (normal drinking water) and DSS-treated group (drinking water containing DSS). After 7 days of treatment, fecal samples were collected from each mouse. The collected feces were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and then stored at -80°C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatography was performed on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). Mobile phase A consisted of 95% water and 5% acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B consisted of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol and 5% water containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. The injection volume was 3 μL and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C.</p>	<p>Raw data were imported into Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for data processing. The processing steps included baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. A data matrix containing retention time, m/z, and peak intensity was generated.</p>	<p>Accurately weigh 50±5 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and add one grinding bead (6 mm diameter).</p><p>Add 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1 (v/v)) containing four internal standards (e.g., L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL).</p><p>Grind using a frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz).</p><p>Perform low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 KHz).</p><p>Place the sample at -20°C for 30 min.</p><p>Centrifuge for 15 min (13,000 g, 4°C), transfer the supernatant to an autosampler vial with a micro-insert for analysis.</p><p>Additionally, transfer 20 μL of supernatant from each sample and mix to prepare a quality control (QC) sample.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.The MS parameters were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate 50 arb, auxiliary gas flow rate 13 arb, heater temperature 425°C, capillary temperature 325°C. The spray voltage was 3500 V in positive mode and -3500 V in negative mode. S-Lens RF Level was set to 50. Normalized collision energy was set to 20, 40, and 60 eV. The resolution was 60000 for full MS and 7500 for MS/MS.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using Progenesis QI v3.0 by matching MS and MS/MS spectra against public databases including HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and METLIN (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as an in-house database. The mass error was set to less than 10 ppm. Metabolites were annotated based on MS/MS matching scores, with higher scores indicating greater confidence. Only metabolites with reliable matching scores were retained.</p>

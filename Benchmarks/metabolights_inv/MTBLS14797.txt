Study Protocol Description	<p>Male Lewis and Brown Norway rats were purchased from were purchased from SiPeiFu Biotechnology (Beijing, China). All the rats were specific-pathogen-free inbred males weighing 250–300 g (10–12 weeks old) and were kept on a 12–hour light–dark cycle with free access to food and water. Orthotopic left LTx was performed in rats as previously described. A CR rat model were established on the basis of previously published methods. Briefly, LTx (Brown Norway to Brown Norway) was performed in the Syn group, whereas MHC-mismatched LTx (Brown Norway to Lewis) was performed in the CR rat model groups. After LTx, all the rats were treated with CsA three times weekly at posttransplant week 1. All the rats were sacrificed at the end of posttransplant week 4. A total of 50 mg of the same anatomical region of the lung graft (Syn group, N = 3; CR group, N = 9) was further performed.</p>	<p>Analysis was conducted on a ABSCIEX UHPLC-Triple TOF system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5:47.5, v/v) (solvent B). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was 45°C.</p>	<p>The raw data were processed in Progenesis QI for peak detection and alignment, which resulted in m/z values and peak intensity information. Accurate masses were used to identify the mass spectra of metabolic features. The pretreatment of the LC/MS raw data was performed with Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) software.</p>	<p>A total of 50 mg of the same anatomical region of the lung graft was added to a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and 6 mm diameter grinding beads were added. Metabolites were extracted using 400 L of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1 (v:v)) containing 0.02 mg/mL of an internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine). A frozen tissue grinder was used to grind the samples for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), followed by ultrasonic extraction for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz). After being frozen at -20°C for half an hour, the samples were spun at 13000 × g for 15 min at 4°C, and the resulting liquid was moved to the injection vial.&nbsp;Quality control (QC) samples are prepared by pooling equal volumes of the extracts from all study samples. Each QC sample is of the same volume as the test samples and is processed and detected using the same methods as the analytical samples. During instrumental analysis, a QC sample is inserted after every 5 to 15 analytical samples to monitor the stability of the overall analytical process.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was conducted using a ABSCIEX UHPLC-Triple TOF system. Mass Spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive mode and negative mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: source temperature at 500℃ ; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) at -4500V in negative mode and 5500V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy , 40±20 rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition utilized the data-dependent acquisition mode, with detection spanning a mass range of 50–1200 m/z.</p>	<p> The metabolites were identified by the HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/), Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/) and Majorbio databases. The data matrix obtained by searching the database was uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com) for data analysis. At least 80% of the metabolic features detected in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, for specific samples whose metabolite levels were below the lower limit of quantification, the minimum metabolite value was estimated, and each metabolic signature was normalized to the sum. The response intensities of the sample mass spectrometry peaks were normalized using the sum normalization method. Moreover, the variables of the QC samples whose relative standard deviation was &gt; 30% were excluded and converted to log10 to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p>

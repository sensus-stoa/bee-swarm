Study Identifier	MTBLS14800
Study Title	Novel Adaptive Strategies of Maize Rhizosphere Microbiome Mediated by strain ST-1 Against Vanadium Stress
Study Description	Vanadium (V) contamination can disrupt plant growth, rhizosphere redox processes, and microbial functions, but how functional bacterial inoculation coordinates rhizosphere ecological adaptation with V behavior in crop systems remains unclear. In this study, a maize pot experiment was conducted to systematically investigate the strain ST-1-mediated rhizosphere response to V(V) stress by integrating plant physiological traits, V accumulation and fractionation, 16S rRNA sequencing, untargeted metabolomics, and metagenomics. V stress inhibited maize growth and induced oxidative damage, whereas strain ST-1 inoculation improved plant performance and antioxidant capacity and reduced V accumulation in aboveground tissues. At maturity, these beneficial effects were accompanied by a redistribution of rhizosphere V toward the reducible fraction. Microbial community analysis showed that strain ST-1 did not simply restore the rhizosphere to an uncontaminated state, but selectively restructured key indigenous microbial groups. This microbial restructuring was associated with a recalibration of the rhizosphere metabolic network, particularly involving carbohydrate metabolism, aromatic and nitrogen-containing metabolism, nucleotide metabolism, membrane lipid metabolism, and cofactor metabolism. MAG analysis further identified key genomic carriers harboring genes related to complex carbon mobilization, reverse TCA-associated carbon skeleton conservation, aminotransferase-mediated transformation of nitrogen-containing intermediates, and nar–nir–nor-linked nitrate/nitrite reduction, providing a potential genomic basis for carbon–nitrogen reorganization and local redox regulation. Collectively, these results support an integrated model in which strain ST-1 inoculation promotes selective rhizosphere microbial restructuring and metabolic reprogramming, which are associated with V fraction redistribution and ultimately contribute to reduced V stress and improved maize adaptation. These findings provide multi-omics evidence for regulating V behavior and restoring rhizosphere function in V-contaminated agricultural soils.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The sample was collected from the rhizosphere soil of a corn pot experiment in the experimental greenhouse of Chengdu University of Technology in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China, in July 2025. After collection, the sample was rinsed with pure water and sent to Shanghai Meiji Biological Testing Co., Ltd. for testing. The sample was stored in a -80°C refrigerator</p>	<p>The instrument platform used for this LC-MS analysis was Thermo Fisher’s ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem Fourier transform mass spectrometry</p><p><br></p><p>UHPLC-Orbitrap Exploris 240 system (Shanghai Meiji Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.).</p><p><br></p><p>Chromatographic conditions:</p><p><br></p><p>A 3 μL sample was separated on an HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) before entering the mass spectrometer for detection.</p><p><br></p><p>Mobile phase A consisted of a water/acetonitrile (95/5, v/v) solution containing 0.1% formic acid; mobile phase B consisted of an acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5/47.5/5, v/v/v) solution containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was 40.</p>	<p>After the analysis was completed, the raw LC-MS data were imported into the metabolomics analysis software Progenesis QI (Waters</p><p><br></p><p>Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, ultimately</p><p><br></p><p>yielding a data matrix containing retention times, mass-to-charge ratios, and peak intensities,</p>	<p>Place 100 mg of solid sample in a 2 mL centrifuge tube and add one 6 mm diameter grinding bead. Add 800 μL of extraction solution</p><p><br></p><p>(methanol:water = 4:1 (v:v)), containing four internal standards (L-2-chlorophenylalanine (0.02 mg/mL), etc.), for the extraction of metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p>Grind the sample solution in a frozen tissue grinder for 6 minutes (-10, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 minutes (5, 40 kHz).</p><p><br></p><p>Allow the sample to stand at -20 for 30 minutes, then centrifuge for 15 minutes (4, 13,000 g). Transfer the supernatant to a sample vial with an insert tube</p><p><br></p><p>and load it into the instrument for analysis.</p>	<p>Sample mass spectrometry signal acquisition was performed in positive and negative ion scan modes, with a mass scan range of m/z 70–1050. Ion spray</p><p><br></p><p>voltage: 3500 V for positive ions and –3000 V for negative ions; sheath gas 50 arb; auxiliary heating gas 13 arb; ion source heating</p><p><br></p><p>temperature 450; collision energy cycle 20–40–60 V.</p>	<p>At the same time, MS and MS/MS mass spectrometry data were matched against the public</p><p><br></p><p>metabolite databases HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), as well as Meiji’s</p><p><br></p><p>in-house database, to obtain metabolite information.</p>

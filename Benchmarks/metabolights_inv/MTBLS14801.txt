Study Identifier	MTBLS14801
Study Title	Untargeted serum metabolomics of early-life antibiotic intervention, fecal microbiota transplantation restoration, and short-term high-fat diet effects in C57BL/6 mice
Study Description	This study profiles the serum metabolome in C57BL/6 mice subjected to early-life antibiotic treatment (vancomycin and gentamicin, 2 weeks), followed by fecal microbiota transplantation and a short-term high-fat diet. Serum samples were collected longitudinally and analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry in both positive and negative ion modes. The non-targeted metabolomics data provide insights into host-microbiome metabolic interactions and the metabolic consequences of microbial restoration and dietary challenge.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>1.Antibiotics (ABX) treatment</strong></p><p>The antibiotic (ABX) cocktail, consisting of vancomycin hydrochloride (125 mg/L; Meilunbio) and gentamicin sulfate (100 mg/L; Meilunbio), was administered in drinking water to mice starting at 4 weeks of age for a duration of 2 weeks. The ABX solution was replaced weekly to ensure stability. Control mice received&nbsp;ABX-free drinking water&nbsp;ad libitum.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>2.The ABX-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis model</strong></p><p>Male and female&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice were used to establish a model of gut microbiota dysbiosis using&nbsp;ABX&nbsp;treatment. At 4 weeks of age, vancomycin and gentamicin were added to the drinking water, and the mice were allowed free access for 2 weeks. A control group consisted of 4-week-old&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice that receive&nbsp;ABX-free&nbsp;drinking water ad libitum.&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p><strong>3.The fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) model</strong></p><p>4-week-old C57BL/6 recipient mice were pretreated with an ABX cocktail for 2 weeks, with treatment discontinued 24 hours prior to FMT. Fresh feces were collected from sex- and age-matched conventionally raised donor mice, which were co-housed by sex. Fecal slurry was prepared immediately in sterile PBS at a concentration of 125 mg/mL and administered to recipients via oral gavage at a volume of 100 μL, three times per week.&nbsp;</p><p><strong>a. The&nbsp;control group&nbsp;(Control-M and Control-F):</strong>&nbsp;4-week-old male or female&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice receive&nbsp;ABX-free drinking water&nbsp;for 2 weeks. At 6 weeks of age, they were orally gavaged with 100 µL of anaerobic, sterile PBS for 3 weeks.</p><p><strong>b. The PBS&nbsp;group&nbsp;(PBS-M and PBS-F):&nbsp;</strong>4-week-old male or female&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice were given&nbsp;ABX-containing drinking water for 2 weeks, followed by oral gavage with 100 µL of anaerobic, sterile PBS for&nbsp;3&nbsp;weeks starting at 6 weeks of age.</p><p><strong>c. The experimental group&nbsp;(MGM and FGF):</strong>&nbsp;4-wee-old male or female&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice received&nbsp;ABX-containing drinking water&nbsp;for 2 weeks&nbsp;as described above. At 6 weeks of age, the&nbsp;ABX&nbsp;were withdrawn, and&nbsp;the mice were administered 100 µL of&nbsp;fecal&nbsp;slurry&nbsp;by oral gavage for 3 weeks.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>4.The short-term HFD model</strong></p><p>The methods for establishing gut microbiota dysbiosis and performing FMT in&nbsp;immature&nbsp;mice were as described above. All experiments were conducted separately by sex.&nbsp;</p><p><strong>a. The&nbsp;HFD control group&nbsp;(Control-M-HFD and Control-F-HFD):&nbsp;</strong>Mice received no&nbsp;ABX&nbsp;treatment during the juvenile stage. Starting at 6 weeks of age, they were orally gavaged with anaerobic, sterile PBS for 3 weeks. From&nbsp;9&nbsp;weeks of age, they were fed HFD for&nbsp;4&nbsp;weeks.</p><p><strong>b. The&nbsp;HFD&nbsp;PBS&nbsp;group&nbsp;(PBS-M-HFD and PBS-F-HFD):</strong>&nbsp;At 4 weeks of age, mice were treated with&nbsp;ABX&nbsp;for 2 weeks. From 6 weeks of age, they received oral gavage with anaerobic, sterile PBS for 3 weeks.&nbsp;From&nbsp;9&nbsp;weeks of age, they were fed&nbsp;HFD&nbsp;for&nbsp;4&nbsp;weeks.</p><p><strong>c. The&nbsp;HFD experimental group&nbsp;(MGM-HFD and FGF-HFD):</strong>&nbsp;At 4 weeks of age, mice were treated with&nbsp;ABX&nbsp;for 2 weeks. From 6 weeks of age, they received oral gavage with&nbsp;fecal&nbsp;slurry&nbsp;from sex-matched donors for 3 weeks.&nbsp;From&nbsp;9&nbsp;weeks of age, they were fed&nbsp;HFD&nbsp;for&nbsp;4&nbsp;weeks.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>5.Sample preparation</strong></p><p>Serum samples were thawed at 4°C, aliquoted (100 μL) into new tubes, and stored in -80°C refrigerator. </p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a&nbsp;Waters ACQUITY UPLC system&nbsp;coupled to the mass spectrometer. The column used was an&nbsp;ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 µm particle size; Waters, Milford, MA, USA)&nbsp;. The column temperature was maintained at&nbsp;40 °C. The mobile phase consisted of&nbsp;(A) 0.1% formic acid in water&nbsp;and&nbsp;(B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The flow rate was set to&nbsp;0.3 mL/min, and the injection volume was&nbsp;5 µL. The gradient elution program was as follows:&nbsp;0–1 min, 2% B; 1–12 min, 2–98% B; 12–14 min, 98% B; 14–14.1 min, 98–2% B; 14.1–16 min, 2% B&nbsp;for re-equilibration. The autosampler temperature was maintained at&nbsp;4 °C.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Scientific) with the following parameters: parent mass tolerance &lt;5 ppm, fragment mass tolerance &lt;10 ppm, and retention time deviation &lt;0.2 min.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted by adding 700 μL of pre-cooled extraction solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water = 4:2:1, v/v/v) containing internal standards (d3-Leucine, 13C9-Phenylalanine, d5-Tryptophan, 13C3-Progesterone). After vortexing for 1 min, samples were incubated at -20°C for 2 h, followed by centrifugation at 25,000 ×g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant (600 μL) was collected and dried under vacuum. The residue was reconstituted in 180 μL methanol:water (1:1, v/v), vortexed for 10 min, and centrifuged again under the same conditions. The final supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots (20 μL) from each sample.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a&nbsp;Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)&nbsp;equipped with an&nbsp;electrospray ionization (ESI) source&nbsp;operating in both positive and negative ion modes. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of&nbsp;70,000 (FWHM)&nbsp;over an&nbsp;m/z range of 70–1050, with an AGC target of 3e6 and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The top 3 precursors were selected for fragmentation using stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 eV. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 17,500 (FWHM) with an AGC target of 1e5 and a maximum injection time of 50 ms. ESI source parameters were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate, 40; auxiliary gas flow rate, 10; spray voltage, 3.80 kV (positive) / 3.20 kV (negative); capillary temperature, 320 °C; auxiliary gas heater temperature, 350 °C.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by querying the BGI Metabolome Database (BMDB), mzCloud, and ChemSpider. Data preprocessing was conducted using MetaX, including probabilistic quotient normalization (PQN), quality control-based robust LOESS signal correction (QC-RLSC), and removal of metabolites with a coefficient of variation &gt;30% in QC samples.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14808
Study Title	In silico derivatization and CCS value prediction improve interoperable semi-untargeted stable-isotope labeling analysis
Study Description	<p>Chemical derivatization can improve the detectability of analytically challenging compounds, but untargeted feature identification and annotation for chemically derivatized samples are limited by the availability of reference spectra. With the workflow presented in this manuscript, challenges associated with library-dependent identification of derivatized species are mitigated by in silico derivatization and spectral prediction, which reduce the need for dedicated experimental reference libraries. Furthermore, an improved CCS-value prediction algorithm provides an orthogonal dimension that increases confidence in feature annotation for both native and derivatized compounds. The annotated feature lists can be used for automated generation of target lists for quantitation. Additionally, they can be used for semi-untargeted stable-isotope labeling analyses, which can be performed, from raw data to results, within the same software environment. Finally, natural isotope abundance correction can be applied on the resulting isotopologue distributions, which allows for a full analysis of stable-isotope labeling data. Consolidation of all processing steps in a single environment minimizes operator intervention, reduces error risk, and makes stable-isotope labeling more widely attainable in metabolomics research.</p><p><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Brown adipocyte samples were prepared following the procedure described in Anagho-Mattanovich et al. (2025). Detailed experimental conditions can be found in the methods section of that publication.</p><p>In brief, immortalized murine brown preadipocytes, which were kindly provided by Dr. Zach Gerhart-Hines, were maintained in 6-well plates in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Thermo Fisher Scientific) with pyruvate supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. Upon reaching confluence (day 0), differentiation was induced using a medium containing 20 nM insulin, 1 nM triiodothyronine (T3), 1 µM dexamethasone, 0.125 mM indomethacin, and 0.5 mM isobutylmethylxanthine (Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA). After two days, the medium was replaced with DMEM supplemented with 20 nM insulin and 1 nM T3, which was renewed every two days. Experiments were conducted on day 7 using cells confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination.</p><p>The control samples were cultured in standard DMEM with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at the same growth conditions as above. For each of the labeling experiments, DMEM with no glucose, no glutamine and no pyruvate was used with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at the same growth conditions as above. This was then supplemented with the labeled versions of one of the tracers used in the study (glucose, glutamine, pyruvate), together with the unlabeled versions of the other two, at an equimolar concentration compared to the standard media. In other words, 3 labeled media mixes were used, one for glucose labeling, one for glutamine and one for pyruvate.</p><p>Samples were collected for metabolomic analysis at 16 h and 20 h with 6 replicates each.</p>	<p>A Bruker Bio-AQ, 50 x 2.1 mm, 2 µm column was used at an oven temperature of 55 °C. At a flow rate of 0.90 mL/min, the mobile phases A (water with 0.1% HCOOH) and B (acetonitrile with 0.1% HCOOH) were combined as follows to create a gradient: starting conditions 0.1 % B, held for 0.5 min, then gradually increased to 50% at 1 min, and thereafter increased to 100 % B at 2.25, held until 2.5 min at 100 % B, ending at 2.55 min 0.1 % B. The injection volume was 2 µL.</p><p><br></p>	<p>All data was processed in MetaboScape 2026 and TASQ 2026 with the features described below.</p><p>For metabolite annotation in MetaboScape, the minimum intensity threshold was set to 500. The following deviation windows were set for library/prediction matching: mobility delta 0.05, m/z delta 0.015, retention time delta 10 s. Features were kept if they appeared in at least 25 % of all samples in their respective group.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Culture media were transferred to collection tubes, and wells were rinsed with 1 mL of in-house prepared phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) before quenching the cells with 500 µL of 90% methanol that contained 10 mg/L nonanoic acid as an internal standard. Cells were scraped, collected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and subjected to three freeze-thaw-vortex cycles. After a 1 h incubation on ice to promote protein precipitation, samples were centrifuged at 16,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were stored at -80 °C until further analysis.</p><p>For NPH derivatization (see below), 20 µL of each supernatant were used.</p><p>The samples were NPH derivatized, after the addition of internal standards, to improve the measurement of compounds relevant for the study of the central carbon metabolism, e.g., carboxylic acids, aldehydes and ketones.</p><p>A calibration curve for the following compounds was prepared: D-glucose, lactic acid, pyruvic acid, citric acid, alpha-ketoglutaric acid, succinic acid, fumaric acid, DL-malic acid, L-alanine, glycine, L-methionine, L-glutamine, glutamic acid, L-serine, L-aspartic acid, L-phenylalanine, L-tryptophan, L-lysine, 3-hydroxybutyric acid and palmitic acid, all purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. All of them were diluted to the same stock mass concentration levels of 50 µg/mL from which the following calibration points were diluted: 50, 25, 10, 7.5, 5, 2.5, 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.075, 0.05, 0.025, 0.01 µg/mL.</p><p>For NPH derivatization (see below), 20 µL of each calibration mix were used.</p><p>The internal standard mix contained 10 µg/mL each of D-glucose (U-13C6), D3-lactic acid, 13C3-citric acid, D6-alpha-ketoglutaric acid, D4-succinic acid, D4-fumaric acid, 13C4-malic acid, D5-glycine, 13C5-L-glutamine, 13C5-15N-L-glutamic acid, 13C9-15N-L-phenylalanine, D5-tryptophan, 13C4-3-hydroxybutyric acid, D31-palmitic acid, all purchased from Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc.</p><p>To each sample, 20 µL of an internal standard mix were added. The resulting 40 µL were dried down in an N2-evaporator before being resuspended in 20 µL 80 % methanol prior to derivatization.</p><p>To prepare 250 mL of the NPH solution (200 mM 3-nitrophenylhydrazine; Sigma-Aldrich, Merck/MilliporeSigma, Burlington, MA, USA) in 50% methanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), 9.480 g of solid 3-nitrophenylhydrazine (NPH) were dissolved in 125 mL of LC-MS grade water (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with vortex mixing until complete dissolution. Subsequently, 125 mL of pure methanol were added, and the resulting solution was vortexed to ensure homogeneity.</p><p>To prepare 250 mL of the EDC solution (120 mM N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N’-ethylcarbodiimide; Sigma-Aldrich) in 50% methanol containing 6% pyridine (Thermo Fisher Scientific), 4.650 g of EDC were dissolved in 117 mL of LC-MS grade water by vortexing. This was followed by the addition of 117 mL of pure methanol and 16 mL of pyridine, after which the solution was vortexed to mix thoroughly.</p><p>To prepare 300 mL of a 0.05 mg/mL BHT solution (3,5-di-tert-butyl-4-hydroxytoluene; Sigma-Aldrich) in 30% methanol containing 0.1% formic acid, 15 mg of BHT were dissolved in the solvent mixture with mixing until a clear solution was obtained.</p><p>For derivatization of tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle intermediates, 20 µL of sample were combined with 20 µL each of the NPH and EDC solutions. The reaction mixture was incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Following incubation, 40 µL of the BHT solution were added to the mixture to quench the derivatization reaction.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Acquisition was made with a Vacuum Insulated Probe Heated ElectroSpray Ionization source (VIP-HESI) operated in negative mode. Capillary voltage was 3600V, nebulizer 4 bar, dry gas flow 8 L/min, dry gas temperature 230 °C, sheath gas temperature 400 °C and sheath gas flow 4 L/min. Mass range was m/z 50-1200, the ion mobility range ranged from a 1/K0 start of 0.45 Vs/cm2 to 1/K0 end 1.39 Vs/cm2, with a ramp time of 75 ms. The ion charge control was enabled and set to 5.0 M.</p><p>For analysis used to generate untargeted data for compound annotation, PASEF was activated. For the precursor ions, 2 PASEF ramps with a cycle time of 0.24 s were set. For MS/MS, the collision energy was set to 35 eV. Acquisition was controlled by timsControl 7.05 and HyStar 6.4.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Features were annotated based on an in house library further expanded by in silico derivatization.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS14844
Study Title	Penicillium-Mediated Toxicity and Microbial Dysbiosis Are Associated with Fruiting Body Abortion in Artificially Cultivated Ophiocordyceps sinensis
Study Description	<p>The Chinese caterpillar fungus, Ophiocordyceps sinensis, is a valuable medicinal entomopathogenic fungus, but artificial cultivation is hindered by fruiting body abortion, severely impacting yield and quality. The interactions between the fungus, its host insects, and soil are critical for its development. Multi-omics analyses were conducted to compare normally and abnormally developing samples, examining microbial communities and metabolites in endophytic larvae and the surrounding mycosphere soil. Abnormal stroma development significantly altered microbial ecology. It increased fungal diversity in both larvae and soil, while reducing bacterial diversity within larvae. The microbial composition shifted markedly, characterized by a high enrichment of Penicillium fungi and a depletion of Bacillus bacteria in and around abnormal samples. Cross-kingdom microbial network analysis in these samples showed fewer connections and lower stability. Metabolomic profiling revealed significant enrichment of the antibiotics N1-hydroxy-roquefortine C and glandicoline A, both derivatives of roquefortine C, alongside the enrichment of multiple antibiotic biosynthesis pathways. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling indicated that Penicillium toxicity likely induces microbial dysbiosis, and this disruption of microbial homeostasis is the primary cause of fruiting body abortion. The findings suggest that Penicillium overgrowth is detrimental to normal stroma formation. Conversely, Bacillus may play a crucial role in suppressing Penicillium, offering a potential target for biocontrol strategies. This study is the first to elucidate the key role of Penicillium-mediated toxicity and microbial imbalance in the developmental disorders of Chinese Cordyceps.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples of normal and abnormal fruiting body development of Chinese Cordyceps were collected on the 50th day after the mummification of the host T. xiaojinensis. Larvae samples of the fruiting body of O. sinensis have been formed (CC: the mature stroma stage: fully elongated stipes (length 2-5 cm) with well-differentiated fertile heads) and the fruiting bodies cannot be formed (WZYM: complete absence of primordium on mummified larval cadavers). Soil samples including soil particles naturally attached to the surface of larvae of CC (T-CC) and WZYM (T-WZYM). Specifically, each larval sample was washed with sterile water to remove surface impurities, sterilized with 75% ethanol (Sigma-Aldrich, 459836) for 2-3 min, sterilized with 10-15% hydrogen peroxide (Sigma-Aldrich, 425044) for 3-5 min, and rinsed with sterile water for 5 times. The washing water obtained from the last washing was inoculated onto Tryptone Soybean Agar (TSA) (Solarbio, T8650) and Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) (Solarbio, P8931) to verify whether the surface sterilization was successful. Fresh samples were frozen at -80 for subsequent analyses.</p>	<p>The LC/MS system for metabolomics analysis is composed of Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-high performance liquid tandem Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high resolution mass spectrometer. The column used was a Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm, Waters, 186003539). Mobile phases for both positive and negative ion modes: A: 0.1% formic acid (TCI, F0654) in LC-MS grade water; B: 0.1% formic acid in LC-MS grade acetonitrile (Merck, 100030).Injection volume 2μL. Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF high resolution mass spectrometer can collect primary and secondary mass spectrometry data in MSe mode under the control of the acquisition software (MassLynx V4.2, Waters). In each data acquisition cycle, dual-channel data acquisition can be performed on both low collision energy and high collision energy at the same time. The low collision energy is off, the high collision energy range is 10~40V, and the scanning frequency is 0.2 seconds for a mass spectrum. The parameters of the ESI ion source are as follows: Capillary voltage: 2500V (positive ion mode) or -2000V (negative ion mode); cone voltage: 30V; ion source temperature: 100°C; desolvent gas temperature 500°C; backflush gas flow rate: 50L/ h; Desolventizing gas flow rate: 800L/h.</p>	<p>The raw data collected using MassLynx V4.2 is processed by Progenesis QI software for peak extraction, peak alignment and other data processing operations, based on the Progenesis QI software online METLIN database and self-built library for identification.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction, 50 mg of each sample was weighed and homogenized with 1000 μL of extraction solvent containing internal standard (20 mg/L) prepared by mixing methanol (Merck, 34860), acetonitrile (Merck, 100030), and LC-MS grade water in a 2:2:1 ratio (v/v/v). The internal standard was L-2-chlorophenylalanine (Aladdin, C105993). The mixture was vortexed for 30 s. Steel beads were then added, and the samples were ground using a grinding mill (45 Hz) for 10 min, followed by ultrasonication in an ice-water bath for 10 min. After incubation at -20°C for 1 h, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. A 500 μL aliquot of the supernatant was carefully transferred to a new Eppendorf tube and dried in a vacuum concentrator. The dried metabolite extract was reconstituted in 160 μL of reconstitution solvent (acetonitrile:water = 1:1, v/v) (Merck, 100030). The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and ultrasonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, 120 μL of the supernatant was transferred to a 2 mL autosampler vial for LC-MS analysis. A quality control (QC) sample was prepared by pooling 10 μL of each sample.</p>	<p>The LC/MS system for metabolomics analysis is composed of Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-high performance liquid tandem Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof high resolution mass spectrometer. The column used was a Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm, Waters, 186003539). Mobile phases for both positive and negative ion modes: A: 0.1% formic acid (TCI, F0654) in LC-MS grade water; B: 0.1% formic acid in LC-MS grade acetonitrile (Merck, 100030).Injection volume 2μL. Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF high resolution mass spectrometer can collect primary and secondary mass spectrometry data in MSe mode under the control of the acquisition software (MassLynx V4.2, Waters). In each data acquisition cycle, dual-channel data acquisition can be performed on both low collision energy and high collision energy at the same time. The low collision energy is off, the high collision energy range is 10~40V, and the scanning frequency is 0.2 seconds for a mass spectrum. The parameters of the ESI ion source are as follows: Capillary voltage: 2500V (positive ion mode) or -2000V (negative ion mode); cone voltage: 30V; ion source temperature: 100°C; desolvent gas temperature 500°C; backflush gas flow rate: 50L/ h; Desolventizing gas flow rate: 800L/h.</p>	<p>After normalizing the original peak area information with the total peak area, the follow-up analysis was performed. Principal component analysis and Spearman correlation analysis were used to judge the repeatability of the samples within group and the quality control samples. The identified compounds are searched for classification and pathway information in KEGG, HMDB and lipidmaps databases.</p>

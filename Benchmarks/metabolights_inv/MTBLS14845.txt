Study Title	Early microbial and metabolic remodeling of the neovagina after laparoscopic peritoneal vaginoplasty in MRKH syndrome
Study Description	This integrated multi-omics study investigated early microbial succession and metabolic remodeling of the neovagina in patients with Mayer-Rokitansky-Kuster-Hauser (MRKH) syndrome after laparoscopic peritoneal vaginoplasty. Preoperative vestibular dimple samples (PRE) and postoperative day 14 neovaginal secretion samples (P14D) were collected and analyzed using untargeted UHPLC-Q-TOF metabolomics, 16S rRNA gene sequencing, and fluorescence quantitative PCR for sexually transmitted infection detection. The metabolomics component aims to characterize biochemical changes associated with early neovaginal epithelialization and to explore microbiota-metabolite associations during postoperative microenvironmental remodeling.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Preoperative vestibular dimple secretion samples (PRE) were collected before laparoscopic peritoneal vaginoplasty, and postoperative day 14 neovaginal secretion samples (P14D) were collected from the neovagina after surgery. All samples were obtained from patients with Mayer-Rokitansky-Kuster-Hauser (MRKH) syndrome using sterile collection procedures, transferred into sterile tubes, and stored at low temperature until untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis. Pooled quality control samples were prepared by combining aliquots from study samples and were inserted throughout the analytical sequence to monitor system stability and data reliability.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an HILIC method. For AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600 assays, samples were separated on an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC UHPLC system. For Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 assays, samples were separated on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC system. A Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 um, 2.1 mm x 100 mm) was used. The column temperature was maintained at 25 C, the flow rate was 0.5 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 uL. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonium hydroxide, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The gradient was 95% B from 0 to 0.5 min, 95-65% B from 0.5 to 5 min, 65-40% B from 5 to 5.5 min, 40-95% B from 5.5 to 6 min, and 95% B from 6 to 8 min. Samples were kept at 4 C in the autosampler and analyzed in randomized order. Pooled QC samples were inserted throughout the analytical sequence to monitor system stability and data reliability.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard MSConvert. The converted files were processed using XCMS for peak detection, peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction. Metabolite feature annotation was performed after peak extraction. Data preprocessing included removal of ion features with more than 50% missing values, missing value imputation using the KNN method, and filtering of features with relative standard deviation (RSD) greater than 50% in quality control samples. Quality control assessment was performed before downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were thawed slowly at 4 C. An appropriate aliquot of each sample was mixed with precooled methanol/acetonitrile/water (2:2:1, v/v/v), vortexed, sonicated at low temperature for 30 min, incubated at -20 C for 10 min, and centrifuged at 14000 g for 20 min at 4 C. The supernatant was collected and vacuum dried. Before LC-MS analysis, the dried extract was reconstituted in 100 uL acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v), vortexed, and centrifuged at 14000 g for 15 min at 4 C. The resulting supernatant was used for untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis. Pooled quality control samples were prepared by mixing aliquots from study samples and were used to monitor analytical stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed using electrospray ionization in both positive and negative ion modes. For AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600 assays, MS data were acquired on an AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600 Q-TOF mass spectrometer. Source parameters were Gas1 60, Gas2 60, curtain gas 30 psi, ion source temperature 600 C, and ion spray voltage +/-5500 V. The MS1 scan range was m/z 60-1000 with an accumulation time of 0.20 s/spectrum. The MS/MS scan range was m/z 25-1000 with an accumulation time of 0.05 s/spectrum. Information-dependent acquisition was performed in high-sensitivity mode with declustering potential +/-60 V, collision energy 35 +/- 15 eV, isotope exclusion 4 Da, and 10 fragment spectra collected per scan.</p><p><br></p><p>For Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 assays, MS data were acquired on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer. Source parameters were Gas1 50, Gas2 2, ion source temperature 350 C, spray voltage 3500 V in positive ion mode, and spray voltage 2800 V in negative ion mode. The MS1 scan range was m/z 70-1200 with a resolution of 60000 and an accumulation time of 100 ms. MS/MS data were acquired using a segmented acquisition method over m/z 70-1200 with a resolution of 60000, an accumulation time of 100 ms, and a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were annotated after peak extraction and preprocessing. Feature annotation was based on accurate mass, retention time, isotope and adduct information, and MS/MS spectral matching when available. The processed feature table was used for metabolite structure annotation and downstream statistical analysis. Features with insufficient quality were removed during preprocessing, including features with more than 50% missing values and features with RSD greater than 50% in quality control samples.</p>

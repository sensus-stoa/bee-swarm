Study Title	Heat stress during Camelina sativa fruit maturation reveals tissue and line specific lipidome remodelling responses (seeds dataset)
Study Description	<p>Increasing global temperatures, alongside extreme heat episodes, threaten crop productivity and present a significant challenge for oilseeds. In response, the low input oilseed Camelina sativa L. Crantz (camelina) has been identified for its abiotic stress resilience. Camelina produces seeds with up to ~40% oil by weight with utility in food, feed and biofuel industries. To determine the response of camelina fruits (seed and siliques) to heat, as well as varietal differences, this study subjected four camelina lines, previously identified for their differential response abiotic stress, to post anthesis thermal stress during the peak period of oil accumulation (three developmental time points). A lipidomic approach was used to understand how lipid remodelling modulated the fruit developmental, tissue and varietal response to heat. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) identified a total of 12 lipid classes in siliques and seeds respectively. An overall fruit lipidomic heat remodelling response was identified along with line and tissue specific heat stress lipid signatures. The study identified line and tissue specific lipid remodelling responses, knowledge that may contribute to improving sustainable camelina production.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Experiments were carried out with the following camelina (Camelina sativa) lines: a spring genotype provided by Camelina Company España (CCE, Spain) (UNT4; geographical origin Spain), commercial line Celine (UNT18; geographical origin France), Przybrodzka (UNT21; geographical origin Poland), and Omskij (UNT46; geographical origin Russia). The four lines were evaluated in ambient and heat stress conditions applied during seed development (30 days after sowing) in a controlled environment glasshouse trial completed at Rothamsted Research (UK). The experimental treatments were: ambient temperature (20/18°C; day/night) and well-watered (50% soil water content, SWC) and high temperature (32/25°C; day/night) under well-watered conditions. For replication, 45 plants per line and treatment were distributed between the two glasshouse compartments (180 plants per glasshouse) using a randomised sub-blocking structure Compartment/Bench/Tray/Pot. Plants were germinated with supplemental LED lighting and a temperature of 26°C to increase efficiency, eight days after sowing (DAS) seedlings were transplanted to individual pre-watered 10cm pots. The high temperature treatment was applied for 16 days at which stage sampling was performed. Siliques were collected from three development stage windows from the main stem of each plant, defined as top, middle, and bottom (youngest to oldest), pooled into five replicates, snap frozen in liquid nitrogen (N2), and stored at -80°C until further use. Each sampling stage represented a development window, namely, top – two to nine days after flowering (DAF), middle - ten to sixteen DAF, and bottom – eighteen to twenty-two DAF. A total of five replicates per line, per treatment and development stage were collected (five replicates x four lines x two treatments, control and heat x three stages). Prior to lipid extraction, samples were lyophilized, and seeds and siliques were separated.</p>	<p><em>Triacylglycerol analysis (LC-MS_positive_reverse-phase ASSAY 2)</em></p><p><br></p><p>For triacylglycerol analysis chromatographic separations were performed on an Accucore C18 column (150 ×2.1 mm, 2.6 μm, 150 Å; Thermo Scientific). Mobile phase A consisted of acetonitrile:water (50:50, v/v) with 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B consisted of isopropanol:acetonitrile:water (88:10:2, v/v/v) with 2 mM ammonium formate and 0.02% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. The injection volume was 1 μL, the column oven was maintained at 40 °C, and the autosampler was set at 10 °C. A needle wash with isopropanol was performed before each injection, using a wash time of 2 s and a wash speed of 10 μL/s. The total run time is 22 minutes with the following solvent gradient:&nbsp;0.0 min, 50% B; 1 min, 50% B; 4 min, 85% B; 13.0 min, 100% B; 16.00 min, 100% B; 16.5 min, 50% B; 22 min, 50% B.</p><p><br></p><p><em>Phospholipids analysis (LC-MS_negative_hilic method ASSAY 1)</em></p><p><br></p><p>For phospholipids analysis chromatographic separations were performed on a Luna NH2 column (4.6x150, 100 Å, 3 um; Phenomenex). Mobile phase A consisted of acetonitrile:water (95:6, v/v) with 10 mM ammonium acetate; while mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile:water (50:50, v/v) with 10 mM ammonium acetate at pH 9.6 (ammonium hydroxide was used to buffer solvent B). The total run time was 22 minutes with the following solvent gradient:&nbsp;0.0 min, 0% B; 2 min, 0% B; 2.1 min, 10% B; 10.0 min, 30% B; 14.10 min, 50% B; 14.2 min, 100% B; 17.2 min, 100% B; 17.5 min, 0% B; 22 min, 100% B. The flow rate was 1 mL/min, injection volumes were 5 µL, the oven temperature was set at 25°C, autosampler temperature was set at 5 °C. A needle wash with isopropanol was performed before each injection, using a wash time of 2 s and a wash speed of 10 μL/s.</p>	<p>The raw PRM HPLC-MS/MS data was processed using Skyline software.</p>	<p>Lipids were extracted from 10-40 mg (dry mass) of, seed or silique material. Glassware was used throughout the procedure. The samples, along with 1 ml of isopropanol, were incubated at 75°C for 20 minutes. Subsequently, 2 ml of chloroform/methanol (1:1) and 0.7 ml of water were added (Bligh and Dyer, 1959). After 30 seconds of vortex, an additional 2 ml of chloroform/water (1:1) was added. The mixture was centrifuged for three minutes at 500 g, and the lower chloroform phase was gently transferred to a new tube. A re-extraction of lipids was performed by adding 1 ml of chloroform to the first tube, repeating the centrifugation, and recovering the lower phase. Both extractions were combined. The chloroform was evaporated using N2 gas while keeping the sample in a 37°C block. Once all the solvent was evaporated, the lipids were resuspended in 200 μl of chloroform and stored at -80°C for subsequent analysis. Before analyses extracted lipid samples were diluted 100 times following way: 10ul sample, 20 ul Internal Standard 1 (IS1), 20 ul Internal Standard 2 (IS2) plus 950 ul of 95% ACN, 5% H20, and 10 mM Ammonium Acetate. The IS1 mix was comprised of 0.53 µM MGDG-(18:0-16:0), 0.42 µM PC-(24:1-24:1), 0.26 µM DGDG-(18:1-18:1d18), 0.16 µM PE-(14:0-14:0), 0.03 µM PG-(14:0/14:0), 0.28 µM PI-(18:0-20:4), 0.12 µM PS-(18:0-18:0), 0.18 SQDG-(18:3-16:0), 0.09 µM LPC-(13:0/0:0), 0.02 µM LPE-(14:0/0:0), 0.22 µM LPG-(14:0/0:0), 0.22 µM&nbsp;LPS-(13:0/0:0), 0.02 µM LPI-(13:0/0:0). The IS2 consisted of 2.61 µM TAG-(15:0-15:0-15:0).</p>	<p><em>Triacylglycerol analysis (LC-MS_positive_reverse-phase ASSAY 2)</em></p><p><br></p><p>Q-Exactive Plus Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used for triacylglycerol analysis. TAGs were ionized using electrospray ionization (ESI) and detected in positive ion mode, using PRM methods. The ion source settings were as follows: the capillary temperature was set to 350 °C, and the ion spray voltage was set to 3 kV. The auxiliary gas heater was maintained at 370 °C. Sheath gas, auxiliary gas, and sweep gas flow rates were set to 60, 20, and 1 (arbitrary units), respectively. The S-lens RF level was set to 50. The list of TAG molecules precursor ions was used as inclusion list to run the method. The MS settings for the PRM analyses were as follows: inclusion mass tolerances were set as 5 ppm, in-source CID was used as default set to 30.0 eV, MS2 resolution was 17500, AGC target 2e5, maximum IT 50 ms, isolation window 1.5 m/z and CE 35 eV.</p><p><br></p><p><em>Phospholipids analysis (LC-MS_negative_hilic method ASSAY 1)</em></p><p><br></p><p>SCIEX Triple Quad 6500+ system (SCIEX, USA) was used for phospholipids analysis. Polar lipids were ionized by ESI in negative ion mode. CUR, GS1, GS2 gases were set to 35, 50, and 60 psi, respectively. Optimal parameters for Ion Spray Voltage (IS), Collision gas (CAD), and Temperature (TEM) were high, -4500 V, and 400°C, respectively. Mass spectra for polar lipids were acquired using scheduled MRM with a cycling time of one second and MRM windows of 120 seconds.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The lists of triacylglycerols and phospholipids targets were used to create inclusion lists for the targeted analyses of lipids: PRM HPLC-MS/MS analysis of TAGs, and MRM HPLC-MS/MS analyses of PLs.&nbsp;</p>

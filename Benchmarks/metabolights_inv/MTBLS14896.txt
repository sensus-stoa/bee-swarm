Study Identifier	MTBLS14896
Study Title	Integrated Metabolomics, Transcriptomics, and Functional Validation Reveal Oxidoreductase-Associated Strictinin Accumulation in Assam tea Germplasm
Study Description	Strictinin, a bioactive hydrolyzable tannin in tea, accumulates at high levels in some Assam tea germplasms, yet the molecular basis underlying its variation remains unclear. Here, a rapid LC-MS/MS method was established for strictinin quantification and applied to profile 157 Assam tea accessions, followed by comparative metabolomics, transcriptomics, weighted gene co-expression network analysis, and antisense oligonucleotide-mediated gene silencing. Strictinin showed extensive natural variation across the germplasm population. Comparative analysis of high- and low-strictinin accessions indicated that elevated strictinin accumulation was associated with differential partitioning of hydrolyzable tannin intermediates rather than increased gallic acid abundance, accompanied by broad shifts in phenylpropanoid-related metabolism. Multi-omics integration identified strictinin-associated modules and four oxidoreductase candidates, CsLAC15, CsCYP81C13, CsCYP75B2, and CsCYP75A1. Silencing of these genes reduced strictinin and increased pentagalloylglucose, while silencing CsCYP81C13 also increased gallic acid. These findings indicate that strictinin variation in Assam tea is primarily associated with downstream oxidoreductase-linked metabolism of galloylglucose intermediates and provide analytical and molecular resources for breeding tea germplasm with elevated strictinin content.

Study Protocol Description	<p>CMP23 and ZK1, from our collection of 157 accessions. For each accession, fresh leaf tissues were harvested from three independent biological replicates. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until simultaneous metabolomic and transcriptomic analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 high-resolution mass spectrometer. Separation was achieved on a Phenomenex Kinetex C18 column (2.1 × 50 mm, 2.6 μm) maintained at 25 °C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 0.01% acetic acid in water and (B) isopropanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). The gradient elution was optimized for comprehensive metabolite separation. Sample plate temperature: 4 ℃, injection volume: 2 μL.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizardand processed with an in-house program. which was developed in R using XCMS for feature detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. The R package was applied in metabolite identification.</p>	<p>Frozen leaves were lyophilized and ground to a fine powder. Exactly 25 mg of powder was homogenized with 1000 μL of pre-cooled extraction solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water, 2:2:1, v/v/v) and spiked with a mixture of isotope-labeled internal standards. The homogenization involved grinding (35 Hz, 4 min) followed by ultrasonication in an ice-water bath (5 min), with this cycle repeated three times. After incubation at -40 °C for 1 hour to precipitate proteins, the extracts were centrifuged (13800 g, 15 min, 4 °C). The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane for LC-MS analysis. A QC sample was generated by combining equal aliquots from all individual samples to monitor instrumental stability throughout the acquisition sequence.</p>	<p>MS data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes with the following parameters: sheath gas flow rate, 50 arb; aux gas flow rate, 15 arb; capillary temperature, 320 °C; spray voltage, ±3.8 kV; full MS resolution, 60000; and data-dependent MS/MS resolution, 15000.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using the collaboratively developed R package 6. The databases used were BiotreeDB (version 3.0, standard library) and BTPlant (version 1.1, plant-specific library).</p>

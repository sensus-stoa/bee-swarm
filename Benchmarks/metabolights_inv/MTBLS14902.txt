Study Description	<p>study investigated the anti-fatigue effect of the Panax ginseng–Astragali Radix (GGJ) herb pair, the core functional unit of Baoyuan Decoction, using a rat fatigue model induced by forced swimming. Serum samples were collected for untargeted metabolomics analysis by LC-MS/MS to profile differential metabolites and associated pathways. The metabolomic data were integrated with transcriptomic and gut microbiota profiling to elucidate the mechanism.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='4046'>After a 28-day feeding period, blood samples were collected from SD rats via abdominal aorta cannulation. The blood was allowed to stand at room temperature for 1 h, followed by centrifugation at 3500 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant (serum) was harvested and stored at −80°C until analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='4641'>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Vanquish Flex ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm; Waters). The column oven was maintained at 40°C, and the autosampler temperature was set at 8°C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (solvent A) and acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid (solvent B), with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–1 min, 5% B; 1–4.7 min, 5–95% B; 4.7–6 min, 95% B; 6–6.1 min, 95–5% B; 6.1–8.5 min, 5% B. The injection volume was 2 μL for all samples.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='4880'>Data conversion was performed using MSConvert from the ProteoWizard software toolkit. The raw instrument files (.raw) were converted into mzXML format to ensure compatibility with open-source data processing pipelines.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='4540'>The test serum samples from SD rats were thawed naturally at 4°C. 50 μL of each sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 200 μL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and then frozen at −20°C for 30 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm at 4°C for 10 min, 200 μL of the supernatant was collected and vacuum-dried to completeness. The residue was reconstituted in 100 μL of 50% methanol containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine, vortexed for 30 s, and centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm at 4°C for 10 min. The resulting supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane prior to analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='10892304319' _msthash='4819'>All data were acquired using a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software (v4.7, Thermo). Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) was performed in both positive and negative ion modes. The HESI source parameters were set as follows: spray voltages of 3.5 kV (positive) and −3.0 kV (negative); sheath gas at 40 arb; auxiliary gas at 10 arb; capillary temperature at 320°C; and auxiliary gas temperature at 300°C. Full MS scans were collected at a resolution of 60,000 across the m/z range of 70–1000, with an AGC target set to standard and a maximum injection time (IT) of 100 ms. The top 4 most abundant precursor ions per cycle were selected for MS/MS fragmentation using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) at 30% normalized collision energy, with a dynamic exclusion window of 4 s, an MS/MS resolution of 15,000, an AGC target set to standard, and auto maximum injection time.</p><p _msttexthash='10892304319' _msthash='4819'>All formal samples and quality control (QC) samples were analyzed following the aforementioned chromatographic and mass spectrometric conditions. Prior to the formal injection sequence, 2–4 QC injections were performed for system equilibration. During the analytical run, one QC sample was injected every 5–10 formal samples. No QC samples were included when the batch size was fewer than 5 samples. These QC data were reserved for subsequent data evaluation and quality control.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='4951'>The acquired raw data files were imported into MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218) for peak picking, alignment, filtering, and metabolite annotation. Peaks that were not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were removed. Missing values were imputed using the Gap filling algorithm within the software, followed by normalization. Metabolite identification was performed against the PerSonalbio PSNGM (PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database), which integrates an in-house standard library, mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/), NIST_2020_MSMS, and an AI-predicted MS/MS spectral library. The key search parameters were set as follows: MS1 tolerance for identification, 0.01 Da; MS2 tolerance for identification, 0.05 Da; smoothing level, 3; minimum peak height, 10,000; minimum peak width, 5; mass slice width, 0.05 Da; and identification score cutoff, 70.</p>	<p _mstvisible='12'>This study employed a single-factor experimental design with 'Drug Dose' defined as the study factor (factor type: dose). Animals were randomly assigned to four groups receiving oral administration of the test compound at doses of 0, 10, 50, and 200 mg/kg body weight, respectively. The 0 mg/kg group served as the vehicle control, while the other three groups represented low, medium, and high dose treatment arms. All groups were subjected to identical experimental procedures and sampling protocols to ensure comparability.</p>

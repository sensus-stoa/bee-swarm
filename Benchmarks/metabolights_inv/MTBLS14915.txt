Study Identifier	MTBLS14915
Study Title	Mouse striatal neurotransmitter metabolomics profiling reveals bacteriophage-mediated restoration of levodopa responsiveness in Parkinson's disease
Study Description	Targeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics profiling was performed on mouse striatal tissues to investigate neurotransmitter metabolic alterations associated with bacteriophage-mediated restoration of levodopa responsiveness in Parkinson's disease. Striatal samples were collected from Parkinson's disease mouse models following bacteriophage intervention. Metabolites were analyzed using reversed-phase (RP) and hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) coupled with tandem mass spectrometry. Raw mzML files generated from both chromatographic methods are included in this study.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mouse corpus striatum tissues were collected from Parkinson's disease mouse models receiving fecal microbiota transplantation and bacteriophage intervention. Samples were immediately frozen and stored at -80°C until metabolite extraction and LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC system. For the reversed-phase (RP) method, compounds were separated on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (1.7 μm, 2.1 × 150 mm). Mobile phase A was water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was methanol:water (95:5) containing 10 mmol/L ammonium formate. For the HILIC method, compounds were separated on an Atlantis Premier BEH Z-HILIC column (1.7 μm, 2.1 × 150 mm). Mobile phase A was water:acetonitrile (90:10) containing 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate, and mobile phase B was water:acetonitrile (10:90) containing 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate. The autosampler temperature was set at 6°C, and the injection volume was 1 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed using SCIEX Analyst Work Station Software version 1.7.32. Target metabolite quantification was performed using BIOTREE BioBud version 2.0.4. Quantitative values were calculated using internal-standard-based calibration curves. Calibration curve performance was evaluated using the correlation coefficient, and curves with correlation coefficients greater than 0.95 were considered acceptable for quantification.</p>	<p>Frozen tissue samples were processed on dry ice. Approximately 25 mg of each tissue sample was transferred into a 2.0 mL EP tube. Then 500 μL of pre-cooled extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, containing internal standards) and two 3.2 mm steel beads were added. Samples were homogenized at 40 Hz for 4 min and sonicated in an ice-water bath for 5 min. The homogenization and sonication cycle was repeated three times. Samples were incubated at -40°C for 1 h and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm at 4°C for 15 min. The supernatant was transferred to LC sample vials for LC-MS/MS analysis. Equal volumes of supernatant from each sample were pooled to generate quality control samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric acquisition was performed on a SCIEX Triple Quad 6500+ triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an IonDrive Turbo V electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The ion source parameters were as follows: Curtain Gas, 35 psi; IonSpray Voltage, +5000 V / -4500 V; source temperature, 400°C; Ion Source Gas 1, 50 psi; and Ion Source Gas 2, 50 psi. Both positive and negative ionization modes were used according to the targeted metabolite panel.</p>	<p>Target metabolites were identified and quantified using a targeted LC-MS/MS method based on predefined retention times and multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions. Authentic standards and isotopically labelled internal standards were used for calibration and quality control. Metabolite concentrations were calculated from calibration curves and normalized to tissue weight.</p>

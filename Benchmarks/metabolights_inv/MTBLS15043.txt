Study Identifier	MTBLS15043
Study Title	Untargeted Metabolomic Profiling of Left Ventricular Myocardium in a Mouse Model of Diabetic Cardiomyopathy
Study Description	This study characterizes metabolic alterations in the left ventricular myocardium of a high-fat diet and streptozotocin-induced mouse model of diabetic cardiomyopathy. Untargeted LC–MS/MS metabolomics was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system coupled to an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer. Data were processed using XCMS and analyzed by principal component analysis, OPLS-DA, differential metabolite screening, hierarchical clustering, and KEGG- and MetaboAnalyst-based pathway analysis. Pooled quality-control samples were used throughout the analytical sequence to monitor system stability.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The animal tissues were quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately after dissection. Thenthetissue were cut on dry ice(~80 mg) into an Eppendorf tube(2 mL).The tissue samples with 200 μL of H2O andfive ceramicbeads were homogenized using the homogenizer. 800 μL methanol / acetonitrile(1: 1, v / v) were added tohomogenizedsolution for metabolite extraction.The mixture was centrifuged for 20 min(14000 g, 4 °C).Thesupernatant was dried in avacuum centrifuge.For LC - MS analysis, the samples were re - dissolved in 100 μLacetonitrile / water(1: 1, v / v) solventand centrifuged at 14000 g at 4 ℃ for 15 min, then the supernatant wasinjected.</p>	<p>Samples were separated on an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a HILIC column. The column temperature was maintained at 25 °C with a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min and an injection volume of 2 μL. Mobile phase A consisted of water supplemented with 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonia water, while mobile phase B was pure acetonitrile. The gradient elution procedure was set as follows: 0–0.5 min, held at 95% B; 0.5–7 min, linear decrease of B from 95% to 65%; 7–8 min, linear decrease of B from 65% to 40%; 8–9 min, held at 40% B; 9–9.1 min, linear increase of B from 40% to 95%; 9.1–12 min, held at 95% B. All samples were kept at 4 °C in the autosampler throughout the analytical run. To eliminate signal fluctuation bias, all samples were analyzed in a randomized continuous sequence. Quality control (QC) samples were interspersed within the sample queue to monitor instrumental stability and evaluate the reliability of experimental data.</p>	<p>The raw MS data were converted to MzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert before importing into freelyavailable XCMS software. For peak picking, the following parameters were used: centWave m/z = 10 ppm, peakwidth = c(10, 60), prefilter = c (10, 100). For peak grouping, bw = 5, mzwid = 0.025, minfrac = 0.5 were used. CAMERA (Collection ofAlgorithms of MEtabolite pRofile Annotation) was sued for annotation of isotopes and adducts. In the extracted ionfeatures, only the variables having more than 50% of the nonzero measurement values in at least one group were kept.Compound identification of metabolites was performed by comparing of accuracy m/z value (&lt;10 ppm), and MS/MSspectra with an in-house database established with available authentic standards.</p>	<p>The tissue samples with 200 μL of H2O andfive ceramicbeads were homogenized using the homogenizer. 800 μL methanol / acetonitrile(1: 1, v / v) were added tohomogenizedsolution for metabolite extraction.The mixture was centrifuged for 20 min(14000 g, 4 °C).Thesupernatant was dried in avacuum centrifuge.For LC - MS analysis, the samples were re - dissolved in 100 μLacetonitrile / water(1: 1, v / v) solventand centrifuged at 14000 g at 4 ℃ for 15 min, then the supernatant wasinjected.</p>	<p>Primary and secondary mass spectra were acquired using an AB Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer. After chromatographic separation by the Agilent 1290 Infinity LC UHPLC system, mass spectrometric detection was performed on the AB SCIEX Triple TOF 6600 instrument under electrospray ionization (ESI) positive and negative modes separately. ESI source parameters were configured as follows: nebulizer gas (Gas 1): 60, heater gas (Gas 2): 60, curtain gas (CUR): 30 psi, ion source temperature: 600 °C, ion spray voltage (ISVF): ±5500 V for positive and negative modes. The m/z scanning range for MS1 was set at 60–1000 Da, and the m/z range for MS2 fragment ions was 25–1000 Da. The accumulation time was 0.20 s per spectrum for MS1 scans and 0.05 s per spectrum for MS2 scans. Data-dependent acquisition (IDA) was applied to collect MS2 spectra based on peak intensity screening. Declustering potential (DP) was set to ±60 V for both ionization modes, and collision energy was 35 ± 15 eV. IDA parameters were set as follows: the dynamic exclusion range of isotopic ions was 4 Da, and a maximum of 10 fragment spectra were triggered per full scan.</p>	<p>After sum-normalization, the processed data were analyzed by R package (ropls), where it was subjected tomultivariate data analysis, including Pareto-scaled principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). The 7-fold cross-validation and response permutation testing were used toevaluate the robustness of the model. The variable importance in the projection (VIP) value of each variable in the OPLS-DA model was calculated to indicate its contribution to the classification. Student’s t test was applied to determine thesignificance of differences between two groups of independent samples. VIP &gt; 1 and p value &lt; 0.05 were used to screensignificant changed metabolites. Pearson’s correlation analysis was performed to determine the correlation between twovariables.</p>

Study Title	Clostridioides difficile-commensal interactions are impacted by diet more than differences between strain
Study Description	<p>The purpose of this study was to investigate how <em>C. difficile </em>metabolism differs between strains as a function of diet. We used three <em>C. difficile </em>strains (R20291, Cd4, and Cd10). Each strain was grown in three different media conditions designed to mimic different diet conditions: brain-heart infusion (BHI); high protein, low carb (HiPLoC); and low protein, high carb (LoPHiC). The samples were collected at 20 hours, spun down (20,000 RCF, 2 minutes), filter-sterilized (0.2 um), and frozen at -70C until analysis at Metabolon.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Protocol Description: Chromatographic separation was executed using a Sciex ExionLC AD UHPLC system. The analytical column configuration depended on the specific modality branch:</p><p><br></p><ul><li>Reverse-Phase (T3 Mode): Performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (, ) analytical column. The column temperature was maintained at &nbsp;with a continuous flow rate of &nbsp;and an injection volume of . Mobile phase A consisted of ultrapure water with 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. Separation was achieved using a 10-minute linear elution gradient: 0–2.0 min, 5% to 20% B; 2.0–5.0 min, 20% to 60% B; 5.0–6.0 min, 60% to 99% B; 6.0–7.5 min, 99% B; 7.5–7.6 min, 99% to 5% B; 7.6–10.0 min, 5% B.</li></ul><p><br></p><ul><li>HILIC Mode: Performed on an ACQUITY Premier BEH Amide (, ) analytical column. The column temperature was kept at &nbsp;with a flow rate of &nbsp;and an injection volume of . Mobile phase A consisted of 60% ACN, 30% water, and 10% MeOH with &nbsp;ammonium formate at pH 10.6. Mobile phase B consisted of 40% ACN and 60% water with &nbsp;ammonium formate at pH 10.6. The multi-step elution gradient spanned 10 minutes.</li></ul>	<p>The original profile raw data files (.wiff) captured from the Sciex TripleTOF system were systematically converted to an open-source mzML format via ProteoWizard. Downstream peak preprocessing workflow (including data peak extraction, alignment across batches, and retention time correction) were executed through the XCMS software program. Spectral clean-up step logic filtered out low-quality features possessing a missing value rate &gt; 50% within any given sample group. Missing values inside the matrix were imputed using K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and final peak intensities/areas were normalized and corrected via Support Vector Regression (SVR) pipelines to ensure batch run consistency.</p>	<p>The samples were collected, spun down (20,000 RCF, two minutes), filter-sterilized (0.2 um), and frozen at -70C until analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was carried out on an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 6600+ mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data acquisition was performed for a total run duration of 10 minutes per sample across both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI-) scan polarities.</p><p><br></p><p>ESI+ Parameters: IonSpray Voltage: 5000V, Source Temperature: 550C, Declustering Potential: 60V, MS1 Collision Energy: 10V, MS2 Collision Energy: 30V.</p><p>ESI- Parameters: IonSpray Voltage: -4000V, Source Temperature: 450C, Declustering Potential: -60V, MS1 Collision Energy: -10V, MS2 Collision Energy: -30V.</p><p><br></p><p>For both polarities, the source gas configuration used Ion Source Gas 1 at 50 psi, Ion Source Gas 2 at 60pse, and Curtain Gas at 35 psi, utilizing a Collision Energy Spread of 15V&nbsp;on a time-of-flight mass analyzer over an active scan&nbsp;<em>m/z </em>range of 70-1000.</p>	<p>Qualitative metabolite identification and structural annotation were completed using automated pipeline search criteria evaluated against several comprehensive compound repositories. Extracted MS2 structural feature patterns and accurate&nbsp;spectra were mapped natively through MetwareBio's in-house database, integrated public metabolomics databases, prediction databases, and metDNA (Metabolite Identification and Network Analysis) software. Features were parsed through strict quality filters: only substances possessing a comprehensive metric identification score higher than 0.5 and exhibiting highly repeatable signal stability (Coefficient of Variation, CV &lt; 0.3) within mixed reference Quality Control samples) were extracted and preserved. </p>	<p>Three <em>C. difficile</em> strains (R20291, Cd10, and Cd4). All strains were grown anaerobically overnight at 37C in BHIS (brain heart infusion supplemented with 5% yeast extract). Overnight cultures were then diluted 1:2 in fresh BHIS and incubated for 2 hours. The 2-hour cultures were then inoculated to the three media: BHIS, LoPHiC (10 mg/mL casaminos, 5 mg/mL sucrose, 5 mg/mL glucose, 1% cysteine, 1% tryptophan, 1% ATCC vitamins, 1% ATCC minerals) and HiPLoC (40 mg/mL casaminos, 0.2 mg/mL sucrose, 0.2 mg/mL glucose, 1% cysteine, 1% tryptophan, 1% ATCC vitamins, 1% ATCC minerals). Growth was measured at OD 600 every 10 minutes for 20-24 hours using a Cerillo Alto plate reader while being shaken continuously at 250 rpm.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Metabolites in the cecal contents of blast-injured mice</p>	<p>A 3 μL aliquot of each sample was injected into an ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography system and separated on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm). Mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was maintained at 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was set at 40°C.</p>	<p>Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition mode. Stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 were applied for tandem mass spectrometric fragmentation. The resolutions of the full MS and MS/MS scans were set to 60,000 and 7,500, respectively.</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg of each solid sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube containing a 6 mm-diameter grinding bead. Subsequently, 400 μL of extraction solvent consisting of methanol and water (4:1, v/v) and containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as the internal standard was added.</p><p><br></p><p>The samples were homogenized using a cryogenic tissue grinder for 6 min at 10°C and 50 Hz, followed by ultrasonic extraction for 30 min at 5°C and 40 kHz. The samples were then incubated at 20°C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was transferred into autosampler vials equipped with glass inserts for LC–MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed in both positive and negative electrospray ionization modes. Full-scan mass spectra were acquired over an m/z range of 70–1050. The sheath gas pressure was set to 50 psi, and the auxiliary gas pressure was set to 13 psi. The auxiliary gas heater temperature and ion transfer tube temperature were maintained at 425°C and 325°C, respectively. The spray voltages were set to 3.5 kV in positive-ion mode and 3.5 kV in negative-ion mode.</p>	<p>Following LC–MS analysis, the raw data were imported into Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA) for data preprocessing. The preprocessing workflow included baseline filtering, peak detection, peak integration, retention-time correction, and peak alignment. A data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity was subsequently generated.</p><p><br></p><p>Metabolite annotation was performed by matching the acquired MS and MS/MS spectral information against public metabolite databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and METLIN, as well as a proprietary in-house spectral library. Metabolite identities were assigned based on the combined matching results.</p>

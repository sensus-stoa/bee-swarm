Study Title	Fecal untargeted metabolomic profiles in aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage and unruptured intracranial aneurysm
Study Description	This study comprises fecal untargeted LC-MS metabolomics data from 30 participants, including 21 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage and 9 patients with unruptured intracranial aneurysm. The data were generated to characterize metabolic alterations associated with disease status, inflammatory endotypes, and cognitive status, and to support integrative analyses with fecal shotgun metagenomic data.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fresh fecal samples (3–5 g) were collected in the morning using a clean sterile spoon and transferred into sterile QIAGEN collection tubes. The tubes were sealed immediately to minimize exposure to ambient air. Samples were transported to the laboratory on dry ice and stored at −80°C until metabolite extraction and LC-MS analysis. Repeated freeze–thaw cycles were avoided.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 20 mg of each fecal sample using 300 µL of pre-cooled methanol containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine. Two steel beads were added, and the samples were vortexed for 30 s and homogenized at 55 Hz for 60 s twice. The samples were then sonicated for 10 min and incubated at −20°C for 30 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 10 min, the supernatants were filtered through a 0.22-µm membrane and transferred into LC-MS vials. Equal aliquots of 10–20 µL from each sample filtrate were pooled to prepare quality-control samples.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Vanquish Flex ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40°C, the autosampler temperature was 8°C, and the injection volume was 2 µL. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The gradient was programmed as follows: 5% B at 0–1 min, increased to 95% B at 4.7 min, maintained at 95% B until 6 min, returned to 5% B at 6.1 min, and maintained at 5% B until 8.5 min.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired using a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software version 4.7. Data were collected in positive and negative heated electrospray ionization modes using data-dependent acquisition. The spray voltages were 3.5 kV in positive mode and −3.0 kV in negative mode. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set to 40 and 10 arbitrary units, respectively. The capillary temperature was 320°C, and the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 300°C. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 60,000 over an m/z range of 80–1000, with an AGC target of Standard and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The four most intense precursor ions were selected for MS/MS fragmentation. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000 using higher-energy collisional dissociation at 30%, with a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s. Before analysis of the biological samples, two to four pooled quality-control injections were performed to equilibrate the analytical system. During the analytical sequence, one pooled quality-control sample was injected after every 6–12 biological samples to monitor analytical stability.</p>	<p>The raw Thermo .raw files were processed using MS-DIAL version 4.9.221218. Data processing included peak detection, peak alignment, filtering, gap filling, normalization, and metabolite annotation. Features not detected in more than 50% of the quality-control samples were removed. Missing feature values were filled using the gap-filling algorithm implemented in MS-DIAL.</p>	<p>Metabolites were annotated against the PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database, which incorporates an in-house standard library, mzCloud, LIPID MAPS, HMDB, MoNA, NIST_2020_MSMS, and AI-predicted MS/MS spectral libraries. The main identification parameters included an MS1 tolerance of 0.01, an MS2 tolerance of 0.05, a smoothing level of 3, a minimum peak height of 10,000, a minimum peak width of 5, a mass slice width of 0.05, and an identification score cut-off of 70.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm) maintained at 40 °C. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile. An 8.5-min gradient elution program was used prior to mass spectrometric detection. The chromatographic method was applied to both positive- and negative-ion acquisition modes.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data were processed to generate a feature intensity matrix. Data processing included chromatographic peak detection, retention-time alignment, and feature grouping. Metabolite annotation was performed using accurate mass and MS/MS spectral information. The resulting feature-abundance matrix was exported for downstream statistical analysis. </p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from 20 mg of each fecal sample using 300 µL of pre-cooled methanol containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard. The mixture was homogenized at 55 Hz for 60 s twice and sonicated for 10 min. Samples were then incubated at −20 °C for 30 min and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min. The resulting supernatant was collected and filtered through a 0.22-µm membrane prior to LC–MS/MS analysis.</p>

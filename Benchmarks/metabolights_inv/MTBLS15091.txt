Study Identifier	MTBLS15091
Study Title	Serum Metabolomic Profiling Reveals Load-Specific Adaptations to Resistance Training
Study Description	This study deposits the untargeted ¹H-NMR serum metabolomics dataset of a parallel-group, longitudinal resistance training intervention designed to identify load-specific and training-status-specific metabolomic signatures in healthy young men. Seventeen healthy young men (age 24 ± 3 yr; height 173 ± 6 cm; body mass 71 ± 13.7 kg) were allocated to one of two resistance training protocols differing exclusively in mechanical load: high-load (HL; 80% of one-repetition maximum [1-RM]; n = 9) or low-load (LL; 30% 1-RM; n = 8). Both groups completed an 8-week supervised programme of twice-weekly lower-limb resistance training comprising bilateral 45° leg press and bilateral knee extension, with three working sets per exercise carried to concentric volitional failure and standardised rest intervals of 90 s between sets and 120 s between exercises. Training loads were reassessed at the intervention midpoint to preserve relative intensity. Fasting venous serum was obtained at two time points — immediately before the first training session (Before) and after the final session of the 8-week intervention (After-HL and After-LL) — yielding a total of 34 serum samples. Blood was drawn from the median cubital vein after a 10-h overnight fast and 1 h of supine rest, with dietary intake recorded for the preceding 24 h. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Untargeted one-dimensional ¹H-NMR spectroscopy was performed at 600 MHz following 3-kDa ultrafiltration to remove macromolecular signal contributions, with phosphate buffer in D2O as field-frequency lock and TSP-d4 as internal chemical shift reference and quantification standard; 256 transients were accumulated per sample. Spectral processing, metabolite identification and absolute quantification were performed using Chenomx NMR Suite, yielding a final matrix of 51 quantified serum metabolites across all 34 samples. The deposition comprises raw 1D ¹H-NMR free induction decay (FID) files, processed spectra, the quantified metabolite concentration matrix, and the associated sample and assay metadata in ISA-Tab format. The downstream analytical pipeline applied to these data — univariate paired t-tests and one-way ANOVA with Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction, principal component analysis, partial least squares-discriminant analysis, and Random Forest classification with stratified 5×5-fold repeated cross-validation, 1000-iteration permutation testing and exhaustive enumeration of 1- to 5-metabolite subsets — is fully described in the associated publication. This dataset is made openly available to support independent re-analysis of training-status-specific and load-specific serum metabolomic signatures of resistance training in healthy young adults.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Venous puncture was performed via the median cubital vein located in the antecubital fossa. After collection (8 mL), blood was allowed to rest for 30 minutes at room temperature. Following this period, samples were centrifuged for 10 minutes at 3,000 RPM at room temperature. After this step, approximately 4 mL of serum was stored at −80°C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The processing of the 1H-NMR spectral data was performed using the Chenomx® NMR Suite 7.6 software (Chenomx Inc., Edmonton, AB, Canada). Spectral processing consisted of spectral phase adjustment, baseline correction, and signal removal from water (4.6–5.1 ppm). Then, a 0.3 Hz line-broadening apodization function was applied and the spectrum was calibrated by the internal reference signal (TMSP-d4).</p>	<p>Prior to analysis, a 3 kDa filter (Amicon Ultra, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was washed using 500 µL of Milli-Q water. After applying Milli-Q water to the filter, it was centrifuged at 14,000 RPM for 10 minutes at 4°C. This process was repeated five times. Following this washing step, 350 µL of previously stored serum was loaded onto the filter and centrifuged at 14,000 RPM for 45 minutes at 4°C. The resulting filtrate (200 µL) was mixed with 60 µL of 0.1 mol/L phosphate buffer (NaH2PO4/Na2HPO4, pH 7.4), 6.06 µL of 50 mmol/L TMSP-d4 in D2O (internal chemical shift reference), and 340 µL of Milli-Q water.</p>	<p>The Chenomx® NMR Suite 7.6 software (Chenomx Inc.), along with the Human Metabolome Database - HMDB 4.0, were used to identify the metabolites. Quantitative analyses were performed with the integral of signals of each metabolite.</p><p>Spectral quality was evaluated against a set of predefined acceptance criteria established for high-precision ¹H-NMR phenotyping, comprising a line width at 50% peak height of the TMSP-d4 reference signal not exceeding 1.0 Hz, and a flat, distortion-free baseline throughout the water-suppression region (δ 4.5–5.0 ppm). The observed line width of 0.79 ± 0.10 Hz and a signal-to-noise ratio of 4,180 ± 523 a.u. confirmed that instrumental performance was well within the defined thresholds. Water suppression was uniform and reproducible across all spectra, with no detectable distortion of adjacent resonances. All 34 spectra fulfilled the established quality criteria; consequently, no spectra were excluded from subsequent analyses. Analytical reproducibility was further substantiated by the systematic use of TSP-d4 as an internal reference standard for both chemical shift calibration and signal normalization. The coefficient of variation (CV) of the TSP-d4 signal was consistently low across the entire dataset (0.49 mM ± 0.001; CV = 0.3%), providing robust evidence of spectrometer stability throughout the analytical run. All 48 annotated metabolites were assigned confidence level 2 according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) reporting framework.</p>	<p>The final solution was transferred to 5-mm NMR tubes (Wilmad-LabGlass, Vineland, NJ, USA) for spectral acquisition. The sample tuning adjustments with the device were performed by a specialized technician.</p>	<p>Spectral acquisition was performed by 1H-NMR spectrometer at 600MHz equipped with triple resonance cryogenic inverse probe (Varian, Agilent Inova). The sample tuning adjustments with the device were performed by a specialized technician, and the other adjustments (lock and shimming) were performed manually for each sample. The analysis was performed at a constant temperature of 298 K (25 °C). The spectrum was acquired via 256 scans, with an interval of 1.5 seconds and an acquisition time of 4 seconds for each scan.&nbsp;</p>

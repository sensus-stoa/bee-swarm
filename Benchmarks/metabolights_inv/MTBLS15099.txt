Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cell treatment</strong></p><p>Curcumin was obtained from TCI Europe N.V., while the curcumin-based benzothiazepane analogues AT164, AT007, and AT096 were synthesised as previously described [1]. Stock solutions were prepared in sterile-filtered DMSO, with the final DMSO concentration in cell cultures not exceeding 0.1%.</p><p>HCT-116 and IPEC-J2 cells were seeded at 50 000 cells/cm² in transparent 6-well plates and cultured for 2 days prior to treatment. Cells were exposed in serum-free medium to:</p><p>- 30 μM compound for 3 h</p><p>- 10 μM compound for 15 h</p><p>under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 10% CO2, humidified atmosphere).</p><p>Control conditions included:</p><p>- untreated cells incubated for 0, 3, or 15 h</p><p>- cell-free medium with and without compounds (10 or 30 μM)</p><p>to assess background signals and non-cellular degradation.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample preparation</strong></p><p>After treatment, extracellular medium was collected and stored at −80 °C. Cells were washed with PBS, scraped in 1.5 mL PBS, transferred to Protein LoBind tubes, and centrifuged (300 g, 5 min). Cell pellets were stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were analysed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC system coupled to an Agilent 6546 QTOF-MS equipped with a Dual Jet Stream electrospray ionisation (Dual AJS ESI) source operating in negative ionisation mode.</p><p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a ZORBAX RRHD Eclipse Plus C18 column (2.1 × 50 mm, 1.8 μm). Mobile phase A consisted of MeOH/acetonitrile (30:70, v/v), while mobile phase B consisted of 10 mM ammonium formate in water. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p><p>The gradient started at 5% A for 1 min, increased linearly to 50% A over 1.5 min, then to 95% A over 2.5 min and held for 5 min. The system was returned to 5% A within 0.5 min followed by 1.5 min re-equilibration.</p><p>The column oven and autosampler temperatures were maintained at 30 °C and 10 °C, respectively.</p>	<p>Raw Agilent .d files were converted to mzML format using MSConvert (ProteoWizard).</p>	<p>After thawing, cell pellets were resuspended in 1 mL methanol (MeOH). Extracellular medium samples were diluted 1:1 (v/v) with MeOH. Samples were vortexed, filtered through 0.20 μm filters, and transferred to glass LC-MS vials.</p>	<p>MS acquisition was performed over an m/z range of 100–1700 at 1.0 spectra/s. MS/MS data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode using collision energies of 10, 20, and 40 eV. A preferred ion list was applied for prioritised fragmentation of target compounds.</p>	<p>Tentative annotation and relative semiquantification of suspect compounds in the experimental samples were performed. Raw data were processed using Agilent MassHunter Profinder (v10.0) for feature extraction, binning and alignment. Recursive batch extraction was carried out with common organic isotope grouping using the following parameters: mass tolerance 20 ppm + 2 mDa, RT tolerance 0.5 min, minimum peak height 5 000 counts (empirically set according to the method’s noise characteristics), and Agile2 integration without smoothing. The adducts +H, +Na, +K, +NH4, -H, and +HCOO were considered. Peak integration was manually checked and corrected where necessary. The resulting feature list was exported to Agilent Mass Profiler Professional (v15.1) for statistical analysis. Samples were organised according to four parameters: treatment, matrix, cell type, and exposure time. Because treatment concentrations differed between the 3 h and 15 h conditions and matrix effects were expected, relative comparisons were restricted to groups comprising: (i) a single matrix type, (ii) one compound with an untreated control, and (iii) either 0 vs. 3 h or 0 vs. 15 h samples. Pairwise differential analyses were performed between conditions differing in only one parameter, using a moderated t-test with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (significance threshold: adjusted p-value (PAdj) ≤ 0.05 and absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) ≥ 2). The resulting feature list was subsequently screened against a pre-defined library of suspected metabolites and degradation products (Appendix B) using a mass tolerance of 10 ppm + 2 mDa. The curcumin library was based on known metabolites and degradation products from the literature. For the structural analogues (AT164, AT007, AT096), potential metabolites were included by analogy to curcumin biotransformation pathways. The library was expanded with additional experimentally observed metabolites, and all samples were re-screened accordingly.</p>

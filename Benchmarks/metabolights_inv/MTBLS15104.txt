Study Identifier	MTBLS15104
Study Title	Untargeted LC-MS metabolomic responses of cotton leaves to organic fertilizer and compound microbial inoculant treatments under mulched drip irrigation
Study Description	This study characterized the untargeted LC-MS metabolomic responses of cotton (Gossypium hirsutum L. cv. Xinluzhong 82) leaves to combined applications of organic fertilizer and a compound microbial inoculant under mulched drip irrigation. The field experiment used a 2 × 4 factorial design comprising two organic fertilizer rates (0 and 30 t ha-1) and four compound microbial inoculant rates (0, 30, 60, and 90 kg ha-1), resulting in eight treatment combinations with three biological replicates per treatment. Leaf samples were collected at the boll stage and analyzed using untargeted liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry in positive and negative ion modes. The dataset contains 24 biological samples, pooled quality-control samples, and solvent blank samples.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cotton leaf samples were collected at the peak boll stage on 15 August 2025 from the field experiment in Wensu County, Aksu Prefecture, Xinjiang, China. Healthy plants with uniform agronomic characteristics and without visible pest or disease symptoms were selected. Functional boll-subtending leaves were rapidly excised using sterile scissors. Each biological replicate consisted of pooled leaves collected from three independent plants, and 0.80–1.20 g fresh leaf tissue was collected for metabolomic analysis. Samples were immediately transferred into pre-cooled sterile tubes, quenched in liquid nitrogen for 8–10 min, transported under ultra-low-temperature conditions, and stored at −80°C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with a Hypersil GOLD C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C and the flow rate was 0.2 mL/min. Mobile phase A was 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate in water adjusted to pH 9.0, and mobile phase B was methanol. The gradient program was as follows: 0–1.5 min, 2% B; 1.5–12.0 min, 2–100% B; 12.0–14.0 min, 100% B; 14.0–14.1 min, 100–2% B; and 14.1–17.0 min, 2% B.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format for data deposition. The datasets were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 for peak detection, retention-time alignment, feature matching, and relative peak-area quantification. Peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity of each sample. Positive- and negative-ion datasets were processed separately.</p>	<p>Approximately 100 mg of frozen cotton leaf tissue was ground in liquid nitrogen and extracted with 500 μL of pre-cooled 80% methanol containing 0.1% formic acid. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly, incubated on ice, and centrifuged at 15,000 × g and 4°C for 20 min. The supernatant was diluted with LC-MS-grade water to a final methanol concentration of 53% and centrifuged again at 15,000 × g and 4°C for 20 min. The resulting supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS analysis. Equal volumes of extracts from all biological samples were pooled to prepare the quality-control sample. A 53% methanol solution processed using the same procedure was used as the solvent blank.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Q Exactive HF high-resolution mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source operated in negative ion mode. Full-scan data were acquired over an m/z range of 100–1500. The spray voltage was 3.5 kV, the sheath gas pressure was 35 psi, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10 L/min, the ion transfer tube temperature was 320°C, the S-lens RF level was 60, and the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 350°C. MS/MS spectra were acquired using data-dependent acquisition.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were annotated by matching accurate mass, retention information, isotope patterns, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against mzCloud, mzVault, and an in-house MassList database. Additional metabolite identities and functional classifications were referenced against the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, the Human Metabolome Database, and LIPID MAPS. Metabolite abundances were represented by normalized relative peak intensities.</p>

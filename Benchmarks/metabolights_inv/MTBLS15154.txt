Study Identifier	MTBLS15154
Study Title	Combined transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling identifies candidate pathways associated with adventitious root inhibition by watermelon scions in pumpkin rootstocks
Study Description	Adventitious root (AR) formation is essential for grafted seedling propagation, yet its suppression in specific scion-rootstock combinations remains a significant challenge. To determine the mechanisms by which watermelon scions inhibit AR formation in pumpkin rootstocks, this study utilized a double-root-cutting system across three comparative treatments: non-grafted pumpkin (P), self-grafted pumpkin (P/P), and watermelon-grafted pumpkin (W/P). Histological analysis revealed that the watermelon scion completely inhibits cellular dedifferentiation and primordium initiation. Hormonal profiling confirmed that W/P rootstocks contained significantly lower indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and higher trans-zeatin riboside (tZR) levels than P and P/P treatments, abolishing the requisite auxin-to-cytokinin ratio for AR induction. Metabolomic analysis revealed significant alterations in 32 key metabolites across the tryptophan, zeatin, and flavonoid/phenylpropanoid pathways, notably including indole-3-carboxylic acid, adenine, isopentenyladenosine, quercetin, and kaempferol. Transcriptomic profiling highlighted the profound enrichment of plant hormone signal transduction pathways, characterized by the systemic down-regulation of core auxin signaling components (AUX1, AUX, ARFs, SAUR) and the simultaneous up-regulation of cytokinin signaling elements (AHPs, ARRs). Furthermore, WGCNA identified specific transcription factors (CAMTA3, ILR3, AIL6) and functional hub genes (GH3.6, CYP735A, LOG, XTH, Pectinesterase) involved in the coordinated suppression of AR development. Collectively, this study elucidates the scion-induced transcriptional and metabolic disruptions that antagonize AR organogenesis, providing crucial genetic targets for improving rooting efficiency in elite grafted cucurbits.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) cv. 'HXX' and pumpkin (Cucurbita moschata) cv. 'Tiezhen No. 3', sourced from the Henan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, were used as the scion and rootstock, respectively. Rootstock hypocotyls from grafted seedlings were collected at 0, 6, and 12 days post-grafting (DAG) and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). The mobile phase consisted of (A) ultrapure water containing 0.1 % formic acid and (B) acetonitrile containing 0.1 % formic acid. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0 min, A/B (100:0, v/v); 1 min, A/B (100:0, v/v); 12 min, A/B (5:95, v/v); 13 min, A/B (5:95, v/v); 13.1 min, A/B (100:0, v/v); and 17 min, A/B (100:0, v/v). During the entire analytical run, samples were kept in the autosampler at 4 °C. To minimise the impact of instrumental signal fluctuations, samples were analysed consecutively in a randomised injection order. Quality control (QC) samples were evenly interspersed throughout the analytical sequence to monitor and evaluate the system stability and the reliability of the experimental data.</p>	<p>The raw data were firstly converted to mzXML format by MSConvert in ProteoWizard software package (v3.0.8789) and processed using R XCMS(v3.12.0) for feature detection, retention time correction and alignment. Key parameters settings were set as follows: ppm=15, peakwidth=c(5, 30), mzdiff=0.01, method=centWave. The batch effect was then eliminated by correcting the data based on QC samples. Metabolites with RSD &gt; 30% in QC samples were filtered and then used for subsequent data analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were slowly thawed at 4 °C. An appropriate amount (50-100 mg) of each sample was accurately weighed into a centrifuge tube. Then, 200 μL of pre-cooled water was added, and the mixture was vortexed for 60 s. Subsequently, 0.8 mL of pre-cooled methanol-acetonitrile mixture (1:1, v/v) was added, vortexed for another 60 s, and then subjected to ultrasonic extraction at low temperature for 30 min. The mixture was placed at -20 °C for 1 h to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected and vacuum-dried. The dried residue was reconstituted with 200 μL of 30 % acetonitrile solution, vortexed, and centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The final supernatant was collected for instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data (MS and MS/MS) were acquired using a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF-X high-resolution mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The source parameters were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate, 40 arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 10 arb; spray voltage, +3000 V (positive ion mode) and -2800 V (negative ion mode); auxiliary gas heater temperature, 350 °C; and ion transfer tube temperature, 320 °C. Data were acquired in Full-MS-ddMS2 (data-dependent MS/MS) mode, operated in both positive and negative ion polarities. The full-MS scan range was set to *m/z* 70-1050, with a resolution of 70,000 (at *m/z* 200) for MS1 and 17,500 for MS/MS (MS2) scans.</p>	<p> The metabolites were identified by accuracy mass and MS/MS data which were matched with HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca), massbank (http://www.massbank.jp/), KEGG (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/), LipidMaps (http://www.lipidmaps.org), mzcloud (https://www.mzcloud.org) and the metabolite database. The molecular weight of metabolites was determined according to the m/z (mass-to-charge ratio) of parent ions in MS data. Molecular formula was predicted by ppm (parts per million) and adduct ion, and then matched with the database to realize MS identification of metabolites. At the same time, the MS/MS data from quantitative table of MS/MS data, were matched with the fragment ions and other information of each metabolite in the database, so as to realize the MS/MS identification of metabolites.</p>

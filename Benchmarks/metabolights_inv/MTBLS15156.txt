Study Identifier	MTBLS15156
Study Title	Non-targeted metabolomic profiling reveals Flo8-associated metabolic alterations in Candida glabrata
Study Description	Flo8 is a transcriptional regulator involved in multiple physiological processes in Candida glabrata. To investigate the metabolic changes associated with Flo8, non-targeted metabolomic profiling was performed using the reference strain and a FLO8 deletion mutant. Intracellular metabolites were extracted from cells cultured under the same conditions and analyzed by liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry. Comparative metabolomic analysis revealed extensive alterations in metabolic pathways related to vitamin B6 metabolism, amino acid metabolism and glutathione metabolism. In particular, the FLO8 deletion mutant exhibited altered levels of pyridoxal 5′-phosphate and its related vitamers, together with changes in glutathione-associated metabolites. This dataset provides a global view of Flo8-dependent metabolic regulation and supports further investigation of the relationship between PLP homeostasis, mitochondrial function, and iron metabolism in C. glabrata.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Log-phase Candida glabrata samples were collected and immediately processed for metabolomic analysis. A total of 12 biological samples were included in this study.Equal aliquots of the supernatant from each sample were pooled to prepare quality control (QC) samples for monitoring analytical stability throughout the LC–MS analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters) on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (95% water and 5% acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid) and solvent B (47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol, and 5% water containing 0.1% formic acid). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C, and the injection volume was 3 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data were processed using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA). Data processing included baseline correction, peak detection, peak integration, retention time alignment, and feature alignment to generate a data matrix containing retention time, m/z, and peak intensity for downstream analyses.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Samples were transferred into 2 mL centrifuge tubes containing a 6 mm grinding bead. Metabolites were extracted with 500 μL of methanol/water (4:1, v/v) containing internal standards. Samples were homogenized at −10°C for 6 min (50 Hz), followed by ultrasonic extraction at 5°C for 30 min. After incubation at −20°C for 30 min, samples were centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatants were transferred into autosampler vials for LC–MS analysis. QC samples were prepared by pooling 20 μL of supernatant from each sample.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed on a Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ion modes. Full-scan data were acquired over an m/z range of 70–1050. Instrument parameters included a sheath gas flow rate of 50 arb, auxiliary gas flow rate of 13 arb, heater temperature of 425°C, capillary temperature of 325°C, spray voltages of +3.5 kV and −3.5 kV, S-Lens RF level of 50, normalized collision energies of 20%, 40%, and 60%, and resolutions of 60,000 for Full MS and 7,500 for MS/MS acquisition.</p>	<p>Metabolites were annotated by matching MS and MS/MS spectra against public databases, including HMDB and METLIN, together with an in-house spectral library. Metabolite identification was performed using a mass tolerance of &lt;10 ppm and MS/MS spectral similarity scores.</p>

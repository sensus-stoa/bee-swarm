Study Identifier	MTBLS15174
Study Title	Lithocholic acid modulates the growth of butyrate-producing bacteria and is decreased in the feces of stunted children
Study Description	<p>This dataset contains raw data for semi-targeted metabolomics measurements of bacteria-bile acid interactions, generated from in vitro experiments using selected bacterial reference strains. The study investigates bacterial responses to exposure to individual bile acids and their conjugated derivatives, including changes in bile acid abundance, bioaccumulation, and deconjugation patterns. The dataset includes measurements of bile acids associated with specific bacterial species, including butyrate-producing taxa and oral-associated taxa, to characterize species-specific bile acid metabolism and sensitivity. These data support the investigation of mechanisms underlying bile acid-mediated modulation of gut microbial composition, including the promotion of beneficial anaerobic bacteria and inhibition of oral-derived taxa.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Twenty microliters of bacterial culture or supernatant (centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 5 min)&nbsp;after overnight incubation with bile acids.</p>	<p>Analysis was performed utilizing reversed-phase chromatography on an InfinityLab Poroshell HPH-C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.9 μm) with an Agilent 6546 LC/qTOF system. Water with 0.1% formic acid and acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid were used as mobile phases A and B, respectively, with a gradient of 5 to 95% phase B in 5.4 min and a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min. </p>	<p>No data transformation has been done; raw .d files are reposited.</p>	<p>Liquid samples (20 μL) were processed for LC–MS analysis using an organic solvent extraction method. In brief, a mixture of acetonitrile:methanol (1:1) was added to the samples (5:1 v/v). The mixtures were then incubated at -20°C for at least 1h and centrifuged at 4500 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. Supernatants were collected and subjected to two successive 30-fold dilutions with water before LC-MS analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The qTOF instrument was operated in negative scanning mode (100–1700 m/z). Online mass calibration was performed using a second ionization source and a constant flow of reference solution (121.0509 and 922.0098 m/z).</p>	<p>No additional metabolite identification has been performed beyond semi-targeted profiling of reported bile acids, assigned based on the authentic chemical standards.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS15190
Study Title	Nicotinamide riboside restores cardiac function in a Barth syndrome mouse model
Study Description	<p>Barth syndrome (BTHS) is an X-linked mitochondrial disorder caused by TAFAZZIN mutations, leading to cardiac and skeletal myopathy as well as neutropenia. The biochemical cause is impaired remodelling of the phospholipid cardiolipin, leading to compromised mitochondrial function. Therapeutic options remain limited and primarily symptomatic. We previously found that supplementation of a Drosophila model of BTHS with the NAD+ precursor nicotinamide riboside (NR) rescued their phenotype, likely through NAD+’s central role in regulating cellular and mitochondrial metabolism. Here, we tested the therapeutic potential of NR in a Taz knockout mouse model. Wild type and Taz knockout mice were fed control or NR-enriched chow for 8 weeks, followed by assessment of cardiac function, lipid composition, and metabolic and proteomic profiles. NR supplementation fully rescued cardiac dysfunction in Taz knockout mice. While cardiolipin abnormalities were unchanged, NR increased cardiac NAD+ levels, enhanced energy metabolism, and improved markers of cellular stress resilience. These findings show that NAD+ boosting restores cardiac function independently of lipid remodelling, highlighting NR as a promising therapeutic strategy for BTHS.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>One week after the ultrasound test mice were sacrificed and a full panel of tissues was collected. Hearts were weighed immediately after extraction, and tissues were appropriately stored for biochemical, molecular and pathology screenings. Hearts were crushed using a pestle and mortar to obtain smaller samples for processing. Crushing was done in liquid nitrogen to avoid defrosting of the tissue. Heart samples were stored at -80ºC.</p>	<p>To analyze metabolites 5 µL of each sample was measured at 12 degrees Celsius using a Waters Acquity ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system attached to a Bruker Impact II™ Ultra-High Resolution Qq-Time-Of-Flight mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was performed using a Merck Millipore SeQuant ZIC-cHILIC column (PEEK, 100 × 2.1 mm, 3 µm particle size) maintained at 30°C. The mobile phase comprised two components: (A) 10% acetonitrile and 90% water (v/v), and (B) 90% acetonitrile and 10% water (v/v), each containing 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate. The flow rate was set to 0.25 mL/min, with the following gradient profile: initial hold at 100% solvent B (0–2 min), followed by a linear gradient to 54% solvent B at 13.5 min, a rapid switch to 0% solvent B at 13.51 min, and a hold at 0% solvent B (13.51–19 min). The column was then re-equilibrated by ramping to 100% solvent B at 19.01 min, holding until 19.5 min, and increasing the flow rate to 0.4 mL/min at 100% solvent B for equilibration from 19.5 to 21 min. </p><p><br></p><p>Lipid analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Ultimate 3000 binary HPLC with a Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Normal phase chromatographic separation was performed by injecting 2 μL of each sample onto a Phenomenex® LUNA silica column (250 × 2 mm, 5 μm particle size, 100 Å pore size), maintained at 25°C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 85:15 (v/v) methanol:water containing 0.0125% formic acid and 3.35 mmol/L ammonia, and (B) 97:3 (v/v) chloroform:methanol containing 0.0125% formic acid. The flow rate was set to 0.3 mL/min with the following gradient profile: initial hold at 10% A (0–1 min), followed by a ramp to 20% A at 4 min, a further ramp to 85% A at 12 min, a rapid increase to 100% A at 12.1 min, a hold at 100% A (12.1–14 min), a ramp back to 10% A at 14.1 min, and a final hold at 10% A (14.1–15 min). 5 μL of every sample was injected onto a Waters HSS T3 column (150 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm particle size) held at 60°C for reverse phase separation. The mobile phase comprised (A) 4:6 (v/v) methanol:water and (B) 1:9 (v/v) methanol:isopropanol, both containing 0.1% formic acid and 10 mmol/L ammonia. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min. The gradient was as follows: initial hold at 100% A (0 min), then ramp up at 1 min to 80% A, after decrease to 0% A at 16 min, hold at 0% A (16–20 min), ramp to 100% A at 20.1 min, and hold at 100% A (20.1–21 min). </p>	<p>Metabolomics data analysis was performed using Bruker TASQ software (version 2.1.22.3). Metabolite intensities were normalized to both freeze-dried tissue weight and internal standards with similar retention times and MS responses (Schomakers&nbsp;et al, 2022). </p><p>Lipidomics data analysis was performed using an in-house lipidomics pipeline implemented in R (Jaspers et al, 2024). All reported lipid abundances were normalized to corresponding internal standards matched by lipid class, as well as to freeze-dried tissue weight. </p>	<p>Metabolomics and lipidomics were performed using an established unified liquid-liquid extraction method (Schomakers et al, 2022, 2025). Metabolomics internal standards dissolved in water and lipidomics internal standards dissolved in 1:1 (v/v) methanol:chloroform were added to a 2 mL tube containing approximately 2-3 mg of freeze-dried heart or skeletal muscle tissue. To achieve a total of 500 µL water, 500 µL methanol, solvents were added. Tissues were homogenized using a 5 mm Qiagen Stainless Steel Bead in a Qiagen TissueLyser II for 2 minutes at 30 times/s. 1mL chloroform was added to each tube, and samples were mixed and subsequently centrifuged for 10 min at 14.000 rpm for layer separation. The top layer, consisting of the polar phase, was dried in a new 1.5 mL tube using a vacuum concentrator at 60 degrees Celsius. 100 µL 6:4 (v/v) methanol:water was added to the dried samples. The bottom layer, consisting of the apolar phase, was evaporated under nitrogen at 60 degrees Celsius in a new 1.5 mL tube, whereafter 100 µL 1:1 (v/v) methanol:chloroform was added. </p>	<p>Metabolomics mass spectrometry data were acquired in both negative and positive ionization modes, scanning the m/z range of 50–1200. Lipidomics mass spectrometry data were acquired in both negative and positive ionization modes, continuously scanning a mass range of m/z 150 to m/z 2000. </p>	<p>Metabolite identification was based on accurate mass, relative retention times, and fragmentation spectra, which were compared to a library of authentic standards. Lipid identification was based on mass, fragmentation, comparison with relevant authentic standards, relative retention times, and analysis of samples with known metabolic defects.</p>

Study Title	Fecal volatile and semi-volatile metabolomics of male giant pandas exposed to estrous female stimulation
Study Description	This study used non-targeted headspace solid-phase microextraction gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC-MS) to profile volatile and semi-volatile metabolites in fecal samples from adult male giant pandas (Ailuropoda melanoleuca) collected before and after exposure to estrous female stimulation. Samples were analysed to characterize metabolic responses in males with differing natural mating ability. Raw Agilent GC-MS data and processed feature tables are provided.

Study Protocol Type Term Source REF		CHMO	OBI	EFO	CHMO	MI
Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from adult male giant pandas before and after exposure to estrous female stimulation. Each sample was assigned a unique study identifier for paired metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites were separated using an Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph equipped with an HP-5MS UI capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). Helium was used as the carrier gas at 1.0 mL/min with a 25:1 split ratio. The oven was held at 50 °C for 5 min, increased to 230 °C at 7 °C/min and held for 3 min, then increased to 320 °C at 40 °C/min and held for 5 min.</p>	<p>Raw GC-MS data were processed in MS-DIAL version 4.60 for peak detection, retention-time alignment and peak-area integration. The resulting peak-area matrix was quality controlled before downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>One gram of chopped fecal sample was placed in a headspace vial and spiked with 20 μL of a 25 μg/mL deuterated internal-standard solution. Samples were equilibrated at 60 °C and 250 rpm for 15 min. Volatile and semi-volatile compounds were extracted by headspace solid-phase microextraction at 60 °C for 30 min, followed by thermal desorption in the GC inlet at 260 °C for 5 min.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were acquired using an Agilent 5975 mass selective detector operated in electron ionization mode at 70 eV. The ion source and quadrupole temperatures were 240 °C and 160 °C, respectively. Full-scan data were collected over m/z 50–500 using a quadrupole mass analyzer.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were annotated by matching mass spectra and retention indices against the NIST 2020 mass spectral library. Annotations are reported as putative identifications based on spectral and retention-index agreement.</p>

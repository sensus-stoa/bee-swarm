Study Protocol Description	<p>Algal cell samples were collected on the 4th day of cultivation. To guarantee sufficient biological biomass for subsequent untargeted metabolomics detection, the cell density of each experimental group was maintained above 2 × 107 cells mL-1. Cell pellets of all samples were harvested via centrifugation at 8,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 minutes. Immediately after centrifugation, the obtained cell pellets were frozen rapidly in liquid nitrogen to terminate intracellular metabolic activities and avoid metabolite degradation. All frozen samples were finally stored in a -80 °C ultra-low temperature refrigerator for subsequent metabolite extraction and LC-MS instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite separation was performed using a Thermo Fisher ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a matched Vanquish autosampler. A Waters HSS T3 analytical column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm) paired with a corresponding HSS T3 guard column was used for chromatographic separation, with the column temperature maintained at 40 ℃ and a constant flow rate of 0.40 mL/min. A total injection volume of 2 μL was set for each sample. The mobile phase consisted of two eluents: phase A containing 95% water and 5% acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid, and phase B composed of 47.5% acetonitrile, 47.5% isopropanol and 5% water supplemented with 0.1% formic acid. The gradient elution program was optimized as follows: mobile phase B increased linearly from 0% to 5% within 0–0.1 min, rose from 5% to 25% during 0.1–2 min, and continuously elevated to 100% from 2–9 min; the proportion of phase B was maintained at 100% for 9–13 min, then decreased linearly to 0% at 13.0–13.1 min, and remained stable at 0% until the end of the 16 min run. This optimized elution procedure achieved effective separation of both polar and non-polar intracellular metabolites of Chaetoceros curvisetus under phosphorus limitation stress.</p>	<p>The raw LC-MS mass spectral data were imported into Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for systematic preprocessing. The original data was sequentially processed with baseline filtering, peak recognition, peak integration, retention time correction and peak alignment to construct a standardized data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and peak intensity information. For data quality control and standardization, an 80% rule was applied to remove missing value variables, retaining variables with non-zero values in more than 80% of samples in each group, and the remaining missing values were filled with the minimum value of the original matrix. To eliminate systematic errors caused by sample preparation and instrument fluctuations, the total sum normalization method was used to calibrate the response intensity of mass spectral peaks. Variables with the relative standard deviation (RSD) of QC samples greater than 30% were deleted to exclude unstable data. Finally, log10 logarithmic transformation was performed on the normalized data matrix to obtain the final dataset for subsequent bioinformatics analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen Chaetoceros curvisetus cell pellets were taken out from the -80 °C refrigerator and thawed moderately on ice. A classic low-temperature organic solvent extraction method for untargeted metabolomics was adopted for intracellular metabolite extraction. In brief, pre-cooled mixed extraction solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water, 2:2:1, v/v/v) was added to the algal cell pellets for full cell lysis and metabolite dissolution. The extraction procedure was performed under continuous low-temperature and dark conditions to prevent oxidation, degradation, and structural transformation of intracellular metabolites. After thorough vortex extraction and static incubation, the mixture was centrifuged to separate the supernatant. The obtained supernatant was further purified and filtered to remove cell debris and insoluble impurities, yielding high-purity metabolite extracts suitable for subsequent UHPLC-MS/MS untargeted metabolomics detection.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was carried out on a Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-orbitrap mass spectrometer fitted with a heated electrospray ionization (H-ESI II) source, operating in both positive and negative ion full-scan modes with a mass-to-charge (m/z) scanning range of 70–1050. The ion spray voltage was set to 3500 V for positive ion mode and 2800 V for negative ion mode, with the sheath gas pressure at 40 psi, auxiliary gas pressure at 10 psi, and ion source temperature stabilized at 400 ℃. A stepped normalized higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) energy of 20–40–60 V was applied for secondary fragment ion acquisition. The resolution was set to 70000 for MS1 full scan and 17500 for MS2 fragment scan. The dual-ion scanning mode ensured comprehensive coverage of algal intracellular metabolites, while the high-resolution mass spectrum parameters guaranteed high accuracy and credibility of the acquired metabolomic data for subsequent metabolite identification and differential analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was implemented by matching the acquired MS and MSMS mass spectral fragment information with multiple authoritative metabolite databases. The public databases including HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin (https://metlin.scripps.edu/), together with the self-built database of Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd., were used for joint annotation. The matching criteria were based on accurate mass-to-charge ratio deviation and secondary fragment ion similarity to realize preliminary identification of metabolites. All annotated metabolite data matrices were uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com) for further screening of differential metabolites, functional enrichment analysis and exploration of metabolic response mechanisms of Chaetoceros curvisetus under different phosphorus limitation conditions.</p>

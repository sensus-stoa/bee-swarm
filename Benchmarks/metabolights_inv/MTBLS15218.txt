Study Identifier	MTBLS15218
Study Title	Integrated ruminal metabolomics reveals metabolic signatures associated with tannin supplementation and methane mitigation in feedlot Nellore cattle
Study Description	This study evaluated the effects of low-dose tannin-based feed additives on the ruminal metabolomic profile of feedlot Nellore cattle and its integration with microbiome, animal performance, and enteric methane (CH4) emission data. Ninety-six bulls were assigned to four dietary treatments: a basal diet without additive (CON) or diets supplemented with tannin-based additives at 0.8 (T08), 1.6 (T16), or 3.2 (T32) g/kg of dry matter. The experiment lasted 148 days, including 28 days of adaptation and 120 days of finishing. Ruminal fluid samples from 15 animals per treatment were analyzed by 1H-NMR-based metabolomics. Principal component analysis (PCA) showed partial separation among treatments, whereas partial least square - discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) indicated treatment-related metabolic discrimination, with T32 showing the most distinct profile. Tannin supplementation altered metabolites associated with carbohydrate fermentation, amino acid metabolism, nitrogen metabolism, sulfur-containing amino acid metabolism, and one-carbon metabolism. Network analyses indicated consistent changes in formate and choline, dose-dependent responses for methionine, and reduced glucose, pyruvate, and lactate in supplemented treatments. These metabolic changes occurred alongside reduced CH4 emissions, supporting an association between ruminal metabolic reconfiguration and methane mitigation. Overall, ruminal metabolomics identified treatment-specific metabolic signatures associated with tannin inclusion and lower methane emissions, particularly involving amino acid, nitrogen, sulfur-containing amino acid, and one-carbon metabolism.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Ruminal fluid samples were collected postmortem at a commercial slaughterhouse immediately after exsanguination from 60 Nellore bulls (15 animals per treatment) following approximately 12 h of solid feed withdrawal with free access to water. Rumen contents were manually collected, homogenized, and filtered through two layers of cotton cloth to obtain the liquid fraction. Samples were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 60 min at 4 °C to remove feed particles and microbial debris. The supernatant was transferred to new tubes and stored at −80 °C until metabolomic analysis. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining equal aliquots from all individual samples and was used only to assess analytical reproducibility and instrument performance.</p>	<p>Free induction decays were Fourier transformed after applying an exponential line broadening of 0.3 Hz. Spectra were manually phase- and baseline-corrected and referenced to the TMSP-d4 signal at 0.00 ppm. Spectral regions corresponding to residual water and contaminants were excluded when appropriate. Metabolites were identified and quantified using Chenomx NMR Suite 8.6 with support from the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB). The resulting metabolite concentration matrix was normalized prior to statistical analyses. Multivariate analyses (PCA and PLS-DA) and univariate analyses were subsequently performed using MetaboAnalyst 6.0.</p>	<p>Ruminal fluid samples were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 60 min at 4 °C to remove large particles (fine feed particles and microbiota). The supernatant was collected and centrifuged again for particulate material sedimentation. Subsequently, 400 μL of the centrifuged ruminal fluid was mixed with 200 μL of 0.10 M phosphate buffer prepared in deuterium oxide (D2O, pD 7.4) containing 0.050% (w/w) sodium 3-trimethylsilyl-2,2,3,3-d4-propionate (TMSP-d4) and 0.02% (w/v) sodium azide. Finally, 600 μL of the mixture was transferred into a standard 5 mm NMR tube. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining equal aliquots from all individual samples and was used exclusively to assess analytical reproducibility and instrument performance during metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified and quantified from the 1H NMR spectra using Chenomx NMR Suite version 8.6 (Chenomx Inc., Edmonton, Canada). Spectral assignments were confirmed using the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) and published literature when necessary. Metabolite identities were assigned based on chemical shift, signal multiplicity and spectral fitting to reference compounds.</p>	<p>Aliquots (2 mL) of ruminal fluid were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 60 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and centrifuged again to remove remaining particulate material. Subsequently, 400 μL of the centrifuged ruminal fluid was mixed with 200 μL of 0.10 M phosphate buffer prepared in deuterium oxide (D2O, pD 7.4) containing 0.050% (w/w) sodium 3-trimethylsilyl-2,2,3,3-d4-propionate (TMSP-d4) and 0.02% (w/v) sodium azide. Finally, 600 μL of the prepared sample was transferred into a standard 5 mm NMR tube for 1H-NMR analysis.</p>	<p>One-dimensional 1H NMR spectra were acquired using a Bruker AVANCE III HD spectrometer operating at 600.13 MHz (14.1 T) equipped with a 5 mm triple-resonance inverse cryoprobe (TCI) and automatic sample changer. Spectra were recorded at 298 K using the noesypr1d pulse sequence for water suppression. A total of 128 scans were collected with a spectral width of 12,019 Hz, acquisition time of 2.73 s, relaxation delay of 4 s, and receiver gain automatically adjusted. Free induction decays were multiplied by an exponential line broadening of 0.3 Hz prior to Fourier transformation. Spectra were manually phase- and baseline-corrected and referenced to the TMSP-d4 signal at 0.00 ppm before metabolite identification and quantification.</p>

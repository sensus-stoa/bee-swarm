Study Identifier	MTBLS15224
Study Title	Maternal MASLD rewires milk bile-acid hydrophobicity to restrict microbiome-dependent neonatal ketone supply_hippocampus
Study Description	Maternal metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) in mice creates a paradoxical neonatal ketone deficit during nursing, despite preserved hepatic ketogenesis, that precedes adult spatial memory impairment. Vulnerable litters show reduced β-hydroxybutyrate (β-HB) in the intestinal lumen, serum, and hippocampus. Increased milk bile-acid hydrophobicity is identified as an upstream determinant of a restrictive neonatal gut niche that suppresses microbiome-dependent depolymerization of poly-β-hydroxybutyrate (PHB) and limits portal β-HB delivery. Early hydrophilization of the neonatal bile-acid pool reopens this gut-to-brain ketone pathway, restores hippocampal β-HB, and prevents later memory impairment, whereas parenteral D-βHB bypass rescues brain ketone availability without correcting the upstream gut defect. A milk bile-acid hydrophobicity threshold prospectively identifies the rescuable high-risk state. These findings define a transferable postnatal metabolic checkpoint upstream of the liver.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Following euthanasia, hippocampal tissue samples were dissected from P7 mice, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C until further use.</p>	<p> Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system coupled online to an Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer. Polar metabolites were resolved on a SeQuant ZIC-pHILIC column (2.1 × 150 mm, 5 µm) maintained at 30°C with a flow rate of 0.30 mL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of 20 mM ammonium carbonate with 0.1% ammonium hydroxide in water, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The gradient held 80% B for 1 min, decreased to 20% B over 14 min, held for 2 min, returned to 80% B within 0.5 min, and was followed by 5.5 min of re-equilibration before the next analytical cycle.</p>	<p> Raw LC–MS files were processed in Compound Discoverer version 3.3. Chromatographic features were aligned and integrated using a 5 ppm mass tolerance and a 0.20 min retention-time window. A pooled quality-control injection was analyzed after every eight study samples. Features were retained only when detected in at least 80% of the samples in one experimental group, when the pooled-QC coefficient of variation was no greater than 25%, and when extraction-blank intensity was below 20% of the pooled-QC signal. Retained peak areas were normalized to the isotope-labeled internal-standard response and the corresponding wet tissue mass. Features that failed these prespecified analytical filters were removed before downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p> Frozen P7 hippocampal tissue (10–20 mg per specimen) was weighed and extracted with 20 tissue volumes of ice-cold 80% methanol containing a pooled isotope-labeled internal-standard mixture. Samples were homogenized with a TissueLyser II at 25 Hz for two 60-s cycles while maintained under cold conditions. The homogenates were held at −20°C for 30 min to promote protein precipitation and metabolite recovery, then centrifuged at 20,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The clarified supernatants were transferred directly to autosampler vials for LC–MS analysis. Equal aliquots from all study extracts were combined to prepare a pooled quality-control sample, and extraction blanks were processed through the complete workflow in parallel.</p>	<p> High-resolution mass-spectrometric data were acquired by negative-mode electrospray ionization on the Orbitrap Exploris 240. Full-scan spectra were collected from m/z 70 to 1,000 at 120,000 resolving power, with an AGC target of 1 × 1000000 and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The spray voltage was −2.8 kV, and the ion-transfer and vaporizer temperatures were 320°C and 300°C, respectively. Sheath, auxiliary, and sweep gas settings were 40, 10, and 1. Data-dependent MS/MS spectra were acquired at 30,000 resolving power using stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 to provide fragmentation evidence for downstream metabolite annotation.</p>	<p> Metabolite identification was performed conservatively using the chromatographically aligned high-resolution full-scan and data-dependent MS/MS evidence. β-HB and acetoacetate were considered confirmed only when both retention time and MS/MS fragmentation agreed with authentic standards analyzed under the same method. Other untargeted signals should be assigned names only when the recorded exact-mass, chromatographic, and fragmentation evidence supports the proposed identity; unresolved signals should remain unassigned features rather than receive definitive structural or isomer labels. The current manuscript does not specify a named external spectral library or database for all hippocampal features. Accordingly, any database name, library score, or annotation tier beyond the stated evidence should be added only after verification against the original Compound Discoverer processing record.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Verification item before external submission</p><p>The revised manuscript specifies authentic-standard confirmation for β-HB and acetoacetate but does not name a global external spectral library or database for all hippocampal features. Do not insert HMDB, METLIN, mzCloud, ChemSpider, Majorbio, or another database unless the original processing record confirms that it was actually used.</p>

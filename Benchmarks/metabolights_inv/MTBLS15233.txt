Study Identifier	MTBLS15233
Study Title	Glycosylation-mediated detoxification of zearalenone by the Tibetan yeast Hannaella zeae: Multi-omics insights into its dual-function mechanism
Study Description	<p>This study investigated the detoxification of zearalenone (ZEN) by the Tibetan Plateau yeast Hannaella zeae via untargeted LC-MS/MS-based metabolomics. H. zeae was cultured in NYDB medium with ZEN supplementation, and samples were harvested after 36 h of incubation for metabolomic analysis. The control group was incubated under identical conditions with the same yeast inoculation, except that no ZEN was added to the medium. Metabolomic profiling was conducted in both positive and negative ion modes to characterize metabolic changes linked to ZEN biotransformation and yeast-mediated detoxification. The study focused on the glycosylation of ZEN and the formation of the low-toxicity metabolites ZEN-14-G and ZEN-16-G.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Hannaella zeae was cultured in NYDB medium supplemented with zearalenone (ZEN). Samples were harvested after 36 h of incubation for untargeted metabolomic analysis. Control samples were cultured under identical incubation conditions, with no ZEN added to the medium. Biological replicates were set for each group. Standard quenching and low-temperature storage operations were performed on collected samples. Detailed quantitative parameters including ZEN dosage, yeast inoculation density, sampling volume and preservation conditions are archived in our original lab notebooks.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System fitted with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 µm) under hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography conditions. The mobile-phase composition, gradient program, flow rate, column temperature, autosampler temperature, injection volume and guard-column information were not reported in the available project documentation.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for peak detection, feature extraction, retention-time alignment and peak integration. Features with more than 20% missing values within an experimental group were removed. Remaining missing values were imputed using the minimum value observed across all samples. Peak-response intensities were normalized using total-sum normalization. Variables with a relative standard deviation greater than 30% in quality-control samples were removed, and the resulting data matrix was log10 transformed. Principal component analysis and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis were performed using the R package ropls version 1.6.2. Pathway-enrichment analysis was performed using the Python scipy.stats package.</p>	<p>Samples were prepared for untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomic analysis. Quality-control samples QC01, QC02 and QC03 were processed alongside the biological samples in both positive- and negative-ion assays. The available project documentation did not report the extraction solvent composition, sample-to-solvent ratio, internal standards, homogenization or sonication conditions, centrifugation conditions, supernatant collection, drying or reconstitution procedure.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer equipped with an Orbitrap mass analyzer. Untargeted LC-MS/MS data were acquired separately in positive- and negative-ion modes over an m/z range of 70–1200. The available project documentation did not specify the ion-source type or detailed acquisition settings, including spray voltage, capillary temperature, vaporizer temperature, sheath-gas flow, auxiliary-gas flow, mass resolution, automatic gain control target, maximum injection time, collision energy and scan rate.</p>	<p>Metabolic features were processed using Progenesis QI software. Metabolite annotations were assigned by matching accurate-mass and tandem mass-spectrometry information against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), METLIN, LipidBlast and the Majorbio in-house metabolite library. KEGG compound identifiers were subsequently assigned for pathway annotation and enrichment analysis. The metabolite-assignment files include metabolite names, internal metabolite identifiers, ionization modes, database identifiers, molecular formulae, mass-to-charge ratios, retention times and ion adducts where available. Identifications not confirmed using authentic reference standards were treated as putative annotations.</p>

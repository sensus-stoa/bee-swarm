Study Identifier	MTBLS15245
Study Title	Pediococcus pentosaceus-Derived Membrane Vesicles Alleviate Colitis-Induced Gut Barrier Disruption via Autophagy-Dependent Claudin-2 Degradation
Study Description	We found that both the culture supernatant of P. pentosaceus D17 and D17-CMVs alleviated DSS-induced colitis. D17-CMVs promoted intestinal barrier repair, accompanied by restoration of the gut microbiota, mucus layer, epithelial integrity, and tight junctions. Mechanistically, D17-CMVs promoted autophagy-dependent degradation of Claudin-2, thereby reducing intestinal barrier permeability. These findings were consistently observed in both DSS-treated mice and TNF-α-stimulated Caco-2 cells.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cecal contents were collected from 6-week-old male C57BL/6J mice immediately after euthanasia. Samples were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until metabolite extraction. Three groups of mice were included: control (C), DSS-induced colitis model (M), and D17-CMVs-treated (CMVs) group (n = 8 per group).</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system equipped with a Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and mobile phase B consisted of 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0-1 min, 1% B; 1-8 min, 1-99% B; 8-10 min, 99% B; 10-10.1 min, 99-1% B; 10.1-12 min, 1% B for re-equilibration. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 1 μL. The autosampler temperature was maintained at 10°C. No guard column was used.</p>	<p>Raw data files (.raw) acquired by MassLynx software (v4.2, Waters) were converted to open-format .mzML files using the msconvert tool from the ProteoWizard package (version 3.0). Peak picking, alignment, and normalization were performed using Progenesis QI software (Waters).</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg of each cecal content sample was accurately weighed and transferred to a microcentrifuge tube. Extraction was performed using 1000 μL of methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v) containing internal standards at 2 mg/L. The mixture was vortexed for 30 seconds, homogenized with ceramic beads at 45 Hz for 10 minutes, and sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 minutes. Samples were then incubated at -20°C for 1 hour to precipitate proteins, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant (500 μL) was collected for further processing.</p><p>Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling 10 μL from each sample and processed identically. Solvent blanks were also prepared and analyzed throughout the sequence.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Waters Xevo G2-XS Quadrupole Time-of-Flight (QToF) mass spectrometer coupled to the UPLC system. Data were acquired in electrospray ionization (ESI) mode. Both positive and negative ionization modes were collected separately (submitted as two independent assays). The mass spectrometer was operated in MSe mode, enabling simultaneous acquisition of low and high collision energy data in a single analytical run. The scan time was 0.2 seconds per spectrum. Low collision energy was set at 2 eV, while high collision energy was ramped from 10 to 40 eV. The ESI source parameters were: capillary voltage, 2000 V (positive mode) or -1500 V (negative mode); cone voltage, 30 V; source temperature, 150°C; desolvation gas temperature, 500°C; cone gas flow, 50 L/h; and desolvation gas flow, 800 L/h. Data were acquired over a mass range of m/z 50-1200. Lock mass correction was applied using leucine-enkephalin as the reference lock mass.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by querying the processed data against the METLIN online database and an in-house custom database, with a mass accuracy tolerance of within 100 ppm. Putative identifications were further confirmed by matching theoretical fragment ions with experimental MS/MS spectra where available. Progenesis QI software (Waters) was used for compound identification and annotation.</p><p><br></p>

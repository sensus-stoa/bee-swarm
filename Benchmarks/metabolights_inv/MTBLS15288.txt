Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal, soil, and water samples were collected from two waterfowl farms located in Gangcun (M2) and Fancun (F4), Xinyang City, Henan Province on 2025/5/1. Each water sample was 1 L, each soil sample was 1 kg, and each fecal sample was 10 g. All samples were immediately preserved in dry ice, transported to the laboratory, and stored at 80°C.</p>	<p>The liquid chromatography system was a **Waters ACQUITY UPLC** (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA) equipped with an **ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3** column (100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters). The column oven temperature was set at **40 °C**, and the autosampler temperature was maintained at **8 °C**. The flow rate was **0.4 mL/min**, and the injection volume was **2 μL**. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% (v/v) formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% (v/v) formic acid. The same gradient elution program was used for both positive and negative ion modes: 0–1 min, 5% B; 1–4.7 min, linear gradient from 5% to 95% B; 4.7–6 min, hold at 95% B; 6–6.1 min, decrease to 5% B; and 6.1–8.5 min, hold at 5% B for column equilibration.</p>	<p>The acquired raw data (.raw format) were imported into MS-DIAL software (version 4.9.221218) for peak picking, alignment, filtering, and metabolite identification. Peaks detected in less than 50% of the quality control (QC) samples were removed. Missing values for the remaining peaks were filled using the software's gap-filling algorithm, and the data were then normalized.</p>	<p>Water samples: Immediately after collection, filter through a 0.22 um membrane filter to remove suspended particulates. Aliquot into clean glass or polypropylene bottles and store at -80 C until analysis. Soil samples: After collection, remove plant debris and stones, then mix thoroughly. Fecal samples: Immediately after collection, aliquot and pre-cool at -80 C overnight. Pooled quality control (QC) samples are prepared by mixing equal aliquots of each test sample. They are interspersed throughout the analytical sequence to assess instrument stability and method repeatability, and to enable data correction and normalization.</p>	<p>The Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was operated under the control of Xcalibur software (version 4.7, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for data-dependent acquisition (DDA) in both positive and negative ion modes, respectively. The heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source was used with spray voltages of +3.5 kV and -3.0 kV for positive and negative modes, respectively; sheath gas at 40 arbitrary units (arb), auxiliary gas at 10 arb, capillary temperature at 320 C, and auxiliary gas heater temperature at 300 C. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 60,000 (at m/z 200) over a mass range of 70-1000 m/z, with the AGC target set to 'Standard' and the maximum injection time (Max IT) set to 100 ms. The top 4 most intense precursor ions were selected for MS/MS fragmentation with a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000, using a normalized HCD collision energy of 30%. The AGC target was set to 'Standard' and the Max IT was set to 'Auto'.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed against the PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database (PSNGM), which comprises an in-house standard library, the mzCloud library (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/), NIST_2020_MSMS, and an AI-predicted MS/MS spectral library. The main parameters used for library searching were as follows: MS1 tolerance for identification: 0.01; MS2 tolerance for identification: 0.05; Smoothing level: 3; Minimum peak height: 10000; Minimum peak width: 5; Mass slice width: 0.05; Identification score cut-off: 70.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS15292
Study Title	Metabolomic analyses in sepsis-associated acute lung injury rats
Study Description	<p>Sepsis-associated acute lung injury (SALI) is a life-threatening pulmonary complication triggered by sepsis, which carries a notably high mortality rate. We constructed a rat model of SALI and administered Xuanbai Chengqi Decoction (XCD) as treatment, after which untargeted serum metabolomics analysis was carried out to screen metabolites that respond to the intervention.Methods: Untargeted serum profiling was performed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry&nbsp;(LC-MS/MS) for the control, LPS (ALI model), and XCD groups. Differentially accumulated metabolites between groups were identified and the related metabolic pathways of these differential metabolites were further enriched and annotated to explore the potential mechanism of XCD in treating SALI.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Euthanasia was performed by injecting propofol emulsion&nbsp;(60mg/kg)&nbsp;via the tail vein, after which blood was collected from the abdominal aorta. After centrifugation, collect the serum in a sterile, enzyme-free EP tube using a laminar flow hood; seal the tube with a sealing film, flash-freeze it in liquid nitrogen, and store it at –80 °C.</p>	<p>Chromatographic column: Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm; Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid/water; Mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid/acetonitrile; Instrument column temperature: 40 °C; Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min; Injection volume: 4 μL</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry data were first converted from mzXML format to mzML format using ProteoWizard; peak extraction was then performed using the XCMS software, followed by alignment and retention time correction. Peaks with a missing rate&gt;50% in each sample group were filtered out; blank values were imputed using the KNN method combined with the 1/5 minimum value (for blank values&gt;50%, imputation was performed using the 1/5 minimum value; for blank values &lt;50%, imputation was performed using the KNN method); finally, the peak areas were corrected using the Support Vector Regression (SVR) method. The filtered peaks were subsequently used for metabolite identification by searching against the laboratory's proprietary database, integrating public databases, and utilizing predictive databases. Ultimately, compounds with an integrated identification score ≥0.5 and a Quality Control (QC) sample coefficient of variation (CV) value &lt;0.3 were selected, after which positive and negative mode data were merged—retaining the compounds with the highest qualitative confidence level and the smallest CV value.</p>	<p>Remove the sample from a-80 °C freezer and place it on ice to thaw until no ice crystals remain in the sample (all subsequent operations shall be performed on ice); After thawing the sample, vortex for 10 s to mix thoroughly, then transfer 50 μL of the sample to a corresponding numbered centrifuge tube; Add 300 μL of a 20 % acetonitrile–methanol internal standard extraction solution, vortex for 3 min, and centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C;Following centrifugation, transfer 200 μL of the supernatant to another corresponding numbered centrifuge tube, and let it stand at-20 °C for 30 min; Centrifuge again at 12,000 rpm for 3 min at 4 °C, then transfer 180 μL of the supernatant to the liner tube of the corresponding injection vial for instrument analysis.</p>	<p>Data acquisition alternated full-scan MS with data-dependent MS/MS under dynamic-exclusion conditions. Electrospray ionization was operated in positive and negative modes across m/z 75-1000 at a resolving power of 35,000. The remaining parameters were set as follows: spray voltage, 3.5 kV in positive mode and 3.2 kV in negative mode; sheath gas, 30 Arb; auxiliary gas, 5; ion-transfer-tube temperature, 320 °C; vaporizer temperature, 300 °C; collision energies, 30, 40, and 50 V; signal-intensity threshold, 1.00E+06 cps; Top N/Top speed, 10; and exclusion duration, 3 s.</p>	<p>Raw mass-spectrometry files were converted to mzML with ProteoWizard before peak detection, alignment, and retention-time correction in XCMS. Features absent from more than 50% of all samples were excluded. Missing observations in the remaining data were addressed using KNN together with one-fifth-minimum imputation: values with a missing proportion above 50% were assigned one fifth of the minimum, whereas values with a missing proportion below 50% were estimated by KNN. Support vector regression (SVR) was subsequently applied to normalize peak areas.</p><p>For annotation, the normalized and filtered features were queried against an internal laboratory library, integrated public resources, and prediction databases. Assignments were retained only when the overall identification score exceeded 0.5 and the coefficient of variation (CV) in quality-control (QC) samples was below 0.3. Positive- and negative-ion records were consolidated by retaining the entry with the highest identification confidence and lowest CV, thereby generating the final metabolite matrix.</p><p><br></p>

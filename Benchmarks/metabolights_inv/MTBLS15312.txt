STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS15312
Study Title	Photosynthetic bioenergetic-redox restoration rejuvenates the aged muscle regenerative niche
Study Description	<p>Aging impairs muscle regeneration, but the key constraint remains unclear. Aged injured muscle fails to restore mitochondrial respiration, membrane potential, ATP/NADPH balance and redox control, forming a bioenergetic-redox bottleneck. To target this, we engineered CM-NTU-Ce, a C2C12 membrane-coated nanothylakoid–ceria system coupling ATP/NADPH generation with redox buffering. In aged mice, CM-NTU-Ce improved mitochondrial fitness, restored ATP/ADP and NADPH/NADP ratios, enhanced myogenesis and functional recovery. Mechanistically, CM-NTU-Ce remodeled macrophages; depletion reduced regeneration, while CM-NTU-Ce-conditioned macrophages supported myoblast differentiation. Metabolomics confirmed restoration of mitochondrial and redox pathways. These findings identify a recoverable constraint limiting aged muscle repair.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection and subsequently euthanized by cervical dislocation. The gastrocnemius muscles were then carefully dissected and harvested for further experimental analysis.</p>	<p>For the separation of polar metabolites, chromatographic analysis was performed on a Vanquish ultra high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm). The mobile phase consisted of phase A (aqueous phase containing 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonium hydroxide) and phase B (acetonitrile). The autosampler temperature was maintained at 4 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard</p><p>and processed with an in-house program. which was developed using R and</p><p>based on XCMS, for feature detection, extraction, alignment, and integration.</p>	<p>Approximately 25 mg of each sample was accurately weighed at low temperature and transferred into an Eppendorf tube containing two homogenization beads. Then, 500 μL of extraction solution (methanol : acetonitrile : water = 2 : 2 : 1, v/v/v) containing isotope-labeled internal standards was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, followed by homogenization using a homogenizer at 35 Hz for 4 min, and then subjected to ultrasonic treatment in an ice-water bath for 5 min. This homogenization-ultrasonication cycle was repeated three times. The samples were then incubated at −40 °C for 1 h, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm (13,800 ×g, rotor radius 8.6 cm) for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were transferred into autosampler vials for LC MS/MS analysis. Additionally, an equal volume of supernatant from each sample was pooled to prepare quality control (QC) samples, which were analyzed under the same conditions.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was carried out on an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software (version 4.4, Thermo Fisher Scientific), enabling both full scan MS and data dependent MS/MS acquisition. The detailed parameters were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate, 50 Arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 15 Arb; capillary temperature, 320 °C; full MS resolution, 60,000; MS/MS resolution, 15,000; stepped normalized collision energy (NCE), 20/30/40; and spray voltage, 3.8 kV (positive ion mode) or -3.4 kV (negative ion mode).</p>	<p>The R package and the BiotreeDB-V3.0 were applied in metabolite</p><p>identification.</p>

Study Title	Fumarate reprograms the ubiquitinated proteome to confer ferroptosis resistance in clear cell renal cell carcinoma
Study Description	<p>Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is a kidney malignancy characterized by aberrant lipid accumulation and is intrinsically sensitive to ferroptosis. However, the mechanisms by which ccRCC escapes ferroptotic vulnerability remain poorly understood. Here, we report that fumarate hydratase (FH) deficiency drives fumarate accumulation, which induces extensive remodeling of the ubiquitinated proteome and confers increased resistance to ferroptosis in ccRCC. Mechanistically, fumarate-mediated C706 succination of ubiquitin-like modifier-activating enzyme 1 (UBA1), the primary E1 activating enzyme, significantly impairs its interaction with ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2 G2 (UBE2G2), concomitant with a global reduction of ubiquitination. Specifically, diminished ubiquitination increases the protein levels of activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) and elevates SLC7A11 expression, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis. Importantly, a designed cell-penetrating peptide attenuates fumarate-induced UBA1 succination, suppresses the ATF4-SLC7A11 axis, and enhances ferroptosis sensitivity of ccRCC. Collectively, these findings reveal that FH deficiency drives ubiquitinome dysregulation and suppresses ferroptosis, thereby linking ubiquitinated proteome reprogramming to tumor progression and unveiling potential therapeutic targets and strategies for treating ccRCC.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Tumor tissues were collected from NSG mice bearing subcutaneous 786-O clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) xenografts under the experimental genetic and pharmacological treatment conditions included in this dataset. Control or FH-knockdown 786-O cells were subcutaneously implanted into NSG mice, and the mice received the indicated treatments during tumor growth. Tumors were harvested at the experimental endpoint and stored at -80°C until targeted oxidized lipidomics analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a SCIEX ExionLC AD UPLC system equipped with a Thermo Accucore C30 column (2.6 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm). Mobile phase A consisted of acetonitrile/water (60:40, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid and 10 mmol/L ammonium formate, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile/isopropanol (10:90, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid and 10 mmol/L ammonium formate. The gradient program was as follows: 0 min, 80:20 A/B; 2 min, 70:30; 4 min, 40:60; 9 min, 15:85; 14 min, 10:90; 15.5–17.3 min, 5:95; 17.5–20 min, 80:20. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 45°C, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed using SCIEX Analyst software (version 1.6.3). Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) chromatographic peaks were inspected, integrated, and corrected to ensure consistent quantitative analysis across samples. Peak areas were used for subsequent lipid quantification with internal-standard correction. Derived spectral data were prepared in mzML format for data deposition.</p>	<p>Tumor tissue samples were thawed on ice, and approximately 20 mg of each sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. Samples were homogenized with a steel bead at 30 Hz for 20 s and centrifuged briefly at 4°C. Lipids were extracted with 1 mL of methyl tert-butyl ether/methanol (3:1, v/v) containing internal standards, followed by vortexing for 15 min. Subsequently, 200 μL of water was added, and the samples were vortexed for 1 min and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. An aliquot of 200 μL of the upper organic phase was collected and dried completely. The dried extracts were reconstituted in 200 μL of acetonitrile/isopropanol (1:1, v/v), vortexed for 3 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 3 min. The resulting supernatants were used for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode in separate positive- and negative-ion assays. The ion source temperature was set to 500°C. The ion spray voltage was 5500 V in positive-ion mode and -4500 V in negative-ion mode. Ion source gas 1 (GS1), ion source gas 2 (GS2), and curtain gas (CUR) were set to 45, 55, and 35 psi, respectively. Precursor/product ion transitions were monitored using individually optimized declustering potentials and collision energies. The m/z ranges were defined according to the precursor ions included in the MRM transition lists for the corresponding positive- and negative-ion assays.</p>	<p>Lipid identification was performed using the in-house MetWare Database (MWDB). Lipid species were annotated based on chromatographic retention times and characteristic precursor/product ion transitions. Quantification was performed using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM), with chromatographic peak areas integrated for each lipid species and corrected using internal standards. For oxidized phosphatidylethanolamine (OxPE) analysis, OxPE species were matched to their corresponding non-oxidized phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) species. The OxPE/PE index was calculated as the summed abundance of the included OxPE species divided by the summed abundance of their corresponding parent PE species.</p>

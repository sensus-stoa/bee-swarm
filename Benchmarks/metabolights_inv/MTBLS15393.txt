Study Title	Untargeted LC-MS metabolomic profiling of Tulipa iliensis leaves during Botrytis tulipae infection
Study Description	This study investigated the metabolic responses of Tulipa iliensis leaves to Botrytis tulipae infection using untargeted LC-MS metabolomics. Leaf tissues were collected at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48 and 72 h post-inoculation, with three biological replicates per time point. All samples were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, stored at -80 °C, and analyzed using a Vanquish UPLC coupled to a Q Exactive HFX high-resolution mass spectrometer in both positive and negative ionization modes.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Tulipa iliensis leaves were used as the experimental material. Leaf tissues were collected at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48 and 72 h post-inoculation with Botrytis tulipae, with three biological replicates collected at each time point. The collected tissues were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish UPLC system (Thermo Scientific, USA) equipped with a Waters HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). Mobile phase A consisted of Milli-Q water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The gradient program was as follows: 0 min, A/B 100:0 (v/v); 1 min, 100:0; 4 min, 40:60; 6.5 min, 5:95; 6.6 min, 100:0; and 8.0 min, 100:0. Samples were maintained at 4 °C in the autosampler and analyzed in randomized order. QC samples were evenly distributed throughout the analytical sequence.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data were acquired using Xcalibur 4.1 (Thermo Scientific) and processed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Raw data preprocessing included baseline filtering, peak detection, peak matching, retention-time correction and peak alignment, resulting in a data matrix containing retention time, m/z and peak intensity information. Features with zero values in more than 80% of all samples were removed. Data were subsequently normalized using the metNor function of the MetNormalizer package in R, and features with an RSD greater than 30% in QC samples were filtered out. Quantified data were exported in spreadsheet format for subsequent statistical and bioinformatic analyses.</p>	<p>Solid tissue samples were slowly thawed at 4 °C. Approximately 50–100 mg of each sample was accurately weighed into a centrifuge tube and 1 mL of pre-cooled extraction solvent consisting of water/acetonitrile/isopropanol (1:1:1, v/v/v) was added. Samples were vortexed for 60 s and extracted by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and incubated at -20 °C for 1 h to precipitate proteins, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was vacuum-dried and reconstituted in 200 μL of 30% acetonitrile. After vortex mixing, the samples were centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was used for LC-MS analysis. Quality-control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of the analytical samples and were regularly inserted throughout the analytical sequence to monitor instrument stability and data reproducibility.</p>	<p>High-resolution mass spectrometry data were acquired using a Q Exactive HFX Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired using the Full-MS/ddMS2 acquisition mode in both positive and negative ionization modes. The sheath gas pressure was set to 40 arb and the auxiliary gas pressure to 10 arb. The spray voltage was +3000 V in positive ion mode and -2800 V in negative ion mode. The source temperature was 350 °C and the ion transfer tube temperature was 320 °C. The MS1 scan range was m/z 70–1050, with an MS1 resolution of 70,000 and an MS2 resolution of 17,500.</p>	<p>Metabolite features containing MS/MS data were identified by matching experimental data against commercial metabolite databases and an in-house MS/MS metabolite database, together with characteristic fragmentation patterns. Metabolite identification considered retention time, accurate molecular mass with a mass error within 10 ppm, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra. MS/MS matching quality was evaluated using the fragment-spectrum matching score, ranging from 0 to 1, with scores greater than 0.35 considered reliable identifications. Identified metabolites were subsequently annotated against the KEGG Compound database for pathway annotation and downstream analysis.</p>

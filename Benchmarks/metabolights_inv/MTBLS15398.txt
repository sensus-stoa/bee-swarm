Study Identifier	MTBLS15398
Study Title	Lipid stoichiometry and biomarkers reflect microbial acclimation and nutrient stress across the Atlantic Ocean
Study Description	<p> The stoichiometry of particulate organic matter (POM) in the ocean influences global elemental cycles, but elemental distributions within macromolecule classes that comprise POM (e.g. lipids) are poorly constrained. Using publicly available datasets of lipids, nutrients, and POM stoichiometry collected in 2013 and 2014, we conduct a basin-scale survey of lipid stoichiometry spanning the western Atlantic Ocean from ~40°S to ~55°N, and demonstrate that surface ocean lipid stoichiometry variability is primarily linked to phosphate availability and is strongly correlated with POM stoichiometry. We evaluate lipid biomarkers of nutrient scarcity , finding they are not uniformly in agreement. We then describe a lipid class, diacylglycerylcarboxyhydroxysulfocholine, only known in the coccolithophore genus Gephyrocapsa, potentially indicative of nitrogen and phosphorus stress. We advance three biomarkers of nutrient scarcity, specific to oligotrophic heterotrophic bacteria and to the coccolithophore, G. (ex Emiliania) huxleyi, and elucidate a transition from nitrogen to phosphorus stress in the Atlantic Ocean. </p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples were analyzed using the following protocol, which has been copied verbatim from Holm et al. (2022, DOI: 10.1126/science.abn7455):</p><p><br></p><p>'High performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis was carried out using reverse phase chromatography on an Agilent 1200 HPLC (Agilent Technologies). The HPLC settings are identical to the one described in Collins et al. 2016 [DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b01260]. The HPLC system consisted of an autosampler, binary pump, column compartment, and diode array detector. This was fed into a Thermo Q-Exactive Orbitrap high resolution mass spectrometer (ThermoFisher Scientific) for high resolution/ accurate mass electrospray ionization mass spectral analysis (HRAM-ESI-MS). [...]The liquid chromatography was as follows: The aqueous Eluent ‘A’ consisted of MilliQ water with 1% 1 M ammonium acetate and 0.1% acetic acid. The organic Eluent ‘B’ consisted of 70% acetonitrile, 30% isopropanol with 1% 1 M ammonium acetate and 0.1% acetic acid. A 20 μL aliquot of the sample was injected at the start of the 40 minute program. For each run the system is kept at a flow rate of 0.4 mL per minute first with a gradient of 45% to 35% eluent ‘A’ between minutes 1-4, 25% to 11% eluent ‘A’ between minutes 4-12, 11% to 1% between minutes 12-15, then holding 1% eluent ‘A’ from 15-25 minutes. Lastly, eluent ‘A’ is returned to 45% for minutes 30-40 to clear the column (Table S3).'</p><p><br></p><p>The column used was a Waters Xbridge 2.1mm x 150mm C8 column with a 5 um particle size.</p>	<p>Mass spectral files were processed using the R-language based LOBSTAHS lipid analysis pipeline as detailed in Collins et al (2016; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b01260).</p><p><br></p><p>In brief, raw files were converted to .mzXML files using ProteoWizard MSConvert software (doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btn323). Features are then identified, grouped, and retention-time corrected using the xcms package(DOI:0.1021/ac051437), then grouped using the CAMERA package (dx.doi.org/10.1021/ac202450g). </p><p><br></p>	<p>Samples were extracted via a modified Bligh and Dyer protocol according to Holm et al (DOI: 10.1126/science.abn7455). The following description is abbreviated from that publication. </p><p><br></p><p>In brief, filters were added to 15 ml centrifuge tubes, to which was added 0.8 mL phosphate buffered saline, 2 mL methanol, and 1 mL dichloromethane. Sample vials were sonicated for 10 minutes, after which 1 mL phosphate buffered saline and 1mL dichloromethane were added. We then added 20 uL of a deuterated internal standard mix to aid in lipid class-specific recovery, and centrifuged at 515g for five minutes to aid phase separation. The organic (lipid) phase was then transfered via combusted Pasteur pipette to a combusted 2 mL microcentrifuge tube that had been prepared with 10 uL of 1.5 mM butylated hydroxytoluene to prevent oxidation. The samples were then kept in a -20 deg C freezer until reconstitution for mass spectral analysis. </p><p><br></p><p>For the reconstitution process, ~100 uL of each sample was transferred into combusted cylindrical glass vial insert in 2mL combusted microcentrifuge vials, dried down under a stream of nitrogen gas, then resuspended in a 7:3 mix of acetonitrile:isopropanol for analysis. </p>	<p>The samples were analyzed using the following protocol, which has been copied verbatim from Holm et al. (2022, DOI: 10.1126/science.abn7455):</p><p><br></p><p>'The Q-Exactive method was programmed to acquire data as follows: Positive-ion full-scan (200-1500 m/z) followed by data-dependent MS/MS (MS2) scans of the top 3 most abundant ions. Then, negative-ion full-scan (200-1500 m/z) followed by data-dependent MS2 scans of the top 3 most abundant ions. Dynamic exclusion settings were such that an ion was excluded from further MS2 analysis for 10 seconds, giving a broader depth of MS2 data whilst also giving MS2 spectra at the start, apex and end of a chromatographic peak. Masses for MS2 were isolated with a 4 m/z window and fragmented with a stepped (normalized) collision energy of 30, 50 and 80. Common background ions were added to an exclusion list. Real time mass calibration was enabled with lock masses of 536.16591 (C14H46NO7Si7+, cyclodimethylpolysiloxane, [M+NH4+]) and 297.27990 (C19H37O2-, n-nonadecanoic acid [M-H-]) were maintained at a low but consistent abundance in the source by placing a silicone autosampler vial septum and a glass vial of nonadecanoic acid in the source during analysis.'</p>	<p>Compounds were then annotated using the LOBSTAHS package (Collins et al, 2016; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b01260)., and manually confirmed using a combination of ms2 fragmentation patterns, adduct hierarchies, simultaneous positive-and-negative peak matching, and retention time pattern prediction.</p><p><br></p><p>Data in MAF file reported in integrated chromatographic peak area.</p>	<p>Whole algal culture samples were filtered onto 0.7 pore-size GF/F glass fiber filters, which were immediately extracted. </p>

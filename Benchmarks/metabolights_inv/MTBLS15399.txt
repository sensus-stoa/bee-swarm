Study Identifier	MTBLS15399
Study Title	AMOTL2 controls germ layer specification through glutamine metabolism in human pluripotent stem cells
Study Description	Emerging evidence highlights metabolic dynamics as instructive signals for differentiation, yet their mechanistic role in human germ layer specification remains poorly understood. Here, we identify AMOTL2, a cortical scaffolding protein, as a regulator of germ layer commitment in hPSCs, whose depletion preferentially promotes ectoderm formation. Although AMOTL2 loss disrupts F-actin organization and activates YAP1, neither interventions alter lineage outcomes, implicating a non-canonical mechanism. Instead, AMOTL2 loss elevates OXPHOS and glycolysis, generating NAD+ surplus that allosterically activates glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH), driving α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) and GABA accumulation in hPSCs. During differentiation, accumulated GABA is consumed through the GABA shunt, depleting α-KG required for endoderm specification and shifting the α-KG/succinate ratio toward ectodermal fate. Glutaminase inhibition rescued endoderm commitment in KO, GDH inhibition produced a partial rescue of the same phenotype and GDH stimulation in WT cells recapitulated the ectodermal bias, functionally validating this axis. Reciprocally, OXPHOS inhibition restored AMOTL2 expression, suggesting a feedback loop coupling mitochondrial activity to lineage balance. This study establishes a NAD+ - GDH -GABA shunt cascade as a metabolic determinant of human germ layer fate.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Untargeted metabolomics was performed at the FIMM-Helsinki Metabolomics unit for wild-type (WT) and AMOTL2 knockout (KO) human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) and definitive endoderm (DE) cells. 4 biological replicates from each genotype and differentiation stage (2x10^6 cells in each condition) were thawed on ice and 400 µl of extraction solvent (40:40:20; Acetonitrile:Methanol:Water) was added to each sample tube.</p>	<p>Samples were reconstituted in 40 µl extraction solvent and transferred to LC-MS vials. Any leftover pellets were stored in a -80°C freezer. A pooled QC sample was prepared by combining 4 µl of each sample. Relative metabolomics profiling was performed as described before (Xing et al., 2024). 2 μl of samples were injected into Thermo Vanquish UHPLC coupled with Q-Exactive Orbitrap quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (H-ESI) source probe (Thermo Fisher Scientific). A SeQuant ZIC-pHILIC (2.1 × 100 mm, 5-μm particle) column (Merck) was used for chromatographic separation. The gradient elution was carried out with a flow rate of 0.100 mL/min using 20 mM ammonium hydrogen carbonate in 100% water, adjusted to pH 9.4 with ammonium solution (25%) as mobile phase A and acetonitrile 100% as mobile phase B. The gradient elution was initiated from 20% Mobile phase A and 80% mobile phase B, and maintained till 2 min; after that, 20% Mobile phase A gradually increased up to 80% till 17 min, then reversed to the initial condition at 17.1 min and was maintained until 24 min. The column oven and auto-sampler temperatures were set to 40 ± 3 and 5 ± 3°C, respectively.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolomics data processing and integration were performed with the TraceFinder 4.1 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using confirmed retention times from 463 metabolite standards (MSMLS-1EA, Merck). The data quality was monitored throughout the run using in-house serum samples as Quality Control (QC) interspersed throughout the run after every 10th sample. The detected metabolites were checked for peak quality (poor chromatograph), QC % RSD (20% cutoff), and background (20% cutoff). Metabolomics data were normalized to total signal intensity.</p>	<p>Sample extraction was done with three cycles of sonication (60 sec) and vortexing (120 sec). Next, the samples were centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 10 min at +4°C, after which the supernatants (350 µl) were transferred to evaporation tubes and evaporated dry under nitrogen stream.</p>	<p>Following settings were used for MS: polarity switching; resolution of 35,000, the spray voltages: 4250 V for positive and 3250 V for negative mode; the sheath gas: 25 arbitrary units (AU); the auxiliary gas: 15 AU; sweep gas flow 0; capillary temperature: 275 °C; S-lens RF level: 50.0. The MS was operated in full scan range at 55-825 <em>m/z</em>. Instrument control was guided with the Xcalibur 4.1.31.9 software (M/s Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).</p>	<p>The following preprocessing steps were performed on the metabolomics data:&nbsp;(1) Metabolites with missing values across all samples or completely missing values for one group were removed; (2) Imputation of missing values was performed using the missForest algorithm (Stekhoven and Bühlmann, 2012); and (3) All values were log2-transformed. Differential abundance analysis was performed using the limma package (Ritchie et al., 2015). Briefly, mixed-effects models, treating technical replicates as random effects, were fit for each metabolite to compute the change in abundance from hPSC to DE stage, and between WT and AMOTL2 KO cells. All analyses were performed in R (version 4.3.2).</p>

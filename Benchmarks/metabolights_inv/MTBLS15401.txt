Study Identifier	MTBLS15401
Study Title	In Vivo CRISPR Screens Identify SLC1A5 as a Metabolic Barrier for Cancer Immunotherapy
Study Description	Tumor metabolic reprogramming profoundly shapes antitumor immunity, but the mechanisms linking glutamine metabolism to immune evasion in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remain incompletely understood. Here, we identify the glutamine transporter SLC1A5 as a tumor-intrinsic regulator of HCC progression and antitumor immunity. Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of SLC1A5 suppressed tumor growth across autochthonous, orthotopic, and subcutaneous tumor models and enhanced the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade. Mechanistically, SLC1A5-dependent glutamine metabolism maintained cellular redox homeostasis and mitochondrial integrity, whereas SLC1A5 loss promoted mitochondrial stress and activation of type I interferon signaling, thereby enhancing antitumor CD8+ T-cell responses. Conversely, SLC1A5 overexpression promoted tumor progression and immune suppression. These findings establish SLC1A5-mediated glutamine metabolism as a metabolic mechanism of immune evasion and suggest SLC1A5 as a potential therapeutic target for improving immunotherapy responses in HCC.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Sample Preparation and Metabolic Profiling Analysis</p>	<p>Reverse-phase liquid chromatography was performed using a Thermo Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters). Separate positive- and negative-ion LC-MS assays were acquired.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed to generate aligned metabolite feature tables containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio and sample-level abundance values for downstream metabolite annotation.</p>	<p>Tumor tissues (~10 mg) were homogenized in 600 µL of cold 80% methanol containing 5 µg/mL internal standards (valine-d8, succinic acid-d4, phenylalanine-d5, tridecanoic acid, citric acid-d4, and myristic acid-d27). The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and homogenized using a high-speed blender. After centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was lyophilized. The dried extract was subjected to oximation with methoxyamine–pyridine solution (20 mg/mL) at 37 °C for 1.5 h, followed by silylation with MSTFA at 37 °C for 1 h. Metabolic profiling was performed using a TQ 8050 NX GC-MS system (Shimadzu) coupled with a DB-5MS column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 µm). The column temperature was initially set to 80 °C for 1 min, ramped to 210 °C at 30 °C/min, then to 320 °C at 20 °C/min, and held for 4 min. Data were analyzed using ChromTOF 4.43 software (Leco Co.) and metabolites were identified by comparing m/z and retention time to a commercial metabolite library (NIST, Wiley) and a custom-built library.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired using a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 in separate positive and negative ionization modes over an m/z range of 100-1000.</p>	<p>Metabolites and metabolite features were annotated using measured m/z, retention time, molecular formula, adduct information and available KEGG/HMDB identifiers. Identification confidence was recorded using MSI evidence levels where available.</p>

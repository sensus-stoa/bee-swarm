Study Identifier	MTBLS15436
Study Title	A paired transcriptomic, metabolomic and fruit-quality data resource from field-grown tomatoes across eight agricultural sites in Ningxia, China
Study Description	<p>Tomato fruit phenotypes are shaped by genotype, agronomic management and local environment, yet matched molecular and environmental datasets from commercial field settings remain scarce. Here we describe a paired resource comprising fruit transcriptomes, untargeted metabolomes, fruit-quality measurements and plant-matched rhizosphere electrical conductivity (EC) and pH measurements from 32 independently sampled tomato plants distributed across eight agricultural sites in Ningxia, China. The resource contains processed abundance profiles for 1,338 annotated metabolite features; and eight recorded fruit and soil variables. Sample identifiers are shared across all data layers, enabling within-sample multi-omic reuse. We provide site-level metadata, per-plant environmental measurements, quality-control summaries. Because sampling location, cultivar and cultivation conditions were not experimentally randomized, this resource is intended for descriptive, comparative and hypothesis-generating analyses rather than causal inference about salinity responses. The dataset will support studies of tomato fruit biology under heterogeneous field conditions.</p><p><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	Leaf samples were collected from Solanum lycopersicum under drought-alkali stress and control conditions. Fully expanded functional leaves were harvested at predefined sampling time-points. Leaf tissues were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen upon harvest to quench metabolic activity. Samples were ground into fine powder under liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C in the dark prior to metabolite extraction. Field sampling was performed across multiple experimental sites in Ningxia, China. Biological replicates were set for each treatment group.	Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Vanquish UHPLC system fitted with an ACQUITY UHPLC C18 analytical column (1.8 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm). Mobile phase A consisted of water with 0.1 % formic acid, while mobile phase B was acetonitrile with 0.1 % formic acid. Column oven temperature was maintained at 40 °C, flow rate at 0.35 mL/min, and injection volume set to 5 μL. Gradient elution profile: 0-1 min, 5 % B; 1-12 min, 5-95 % B; 12-15 min, 95 % B; 15-15.1 min, 95-5 % B; 15.1-18 min, 5 % B for column re-equilibration.	Raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI v3.0 software for peak picking, retention-time alignment, feature detection and signal normalization. Feature intensities were normalized based on total ion intensity. QC-based signal correction was performed, and metabolic features with RSD > 30 % in pooled QC samples were discarded. Blank subtraction was not applied during data transformation.	Metabolites were extracted from frozen tomato leaf powder. Briefly, 100 ± 5 mg leaf tissue powder was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube together with one 6 mm-diameter grinding bead. A total of 400 μL cold extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing four internal standards (0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine included) was added. Samples were homogenized with a Wonbio-96c frozen tissue grinder (Shanghai Wanbo Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) for 6 min at -10 °C under 50 Hz. Afterwards, low-temperature ultrasonic extraction was carried out for 30 min (5 °C, 40 kHz). The mixture was incubated at -20 °C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was collected and transferred to LC-MS injection vials. Pooled quality-control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of supernatant aliquots from all individual samples. QC samples underwent identical pretreatment as experimental samples and were injected periodically (every 5-15 analytical samples) during data acquisition to assess analytical system stability.	Mass spectrometric detection was performed on a Thermo Q-Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. Both positive-ion and negative-ion electrospray modes were acquired using data-dependent acquisition (DDA) for MS/MS fragmentation with HCD normalized collision energies (NCE) of 20 %, 40 %, and 60 %. Common source parameters: sheath gas flow rate 50 arb, auxiliary gas flow rate 13 arb, heater temperature 450 °C, capillary temperature 320 °C, S-Lens RF level 40. Full-scan MS resolution was set to 70 000 at m/z 200; MS/MS resolution was 17 500 at m/z 200. The mass-to-charge (m/z) scan range was set to 70-1050 for both ion modes. Positive-ion mode: spray voltage +3500 V. Negative-ion mode: spray voltage -3000 V.	Metabolite annotation was performed by matching accurate mass-to-charge ratio, retention time and MS/MS fragment spectra (both positive- and negative-ion modes) against public databases including HMDB, METLIN and Massbank. Mass tolerance was set to 5 ppm for precursor ions. Metabolite confidence levels were assigned according to MSI standards.

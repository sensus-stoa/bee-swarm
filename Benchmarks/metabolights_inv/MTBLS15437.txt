Study Identifier	MTBLS15437
Study Title	Untargeted serum metabolomics of dairy goats under heat stress and melatonin supplementation
Study Description	Serum untargeted metabolomic profiling was performed to characterize metabolic alterations induced by heat stress and to evaluate the potential protective effects of melatonin supplementation in dairy goats. Serum samples were collected from dairy goats assigned to three experimental groups: a normal control group (NC), a heat-stressed group (HS), and a melatonin-treated heat-stressed group (MT). Untargeted metabolomics was used to comprehensively detect serum metabolites and identify differential metabolic features and pathways associated with heat stress and melatonin-mediated metabolic recovery. This dataset provides a resource for investigating systemic metabolic responses to heat stress and the metabolic regulatory mechanisms underlying the protective effects of melatonin in dairy goats.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Blood samples were collected from dairy goats assigned to the normal control (CON), heat-stress (HS), and melatonin-treated heat-stress (MLT) groups. Blood samples were allowed to clot and centrifuged at 3000g/rpm for 5 min at 4°C to separate serum. The obtained serum samples were aliquoted and stored at −80°C until untargeted LC-MS metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Vanquish Flex ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C and the autosampler temperature at 8°C. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 μL. For both positive and negative ionization modes, mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The gradient was as follows: 0–1.0 min, 5% B; 1.0–4.7 min, 5–95% B; 4.7–6.0 min, 95% B; 6.0–6.1 min, 95–5% B; and 6.1–8.5 min, 5% B.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data files in .raw format were imported into MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218) for data processing. Peak detection, peak alignment, filtering, gap filling, and normalization were performed using MS-DIAL. Features not detected in more than 50% of the QC samples were removed. Missing values were filled using the gap-filling algorithm implemented in MS-DIAL, followed by normalization of metabolite peak areas. Major processing parameters included a smoothing level of 3, minimum peak height of 10,000, minimum peak width of 5, and mass slice width of 0.05.</p>	<p>Samples were prepared for metabolite extraction according to the LC-MS untargeted metabolomics protocol. Briefly, 30 mg of sample was transferred into a 2-mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 200 μL of pre-cooled water. Samples were homogenized at 55 Hz for 60 s twice. Subsequently, 800 μL of methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) was added, followed by sonication for 30 min and incubation at −20°C for 45 min. Samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4°C, and 800 μL of the supernatant was collected and vacuum-dried. The dried extracts were reconstituted in 150 μL of 50% methanol containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine, vortexed for 30 s, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatant was passed through a 0.22-μm filter and centrifuged again prior to LC-MS analysis. A pooled quality-control (QC) sample was prepared by combining 10–20 μL aliquots from individual samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Q Exactive HF-X high-resolution mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software (version 4.7, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes using data-dependent acquisition (DDA) with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. The spray voltage was 3.5 kV, sheath gas was set to 40 arb, auxiliary gas to 10 arb, capillary temperature to 320°C, and auxiliary gas heater temperature to 300°C. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 60,000 over an m/z range of 70–1000 with an AGC target set to Standard and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The top 10 precursor ions were selected for MS/MS fragmentation with a dynamic exclusion time of 4 s. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000 using HCD with a normalized collision energy of 30%, an AGC target of Standard, and an automatic maximum injection time.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation and identification were performed using MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218) against the PSNGM (PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database). The database integrates an in-house authentic standard library, mzCloud, LIPID MAPS, HMDB, MoNA, NIST 2020 MS/MS, and an AI-predicted MS/MS spectral library. Metabolites were identified based on accurate precursor mass and MS/MS spectral matching. The MS1 mass tolerance for identification was 0.01, the MS2 tolerance was 0.05, and the identification score cutoff was 70.</p>

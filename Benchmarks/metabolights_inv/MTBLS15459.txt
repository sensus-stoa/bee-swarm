Study Title	Metabolomics-Based Analysis of Adaptive Mechanism of Eleutheronema tetradactylum to Low-Temperature Stress
Study Description	<p>Temperature is a critical environmental factor influencing fish physiology. Eleutheronema tetradactylum is an economically important warm-water species, but its response to long-term cold stress remains unclear. In this study, we exposed juvenile fish to 18 °C for 7 and 14 days and investigated hepatic adaptations using LC-MS-based untargeted metabolomics, combined with histological examination and antioxidant enzyme assays. We observed that cold stress initially elevated the activities of superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase at day 7, but these declined by day 14, while malondialdehyde levels progressively increased, indicating accumulated oxidative damage. Histological analysis revealed vacuolation and nuclear atrophy in hepatocytes, especially after 14 days. Metabolomic profiling identified 87 and 116 differentially expressed metabolites at day 7 and day 14, respectively. Pathway enrichment showed early activation of carbohydrate digestion and absorption, starch and sucrose metabolism, and glutathione metabolism, suggesting enhanced energy mobilization and antioxidant defense. At day 14, significant alterations emerged in glycerophospholipid metabolism and phosphatidylinositol signaling, reflecting adjustments in membrane fluidity and lipid homeostasis. Our findings demonstrate that E. tetradactylum copes with cold stress by upregulating energy metabolism and antioxidant systems in the short term, but prolonged exposure overwhelms these compensatory mechanisms, leading to liver damage. This study provides comprehensive metabolic and physiological insights that can inform aquaculture management under fluctuating thermal conditions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p _msttexthash='162502353' _msthash='1899'>Juvenile Eleutheronema tetradactylum were obtained from a commercial fish farm in Zhanjiang, China. Fish were acclimated at 28 degrees Celsius for one week in laboratory tanks. The water temperature was decreased gradually by 2 degrees Celsius per day and stabilized at 18 degrees Celsius for the cold-stress groups. Liver tissues were collected at three time points: 0 days (control group), 7 days, and 14 days after cold exposure. Three biological replicates were sampled per group. For metabolomics analysis, liver tissues were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at minus 80 degrees Celsius until metabolite extraction. Pooled quality control samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of all liver sample extracts and were used to monitor analytical stability.</p>	<p>UHPLC system: Agilent 1290 Infinity LC (Agilent Technologies). Column: ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters). Column temperature: 25 °C. Injection volume: 2 μL. Mobile phase A: water containing 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonium hydroxide; Mobile phase B: acetonitrile. Gradient: 0–1 min 85% B; 1–12 min linear decrease to 65% B; 12–12.1 min decrease to 40% B, hold 4 min; 16.1–16.2 min return to 85% B, followed by 5 min re-equilibration. Flow rate: 0.3 mL/min.</p>	<p _msttexthash='283981880' _msthash='531'>Raw LC-MS data files (.wiff) were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard MSConvert. Peak detection, retention time alignment, and feature grouping were performed using XCMS software (v3.18.0) in R. Peak picking was conducted using the centWave method with the following parameters: mass tolerance = 10 ppm, peak width = c(10, 60), prefilter = c(10, 100). Peak grouping parameters were set as bw = 5, mzwid = 0.025, and minfrac = 0.5. Isotope and adduct annotation were performed using CAMERA. Features with more than 50% non-zero measurements in at least one group were retained. The resulting peak intensity table was used for subsequent multivariate statistical analysis.</p>	<p _msttexthash='166033569' _msthash='858'>Approximately 80 mg of frozen liver tissue from each sample (n=3 per group) was homogenized with 200 µL of water and ceramic beads using a homogenizer. Then 800 µL of pre-cooled methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) was added for metabolite extraction. The mixture was vortexed and centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4 degrees Celsius. The supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube and dried under vacuum using a centrifugal concentrator. The dried residue was reconstituted in 100 µL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) prior to LC-MS analysis. Pooled quality control samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of all individual samples and were injected periodically throughout the analytical run to monitor system stability and reproducibility. No internal standard was added.</p>	<p _msttexthash='150333911' _msthash='1204'>Mass spectrometer: AB Sciex Triple TOF 6600. Ion source: Electrospray ionization (ESI). Ionization mode: Positive and negative modes. Source temperature: 600 C. Ion source gas 1: 60 psi, ion source gas 2: 60 psi, curtain gas: 30 psi. Ion spray voltage floating: plus 5500 V for positive mode and minus 5500 V for negative mode. MS scan range: m/z 60 to 1000 Da. MS/MS scan range: m/z 25 to 1000 Da. MS accumulation time: 0.20 seconds per spectrum. MS/MS accumulation time: 0.05 seconds per spectrum. Declustering potential: 60 V for positive and minus 60 V for negative. Collision energy: 35 V with a spread of plus/minus 15 eV. Acquisition mode: Information dependent acquisition (IDA) with data-dependent acquisition (DDA); maximum 10 fragment spectra per cycle.</p>	<p _msttexthash='283981880' _msthash='1554'>Raw data files in .wiff format were converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard MSConvert. Peak detection, alignment, and feature extraction were performed using the XCMS R package version 3.18.0. Peak picking used the centWave method with mass tolerance of 10 ppm, peak width of 10 to 60 seconds, and prefilter of 10 and 100. Peak grouping parameters were set as bw equal to 5, mzwid equal to 0.025, and minfrac equal to 0.5. Isotope and adduct annotation were performed using CAMERA. Features with more than 50 percent non-zero measurements in at least one group were retained. Metabolites were identified by matching accurate m/z values and MS/MS fragment spectra against the KEGG database. Differential metabolites were selected based on VIP values greater than 1.0 and p-values less than 0.05 from OPLS-DA analysis. Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using KEGG pathway mapping.</p>

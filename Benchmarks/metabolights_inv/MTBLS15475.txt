Study Title	Germacrone Protects Against DSS Induced Colitis Through a Gut Microbiota Derived Indole-3-Carboxylic Acid-AhR-ILC3/IL-22 Axis
Study Description	Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic recurrent inflammatory bowel disease, which is characterized by intestinal microbiota dysbiosis, intestinal barrier and mucosal immune dysfunction. Given the limited effect of biological agents for UC, there is an urgent need to develop more effective pharmacological treatment. Gemacrone (GER), a natural product, has shown significant anti-inflammatory effects in previous studies. Here, we confirm that GER significantly alleviates dextran sulfate sodium (DSS) induced colitis in mice, which is mainly manifested by weight loss, shortened colon length, reduced disease activity index (DAI) score, macroscopic and microscopic damage of mucosa, as well as intestinal barrier dysfunction, inflammatory factors expression and the proportion of ILC3 and IL-22 in the intestinal lamina propria. 16S rRNA sequencing and fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) experiments revealed that the remission of enteritis by GER depended on intestinal flora, especially Lactobacillus. Metabolomic analysis showed that tryptophan metabolite indole-3-carboxylic acid (I3CA) was significantly enriched in GER treatment. Functional experiments confirmed that I3CA could simulate the therapeutic effect of GER, meanwhile antagonizing inflammation via the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) signaling pathway both in vivo and in vitro. In vivo administration of AhR antagonist CH223191 further verified that GER and I3CA alleviate enteritis which are depend on AhR signaling. Collectively, our findings displayed a novel microbiota-derived I3CA-AhR-ILC3/IL-22 regulatory axis modulated by GER, highlighting its potential as a promising therapeutic strategy for UC.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Mouse biological samples were collected, accurately weighed, and stored at low temperature prior to metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was the UHPLC-TripleTOF 6600 system, an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-tandem time-of-flight mass spectrometry system from ABSCIEX. The chromatographic column was an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 ($100\text{ mm} \times 2.1\text{ mm i.d.}, 1.8\ \mu\text{m}$; Waters, Milford, USA).</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, ultimately yielding a data matrix containing information such as retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. </p>	<p>Take 17.8 mg of the sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, add one grinding bead with a diameter of 6 mm, and add 400 $\mu$L of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1 (v:v)) containing four internal standards (such as L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL). The mixture is then ground using a frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (-10°C, 50 Hz), extracted by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min (5°C, 40 kHz), and allowed to stand at -20°C for 30 min. After centrifugation for 15 min (13000 g, 4°C), the supernatant is transferred into an autosampler vial with an insert for instrumental analysis. Additionally, 20 $\mu$L of the supernatant from each sample is transferred and mixed together to serve as a quality control (QC) sample.</p>	<p>The instrument platform for this LC-MS analysis was the UHPLC-TripleTOF 6600 system, an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-tandem time-of-flight mass spectrometry system from ABSCIEX. The chromatographic conditions were as follows: the chromatographic column was an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, USA); mobile phase A was 95% water + 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B was 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid); the flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, the injection volume was 10 μL, and the column temperature was 45℃.</p>	<p>The raw data were imported into the metabolomics processing software Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment, ultimately yielding a data matrix containing information such as retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and peak intensity. Subsequently, the software was used for characteristic peak database searching and identification by matching the MS and MS/MS mass spectral information with metabolite databases. The MS mass error was set to less than 10 ppm, and metabolites were identified based on the secondary mass spectrometry matching score.</p>

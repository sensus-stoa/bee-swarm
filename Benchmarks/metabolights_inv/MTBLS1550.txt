STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS1550
Study Title	Metabolomics analysis identifies metabolites associated with systemic acquired resistance in Arabidopsis
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:</strong> Systemic acquired resistance (SAR) is a plant defense response that provides long-lasting, broad-spectrum pathogen resistance to uninfected distal leaves following an initial localized infection. However, little information is available on the molecular biological basis of SAR especially in uninfected distal leaves. </p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> Here, we used two SAR-inducing pathogen to induce SAR in Arabidopsis, and a metabolomics approach based on ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) and mass spectrometry (MS) technique was used to identify SAR-related metabolites in SAR-inducing pathogen infected local leaves and uninfected distal leaves. </p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> Multiple statistical analyses were used to identify differentially expressed metabolites. The result showed that primary metabolism and secondary metabolism were significantly altered in local leaves and distal leaves, including phenolic compounds, amino acids, nucleotides, organic acids and many other metabolites.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> The content of amino acids and phenolic compounds increased in distal leaves, suggesting their contribution to the establishment of SAR in distal leaves. In addition, 2’-hydroxy-4, 4’, 6’-trimethoxychalcone, phenylalanine and p-coumaric acid were identified as potential components which may play important roles both in basic resistance and SAR. This study provided a reference for understanding metabolic mechanism associated with SAR in Arabidopsis, which will be useful for further investigation of the molecular basis of SAR.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Arabidopsis thaliana (L.) Heynh. ecotype Col-0 were grown in individual pots on a matrix of perlite, vermiculite and nutrient soil (1:1:1, v/v/v) in a controlled environment chamber under the conditions of 65% relative humidity (RH) and 16 h light/8 h dark cycles. Growth temperatures during the day and night period were set to 22 °C and 18 °C, respectively. 4-5 week-old plants exhibiting a uniform appearance were used for systemic acquired resistance (SAR) experiments.</p><p><br></p><p>The avirulent P. syringae pv. maculicola ES4326 harboring avrRpm1 (Psm avrRpm1, termed as Avr) and virulent P. syringae pv. maculicola ES4326 (Psm ES4326, termed as Vir) were used for induction of SAR. Bacterium strains were cultivated in King's B medium supplemented with 50 mg/l streptomycin and 50 mg/l tetracycline (for Avr) or 50 mg/l streptomycin (for Vir), respectively, and were grown at 200 r/m in 28 °C. For SAR induction, a suspension of Avr, Vir in 10 mM MgCl2 (OD600=0.002) were infiltrated into 3 local leaves (LL). Infiltration with 10 mM MgCl2 served as mock-control treatment (CK). 48 h later, the Avr, Vir and CK inoculated LL (LL-Avr, LL-Vir and LL-CK) and distal leaves (termed as DL) of locally Avr, Vir and CK inoculated plants (DL-Avr, DL-Vir and DL-CK) were harvest separately, and stored in liquid nitrogen. Together, 6 biologically independent SAR experiments for each treatment were performed for metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Each sample (50 mg) was ground in liquid nitrogen, followed by the addition of 0.5 ml pre-cooled methanol/water (3:1, v/v), mixed by vortexing and incubation for 1 h at −20 °C for protein precipitation. Then the sample was centrifuged at 13000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. After filtering with a 0.22 μm filter, the supernatant was dried with nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until use. QC samples, a pooled mixture made with an equal amount of each sample, were prepared using the same steps above.</p>	<p>The samples were placed in a 4 °C autosampler and separated on a DIONEX UltiMate 3000 ultra high performance liquid chromatography system (UHPLC) using a ACQUITY UPLC CSH C18 Column 130 Å (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm, Waters). The sample volume was 3 μl, the column temperature was 45 °C, and the flow rate was 0.35 ml/min. Chromatographic mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid; B: acetonitrile in 0.1% formic acid; chromatographic gradient elution programme: 0-0.5min, 98% A; 0.5-15 min, A with a linear change from 98% to 2%; 15-17 min, 2% A; 17.1-20 min, A with a linear change from 2% to 98%. QC samples were inserted every 6 samples to evaluate the stability of the system and the reliability of experimental data.</p>	<p>Each sample was electrospray-ionized (ESI) and detected with positive and negative ion mode, respectively. Samples were separated by UPLC and analysed by a Q-Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, San Jose, CA, USA), The ESI source conditions were as follows: spray voltage (kV), 3.5 ESI+ and 3.2 ESI- (positive and negative modes); source temperature, 320 °C; sheath gas flow rate (Arb), 45; aux gas flow rate (Arb), 15; mass range (m/z), 80-1200; full MS resolution, 70000; MS/MS resolution, 17500.</p>	<p>No data transformation was carried out on the mass spectrometer RAW files in this study.</p>	<p>Compound Discoverer 3.0 was used to search the raw data and then used for peak alignment, peaks deconvoluted, treatment of adducts, compound detection, grouping and metabolite identification. A blank sample was used for background subtraction and noise removal during the pre-processing step. The precision mass matching (5 ppm)<strong>[1]</strong> and MS and MSMS matching to search MZcloud and ChemSpider database were used to determine the metabolite structure. The detailed workflow and parameters are provided in Table S5 of the paper associated with this study. The SIMCA-P14.1 application software (Umetrics, Umea, Sweden) was used for pattern recognition. After processing by Pareto scaling, the data were subjected to multidimensional statistical analysis, including orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Cao J, Li M, Chen J, Liu P, Li Z. Effects of MeJA on Arabidopsis metabolome under endogenous JA deficiency. Sci Rep. 2016 Nov 24;6:37674. doi:10.1038/srep37674. PMID:27883040</p>

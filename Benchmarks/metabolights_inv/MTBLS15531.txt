Study Title	Live Combined Bifidobacterium Attenuates Rivaroxaban-Associated Colonic Bleeding in Mice: Evidence for a Substantial Gut Microbiota-Dependent Component
Study Description	This study investigated fecal metabolic alterations associated with live combined Bifidobacterium (LCB) treatment in a mouse model of rivaroxaban-associated colonic bleeding. Male C57BL/6J mice were included in two experiments comprising normal control, rivaroxaban-treated, rivaroxaban followed by LCB treatment, antibiotic-treated plus rivaroxaban, and antibiotic-treated plus rivaroxaban followed by LCB treatment groups. Four mice per group were randomly selected for untargeted fecal metabolomics analysis. Metabolomic profiling was performed by UHPLC-Q Exactive LC-MS in both positive and negative ion modes. The study aimed to characterize treatment-associated changes in the fecal metabolome and to evaluate their relationship with gut microbiota-dependent effects of LCB.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from male C57BL/6J mice assigned to the normal control (NC), rivaroxaban-treated (R), rivaroxaban followed by live combined Bifidobacterium treatment (RB), antibiotic-pretreated plus rivaroxaban (ABX-R), and antibiotic-pretreated plus rivaroxaban followed by live combined Bifidobacterium treatment (ABX-RB) groups. Fresh fecal samples were collected into sterile centrifuge tubes and immediately stored at -80°C until subsequent untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis. Samples selected for metabolomics analysis were obtained from four mice per experimental group.</p>	<p>Reversed-phase UHPLC separation was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific UHPLC system equipped with a Waters HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm). The injection volume was 3 μL. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v), and mobile phase B consisted of 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v). The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C. Gradient elution was used for chromatographic separation.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were imported into Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA) for baseline filtering, peak detection, peak integration, retention-time correction, and peak alignment. A data matrix containing retention time, mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and peak intensity was subsequently generated. Features were retained according to the 80% rule, requiring non-zero values in at least 80% of samples within at least one experimental group. Missing values were imputed using the minimum value in the original data matrix. Peak intensities were normalized using sum normalization to reduce systematic variation among samples. Features with a relative standard deviation (RSD) greater than 30% in quality-control samples were removed. The resulting normalized data matrix was log10-transformed prior to downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Fecal samples were processed for untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis. Approximately 100 mg of each fecal sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and 800 μL of extraction solvent consisting of methanol/water (4:1, v/v) containing four internal standards, including L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL, was added. Samples were subjected to cryogenic grinding for 6 min at -10°C and 50 Hz, followed by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min at 5°C and 40 kHz. The samples were then incubated at -20°C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was transferred into autosampler vials for LC-MS analysis. Quality-control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all study samples. One pooled QC sample was injected every 5–10 analytical samples to monitor analytical stability and reproducibility.</p>	<p>LC-MS analysis was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Q Exactive high-resolution mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in positive ion mode over an m/z range of 70–1050. The spray voltage was set to 3500 V. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set to 50 and 13 arbitrary units, respectively. The ion source heater temperature was maintained at 450°C. Stepped collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 V were used for MS/MS acquisition.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were putatively annotated based on accurate mass and MS/MS spectral information using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA). The acquired MS and MS/MS data were matched against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), METLIN, and an in-house metabolite database. Metabolite annotations were assigned based on spectral and mass matching results. Because authentic reference standards were not used for systematic confirmation of all detected metabolites, the reported metabolite identities should be considered putative annotations rather than definitive structural identifications.</p>

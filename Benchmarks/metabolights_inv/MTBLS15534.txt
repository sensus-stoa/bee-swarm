Study Title	Epimedium-Derived Extracellular Vesicles Alleviate Cisplatin-induced Acute Kidney Injury: Integrated Multi-Omics Analysis and Molecular Simulations Reveal Flavonoid Interactions with PPARα
Study Description	This study explores the protective effects and potential underlying mechanisms of Epimedium-derived extracellular vesicles (EpEVs) against cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury (CIAKI) in mice. EpEVs were isolated and purified via differential centrifugation combined with sucrose and iodixanol gradients, characterized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) showing bilayer membranes and spherical morphology, with particle sizes ranging from 50 to 210 nm and a diameter of 141.5 nm, and analyzed via metabolomics-lipidomics revealing flavonoid dominance (e.g., 2''-O-rhamnosylicariin II, baohuoside VI, sagittatoside A and isoicaritin) and enriched glycerophospholipids. In vivo fluorescence imaging demonstrated tail vein-administered EpEVs accumulated in renal tissues, peaking at 12 hours post-injection. In vivo functional animal experiments verified that EpEVs treatment markedly reduced the levels of creatinine(cr), urea nitrogen(BUN), as well as renal injury biomarkers KIM-1 and NGAL; alleviated oxidative stress by lowering malondialdehyde (MDA) while elevating superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione (GSH); and suppressed inflammation response by downregulating mRNA levels of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and TGF-β. Transcriptome sequencing indicated that the PPAR signaling pathway mediates the renal protective effect of EpEVs. To clarify the molecular interaction between EpEV-derived characteristic metabolites and PPARα, molecular docking was performed on the top ten abundant metabolites identified by multi-omics profiling. All candidates exhibited docking binding free energies lower than 5 kcal/mol, indicating strong binding capacity to PPARα. Four dominant flavonoids (2''-O-rhamnosylicariside II, baohuoside VI, sagittatoside A and isoicaritin) were selected for subsequent 100 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Comprehensive trajectory analyses including root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), radius of gyration (Rg), solvent-accessible surface area (SASA), root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF), hydrogen bond occupancy statistics, and free energy landscape (FEL) verified stable conformation of all four PPARα-flavonoid complexes with persistent intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Furthermore, MM/PBSA calculations quantified the binding free energies of four complexes as 44.13, 27.41, 24.44 and 19.48 kcal/mol for 2''-O-rhamnosylicariside II, baohuoside VI, sagittatoside A and isoicaritin respectively, which confirmed that 2''-O-rhamnosylicariside II possessed the strongest binding affinity toward PPARα. Residue decomposition energy analysis further identified key amino acid residues responsible for ligand binding in each complex, consistent with the interacting residues observed in molecular docking visualization. These in silico analyses collectively validated that the flavonoid cargos of EpEVs can stably occupy the ligand pocket of PPARα via diverse intermolecular forces, providing sufficient molecular evidence for the regulatory effect of EpEVs on PPAR signaling. Integrating in vivo functional assays, multi-omics data and computational simulation results, we conclude that flavonoid cargos encapsulated in EpEVs modulate the PPAR signaling axis to mitigate cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury. This study provides an experimental basis for the development of adjuvant agents to alleviate cisplatin-associated nephrotoxicity in clinical chemotherapy.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Kidney tissues were harvested from 8-10-week-old male C57BL/6 mice including control, cisplatin and EpEVs-treated groups at day 4 after model establishment. Tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen after removing capsule and adipose tissues, stored at 80 degrees C without repeated freeze-thaw, and shipped on dry ice for untargeted metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class Plus ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm, 1.8 micro m, Waters, USA). The column temperature was maintained at 45 degrees C. Mobile phase A consisted of water with 0.1 % formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min and the injection volume was 4 micro L. The gradient elution program was set as follows: 0-2 min 5 % B; 2-4 min 5 %-30 % B; 4-8 min 30 %-90 % B; 8-10.2 min 90 %-80 % B; 10.2-14.2 min 80 %-100 % B; 14.2-15.2 min 100 % B; 15.2-15.3 min 100 %-5 % B; 15.3-16.2 min 5 % B for column re-equilibration.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data files were processed using Progenesis QI v2.3 software (Nonlinear Dynamics, Newcastle, UK). The processing pipeline included baseline filtering, peak picking, peak integration, retention-time correction, peak alignment, and data normalization. Compound annotation was performed against HMDB, METLIN, LipidMaps, KEGG and PubChem databases based on exact mass-to-charge ratio, fragment spectra and isotopic patterns. Feature matrices from positive- and negative-ion modes were merged for subsequent statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Mouse kidney tissue samples were weighed, homogenized in methanol-water solvent containing L-2-chlorophenylalanine as internal standard. Metabolites were extracted by sonication in ice-water bath, incubated at low temperature for protein precipitation, then centrifuged at 4 degrees C at high speed to collect supernatant. The supernatant was vacuum-dried, reconstituted with methanol-water solvent, re-sonicated and centrifuged, filtered through 0.22 micro m filter prior to LC-MS measurement. Pooled quality-control (QC) sample was generated by mixing equal aliquots from all biological samples, and solvent blank control was also included to monitor pre-processing and instrumental performance.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric acquisition was performed on a Thermo Q Exactive HF high-resolution mass spectrometer fitted with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. Both positive-ion and negative-ion electrospray modes were applied. Full-scan resolution was set to 60 000, MS/MS resolution was 15 000. The spray voltage was 3800 V for positive mode and 3000 V for negative mode. Capillary temperature was kept at 320 degrees C, sheath gas flow 35 arbitrary units, auxiliary gas flow 8 arbitrary units. The m/z scanning range was 100-1500.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and annotation were performed using Progenesis QI v2.3 (Nonlinear Dynamics, Newcastle, UK). Experimental exact m/z values, MS/MS fragment spectra and isotopic distribution patterns from both positive- and negative-ion modes were matched against public databases including HMDB, METLIN, LipidMaps and PubChem. Annotation confidence was classified according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) metabolite identification confidence levels (Level 1-4). No in-house authentic reference standards were co-injected for compound validation. Low-confidence features with poor spectral matching scores were filtered to minimise false-positive annotations.</p>

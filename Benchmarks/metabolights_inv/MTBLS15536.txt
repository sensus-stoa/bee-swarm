Study Protocol Description	<p>Largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) were subjected to different dietary treatments, including a control diet (C), a high-fat diet (H), and a high-fat diet supplemented with Cetobacterium somerae LY-01 (HCS9). Intestinal contents were collected on June 16, 2025. For each biological replicate, intestinal contents collected from randomly selected fish were pooled to generate one sample. Samples were collected aseptically after dissection, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until untargeted LC-MS metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Vanquish Flex UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, the autosampler temperature was set at 8 °C, the flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 μL. For both positive and negative ion modes, mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The gradient elution program was as follows: 5% B at 0–1.0 min, increased to 95% B at 4.7 min, maintained at 95% B until 6.0 min, returned to 5% B at 6.1 min, and maintained at 5% B until 8.5 min.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data files in Thermo .raw format were imported into MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218) for data processing. The workflow included peak detection, peak alignment, filtering, gap filling of missing values, and normalization. Features not detected in more than 50% of quality-control (QC) samples were removed. Missing values were subsequently filled using the Gap Filling algorithm implemented in MS-DIAL, followed by data normalization to generate the processed feature intensity matrix for downstream analysis.</p>	<p>Approximately 20 mg of each intestinal content sample was placed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. A total of 300 μL of pre-cooled methanol containing 5 ppm L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard was added, together with two steel beads. Samples were vortexed for 30 s and homogenized at 55 Hz for 60 s twice, followed by sonication for 10 min. The extracts were incubated at −20 °C for 30 min and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane prior to LC-MS analysis. A pooled quality-control (QC) sample was prepared by combining 10–20 μL aliquots from each filtered sample and was used to evaluate analytical stability and data quality.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 high-resolution mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software (version 4.7). Data were acquired in negative ion mode using a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. The spray voltage was set to −3.0 kV, with sheath gas at 40 arb and auxiliary gas at 10 arb. The capillary temperature was 320 °C and the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 300 °C. Full-scan MS data were acquired over an m/z range of 70–1000 at a resolution of 60,000, with AGC target set to Standard and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) was performed by selecting the top four precursor ions for MS/MS fragmentation. The dynamic exclusion time was 4 s. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000 using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with a collision energy of 30%, an AGC target of Standard, and automatic maximum injection time.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218) based on the PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database (PSNGM), which integrates an in-house standard library with mzCloud, LIPID MAPS, HMDB, MoNA, NIST 2020 MS/MS, and AI-predicted MS/MS spectral libraries. The main identification parameters were set as follows: MS1 tolerance, 0.01; MS2 tolerance, 0.05; smoothing level, 3; minimum peak height, 10,000; minimum peak width, 5; mass slice width, 0.05; and identification score cutoff, 70. Metabolite identities were assigned based on accurate mass, retention behavior, and MS/MS spectral matching where available.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Adult female zebrafish (Danio rerio, AB strain) were exposed to 5,000 ng/L ciprofloxacin for 30 days, beginning at 120 days post-fertilization. An unexposed control group was maintained in parallel. At 150 days post-fertilization, intestinal tissues were collected for untargeted metabolomic analysis. Each biological replicate comprised pooled intestinal tissues from five fish. Three pooled biological replicates were prepared per group, giving six biological samples in total. Samples were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until processing.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 µL.</p><p>For positive-ion analysis, mobile phase A was water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. For negative-ion analysis, mobile phase A was aqueous 5 mM ammonium formate, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile.</p><p>The following gradient was used in both modes: 0–1 min, 10% B; 1–5 min, 10–98% B; 5–6.5 min, 98% B; 6.5–6.6 min, 98–10% B; and 6.6–8 min, 10% B.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry files were converted to mzXML format using MSConvert from ProteoWizard (version 3.0.8789). Data preprocessing was performed using the XCMS package (version 3.12.0) in R, including peak detection, peak filtering, and peak alignment.</p><p>The processing parameters were bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, and method = 'centWave'.</p><p>Quality-control (QC)-based support vector regression was applied to correct systematic variation. Features with a relative standard deviation (RSD) greater than 30% in the QC samples were excluded before downstream analysis.</p>	<p>Approximately 100 mg of homogenized intestinal tissue was transferred into a 2 mL microcentrifuge tube and extracted with 1 mL of tissue extraction buffer. Samples were processed twice using a high-throughput tissue grinder, followed by ultrasonication for 25 min at 25 °C. The extracts were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min. Supernatants were collected and filtered through a 0.22 µm membrane before LC-MS analysis.</p><p>A pooled quality-control (QC) sample was prepared by combining 20 µL aliquots of the individual sample extracts.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Q Exactive Focus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization source. Data were acquired separately in positive- and negative-ion modes. Spray voltages were +3.50 kV and −2.50 kV for positive- and negative-ion acquisition, respectively. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas settings were 40 and 10 arbitrary units, respectively. The capillary temperature was 325 °C.</p><p>Data were acquired in Full MS–ddMS2 mode. Full-scan MS1 spectra were collected over m/z 81–1000 at a resolving power of 70,000 FWHM. The top three precursor ions were selected for data-dependent MS/MS using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD). MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolving power of 17,500 FWHM. Dynamic exclusion was enabled to reduce repeated fragmentation of the same precursor ions.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using HMDB, MassBank, LIPID MAPS, mzCloud, KEGG, and the in-house reference-standard spectral library maintained by Panomix.</p><p>Candidate molecular formulas and metabolite annotations were assigned using precursor-ion mass-to-charge ratios (m/z), mass accuracy, and adduct information, followed by database matching with a mass tolerance of less than 30 ppm. For features with acquired MS/MS spectra, fragment-ion information was further compared with reference spectra in the databases to support metabolite annotation.</p>

Study Title	Bacillus licheniformis promotes the enrichment of Prevotella improving levels of nicotinamide in alleviating colitis
Study Description	<p>This study investigated the effects of Bacillus licheniformis supplementation on the gut metabolic profile of patients with mild-to-moderate active ulcerative colitis (UC). Patients received either mesalazine alone or mesalazine combined with Bacillus licheniformis. Fecal samples were collected and subjected to untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics analysis. Bacillus licheniformis intervention was associated with alterations in the gut microbiota and metabolite profiles, including enrichment of Prevotella and increased levels of nicotinamide (NAM). Multi-omics analysis indicated a close relationship between Prevotella abundance and NAM metabolism. Further experimental validation suggested that Bacillus licheniformis interacts with Prevotella through bacterial cross-feeding, contributing to increased NAM production and activation of the Wnt signaling pathway. These findings provide evidence for a potential Bacillus licheniformis–Prevotella–nicotinamide–Wnt axis involved in the alleviation of ulcerative colitis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from patients with mild-to-moderate active ulcerative colitis (UC). Patients were divided into two treatment groups: one group received mesalazine alone, while the other group received mesalazine in combination with Bacillus licheniformis. Participants were instructed to collect fecal samples after defecation. Fresh fecal samples were collected into cryogenic tubes, immediately stored at -80°C, and subsequently transported for metabolomics analysis. Samples were maintained under frozen conditions until further processing.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Germany) equipped with a Hypersil Gold C18 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C and the flow rate was 0.2 mL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water, and mobile phase B was methanol. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0 min, 98% A/2% B; 1.5 min, 98% A/2% B; 3 min, 15% A/85% B; 10 min, 0% A/100% B; 10.1 min, 98% A/2% B; 11 min, 98% A/2% B; and 12 min, 98% A/2% B. The injection volume was not specified in the provided experimental protocol.</p>	<p>Raw UHPLC-MS/MS data files were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard. The converted data were processed using XCMS for peak detection, peak extraction, peak alignment, and relative quantification across samples. Features detected in solvent blank samples were used to remove background ions. Data processing was performed under a Linux operating system (CentOS 6.6) using R and Python.</p>	<p>Approximately 100 mg of each fecal sample was homogenized under liquid nitrogen and transferred into an Eppendorf tube. A total of 500 μL of prechilled 80% methanol was added to the sample. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly, incubated on ice for 5 min, and centrifuged at 15,000 × g at 4°C for 20 min. An aliquot of the supernatant was diluted with LC-MS grade water to obtain a final methanol concentration of 53%. The diluted extract was subsequently centrifuged at 15,000 × g at 4°C for 20 min, and the resulting supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS analysis. Solvent blank samples containing 53% methanol in water were prepared and processed using the same procedure as the experimental samples to facilitate removal of background ions.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a high-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometer coupled to the UHPLC system and equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes over an m/z range of 100-1500. The spray voltage was set to 3.5 kV, the capillary temperature was 320°C, the sheath gas flow rate was 35, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10, the S-lens RF level was 60, and the auxiliary gas heater temperature was 350°C. MS/MS spectra were acquired using data-dependent acquisition (DDA).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed by matching detected features against the Novogene in-house high-quality MS/MS spectral database (NovoMetDB). Metabolite annotation was based on accurate mass, retention information, adduct ions, and MS/MS spectral information, using a mass tolerance of 10 ppm. Background ions detected in solvent blank samples were removed prior to final metabolite identification and relative quantification. Identified metabolites were further annotated using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), and LIPID MAPS databases.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS15673
Study Title	Helicobacter pylori infection promotes TMAO accumulation through Escherichia coli enrichment with gut–liver circadian disruption and hepatic metabolic injury
Study Description	Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection is associated with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, but the microbial changes and circadian disturbances accompanying distal hepatic injury remain unclear. In specific pathogen-free (SPF) mice exposed to dietary choline, H. pylori infection was accompanied by trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) accumulation, ileal barrier disruption, and liver injury. 16S rRNA gene sequencing identified H. pylori-associated intestinal Escherichia enrichment, further supported by increased fecal Escherichia coli (E. coli) 16S rRNA levels and higher E. coli viable counts in medium supplemented with H. pylori culture supernatant. In germ-free–derived gnotobiotic mice, the H. pylori–E. coli co-colonized group had the highest trimethylamine (TMA) and TMAO levels and the most severe gut–liver phenotype. Transcriptomic analyses further identified circadian-pathway alterations in both the ileum and liver. In the liver, high TMAO was associated with reduced rhythmic CLOCK:BMAL1 target-gene output, lower Dbp expression, higher Nfil3 expression, and reduced Ppara expression and PPARα abundance; these changes were not fully explained by a uniform phase shift. In synchronized HepG2 cells, TMAO reproduced the DBP–NFIL3 imbalance and PPARα reduction, whereas DBP overexpression partially restored PPARα and selected metabolic and injury-related readouts. Together, these findings identify intestinal E. coli enrichment as a key contributor to H. pylori-related TMAO accumulation and show that the resulting high-TMAO state involves altered gut–liver circadian regulation and hepatic metabolic injury.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Serum was collected at designated endpoints from SPF, antibiotic-treated microbiota-depleted, and germ-free-derived gnotobiotic mice. Cell-free supernatants were collected from three independent E. coli and three H. pylori–E. coli cultures.</p>	<p>SPF targeted samples were separated on an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC using HILIC and C18 methods. Other targeted serum used amide HILIC, whereas untargeted serum and bacterial supernatants used a Waters ACQUITY UPLC with an HSS T3 column.</p>	<p>Untargeted files were centroided with Thermo/Xcalibur peak picking and converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard 3.0.24054. Targeted signals were integrated and quantified from internal-standard calibration curves.</p>	<p>Targeted serum was extracted with methanol/acetonitrile/water at 2:2:1. Untargeted bacterial supernatants used methanol/acetonitrile at 1:1; gnotobiotic serum was protein-precipitated. Pooled QC samples were included in untargeted runs.</p>	<p>SPF targeted data were acquired by positive-ion MRM on an AB SCIEX 6500+ QTRAP. Other targeted serum used an Agilent triple quadrupole. Untargeted data were acquired in separate positive- and negative-ion modes on an Orbitrap Exploris 120.</p>	<p>Targeted analytes were identified by predefined MRM transitions and retention times against authentic standards. Untargeted features were annotated using accurate mass and retention time, with HCD MS/MS spectra used where available.</p>

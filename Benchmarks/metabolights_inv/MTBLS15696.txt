Study Identifier	MTBLS15696
Study Title	Untargeted LC–MS metabolomic profiling of fecal samples from azithromycin- and cefaclor-exposed C57BL/6J mice
Study Description	This study investigated metabolic alterations associated with antibiotic exposure and increased body-weight gain in male C57BL/6J mice. Animals were assigned to a control group, an azithromycin (AZM) exposure group, or a cefaclor (CEC) exposure group. Fecal samples collected after antibiotic treatment were analyzed using untargeted liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS). Metabolomic profiles were compared among the three groups to identify antibiotic-associated alterations in bile acid, lipid, amino acid, sphingolipid, and redox-related metabolism, and to explore potential metabolic mechanisms linking antibiotic exposure, gut microbial disturbance, and obesity-related phenotypes.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from male C57BL/6J mice assigned to control, azithromycin, or cefaclor exposure groups after the experimental treatment period. Samples were collected using the same procedure for all animals and retained for untargeted LC-MS metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with a Thermo Scientific Hypersil GOLD C18 column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.9 micrometre). The column temperature was 40 degrees C and the flow rate was 0.2 mL/min. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water and mobile phase B was methanol. The gradient was 98% A/2% B at 0-1.5 min, changed to 15% A/85% B at 3 min and 0% A/100% B at 10 min, followed by re-equilibration at 98% A/2% B from 10.1 to 12 min.</p>	<p>Mass-spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard. Peak detection, retention-time correction, alignment, and feature integration were performed with XCMS in R. The resulting feature-intensity matrix was subjected to quality-control assessment and normalization before univariate and multivariate statistical analyses.</p>	<p>Fecal samples were extracted with 53% methanol in water, and the resulting extracts were used for untargeted LC-MS analysis. A pooled quality-control sample was prepared by combining aliquots from the study samples, and a solvent blank was analyzed. No derivatization was performed.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer with electrospray ionization and a quadrupole-Orbitrap mass analyzer. Positive- and negative-ion data were acquired in separate runs over m/z 100-1500. The spray voltage was 3.5 kV, sheath gas pressure was 35 psi, auxiliary gas flow was 10 L/min, capillary temperature was 320 degrees C, auxiliary gas heater temperature was 350 degrees C, and S-lens RF level was 60. Data-dependent MS/MS scans were acquired.</p>	<p>Detected LC-MS features were annotated using accurate precursor m/z, retention time, isotope and adduct information, and available MS/MS spectra. Candidate metabolite identities were assigned by comparison with the reference spectral and compound databases used by the analysis provider, and the reported identification-confidence level was retained in the results. Features without a confident annotation were retained as unique m/z-retention-time features.</p>

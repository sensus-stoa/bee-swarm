Study Identifier	MTBLS15762
Study Title	JEV-induced gut dysbiosis disrupts astrocyte-mediated blood-brain barrier integrity via the kynurenine-MMP17-AQP4 axis
Study Description	This study investigated how Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV)-induced gut dysbiosis contributes to blood-brain barrier disruption and neuroinflammation. Metabolomic profiling was performed in a murine JEV infection model to identify altered metabolic pathways. Integrative metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses highlighted the tryptophan-kynurenine pathway and supported a mechanistic link between Kyn accumulation, MMP17 activation, AQP4 dysfunction, and blood-brain barrier impairment.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Serum samples were collected from C57 mice used in the Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) study. Samples were collected for LC-MS/MS-based metabolomic analysis. Detailed sampling time points, serum separation procedures, and storage conditions were not specified in the provided experimental information.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a SCIEX ExionLC AD ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm). Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, the flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 µL.</p><p>The gradient program was as follows: 0 min, A&nbsp;= 95:5; 2 min, 80:20; 5 min, 40:60; 6 min, 1:99; the composition was maintained at 1:99 until 7.5 min; at 7.6 min the mobile phase was returned to 95:5, and the run was completed at 10 min.</p>	<p>MRM raw data were processed using SCIEX Analyst 1.6.3 software. Chromatographic peak integration and quantitative analysis based on peak area were performed using MultiQuant software.</p><p>Data quality was evaluated using QC samples based on TIC overlap, correlation analysis, and internal-standard stability. Metabolites with more than 20% missing values were removed. Metabolites were further filtered according to their coefficients of variation in QC samples, with metabolites having QC CV values ≤15% retained.</p><p>Missing values were imputed using the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) method implemented in the R impute package version 1.56.0 with default parameters. Data were subjected to unit-variance (UV) scaling prior to multivariate analysis. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using the R prcomp function. OPLS-DA was performed using MetaboAnalystR version 1.0.1 after log2 transformation and centering, with 200 permutation tests used for model validation.</p><p>For two-group comparisons, differential metabolites were identified using VIP &gt;1 and Student's t-test P&lt;0.05. For comparisons involving multiple groups, VIP &gt;1 and ANOVA P&lt;0.05 were used.</p>	<p>Sample extraction and preparation procedures prior to LC-MS/MS analysis were not specified in the provided experimental information. Quality-control (QC) samples were included for data-quality assessment. QC performance was evaluated based on total ion chromatogram (TIC) overlap, sample correlation, and internal-standard stability, with a coefficient of variation (CV) of ≤15% used as a quality criterion. Detailed preparation procedures for pooled QC samples, standards, and solvent blanks were not provided.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a SCIEX QTRAP LC-MS/MS system equipped with a triple-quadrupole linear ion-trap (QQQ-LIT) mass analyzer and a Turbo IonSpray electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM). MRM transitions were monitored according to the retention-time windows of individual metabolites.</p><p>The source temperature was set to 500 °C. The ion-spray voltage was +5500 V in positive-ion mode and −4500 V in negative-ion mode. Ion source gas 1 (GS1) was set to 55 psi, ion source gas 2 (GS2) to 60 psi, and curtain gas (CUR) to 25 psi. Collision-activated dissociation gas (CAD) was set to High. Instrument tuning and calibration were performed using 10 and 100 µmol/L polypropylene glycol solutions for QQQ and LIT modes, respectively. Data acquisition was controlled using SCIEX Analyst 1.6.3 software.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified and quantified using the in-house MWDB (Metware Database) widely targeted metabolomics library. Metabolite identification was based on multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) precursor-to-product ion transitions together with chromatographic retention-time matching.</p><p>Authentic reference standards used for metabolite identification were obtained from BioBioPha and Sigma-Aldrich. The analytical workflow followed the widely targeted metabolomics strategy described by Chen et al. (Molecular Plant, 2013).</p><p>Identified differential metabolites were subsequently annotated using the KEGG Compound database and mapped to KEGG Pathway. Pathway enrichment analysis and metabolite set enrichment analysis (MSEA) were subsequently performed.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS15815
Study Title	Untargeted LC–HRMS Intracellular Metabolomic Fingerprinting Reveals Isolate-Specific Chemical Biodiversity in Sugarcane-Derived Endophytic Bacteria
Study Description	<p>Endophytic bacteria are underexplored reservoirs of molecular diversity. We compared the intracellular metabolomic fingerprints of three endophytic bacterial isolates from sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum), assigned to Streptomyces sp., Pantoea sp., and Paracoccus sp. Extracts from three independent biological replicates per isolate were analyzed by HILIC–UHPLC–HRMS in positive and negative electrospray ionization modes using MSE acquisition. After quality-control filtering, 5,110 features were retained in ESI+ and 6,563 in ESI−. Principal component analysis separated the three isolates, with the first two components explaining 81.5% and 79.8% of the variance, respectively. One-way ANOVA with false discovery rate correction identified 1,663 differential features in ESI+ and 1,097 in ESI− at an adjusted p-value 0.01. Annotation using MSE fragmentation data and manual curation retained 106 differential features assigned to 96 distinct putative annotation names, encompassing nucleotides, amino acids, peptides, lipids, carbohydrates, polyamines, and cofactors. These findings describe chemical differences among the three isolates under the tested culture conditions and identify candidate metabolites for structural confirmation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Endophytic bacteria were isolated from healthy sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) leaves collected in the metropolitan region of Campinas, São Paulo State, Brazil. Leaves were thoroughly washed under running water and surface sterilized by immersion in 3% sodium hypochlorite for 1 min, followed by 70% ethanol for 1 min and two rinses with sterile distilled water for 3 min each. After surface sterilization, leaf fragments were aseptically cut and placed onto mineral solid medium supplemented with atrazine at 30 mg L-1 (99.1% purity; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), adjusted to pH 6.9, and incubated at 28 °C</p>	<p>ACQUITY UPLC system with chromatographic separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 µm). The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C and the autosampler temperature at 4 °C.</p><p>Mobile phase A consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid, while mobile phase B consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL min-1. The gradient was programmed as follows: 1% B from 0.0 to 0.1 min; increased to 70% B from 0.1 to 7.0 min; maintained at 70% B until 8.0 min; returned to 1% B from 8.0 to 8.1 min; and maintained at 1% B until 11.0 min for column reequilibration. Injection volumes were 1 µL in ESI+ and 2 µL in ESI−.</p>	<p>Raw UHPLC–HRMS data were processed separately for ESI+ and ESI− using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA) for retention-time alignment, peak detection, deconvolution, adduct grouping, and feature table generation. The following adducts were considered in positive ionization mode: M+H, M+Na, M+K, and M+H−H2O. In negative ionization mode, M−H, M+Cl, M−H2O−H, and M+FA−H were considered.</p><p>Feature filtering was performed separately for each ionization mode. Only features with coefficients of variation 15% across pooled-QC injections were retained for downstream analysis. Features showing substantial contribution from solvent, extraction, or culture medium blanks were excluded.</p>	<p>Cell pellets were washed three times with ice-cold sterile 0.9% NaCl solution to reduce carryover from the culture medium. Intracellular metabolites were extracted using ice-cold methanol:water (80:20, v/v; 1 mL per pellet), following established principles for microbial intracellular metabolomics. Glass beads were added at a ratio of 1 g mL-1 of extraction solvent, and cells were disrupted by bead-beating/vortexing for three cycles of 60 s, with cooling on ice between cycles. Lysates were centrifuged at 16,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and supernatants were collected. Extracts were dried under a gentle nitrogen stream and reconstituted in 200 µL acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v) before UHPLC–HRMS analysis.</p>	<p>A Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source and operated in separate positive and negative ionization sequences. Data were acquired using MassLynx 4.2 software (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, USA). The mass spectrometer was operated in MSE data-independent acquisition mode over an m/z range of 50–1200 with a scan time of 0.5 s. Low collision energy acquisition was performed at 6 eV, whereas high energy acquisition used a collision-energy ramp from 20 to 50 eV.</p><p>Leucine enkephalin was used as the lock-mass reference at 200 µg/mL, infused through the LockSpray source at 20 µL min-1 and sampled every 15 s. Sodium formate was used for mass calibration. Source parameters were as follows: source temperature, 140 °C; desolvation temperature, 550 °C; desolvation gas flow, 900 L h-1; capillary voltage, 3.0 kV in ESI+ and 2.0 kV in ESI−; sampling cone voltage, 40 V; and cone gas flow, 10 L h-1 in ESI+ and 50 L h-1 in ESI−.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was based on accurate precursor mass, isotope pattern, plausible adduct assignment, retention behavior when informative, and MSE fragmentation data. Candidate annotations were screened using an absolute mass error ≤5 ppm and isotope similarity ≥80%. When suitable fragmentation data were available, an absolute fragment-ion mass error ≤10 ppm was applied. Searches used MoNA, HMDB, LIPID MAPS, and in-house libraries. Library files were converted to a Progenesis-compatible format using SDF2PQI v1.0 (https://github.com/pedrohgodoys/sdf_to_pqi).</p><p>Structural annotation was based exclusively on analytical evidence. Biological plausibility was considered only during downstream manual curation and interpretation and was not used as evidence supporting molecular identification. Annotation confidence was assessed individually using Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI)</p><p><br></p>

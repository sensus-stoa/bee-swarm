Study Identifier	MTBLS15823
Study Title	Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis elucidates melatonin-mediated cold and drought tolerance during grapevine cane overwintering
Study Description	<p>Cold and drought stress limits grapevine cane overwintering. Melatonin is known to enhance plant stress tolerance, but its regulatory mechanisms under natural overwintering remain unclear. We used the new grapevine line ‘140902’ to investigate molecular responses to exogenous melatonin under combined cold and drought stress via integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. Melatonin increased endogenous hormones and antioxidant enzyme activities (e.g., SOD activity increased 23.6% under M3) and reduced MDA content by up to 31.9%, alleviating physiological damage. DEGs and DAMs were enriched in phenylpropanoid and flavonoid biosynthesis, hormone signaling, and starch and sucrose metabolism pathways. Melatonin upregulated JA and ABA signaling genes (GH3, JAZ, PYL), phenylpropanoid and flavonoid biosynthetic genes (C4H, 4CL, CAD, FLS), and carbon metabolism genes (β-glucosidase, cellulase), altering flavonoid (naringenin, eriodictyol) relative abundance. These coordinated changes may contribute to the melatonin mediated alleviation of cold and drought injury in grapevines, offering insights into potential regulatory networks and a basis for non-burial cultivation in cold, arid northwestern China.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>One-year-old cane samples of grape hybrid ‘140902’ were harvested at different time points under cold-drought stress with or without melatonin treatment. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen after collection and stored at −80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters). Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water; mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. Gradient: 0–2 min, 5% B; 2–10 min, 5–95% B; 10–12 min, 95% B; 12–12.1 min, 95–5% B; 12.1–15 min, 5% B. Flow rate: 0.4 mL/min; column temperature: 40 °C; injection volume: 5 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software for peak picking, alignment, retention time correction and feature intensity normalization. The features with RSD&gt;30% in QC samples were filtered out. Peak intensities were normalized to the total ion intensity of each sample. Missing values were imputed using the minimum value of the corresponding feature.</p>	<p>Cane tissues were ground into fine powder under liquid nitrogen. Approximately 100 mg powder was weighed, and metabolites were extracted using pre-cooled methanol/water solution. After vortexing, sonication and centrifugation, the supernatant was collected. Pooled QC samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of all sample supernatants for monitoring instrument stability.</p>	<p>MS detection was performed on a quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer with ESI source. Data were acquired in positive and negative ionization modes. Capillary voltage: 3.0 kV (ESI+), 2.0 kV (ESI−). Source temperature:120 °C; desolvation gas temperature:500 °C; desolvation gas flow:800 L/h. Full scan range m/z 50–1200 Da. Lock mass was used for real-time mass calibration.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed by matching accurate mass, MS/MS fragment spectra and retention time against the HMDB, METLIN and MassBank databases. Level 2 metabolite identification was assigned according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) classification.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was performed using an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-QTOF-MS). Samples were separated on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters). Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water; mobile phase B: 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. Gradient elution program: 0–2 min, 5% B; 2–10 min, 5–95% B; 10–12 min, 95% B; 12–12.1 min, 95–5% B; 12.1–15 min, 5% B. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, column temperature was 40 °C, and injection volume was 5 μL.</p><p>Mass spectrometry acquisition was operated under both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI−) electrospray ionization modes. The capillary voltage was set at 3.0 kV (pos) and 2.0 kV (neg). Source temperature: 120 °C; desolvation gas temperature: 500 °C; desolvation gas flow: 800 L/h. MS data were acquired in full scan mode with m/z range 50–1200 Da. Lock mass calibration was applied for real-time mass correction. QC samples were injected every 10 samples to monitor system stability and repeatability.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cecal content samples were collected from Gallus gallus (chicken). The samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen after collection and stored at -80°C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm x 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm, Waters). The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (aqueous 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 5 mmol/L acetic acid) and solvent B (acetonitrile), delivered in gradient mode as follows: 0-1 min, 1% B; 1-9 min, 1%-99% B; 9-9.1 min, 99%-1% B; 9.1-12 min, 1% B. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min, the injection volume was 2 µL, and the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using the XCMS software for peak extraction, alignment, and quality control.</p>	<p>Samples (20 mg ± 2 mg) were homogenized and extracted using 720 μL of Methanol/Acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). Protein precipitation was performed at -20°C for 1 h, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant was collected and concentrated at 4°C. Samples were reconstituted based on protein quantification results (determined by BCA assay using a 20 μL aliquot) to normalize for protein content. Quality Control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The ion source parameters were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate, 50 Arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 15 Arb; ion spray voltage, +3.8 kV for positive mode and -3.4 kV for negative mode; capillary temperature, 350 °C. Full scan MS spectra were acquired in the mass range of m/z 70–1050 at a resolution of 70,000 (at m/z 200). Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode was used to trigger MS/MS scans (Top 5) at a resolution of 17,500 with an AGC target of 3E6 and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The dynamic exclusion time was set to 6 s.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using the metaX software. Identified metabolites were annotated against common functional databases, including KEGG, HMDB, and LipidMaps. Subsequent analyses included metabolite quantification, sample correlation analysis, and differential analysis. For screened differential metabolites, functional enrichment analysis (KEGG, GSEA), interaction network analysis, and metabolite correlation analysis were conducted to interpret biological functions.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS1584
Study Title	Plasma Lipid Profile Reveals Plasmalogens as Potential Biomarkers for Colon Cancer Screening
Study Description	<p>In this era of precision medicine, there is an increasingly urgent need for highly sensitive tests for detecting tumors such as colon cancer (CC), a silent disease where the first symptoms may take 10-15 yr to appear. Mass spectrometry-based lipidomics is an emerging tool for such clinical diagnosis. We used ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled to electrospray ionization quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry operating in high energy collision spectral acquisition mode (MSE) mode (UPLC-QTOF-MSE) and gas chromatography (GC) to investigate differences between the plasmatic lipidic composition of CC patients and control (CTR) subjects. Key enzymes in lipidic metabolism were investigated using immuno-based detection assays. Our partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) resulted in a suitable discrimination between CTR and CC plasma samples. 42 statistically significant discriminating lipids were putatively identified. Ether lipids showed a prominent presence and accordingly, a decrease in glyceronephosphate O-acyltransferase (GNPAT) enzyme activity was found. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve built for three plasmalogens of phosphatidylserine (PS), named PS(P-36:1), PS(P-38:3) and PS(P-40:5), presented an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.998, and sensitivity and specificity of 100 and 85.7% respectively. These results show significant differences in CC patients' plasma lipid composition that may be useful in discriminating them from CTR individuals with a special role for plasmalogens.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Plasma samples (0.8 mL) were extracted with <strong>2.5 ml of chloroform:methanol (2:1)</strong> and 0.5 mL of an aqueous solution of NaCl (0.1 M) as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. The lower organic layer was collected and separated into 2 fractions that were dried under nitrogen flow and stored at -80 °C for analysis in a period no longer than 6 mth. For LC-MS analysis, dried lipid samples were reconstituted in 1 mL of <strong>isopropanol/acetonitrile/water (2:1:1, v/v/v)</strong> solution.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Folch J., LEES M., Stanley G.H.S. A simple method for the isolation and purification of total lipides from animal tissues.&nbsp;J. Biol. Chem.&nbsp;1957;226:497–509.&nbsp;PMID:&nbsp;13428781.</p>	<p>Data were acquired using a <strong>Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class System (SM-FTN)</strong> liquid chromatography instrument, equipped with an <strong>ACQUITY UPLC CSH C18 (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) </strong>column. The mobile phase consisted of 10 mM of ammonium formate with 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile/water (60:40, v/v) (A) and 10 mM ammonium formate with 0.1% formic acid in isopropanol/acetonitrile (90:10, v/v) (B) at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min with a linear gradient (in % B): 0 - 0.5 min: 40 - 43%; 0.5 - 0.6 min: 63%; 0.6 - 4 min: 68%; 4 - 4.1 min: 70%; 4.1 - 6.5 min: 99%; 6.5 min: decrease to 40%; 6.5 - 8.1 min: 40% (with a further 1.9 min for column re-equilibration) resulting in a 10 min analysis. The injection volume was 0.1 µL. <strong>Pooled samples </strong>were injected every 20 injections.</p>	<p>Analysis were performed using a <strong>Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof</strong> mass spectrometer (Waters, Milford, MA, USA) using the MassLynx 4.1 software. For the electrospray ionization source, the parameters were set as follows: capillary voltage of 2 kV, sampling cone of 30 V, source temperature of 130 °C, desolvation temperature of 450 °C, cone gas flow of 100 L/h, desolvation gas flow of 800 L/h for <strong>positive mode</strong>; capillary voltage of 1.0 V, sampling cone of 40 V, source temperature of 130 °C, desolvation temperature of 450 °C, cone gas flow of 50 L/h, desolvation gas flow of 800 L/h for <strong>negative mode</strong>. The acquisition scan range was from <strong>50 to 2000 Da</strong> and the data were acquired using high energy mass spectrum (<strong>MSe</strong>) approach. Leucine encephalin (molecular weight = 555.62; 200 pg/μL, in 1:1 ACN:H2O) was used as a lock mass for accurate mass measurements, and a 0.5 mM sodium formate solution was used for instrument calibration.</p>	<p>Data were normalized by sum and Pareto scaled before performing statistics.</p>	<p>Liquid chromatographic mass spectrometry raw data was processed with <strong>Progenesis QI software (Waters)</strong> for peak detection, alignment, integration, deconvolution, data filtering, ion annotation and MSE based putative identification of compounds. The <a href=' http://www.lipidmaps.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Lipid Maps</strong></a> database was used for this identification with the following search parameters: precursor mass error ≤ 5 ppm, fragment tolerance ≤ 10 ppm. Fragmentation score, mass accuracy, isotope similarity and the Human Metabolome Database (<a href=' http://www.hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>HMDB</strong></a>) matching were considered for the identification of the molecules. Statistical, biomarker and pathway analyses were performed using the<a href='https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'> <strong>MetaboAnalyst 4.0</strong></a><strong> </strong>web platform.</p>	<p>This cross-sectional study included <strong>50 healthy volunteers</strong> from the<strong> control (CRT)</strong> group and <strong>50 colon cancer (CC)</strong> patients from the São Francisco University Hospital (HUSF).&nbsp;The study was approved by the ethics committee of the São Francisco University, CAAE 57114716.8.1001.5514. Venous blood was collected from fasted individuals from 12 h using a potassium ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) tube. After collection, the blood was centrifuged for 10 min at 2500 x g at 20 °C and the plasma was fractionated into 100 μL aliquots in micro centrifuge tubes and immediately stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p>

Study Title	Transcriptomic and Metabolomic Study of *Fp* Induced by Bacterial Strain 2b14
Study Description	<p>The main experimental workflow consists of sample collection, metabolite extraction, quality control sample preparation, liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry measurement, and downstream bioinformatic analysis. Samples are preprocessed for metabolite extraction, and pooled QC samples are generated to assess instrument stability and assay reproducibility. All experimental samples and pooled QC samples are analyzed using liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry to acquire raw mass spectrometry data. The raw MS files undergo format conversion, peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction. Subsequent data preprocessing includes metabolite structural annotation, missing value imputation, and data normalization. The overall dataset quality is evaluated based on signals from QC samples; outlier samples and low-quality feature peaks are removed. Systematic data analysis involves univariate statistics, multivariate statistics, differential metabolite screening, metabolite correlation analysis, machine learning modeling, curve analysis, and pathway enrichment analysis. These analyses aim to resolve metabolic phenotypic differences among samples and uncover the underlying biological regulatory mechanisms.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>For transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses of the bacterial strain, mycelial samples of the control and treatment groups were collected at the representative inhibitory stage. All samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C. Three biological replicates were included for each experimental group to ensure the accuracy of subsequent analyses.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='547'>An ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used. The flow rate was set at 0.4 mL/min, column temperature at 40 °C, autosampler temperature at 8 °C, and injection volume at 2 μL. The positive ion mode mobile phase system was adopted. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile.</p>	<p>Raw files were imported into dedicated software for peak picking, alignment, filtering and metabolite annotation. Compounds with more than 80 % missing values in QC samples and compounds with over 50 % missing values across all samples were removed. Missing peak intensities were imputed using the neighbor algorithm, followed by data normalization.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='448'>Weigh 30 mg of sample into a 2 mL centrifuge tube; add 1 mL of pre-cooled 50% methanol and 2 glass beads, then vortex for 30 s. Immerse the tube in liquid nitrogen for rapid freezing for 5 min. Take out the centrifuge tube, thaw at room temperature, and place it in a high-throughput tissue grinder at 55 Hz for 60 s. Repeat the freezing-thawing and grinding steps twice. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 15 min, transfer 850 μL of the supernatant and concentrate it under vacuum to complete dryness. Add 150 μL of 50% methanol (containing 5 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine), and vortex for 30 s. Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min, collect the supernatant and filter through a 0.22 μm membrane. Transfer the filtrate to an autosampler vial. Mix 10–20 μL filtrate from each sample to prepare one quality control (QC) sample. QC sample is not prepared when the total number of samples is ≤5. QC samples are used to evaluate instrument stability and data reliability.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='623'>Mass spectrometry data in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode under both positive and negative ion modes were acquired using a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer controlled by Xcalibur software (version 4.7, Thermo). A heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source was employed. The spray voltage was set at 3.5 kV for positive mode and −3.0 kV for negative mode. The sheath gas was 40 arb, auxiliary gas 10 arb, capillary temperature 320 °C, and auxiliary gas heater temperature 300 °C. The full scan resolution was 60,000 with a scan range of 70–1000 m/z, AGC Target set to Standard, and maximum injection time (Max IT) of 100 ms. The top 4 most intense ions were selected for tandem MS fragmentation. The dynamic exclusion duration was 4 s. The MS/MS resolution was 15,000, with HCD collision energy of 30%, AGC Target as Standard, and Max IT set to Auto.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='774'>Metabolite identification was performed against the PerSonalbio Next-Generation Metabolomics Database (PSNGM, Personalbio), which integrates an in-house library built from authentic standards, mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/), LIPID MAPS (https://www.lipidmaps.org/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/), MoNA (https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/), NIST_2020_MSMS, and an AI-predicted MS/MS spectral library. The main parameters for library searching were set as follows: MS1 tolerance for identification = 0.01, MS2 tolerance for identification = 0.05, Smoothing level = 3, Minimum peak height = 10000, Minimum peak width = 5, Mass slice width = 0.05, and Identification score cut-off = 70.</p>

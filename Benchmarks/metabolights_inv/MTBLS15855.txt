Study Title	Integrated Metabolomics and Amplicon Sequencing Reveal Microbial Succession and Metabolite Dynamics during Sojae Semen Praeparatum Fermentation
Study Description	Sojae Semen Praeparatum (SSP) is a traditional fermented soybean product with both dietary and medicinal uses in China. However, the dynamic metabolic changes and microbial community succession during SSP fermentation remain insufficiently characterized. In this study, untargeted metabolomics based on UHPLC-HRMS was integrated with 16S rRNA gene and ITS amplicon sequencing to investigate metabolite remodeling, bacterial and fungal community succession, and their potential associations during SSP fermentation. Clear stage-dependent separation of metabolite profiles was observed, and 22,345 metabolic features were detected, of which 4,281 were putatively annotated. Differential metabolites were mainly assigned to amino acids, dipeptides, lipids, carbohydrates, and isoflavones. Sucrose and other carbohydrates were progressively consumed during fermentation, while N-acetyl amino acids, short dipeptides, proline betaine and a range of lipid metabolites built up towards the later stages. The microbiota did not drift at random: amplicon sequencing resolved a clear successional sequence in both kingdoms. Bacteria converged on genera within the Enterobacteriaceae — mainly Enterobacter, Kosakonia and Citrobacter — whereas the fungi started out dominated by Rhizopus and ended in a co-dominant configuration of Aspergillus, Mucor and several minor taxa. When the two data sets were linked, the dominant taxa associated with metabolites assigned to nitrogen, lipid and phenolic pathways. Procrustes analysis suggested a potential association between the fungal community and the metabolome, whereas the bacterial association was marginal; however, the difference in coupling strength between the two was not directly tested. These results provide preliminary evidence and a foundation for quality control and process optimization, but require validation across independent batches.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fermented black soybean (Sojae Semen Praeparatum, SSP) samples were prepared following the Chinese Pharmacopoeia protocol. Metabolite samples were collected at three key fermentation stages: unfermented steamed beans (0 d, YD0), pre-fermentation endpoint with yellow mycelia (5 d, YD1), and post-fermentation endpoint (20 d, YD2). Three independent parallel fermentation tanks were used at each time point, providing three tank-level biological replicates (n = 3). All samples were immediately frozen and stored at −80 °C prior to metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Metabolite separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class system equipped with a Waters BEH C18 column (1.7 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm). Two mobile-phase systems were used for positive and negative ionization modes. The elution gradient was 0–1 min 2% B, 1–9 min 2%–98% B, 9–12 min 98% B, 12–12.1 min 98%–2% B, and 12.1–15 min 2% B. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min, column temperature 45 °C, and injection volume 5 μL.</p>	<p>Raw MS data (.raw) were imported directly into Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for peak extraction and alignment. The exported results were then processed in metaX for data preprocessing, which included: (1) Probabilistic Quotient Normalization (PQN) for relative peak areas; (2) QC-RLSC (quality control-based robust LOESS signal correction) for batch effect correction; and (3) removal of features with a relative standard deviation (RSD, equivalent to the coefficient of variation, CV) &gt; 30% in QC samples. The resulting peak area matrix was exported for subsequent multivariate statistical analysis in SIMCA 14.1.</p>	<p>Approximately 1 g of each SSP sample was weighed and extracted with 25 mL of 75% methanol via ultrasonic extraction for 30 min. The supernatant was centrifuged at 3500 r/min for 5 min and filtered through a 0.22 μm organic membrane to obtain the test solution. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal volumes of all sample extracts. One QC sample was inserted every five analytical runs to monitor instrument stability and signal drift.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was performed on a Q Exactive HF high-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometer with an ESI source. Full-scan MS and data-dependent MS/MS (DDA) acquisition were conducted in positive and negative ion modes with a scan range of m/z 70–1050. MS1 resolution was 120,000, and MS/MS fragmentation was performed on the top 3 abundant precursor ions with stepped NCE of 20, 40, and 60 eV. Ion spray voltages were 3.80 kV (positive) and 3.20 kV (negative).</p>	<p _msttexthash='2341944722' _msthash='1405'>Metabolite annotation was performed using Compound Discoverer 3.3, supported by the BGI HR-PMDB (BGI in-house metabolomics database), mzCloud, and ChemSpider. Annotation criteria: precursor mass error &lt; 5 ppm, retention time deviation &lt; 0.2 min, and MS/MS fragment mass error &lt; 10 ppm. Metabolite confidence levels were assigned using a customized classification framework from BGI (adapted from the MSI framework), categorized into Level 1 to Level 5 (Level 5 represents unclassified). No authentic reference standards were injected for confirmation in this study.</p>

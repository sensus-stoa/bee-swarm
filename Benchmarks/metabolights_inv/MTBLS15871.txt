Study Title	Spirulina Alleviates Functional Constipation by Remodeling Gut Microbiota and Fecal Metabolites: A Metagenomic and Metabolomic Study
Study Description	Abstract Objective: Functional constipation (FC) is a common gastrointestinal disorder that significantly affects quality of life. Increasing evidence suggests that gut microbiota and their derived metabolites play a critical role in FC, yet the therapeutic potential of Spirulina and its underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. Methods: Fecal samples were collected from 21 patients with FC before and after Spirulina treatment (42 samples in total) and from healthy controls (HC) recruited during the same period, with 63 participants included overall. Metagenomic sequencing and metabolomic profiling were performed on all fecal samples to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of Spirulina and to identify key gut microbiota and microbial-derived metabolites associated with FC. Multi-omics analyses, including integrative metagenomic–metabolomic correlation, were conducted to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of Spirulina on FC. Results: Patients with FC exhibited significantly altered gut microbial composition and reduced microbial diversity compared with HC. Spirulina treatment markedly improved clinical symptoms, accompanied by a notable increase in beneficial taxa such as Bacteroides uniformis and Phocaeicola dorei, and a decrease in potentially pathogenic species including Escherichia coli. Metabolomic profiling revealed that Spirulina treatment reshaped the fecal metabolite landscape, with a significant enrichment of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), particularly butyrate, and a reduction in pro-inflammatory metabolites. Integrated metagenomic and metabolomic analyses identified Bacteroides uniformis and its derived metabolite butyrate as key mediators associated with symptom improvement. Conclusion:Our findings suggest that Spirulina alleviates functional constipation by remodeling gut microbiota and fecal metabolites. These observations provide a preliminary basis for further investigating Spirulina as a therapeutic strategy for functional constipation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples (25 mg±1 mg) were taken, mixed with beads and 500 μL of extraction solution (MeOH:ACN:H2O, 2:2:1 (v/v)). The extraction solution contain deuterated internal standards. The mixed solution were vortexed for 30 s.</p>	<p>For polar metabolites, LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted of (A) water containing 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonium hydroxide (pH 9.75) and (B) acetonitrile. The auto-sampler temperature was 4 ℃, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed with an in-house program.</p>	<p>Then the mixed samples were homogenized (35 Hz,4 min) and sonicated for 5 min in 4 ℃ water bath, the step repeat for three times. The samples were incubated for 1 h at -40 ℃ to precipitate proteins. Then the samples ware centrifuged at 12000 rpm (RCF=13800(×g),R= 8.6cm) for 15 min at 4 ℃. The supernatant was transferred to a fresh glass vial for analysis. The quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing an equal aliquot of the supernatant of samples.</p>	<p>The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the full scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following:sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary</p><p>temperature 320 ℃, full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000, collision energy: SNCE 20/30/40, spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV (negative), respectively.</p>	<p>which was developed using R and based on XCMS, for feature detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. The R package and the BiotreeDB(V3.0) were applied in metabolite identification.</p>

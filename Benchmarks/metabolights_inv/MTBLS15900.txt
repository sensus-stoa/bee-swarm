Study Title	Medicarpin suppresses gastric cancer growth through modulation of mevalonate pathway-associated cholesterol biosynthesis
Study Description	Background: Gastric cancer (GC) is an aggressive malignancy characterized by metabolic reprogramming, including dysregulated cholesterol biosynthesis. Medicarpin (Med), a natural isoflavan isolated from Radix Hedysari, has shown pharmacological potential; however, its anti-GC activity and metabolic mechanisms remain unclear. Methods: The effects of Med on cell viability, proliferation, colony formation, and migration were evaluated in AGS and HGC-27 GC cells. Its antitumor efficacy and preliminary tolerability were assessed in nude-mouse xenograft models. Untargeted metabolomics and quantitative proteomics were integrated to identify Med-associated metabolic alterations and targets. Western blotting, cholesterol measurement, Filipin staining, and exogenous cholesterol rescue assays were performed to validate the mechanism. Results: Med significantly inhibited the viability, proliferation, clonogenicity, and migration of AGS and HGC-27 cells in a concentration- and time-dependent manner, while showing minimal cytotoxicity in normal gastric mucosal GES-1 cells. In xenograft models, Med reduced tumor volume and weight without body-weight loss or histopathological injury to major organs. Multi-omics analyses revealed extensive metabolic remodeling, with marked suppression of lipid, steroid, and primary bile acid biosynthesis. Proteomic analysis and Western blotting showed that Med downregulated the key mevalonate pathway enzymes MVK, HMGCS1, and LSS, thereby reducing intracellular cholesterol levels. Exogenous cholesterol supplementation substantially reversed Med-induced inhibition of GC cell viability, proliferation, and colony formation. Conclusion: Med suppresses GC progression by downregulating MVK, HMGCS1, and LSS, thereby inhibiting mevalonate pathway-mediated cholesterol biosynthesis. These findings support Med as a promising natural candidate for targeting lipid metabolic reprogramming in GC.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Logarithmic-phase human gastric cancer cell lines AGS and HGC-27, as well as normal gastric mucosal epithelial cells GES-1, were collected. Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 or DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. After treatment with Medicarpin (Med) (Treated group) or an equal volume of vehicle solvent (Control group), the medium was discarded, cells were washed with PBS, and cell pellets were collected and stored at -80°C until analysis. A total of 12 samples were analyzed (6 in the Control group and 6 in the Treated group).</p>	<p>Separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, UK). The column oven was maintained at 40°C, and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of water with 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 5 mmol/L acetic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–0.8 min, 2% B; 0.8–2.8 min, 2%–70% B; 2.8–5.6 min, 70%–90% B; 5.6–6.4 min, 90%–100% B; 6.4–8.0 min, 100% B; 8.0–8.1 min, 100%–2% B; 8.1–10 min, 2% B. The injection volume was 4 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data files were converted to mzXML format using MSConvert software from Proteowizard.</p>	<p>To the cell pellet, 1000 μL of pre-chilled methanol/acetonitrile/water (MeOH/ACN/H2O, 2:2:1, v/v/v) was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and sonicated at 4°C for 10 min. This was followed by flash-freezing in liquid nitrogen for 1 min and sonication at 4°C for 10 min, repeated three times. The samples were then incubated at -20°C for 1 h to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 13,000 rpm and 4°C for 15 min, 720 μL of supernatant was collected and vacuum-dried at 4°C. The dried extract was reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (ACN:H2O, 1:1, v/v), vortexed for 30 s, and sonicated at 4°C for 10 min. Following centrifugation at 13,000 rpm and 4°C for 15 min, the supernatant was transferred for UPLC-HRMS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from each sample, and blank controls were also prepared for quality assurance.</p>	<p>A Q-Exactive high-resolution mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source was used, with data acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. For full-scan MS, the scan range was 70–1050 m/z at a resolution of 70,000 (@ m/z 200), with an AGC target of 3e6 and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. For MS/MS, data-dependent acquisition (DDA) was employed at a resolution of 17,500 (@ m/z 200) with a maximum injection time of 50 ms. A QC sample was injected after every 10 samples to calibrate the systematic error of the entire batch based on mass differences among QC samples.</p>	<p>Peak extraction was performed using XCMS software, adduct ion annotation using CAMERA, and primary identification using metaX software. Metabolites were annotated by combining retention time (RT) and m/z data, matching against the online KEGG and HMDB databases as well as an in-house standard MS/MS spectral library. A metabolite was annotated if the mass difference between the observed and database values was less than 10 ppm. The MS/MS identification parameters were as follows: MS1 mass tolerance 0.01 Da, MS2 mass tolerance 0.05 Da, and identification score cutoff 75%. Databases used included in-house, Massbank, HMDB, and Lipidblast.</p>

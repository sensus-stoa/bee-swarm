Study Identifier	MTBLS15909
Study Title	Hepatic Lipid Alterations in Early and Advanced-stage MASH
Study Description	MASH is a metabolic disorder disease initially driven by imbalance between lipid acquisition and lipid disposal, yet the hepatic lipidomic landscape that underpins this imbalance remains incompletely defined. Increasing studies indicated that special lipotoxic species play an important role in the development and progression of MASH. Currently, evidence regarding the lipid classes associated with MASH pathogenesis is still limited and inconsistent. Our study utilized non-targeted lipidomic analysis to systematically analyze the hepatic lipid alterations in HFHC diet-induced early-stage MASH mice and MCD diet-induced advanced stage MASH mice. A total of 15 lipid classes were detected in both MASH models, including TAGs, DAGs, PCs, PEs, Cers, SMs, PIs, PSs, LPCs, PGs, PAs, DLCLs, LPEs, LPIs, and LPSs. Among these, hepatic TAGs, PCs, PEs, Cers, and SMs were highly abundant in HFHC diet-induced MASH mice, whereas hepatic TAGs, PCs, SMs, DAGs, and Cers were highly abundant in MCD diet-induced MASH mice. Notably, the majority of TAGs, DAGs, and Cers were significantly increased, while most PEs were significantly decreased in both models. In contrast, most PCs were significantly decreased in HFHC diet-induced MASH mice but displayed bidirectional changes in MCD diet-induced MASH mice, and SMs exhibited bidirectional changes in both models. Collectively, these findings reveal both shared and model-specific features of hepatic lipid remodeling in MASH, and provide important scientific evidence to guide the selection of appropriate high-fat diet MASH models for lipid metabolism research.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples comprised liver tissues from control and HFHC mice. Mouse strain, sex, age, housing conditions, and sampling time were kept consistent, with a high-fat, high-cholesterol, and high-fructose diet (HFHC) treatment as the main variables.</p>	<p>In this study, a Vanquish (Thermo Fisher Scientific) ultra-performance liquid chromatograph was employed, and the target compounds were separated using a Phenomenex Kinetex C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 2.6 μm). The mobile phase A consisted of a mixture of 40% water and 60% acetonitrile, containing 10 mmol/L ammonium formate; mobile phase B consisted of 10% acetonitrile and 90% isopropanol, containing 10 mmol/L ammonium formate aqueous solution. The default injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The original mass spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard software. Subsequently, XCMS was employed for retention time correction, peak identification, peak extraction, peak integration, and peak alignment, with minfrac set to 0.5 and cutoff set to 0.3. Lipid identification was performed using our laboratory's XCMS-based custom R package and the lipidblast 2022 database.</p>	<p>1. Weigh approximately 25 mg of sample onto dry ice and transfer it into an EP tube; add 200 μL of pure water; 2. Add steel beads, grind at 35 Hz for 4 min; perform ultrasonication in an ice-water bath for 5 min; repeat the above steps three times; 3. Add 480 μL of pre-cooled extraction solution (MTBE:MeOH = 5:1, v/v) maintained at 40 °C, which contains an isotopically labeled internal standard; vortex-mix for 30s, then ultrasonicate in an ice-water bath for 10 min, followed by standing at –40 °C for 1 h; 4. Centrifuge the sample at 4 °C, 3000 rpm, for 15 min; collect 300 μL of the supernatant into a 2.0 mL EP tube and perform vacuum drying; 5. Add 200 μL of pre-cooled reconstitution solution (DCM:MeOH = 1:1, v/v) to the dried metabolites; vortex-mix for 30s, then ultrasonicate in an ice-water bath for 10 min; 6. Centrifuge the sample at 4 °C, 12000 rpm, for 15 min; 7. Transfer 100 μL of the supernatant into an injection vial for analytical instrumentation analysis; 8. For all samples, collect an equal volume of supernatant and mix it to prepare QC samples for analytical instrumentation analysis.</p>	<p>The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer enables first-and second-stage mass spectrometry data acquisition under the control of the control software (Xcalibur, version 4.4, Thermo). The detailed parameters are as follows: Sheath gas flow rate: 30 Arb; Auxiliary gas flow rate: 10 Arb; Capillary temperature: 320 °C (positive) or 320 °C (negative); Full MS resolution: 60,000; MS/MS resolution: 15,000; Collision energy: 15/30/45 in NCE mode; Spray voltage: 3.8 kV (positive) or −3.4 kV (negative).</p>	<p>The original mass spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard software. Subsequently, XCMS was employed for retention time correction, peak identification, peak extraction, peak integration, and peak alignment, with minfrac set to 0.5 and cutoff set to 0.3. Lipid identification was performed using our laboratory's XCMS-based custom R package and the lipidblast 2022 database.</p>

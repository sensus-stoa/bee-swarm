Study Protocol Description	<p>Six contaminated soil samples were included in this study. The samples were divided into two experimental groups, A and B, with three samples in each group. The samples were subsequently subjected to untargeted LC-MS metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, USA). Mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40°C, and the injection volume was 3 µL. The gradient program was as follows: 0–1.5 min, 0–5% B; 1.5–2.0 min, 5–10% B; 2.0–4.5 min, 10–30% B; 4.5–5.0 min, 30–100% B; 5.0–6.3 min, 100% B; 6.3–6.4 min, 100–0% B; and 6.4–8.0 min, 0% B.</p>	<p>LC-MS raw data were processed using Progenesis QI v3.0 (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Raw data processing included baseline filtering, peak detection, peak integration, retention-time correction, and peak alignment. A data matrix containing retention time, m/z, and peak intensity was generated. The resulting data matrix was subsequently processed using the Majorbio Cloud Platform. Metabolic features detected in at least 80% of samples in any group were retained. Missing values were replaced with the minimum value in the original data matrix, and peak intensities were normalized using sum normalization. Features with a relative standard deviation (RSD) greater than 30% in QC samples were removed, followed by log10 transformation for downstream analysis.</p>	<p>Approximately 1000 ± 5 mg of each soil sample was transferred into a 2 mL centrifuge tube containing a 6 mm grinding bead. A total of 1000 µL extraction solvent (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v) containing four internal standards, including L-2-chlorophenylalanine at 0.02 mg/mL, was added. Samples were ground for 6 min at −10°C and 50 Hz, followed by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min at 5°C and 40 kHz. The samples were kept at −20°C for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant was collected and dried under nitrogen. The dried extract was reconstituted with 120 µL acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v), vortexed for 30 s, sonicated for 5 min at 5°C and 40 kHz, and centrifuged again at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant was transferred to autosampler vials for LC-MS analysis. For quality control, 20 µL of supernatant from each sample was pooled to prepare the QC samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired separately in positive and negative ionization modes over an m/z range of 70–1050. The sheath gas flow rate was 60 arb and the auxiliary gas flow rate was 20 arb. The heater temperature was 350°C and the capillary temperature was 320°C. Spray voltages were 3400 V in positive ion mode and −3000 V in negative ion mode. The S-Lens RF level was 60. Normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 were applied. The Full MS resolution was 60,000 and the MS/MS resolution was 15,000.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using Progenesis QI v3.0 by matching MS and MS/MS spectral information against metabolite databases. The MS mass tolerance was set to &lt;10 ppm, and metabolite identification was additionally evaluated using MS/MS spectral matching scores. The reference databases included the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), METLIN, NIST, MassBank, LipidBlast, GNPS, and the in-house Majorbio Database (MJDB).</p>

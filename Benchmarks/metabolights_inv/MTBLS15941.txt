Study Description	The metagenome and metabolome data revealed that thioproline treatment significantly altered the gut microbiota community composition and metabolism in CRC mice. These CRC-associated gut microbiota and bacterial metabolites returned to normal levels. Moreover, under thioproline treatment, the microbial–metabolite interactions in CRC mice shifted toward patterns more closely resembling those observed in healthy mice.

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 15 four-week-old C57BL/6 mice were obtained from Nanjing Medical University (SPF grade, SCXK 2016-0002). The mice were evenly divided into three groups: H group (healthy mice), CRC group (CRC mice without thioproline treatment) and T group (CRC mice with thioproline treatment), each with five replicates. After one week of acclimatization, the H group mice were intraperitoneally injected with saline, while the CRC group and T group mice were intraperitoneally injected with AOM (12.5mg/kg). One week later, DSS (2.5%, w/w) was administered cyclically in the drinking water to CRC and T group mice: 1 week of treatment followed by 2 weeks of withdrawal, repeated for three consecutive 3-week cycles. Starting from the tenth week, mice in T group were administered with thioproline (50 mg/kg) for two weeks. Throughout the entire experiment, the H group mice were fed with normal drinking water. At the end of the experiment, all mice wereeuthanized. Fecal and intestinal tissue samples were collected, and the intestinal tissues were analyzed by HE staining.</p>	<p>This study selected the HM350 compound library from Beijing Genomics institution (BGI). HM350 compound library enables targeted detection of over 350 metabolites across more than ten categories, including amino acids, fatty acids (including short-chain fatty acids), organic acids, bile acids, carbohydrates, carnitine, and indole. The compound library also contains 200 metabolites related to gut microbiota. Metabolites were characterized by LC-MS/MS. The chromatographic column used in this study was a BEH C18 column (2.1mm × 10cm, 1.7μm, waters). The mobile phase A was consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water and mobile phase B was composed by 70% acetonitrile and 30% isopropanol. Elution was carried out using the following gradient: 0-1.00 min, 5% solvent B; 1.00-5.00 min, 5% solvent B; 5.00-9.00 min, 70% solvent B; 9.00-11.00 min, 50% solvent B; 11.00-13.50 min, 22% solvent B; 13.50-14.00 min, 95% solvent B, at a flow rate of 0.400 ml/min; 14.00-16.00 min, 100% solvent B, at a flow rate of 0.600 ml/min; 16.00-18.00 min, 5% solvent B, at a flow rate of 0.400 ml/min. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>The raw data were automatically identified and integrated for each MRM transition (ion pair) using default parameters in the Skyline software (MacCoss Lab Software), and were further validated through manual inspection. Accurate metabolite concentrations are determined using isotope internal standards. The content of each substance in HM350 (umol/g) is calculated using the formula C*A/m, where C represents the concentration value (μmol/L) obtained by inputting the integrated peak area of the target analyte from the sample into the standard curve; A is the dilution factor, 0.14; m is the mass of the weighed solid (mg). This process yields the final concentration values for each substance.</p>	<p>The raw sequences were processed using Trimmomatic [44], and sequence quality control was conducted using the FastQC [45] tool. The sequences were denoised using the deblur [46] algorithm in QIIME2 (v2021.11) [47] to obtain the ASV matrix. The parameter --p-min-reads was set to 2. The species annotation of the representative reads in each ASV was based on the SILVAdatabase (version 138 ) [48, 49] by using the q2-feature-classifier [50]. Based on the ASVs information, rarefaction curves and alpha diversity indices including observed OTUs, Chao1 richness and ACE index were calculated with QIIME2 (v2021.11) The similarity among the microbial communities in different samples was determined principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) based on Bray-Curtis dissimilarity using Vegan v2.6.4 package. The PERMANOVA test was used to assess the percentage of variation explained by the treatment along with its statistical significance using Vegan v2.6.4 package. Linear discriminant analysis (LDA) coupled with effect size (LEfSe) [51] (https://huttenhower.sph.harvard.edu/LEfSe)was applied to identify the significantly abundant taxa (phylum to genera) of bacteria among the different groups (LDA score &gt; 2, P &lt; 0.05). The analysis of microbial differences atthe genus level was conducted using the limma [52] (3.50.3) package. The microbial functional analysis was conducted using PICRUSt2 [53], and the differential analysis of functional profiles was performed using STAMP [54].</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer used in this study was the QTRAP 6500 Plus equipped with an ESI Turbo ion spray interface, the spectrometric parameters are set as follows: ion source temperature: 550 °C; ion spray voltage (IS): 5500 V (positive mode) and -4500 V (negative mode); ion source gases I (GS1), II (GS2), and curtain gas (CUR) are set at 60, 60, and 35 psi respectively. MRM mode is employed with MRM transitions containing information on the precursor and product ions of the target metabolites, collision energy (CE), declustering potential (DP), and retention time.</p>	<p>The raw data were automatically identified and integrated for each MRM transition (ion pair) using default parameters in the Skyline software (MacCoss Lab Software), and were further validated through manual inspection. Accurate metabolite concentrations are determined using isotope internal standards. The content of each substance in HM350 (umol/g) is calculated using the formula C*A/m, where C represents the concentration value (μmol/L) obtained by inputting the integrated peak area of the target analyte from the sample into the standard curve; A is the dilution factor, 0.14; m is the mass of the weighed solid (mg). This process yields the final concentration values for each substance.</p>

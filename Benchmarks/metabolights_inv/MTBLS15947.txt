Study Identifier	MTBLS15947
Study Title	TaANT11-4A Enhances Grain Yield by Orchestrating Photosynthesis and Carbohydrate Metabolism through Direct Transcriptional Activation
Study Description	<p>Photosynthesis and carbohydrate metabolism are crucial for plant growth and yield. Given that the overexpression of TaANT11-4A not only increases grain size and weight in wheat but also enhances overall biomass, we hypothesize that TaANT11-4A may strengthen photosynthesis in source organs, thereby leading to a comprehensive increase in sink products. Metabolites are the end products of cellular processes and can reflect gene transcription levels and protein expression. Therefore, metabolomics has been widely used to detect the relationships between metabolites and agronomic traits. Since carbohydrates are the direct products of photosynthesis and carbohydrate metabolism, we performed untargeted metabolomics analysis to further understand the role of TaANT11-4A in these pathways. Leaves of the wild-type (WT) and TaANT11-4A overexpression line OE-64 were harvested at the three-leaf stage for metabolomics analysis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) wild-type (WT) plants and TaANT11-4A overexpression line OE-64 (3xFlag-tagged) were grown under normal greenhouse conditions. Leaves were harvested at the three-leaf stage, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Frozen leaf tissue (0.3 g) was homogenized in 1 ml of 75% (v/v) methanol-water using a tissue grinder with magnetic beads. The homogenate was centrifuged at 17,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C and the supernatant was collected, evaporated to dryness under vacuum, and redissolved in 100 µl of 50% (v/v) methanol-water. After a second centrifugation at 17,000 × g for 15 min, the supernatant was transferred to an insert vial for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Scientific Dionex UltiMate 3000 UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) maintained at 40 °C. The flow rate was 400 µl/min and the injection volume was 3 µl. In positive ion mode the mobile phases were water with 0.1% formic acid (A) and acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid (B); in negative ion mode they were water with 2 mM ammonium acetate (A) and acetonitrile (B). The elution gradient was: 0–1.5 min, 5% B; 1.5–2.5 min, 5–10% B; 2.5–14 min, 10–40% B; 14–22 min, 40–95% B; 22–25 min, 95% B; 25–26 min, 95–5% B; 26–30 min, 5% B.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on an AB SCIEX TripleTOF 5600 quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer controlled by Analyst TF 1.7 software in information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in positive and negative ion modes. Full-scan MS spectra were acquired over the m/z range 50–1200. In each cycle, the most intense precursor ions (intensity greater than 100) were selected for MS/MS acquisition with a collision energy of 30 eV (10 MS/MS spectra per 50 ms). Electrospray ionization source parameters were: ion source gas 1, 60 psi; ion source gas 2, 60 psi; curtain gas, 35 psi; source temperature, 650 °C; ion spray voltage, 5000 V (positive mode) or −4000 V (negative mode).</p>	<p>Raw data were converted to mzML format using AnalysisBaseFileConverter and processed with MS-DIAL 4.6 for peak detection and peak alignment (accurate mass tolerance: MS1 0.01 Da, MS2 0.05 Da; identification score cut-off 60%). Blank samples were subtracted from the dataset.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by matching MS1 accurate masses and MS/MS spectra against an in-house integrated database combining Metlin, MassBank, MoNA and HMDB (version 6.0) using MS-DIAL 4.6, with an identification score cut-off of 60%.</p>

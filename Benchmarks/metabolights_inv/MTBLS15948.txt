Study Identifier	MTBLS15948
Study Title	Metabolome profiling on resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma patients with early liver metastasis
Study Description	Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains difficult to cure because many patients develop liver metastasis even after apparently curative resection. Bile is in close contact with the pancreatobiliary system, but its multi-omic features in resectable PDAC and their relationship to postoperative liver metastasis remain poorly defined. Here, we performed integrated metabolomic profiling of bile samples from 96 patients, including 40 with benign biliary disease and 56 with resectable PDAC, among whom 30 developed liver metastasis within 6 months after surgery and 26 remained disease free.

Study Protocol Description	<p>All samples were collected using sterile instruments and containers. For patients with PDAC, bile was aspirated directly from the bile duct after operative exposure, while for patients with benign biliary diseases, bile was aspirated during clinically indicated endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP). </p>	<p>Protein extraction was performed using SDT lysis buffer (4% SDS, 100 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.6), and protein concentration was determined by the BCA assay. Protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and visualized with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250 staining. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal amounts of protein from all individual samples. All samples were digested with trypsin using the filter-aided proteome preparation (FASP) method. Tryptic peptides were desalted with C18 cartridges, lyophilized, and reconstituted in 0.1% formic acid. Peptide concentration was measured at OD280, and iRT standard peptides were spiked into each sample before analysis.</p>	<p>The raw mass spectrometry acquisition files were converted to mzXML format using the MSConvert tool within the Proteowizard software package (v3.0.8789).</p>	<p>Fresh bile was immediately transferred to sterile tubes, placed on ice, and processed within 2 hours of collection. Samples were centrifuged to remove cellular debris, and the supernatant was aliquoted and stored at −80 °C until further analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>DIA data were acquired on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer coupled with a Vanquish Neo nano-LC system in positive ion mode. Full-scan MS spectra were acquired over a mass range of 380–980 m/z at a resolution of 240,000 (at 200 m/z). For DIA mode, 299 isolation windows of 2 m/z were used with HCD collision energy at 25 eV. MS2 spectra were collected with normalized AGC target of 500% and maximum injection time of 3 ms. Raw DIA data were processed using a library-based search engine.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The RXCMS package was used for peak detection, peak filtering and peak alignment with the following parameters: bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, method = 'centWave', to generate the metabolite quantification table.</p>

Study Title	PP2Cm Dysregulation Is Associated with Impaired BCAA Catabolism and cGAS-STING Activation in Diabetic Kidney Disease
Study Description	Background and Aims:Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) are increasingly implicated in diabetic kidney disease (DKD), yet the role of protein phosphatase 2C mitochondrial (PP2Cm), encoded by PPM1K and regulating BCAA catabolism through the branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKDH) complex, remains unclear. We aimed to characterize PP2Cm-associated BCAA metabolic alterations and their clinical relevance in DKD.Methods and Results: Integrated analyses of kidney transcriptomics, whole-blood RNA sequencing, and urinary metabolomics, followed by validation in 256 individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and DKD, revealed coordinated downregulation of PPM1K and BCKDHA, indicating suppression of BCAA catabolic pathways. Circulating BCAA levels displayed a stage-dependent pattern during DKD progression, with early elevation followed by reduction in advanced disease, [ Present/permanent address:The First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University No. 137 Liyushan South Road, Xinshi District, Urumqi 830054, Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, P.R. China]whereas urinary BCAA-to-creatinine ratios increased progressively. Serum PP2Cm levels declined with DKD severity and were independently associated with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). In DKD mice, reduced renal PP2Cm expression was accompanied by increased inhibitory BCKDHA phosphorylation and activation of the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS-STING) pathway. Conclusions: DKD is characterized by disruption of PP2Cm-associated BCAA metabolic regulation involving both transcriptional and post-translational mechanisms. The association between altered BCAA metabolism and cGAS-STING activation provides insights into metabolic–inflammatory interactions in DKD. Serum PP2Cm and urinary BCAA-to-creatinine ratios may represent candidate biomarkers of DKD-associated metabolic alterations.

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of eight mouse kidney tissue samples were included in this study for targeted amino acid metabolomic profiling. The samples were divided into two groups, including four normal control (NC) samples (NC1, NC2, NC3, and NC4) and four DKD group samples (DKD1, DKD2, DKD3, and DKD4). Each kidney tissue sample was assigned a unique identifier and subsequently subjected to sample preparation and targeted LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The sample extracts were analyzed using an LC-ESI-MS/MS system (UPLC, ExionLC AD, https://sciex.com.cn /; MS, QTRAP 6500+ System, https://sciex.com /). The analytical conditions were as follows, HPLC: column, ACQUITY BEH Amide (i.d.2.1×100 mm, 1.7 μm); solvent system, water with 2 mM ammonium acetate and 0.04% formic acid (A), acetonitrile with 2 mM ammonium acetate and</p><p>0.04% formic acid (B); The gradient was started at 90% B (0-1.2 min), decreased to 60% B (9 min), 40% B (10-11 min), finaly ramped back to 90% B (11.01-15 min); flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; temperature, 40°C; injection volume: 2 μL</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed using Analyst software (version 1.6.3, SCIEX), and chromatographic peaks were integrated and corrected using MultiQuant software (version 3.0.3, SCIEX). Peak integration and correction were performed with reference to the retention times and chromatographic peak shapes of authentic standards to ensure accurate quantification. The integrated peak-area ratios were converted to analyte concentrations using compound-specific calibration curves. The final metabolite concentrations in kidney tissues were calculated in ng/g using the equation C = c × V/(1000 × m), where c represents the concentration obtained from the calibration curve (ng/mL), V represents the extraction solvent volume (μL), and m represents the sample mass (g). For downstream multivariate statistical analysis, metabolite abundance data were subjected to unit variance scaling using R software (version 3.5.1).</p>	<p>Approximately 50 mg (± 2.5 mg) of each mouse kidney tissue sample was weighed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube. Subsequently, 500 μL of 70% methanol/water extraction solution, pre-cooled to 20°C, was added to each sample, followed by vortexing for 3 min. The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C, and 300 μL of the supernatant was transferred into a new 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. The supernatant was incubated at 20°C for 30 min and centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. Finally, 200 μL of the clarified supernatant was passed through a protein precipitation plate for subsequent LC-MS/MS analysis. The prepared extracts were stored at 20°C until instrumental analysis.</p>	<p>AB 6500+ QTRAP LC-MS/MS System, equipped with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, operating in both positive and negative ion modes and controlled by Analyst 1.6 software (AB Sciex). The ESI source operation parameters were as follows: ion source, turbo spray; source temperature 550°C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (Positive),-4500 V(Negative); curtain gas (CUR) were set at 35.0 psi; DP and CE for individual MRM transitions was done with further DP and CE optimization. A specific set of</p><p>MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the amino acid eluted within this period</p>	<p>Targeted identification of amino acids and related metabolites was performed using the MetWare Database (MWDB), which was established based on authentic reference standards. The analytical method targeted 94 amino acids and related metabolites, and metabolite identities were determined by comparing chromatographic retention times and peak characteristics with reference standards, together with compound-specific multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions. MRM analysis was performed on a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ tandem mass spectrometer using optimized precursor-to-product ion transitions, declustering potentials, and collision energies to improve detection specificity. Quantitative analysis was performed by integrating chromatographic peak areas and calculating analyte concentrations using compound-specific calibration curves based on standard solutions. The resulting metabolite concentrations were expressed as ng/g of tissue.</p>

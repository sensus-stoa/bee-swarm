Study Identifier	MTBLS15990
Study Title	Omics and Computational Chemistry Reveal Environmental Residues of Anti-COVID-19 Drug Chloroquine Phosphate Drive Antibiotic Resistance Gene Dissemination
Study Description	The widespread use of chloroquine phosphate (CQP) during the COVID-19 pandemic has led to its persistent residues in aquatic environments, yet its impact on the horizontal transfer of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) remains unknown. Herein, the effects and molecular mechanisms of CQP on plasmid-mediated ARGs transfer using an E. coli conjugation model and natural aquatic microbial communities were investigated. Environmentally relevant CQP concentrations (<1.0 mg/L) significantly promoted the conjugative transfer of ARGs, with a maximum 1.42 fold increase at 1.0 mg/L, while higher concentrations (4.0 mg/L) inhibited ARGs transfer. This biphasic effect was independent of bacterial viability and conserved in complex lake water. Mechanistically, low dose CQP induced moderate oxidative stress, activated the SOS response, increased membrane permeability, upregulated pilus and quorum sensing related genes, and enhanced ATP production. Molecular docking and density functional theory further revealed that CQP bond to key conjugation proteins (AcrB and TraD) and possessed electron accepting capacity, facilitating membrane interactions. Comprehensive transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses showed CQP remodeled membrane lipid and optimized donor-recipient contact. This study uncovers a hidden antimicrobial resistance risk of residual CQP, highlighting the need to concern post-pandemic pharmaceutical pollution risks in wastewater treatment.

Study Protocol Description	<p>E. coli cells were co-cultured under different chloroquine phosphate (CQP) exposure conditions. Bacterial cell pellets were collected by centrifugation. Cell pellets were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 C prior to metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on Vanquish Flex UHPLC system (Thermo). ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 Å, 1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) was used. Flow rate was set at 0.4 mL/min, column temperature at 40 °C, auto-sampler temperature at 8 °C, injection volume 2 µL. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water, mobile phase B was acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. Gradient elution was applied for both positive and negative ion modes.</p>	<p>Raw data files were processed using MS-DIAL version 4.9.221218 for peak picking, alignment and filtering. Peaks present in less than 50% QC samples were filtered out. Missing values were imputed by gap-filling algorithm followed by data normalisation. Metabolite annotation was performed against PSNGM, mzCloud, LIPID MAPS, HMDB, MoNA and NIST_2020_MSMS libraries. MS1 tolerance 0.01, MS2 tolerance 0.05, identification score cut-off 70.</p>	<p>Approximately 30 mg cell sample was weighed into 2 mL centrifuge tube. 1 mL pre-cooled 50% methanol and glass beads were added, followed by vortex. Samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen and thawed at room temperature, then homogenized in high-throughput tissue grinder. After repeated extraction and -20 °C incubation, samples were centrifuged at 12000 rpm at 4 °C for 20 min. The supernatant was vacuum-dried, re-dissolved in 50% methanol containing 5 ppm L-2-chlorophenylalanine, centrifuged before LC-MS injection.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric acquisition was carried out on Orbitrap Exploris 120 high resolution mass spectrometer (Thermo) with HESI ion source under Xcalibur 4.7 software control. Data dependent acquisition (DDA) was performed in both positive and negative ionization modes. Spray voltage: 3.5 kV (positive), -3.0 kV (negative); sheath gas 40 arb, auxiliary gas 10 arb; capillary temperature 320 C, auxiliary gas temperature 300 C. MS1 resolution 60000 with scan range m/z 70-1000. The top 4 intense ions were selected for MS2 fragmentation.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed using MS-DIAL (version 4.9.221218). The MS1 and MS2 spectra were matched against multiple reference databases, including PSNGM, mzCloud, LIPID MAPS, HMDB, MoNA and NIST_2020_MSMS. The mass tolerance for MS1 was set to 0.01 Da, and MS2 mass tolerance was 0.05 Da. Metabolite identification was accepted when the matching score was ≥70.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Verticillium dahliae strain VD991 was maintained as a laboratory stock. Three independent cultures were prepared for each treatment in Czapek liquid medium containing 0, 100 or 500 mg/L succinic acid (SA0, SA100 and SA500, respectively). Cultures were incubated at 25 degrees C with shaking at 180 rpm for 3 days. Mycelial biomass was collected on 9 February 2026 by centrifugation, washed, centrifuged again and rapidly frozen before submission for metabolomic analysis. Nine biological samples were analyzed, with three biological replicates per treatment. Each biological sample was analyzed in both positive- and negative-ion modes.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class PLUS system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.8 um). The column temperature was 50 degrees C and the autosampler temperature was 10 degrees C. Mobile phase A was water containing 0.1% formic acid and mobile phase B was acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 uL. The gradient set points were 5% B at 0.0 and 0.5 min, 50% B at 5.0 min, 95% B at 7.5 and 8.5 min, and 5% B at 10.0 min. The acquisition run time was 10 min. The same chromatographic conditions were used for positive- and negative-ion acquisitions. The autosampler was a Waters ACQUITY FTN autosampler.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were acquired in Waters .raw format using MassLynx version 4.2 and processed using Progenesis QI for peak detection, chromatographic alignment and generation of feature-abundance tables. Positive- and negative-ion data were processed as separate acquisition modes. Feature tables included feature identifiers, m/z, retention time and per-sample abundance values. Vendor exports included both raw and normalized abundance tables. Abundance values represent relative LC-MS signal intensities rather than absolute metabolite concentrations. Principal component analysis and Spearman correlation analysis were used to assess sample relationships and analytical reproducibility, including pooled QC samples.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted from mycelial pellets using a methanol/acetonitrile-based extraction solvent containing L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard at 20 mg/L. Samples were vortexed for 30 s, homogenized with steel beads at 45 Hz for 10 min and sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min. Extracts were held at -20 degrees C for 1 h and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 degrees C. A 500 uL aliquot of supernatant was transferred to a clean tube and dried in a vacuum concentrator. The dried extract was reconstituted in 160 uL acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v), vortexed for 30 s, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min and centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 degrees C. A 120 uL aliquot of supernatant was transferred to an LC-MS vial. A pooled quality-control sample was prepared by combining 10 uL aliquots from each biological sample extract.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed using a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source. Samples were acquired separately in positive- and negative-ion modes using MSe data-independent acquisition, controlled by MassLynx version 4.2. The acquisition range was m/z 50-1200 and the scan time was 0.2 s. Capillary voltages were +2.5 kV in positive-ion mode and -2.0 kV in negative-ion mode. The sampling cone voltage was 40 V. The source temperature was 100 degrees C and the desolvation temperature was 500 degrees C. Cone gas and desolvation gas flow rates were 50 and 800 L/h, respectively. Low- and elevated-energy data were acquired, with an elevated collision-energy ramp of 10-40 eV. Both biological sample extracts and pooled QC extracts were acquired in each ionization mode.</p>	<p>Putative metabolite annotations were generated using Progenesis QI by comparing LC-MS features with the METLIN database and the in-house metabolite library maintained by Biomarker Technologies. Annotation used accurate-mass information and fragmentation information, including theoretical fragment matching as described in the vendor workflow. Available annotation outputs included compound names, molecular formulas, mass errors and match scores. KEGG, HMDB and LIPID MAPS were used for subsequent pathway and chemical-class annotation. Features without a supported compound annotation were retained using their feature identifiers. The reported compound names are treated as putative annotations; confirmation using authentic reference standards is not documented in the available records.</p>

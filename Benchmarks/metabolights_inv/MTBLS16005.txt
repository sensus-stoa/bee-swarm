Study Protocol Description	<p>Collect milk samples from sows, immediately divide the samples into equal parts, rapidly freeze them, and store them at -80 ° C.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system using an ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) at 25°C. The mobile phase consisted of water containing 25 mM ammonium acetate and 25 mM ammonium hydroxide (A) and acetonitrile (B). The gradient program was as follows: 0–0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5–5 min, linear decrease from 95% to 65% B; 5–5.5 min, linear decrease from 65% to 40% B; 5.5–6 min, linear increase from 40% to 95% B; 6–8 min, held at 95% B. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data files (Thermo Scientific .raw format) were converted to the open mzML format using ProteoWizard (MSConvert) with centroiding (vendor peak picking). The converted data were then processed using XCMS (R package) for peak detection, retention time alignment, feature grouping, and gap filling. Quality control (QC) samples were used for batch correction and normalization. The resulting feature matrix was exported for downstream statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Frozen milk aliquots were thawed on ice. Metabolites were extracted with pre-cooled methanol/acetonitrile/water (2:2:1, v/v/v). After vortexing, samples were sonicated on ice for 30 min, incubated at -20°C for 10 min, and centrifuged at 14,000 g for 20 min at 4°C. Supernatants were vacuum-dried and reconstituted in acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v/v); after vortexing and centrifugation at 14,000 g for 15 min at 4°C, the supernatant was used for LC-MS. Pooled QC was prepared by mixing equal aliquots of all milk extracts. Solvent blanks were also included.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The instrument was operated in positive ion mode. The mass spectra were acquired in a full scan range of m/z 70–1000. The capillary temperature was set at 320°C, and the spray voltage was 3.5 kV. The sheath gas flow rate was 40 arb, the aux gas flow rate was 10 arb, and the sweep gas flow rate was 1 arb. The RF lens was set to 50%, and the resolution was set to 60,000.Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The instrument was operated in negative ion mode. The mass spectra were acquired in a full scan range of m/z 70–1000. The capillary temperature was set at 320°C, and the spray voltage was -2.5 kV (or 3.0 kV). The sheath gas flow rate was 40 arb, the aux gas flow rate was 10 arb, and the sweep gas flow rate was 1 arb. The RF lens was set to 50%, and the resolution was set to 60,000.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification and annotation were performed using accurate mass (m/z), isotopic distribution, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra. Features were annotated by matching against public spectral libraries and reference databases, including METLIN, HMDB, KEGG, PubChem, ChEBI, MassBank, NIST, and mzCloud. Additional annotation of unknown metabolites was supported by in-silico fragmentation tools (e.g., SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID, CFM-ID, or MS-FINDER) and the GNPS platform. Metabolites were classified according to the confidence levels suggested by the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI).</p>

Study Title	Gut Microbiota-Derived TMAO Exacerbates Alcohol-Induced Osteonecrosis of the Femoral Head via IL-17 Signaling
Study Description	This study investigated the role of gut microbiota-derived trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) in alcohol-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head (AONFH). Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed on intestinal contents collected from rat models, including Normal control, AONFH model, and DMB-treated groups. The metabolic profiles were analyzed by LC-MS to identify differential metabolites and explore potential mechanisms associated with IL-17 signaling, osteogenesis, and gut microbiota-mediated regulation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Rat intestinal contents were collected after experimental treatment. The samples were obtained from three groups: Normal control group, alcohol-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head (AONFH) model group, and DMB-treated AONFH group. A total of 18 intestinal content samples were collected. Samples were immediately stored under appropriate conditions and transported under dry ice cold-chain conditions before metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Vanquish UHPLC system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters). The column temperature was maintained at 40 ℃. The flow rate was set at 0.3 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p><p>For positive ion mode, mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B).</p><p>For negative ion mode, mobile phases consisted of 5 mM ammonium formate in water (A) and acetonitrile (B).&nbsp;</p>	<p>Raw MS data were converted into mzXML format using MSConvert from ProteoWizard software (v3.0.8789). Peak detection, peak filtering, and peak alignment were performed using the R XCMS package. Data were normalized using total peak area normalization to reduce systematic variation.</p>	<p>Approximately appropriate amounts of intestinal content samples were transferred into 2 mL centrifuge tubes. Samples were extracted with methanol containing 2-chloro-L-phenylalanine (4 ppm) as an internal standard. The samples were vortexed for 30 s, homogenized with steel beads at 50 Hz for 120 s, and ultrasonicated at room temperature for 10 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃, the supernatants were collected and filtered through 0.22 μm PTFE membranes before LC-MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling aliquots from individual extracted samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Thermo Q Exactive Focus mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. The spray voltage was 3.50 kV in positive mode and -2.50 kV in negative mode. The capillary temperature was 325 ℃, sheath gas flow was 40 arb, and auxiliary gas flow was 10 arb. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 70,000 FWHM with an m/z range of 100–1000. MS/MS spectra were acquired using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with a normalized collision energy of 30 eV and a resolution of 17,500 FWHM.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were annotated based on accurate mass, retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation information. Differential metabolites were screened using multivariate statistical analysis and statistical tests. PCA, PLS-DA, and OPLS-DA analyses were performed using the R ropls package. Differential metabolites were selected based on VIP values and p-values.&nbsp;</p>

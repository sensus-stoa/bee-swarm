Study Identifier	MTBLS16031
Study Title	A KRAS-triggered lactylation cascade forms the basis of chemotherapy resistance in colorectal cancer
Study Description	<p>KRAS-mutant colorectal cancer (KRASmut CRC) exhibits metabolic plasticity and chemoresistance, but the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Here, we show in CRC cells and organoids that KRAS-driven lactate accumulation promotes MCT1 K224 lactylation, which stabilizes mitochondrial MCT1 by limiting MUL1-dependent ubiquitination and degradation. This increases mitochondrial pyruvate availability and enhances pyruvate carboxylase-dependent aspartate and pyrimidine synthesis, promoting 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) resistance in CRC cells and xenografts. Mechanistically, SUGCT, previously characterized as a CoA transferase, functions as a lactyl-CoA-dependent lysine lactyltransferase that directly catalyzes MCT1 K224 lactylation. KRAS-ERK-TFCP2 signaling upregulates SUGCT, coupling lactate production to anabolic metabolism. Pharmacological TFCP2 inhibition with FQI1 suppresses SUGCT expression and MCT1 lactylation and sensitizes KRASmut CRC organoids and xenografts to 5-FU. Together, these findings define a KRAS-ERK-TFCP2-SUGCT-MCT1 lactylation axis linking glycolytic rewiring to pyrimidine-dependent chemoresistance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cell lines were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C under 5% CO2. Upon reaching 2 × 10000000 cells, the cells were washed twice with PBS and then harvested.</p>	<p>For positive ion mode, the mobile phase consisted of water with 0.1% formic acid (solvent A) and methanol with 0.1% formic acid (solvent B). For negative ion mode, the mobile phase consisted of water with 10 mM ammonium formate (solvent A) and 95% methanol with 10 mM ammonium formate (solvent B). The following gradient was applied at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min: 0-1 min, 2% B; 1-9 min, 2% to 98% B; 9-12 min, 98% B; 12-12.1 min, 98% to 2% B; 12.1-15 min, 2% B. The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>The raw MS data were imported into Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and analyzed using the BMDB (BGI Metabolome Database)</p>	<p>For cellular untargeted metabolomic profiling, 20 mg of colon cancer tissue was homogenized in pre-cooled methanol/acetonitrile/water (2:2:1, v/v), followed by vortexing and sonication at 50 Hz for 50 min. The mixture was incubated at -20 °C for 2 h and then centrifuged at 4 °C, 25,000 × g for 15 min. The supernatant was vacuum-dried, and the residue was reconstituted in 50% methanol.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry parameters were set as follows: full-MS scans were acquired over m/z 70-1050 with a resolution of 120,000, an AGC target of 3 × 1000000, and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The top three most intense ions were selected for data-dependent MS2 fragmentation with a resolution of 30,000, an AGC target of 1 × 100000, a maximum injection time of 50 ms, and stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 eV. ESI source settings were: sheath gas flow, 40 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow, 10 arbitrary units; spray voltage, 3.80 kV (positive) or 3.20 kV (negative); capillary temperature, 320 °C; auxiliary gas heater temperature, 350 °C.</p>	<p>The data were analyzed against the BMDB (BGI Metabolome Database), mzCloud, and ChemSpider online databases for feature identification and/or metabolite annotation, yielding a data matrix containing metabolite peak areas and identification results, which was then subjected to further data processing and analysis.</p>

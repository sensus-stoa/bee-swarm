Study Identifier	MTBLS16036
Study Title	AMF-PGPR synergy improves Suaeda salsa phytoremediation: root morphology modulates diverse core exudates to drive core microbiota
Study Description	We conducted microbial inoculation and plant-derived root exudates validation experiments at four growth stages. Inoculation experiment demonstrated that co-inoculation optimized root morphology; promoted biomass (2.01-4.74 fold), Na+ (1.03-4.42 fold) and nutrient (1.04-13.94 fold) accumulation, antioxidase activities (1.19-5.09 fold), soil nutrient (1.05-3.79 fold), and desalination rate (1.93-3.29 fold); declined total dissolved salt (0.95-0.99 fold); revealed temporal regulation mechanism of co-inoculation as plant grew: root morphology (e.g., root length, etc.) regulated key exudates (e.g., flavonoids, etc.) to enrich key microbes (e.g., Nocardioides, etc.) to improve phytoremediation; and its synergistic regulatory mechanism at each stage: root morphology (e.g., root volume, etc.) effected multiple core exudates (CEs, e.g., terpenoids, etc.) to recruit diverse core microbiota (CMs, e.g., Proteobacteria, etc.) to improve phytoremediation. CEs repeated enriched same CMs (e.g., Chloroflexi, etc.) across stages but also specific enriched others (e.g., Gemmatimonadota, etc.). Validation confirmed that co-inoculation plant-derived root exudates modulated abundant CMs to affect plant growth. Overall, this study emphasized the significance of multiple exudates-microbes interactions in phytoremediation, providing new insight for microbial-assisted halophyte remediation of saline soil.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Root exudates were collected at SS, VS I, VS II, and FS for four treatments. The flowerpots were removed from the collection devices, and exposed roots were surface sterilized with 0.5% NaClO for 2 min and then rinsed three times with sterile deionized water. The water in the collection devices was replaced with fresh sterile deionized water, with the liquid level maintained approximately 1 cm below the flowerpots bottom. Sterilized roots were then immersed in new sterile deionized water and placed in growth room to collect root exudates under ventilation and avoid light. After 4 h, the root exudates solution was filtered through a 0.22 μm aqueous membrane to remove root detritus and microbial cells. The filtrate was concentrated to 50 mL with a rotary evaporator, lyophilized, and stored at -80 ℃ until analysis.</p>	<p>The mobile phases consisted of 95% solvent A (ultrapure water with 0.1% formic acid added) and 5% solvent B (acetonitrile and isopropanol, v/v=1:1) with 0.1% formic acid added). The solvent gradient changed according to the following conditions: from 0 to 3 min, 95% (solvent A): 5% (solvent B) to 80% (solvent A): 20% (solvent B); from 3 to 9 min, 80% (solvent A): 20% (solvent B) to 5% (solvent A): 95% (solvent B); from 9 to 13 min, 5% (solvent A): 95% (solvent B) to 5% (solvent A): 95% (solvent B); from 13 to 13.1 min, 5% (solvent A): 95% (solvent B) to 95% (solvent A): 5% (solvent B), from 13.1 to 16 min, 95% (A): 5% (B) to 95% (A): 5% (B) for equilibrating the systems. The sample injection volume was 2 µL and the flow rate was set to 0.4 mL min-1. The column temperature was maintained at 40.&nbsp;Samples were analyzed using a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system equipped with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm; Waters, USA).</p>	<p>Raw data preprocessing was conducted using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) to generate a data matrix containing sample identifiers, metabolite names, and mass spectral response intensities. After removing internal standards peaks and known false positive peaks, redundant peaks were consolidated. Only metabolic features detected in at least 50% of the samples within any evaluation group were retained. After filtering, missing metabolite values were imputed with the minimum measured value, and each metabolic profile was normalized to its sum. To reducing the errors caused by sample preparation and instrument instability, the response intensities of the sample mass spectrometry peaks were normalized using the sum normalization method, to obtain the normalized data matrix. Variables in QC samples with relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% were excluded and the data were log10-transformed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>50 mg of freeze-dried powder was accurately weighed into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, and adding 400 μL of extraction solvent (acetonitrile: methanol = 1:1, v/v). After vortex for 30 s, the mixture was subjected to ultrasonic extraction at 5 °C and 40 kHz for 30 min. The extracts were then incubated at -20&nbsp;for 30 min and centrifuged at 13,000 r min-1 for 15 min at 4 . The supernatant was transferred and dried under nitrogen. The sample was reconstituted in 120 µL of an acetonitrile: water (1:1, v/v) solution, ultrasonicated at 5 °C (40 kHz) for 5 min, and centrifuged again at 13,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C. Finally, the supernatant was transferred to a sample bottle for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>The MS data was acquired using a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive Mass Spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scienti­fic, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. During the period of analysis, all these samples were stored at 4. The optimal conditions of MS were set as follows: heater temperature, 400; Capillary temperature, 320; sheath gas flow rate, 40 arb; Aux gas flow rate, 10 arb; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF), -2800V in negative mode and 3500V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy, 20-40-60V rolling for MS/MS. Full MS resolution was 70000, and MS/MS resolution was 17500. Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 70-1050 m/z.</p>	<p>HMDB(http://www.hmdb.ca/) and Metlin( https://metlin.scripps.edu/)&nbsp;</p>

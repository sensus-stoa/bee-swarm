STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS16037
Study Title	A KRAS-triggered lactylation cascade forms the basis of chemotherapy resistance in colorectal cancer
Study Description	<p>KRAS-mutant colorectal cancer (KRASmut CRC) exhibits metabolic plasticity and chemoresistance, but the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Here, we show in CRC cells and organoids that KRAS-driven lactate accumulation promotes MCT1 K224 lactylation, which stabilizes mitochondrial MCT1 by limiting MUL1-dependent ubiquitination and degradation. This increases mitochondrial pyruvate availability and enhances pyruvate carboxylase-dependent aspartate and pyrimidine synthesis, promoting 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) resistance in CRC cells and xenografts. Mechanistically, SUGCT, previously characterized as a CoA transferase, functions as a lactyl-CoA-dependent lysine lactyltransferase that directly catalyzes MCT1 K224 lactylation. KRAS-ERK-TFCP2 signaling upregulates SUGCT, coupling lactate production to anabolic metabolism. Pharmacological TFCP2 inhibition with FQI1 suppresses SUGCT expression and MCT1 lactylation and sensitizes KRASmut CRC organoids and xenografts to 5-FU. Together, these findings define a KRAS-ERK-TFCP2-SUGCT-MCT1 lactylation axis linking glycolytic rewiring to pyrimidine-dependent chemoresistance.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Organoids cultured in dishes were switched to fresh medium containing [U-13C] glutamine (4 mM, CLM-1822, Cambridge Isotope Laboratories). Following 24 h of incubation, the cells were harvested, washed with PBS, and lysed.</p>	<p>For negative ion mode, the mobile phase consisted of water with 10 mM ammonium formate (solvent A) and 95% methanol with 10 mM ammonium formate (solvent B). The following gradient was applied at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min: 0-1 min, 2% B; 1-9 min, 2% to 98% B; 9-12 min, 98% B; 12-12.1 min, 98% to 2% B; 12.1-15 min, 2% B. The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C and the injection volume was 5 μL.</p>	<p>The raw MS data were imported into Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), mzCloud, and ChemSpider online databases. A data matrix containing metabolite peak areas and identification results was generated and then subjected to further processing and analysis.</p>	<p>After the indicated incubation times, cells were rapidly collected and lysed in acetonitrile by sonication for 2 min, followed by centrifugation at 15,000 × g for 30 min. The supernatant was collected for subsequent Vanquish F and Q Exactive HF-X (Thermo, USA).</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry parameters were set as follows: full-MS scans were acquired over m/z 70-1050 with a resolution of 120,000, an AGC target of 3 × 1000000, and a maximum injection time of 100 ms. The top three most intense ions were selected for data-dependent MS2 fragmentation with a resolution of 30,000, an AGC target of 1 × 100000, a maximum injection time of 50 ms, and stepped normalized collision energies of 20, 40, and 60 eV. ESI source settings were: sheath gas flow, 40 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow, 10 arbitrary units; spray voltage, 3.80 kV (positive) or 3.20 kV (negative); capillary temperature, 320 °C; auxiliary gas heater temperature, 350 °C.</p>	<p>mzCloud, and ChemSpider online databases, which were used to identify features and/or annotate metabolites. This yielded a data matrix containing metabolite peak areas and identification results, which was then subjected to further data processing and analysis.</p>

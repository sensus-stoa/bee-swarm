Study Identifier	MTBLS16054
Study Title	ApoE4 drives ovarian susceptibility to low-dose cisplatin-induced premature ovarian insufficiency through PPARα-mediated lipid metabolism reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction
Study Description	<p>This study investigated the effects of apolipoprotein E (ApoE) genotype on cellular metabolism and mitochondrial homeostasis. Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells were used for untargeted metabolomic profiling to characterize metabolic alterations associated with ApoE-related experimental conditions. Six samples were analyzed, comprising three biological replicates in each of the Control2 and Treat2 groups. Metabolomic profiling was performed using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS) in positive and negative electrospray ionization modes. The analysis aimed to identify metabolic differences between the two groups and provide insights into ApoE-associated metabolic regulation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Six Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell samples (Cricetulus griseus) were divided into two groups, Control2 and Treat2, with three biological replicates per group. Untargeted metabolomics was performed to investigate metabolic differences between the two groups. The samples were stored at -80 °C before metabolite extraction.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Scientific, USA) equipped with a Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, the flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 3 µL. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The gradient was programmed as follows: 0 min, 5% B; 1 min, 20% B; 3–4.5 min, 99% B; and 4.6–6 min, 5% B. The chromatographic conditions were used for both positive and negative ionization modes.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data acquired in positive and negative ionization modes were converted to mzML format using ProteoWizard. Peak detection, peak alignment and retention time correction were performed using XCMS. Features with more than 50% missing values within a sample group were filtered. Missing values were imputed using the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) method when the missing proportion was below 50%, or one-fifth of the minimum value when the missing proportion exceeded 50%. Peak areas were corrected using support vector regression (SVR). Metabolite signals were further normalized using protein concentrations measured by the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay. Following quality filtering and metabolite annotation, results from positive and negative ionization modes were combined, and duplicate annotations were resolved by retaining the entries with the highest identification confidence and matching scores.</p>	<p>Cell samples were thawed on ice, and all subsequent extraction steps were performed on ice. A total of 500 µL of 80% aqueous methanol containing internal standards was added to each sample, followed by vortexing for 3 min. The samples were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen for 5 min, thawed on dry ice for 5 min and then on ice for 5 min, and vortexed for 2 min. This freeze-thaw procedure was repeated three times. The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and 300 µL of supernatant was transferred to a new tube and incubated at -20 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 3 min at 4 °C, 200 µL of supernatant was transferred into an autosampler vial for LC-MS/MS analysis. Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing sample extracts to monitor analytical reproducibility and instrument stability.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in both positive and negative ionization modes. The spray voltages were 3,800 V in positive mode and 3,400 V in negative mode. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas flow rates were 60 and 20 arbitrary units, respectively. The ion transfer tube temperature was maintained at 320 °C, while the vaporizer temperatures were 350 °C and 650 °C in positive and negative modes, respectively. Full MS scans were acquired over an m/z range of 70–1000 at a resolution of 60,000, with an automatic gain control (AGC) target of 3 × 10^6. MS/MS spectra were acquired over an m/z range of 70–1000 at a resolution of 15,000, with an AGC target of 1 × 10^5. Stepped collision energies of 30, 40 and 50 V were applied using a Top 10 acquisition method with a dynamic exclusion duration of 3 s.</p>	<p>Metabolites were annotated by matching processed mass spectrometric data against an in-house spectral database, integrated public databases and prediction libraries. Metabolite identification was based on the available mass spectrometric information and database matching scores. Metabolites with an identification score greater than 0.5 and a coefficient of variation (CV) below 30% in quality control samples were retained for subsequent analysis. Metabolite annotations obtained in positive and negative ionization modes were combined, and duplicate annotations were resolved by retaining the entries with the highest annotation confidence and matching scores.</p>

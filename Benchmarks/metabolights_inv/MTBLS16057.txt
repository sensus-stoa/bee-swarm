Study Identifier	MTBLS16057
Study Title	A Transferable Workflow for Confident Annotation of Polar Analytes in 4D-Metabolomics
Study Description	Introduction High-confidence annotation of polar metabolites remains a central challenge in non-targeted metabolomics. While derivatization improves chromatographic retention and ionization of derivatized compounds, it changes the molecules, making annotation using conventional annotation workflows difficult. Trapped ion mobility spectrometry (TIMS) provides collisional cross section (CCS) values as an orthogonal, instrument-agnostic molecular descriptor, but CCS reference data for derivatized metabolites is scarce. Objectives We present a four-dimensional annotation strategy integrating 3-nitrophenylhydrazine 3-NPH derivatization with orthogonal retention time (RT), CCS, accurate mass, isotopic pattern, and MS/MS information acquired by LC-TIMS-HRMS, providing a large-scale experimental CCS reference dataset for 3-NPH-derivatized metabolites. Methods Experimentally measured TIMS CCS values were acquired for 3-NPH derivatives on a timsTOF Pro2. Combined with standardized RTs from the biocrates MxQuant kit workflow, these values form a curated 4D target list for automated annotation that is instrument independent and transferable. Coverage was further extended via an in silico derivatization workflow scoring candidate structures against experimental MS/MS and CCS data. Results CCS values were transferred directly to the timsMetabo platform with inter-instrument agreement consistent with published benchmarks (<2 % ΔCCS). Applied to time-resolved brown adipocyte extracts following norepinephrine stimulation, the workflow resolved temporal trajectories across fatty acids, organic acids, and amino acid derivatives matching previous reports. Conclusion Anchoring 3-NPH metabolite annotation to experimentally measured TIMS CCS values and standardized biocrates kit retention times substantially reduces annotation ambiguity, allowing confident profiling of polar analytes in complex biological systems.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Immortalized murine brown preadipocytes were cultured as previously described in (Anagho-Mattanovich et al. 2025). To induce thermogenic activation, fully differentiated adipocytes were stimulated with norepinephrine (1 µmol L-1, Sigma-Aldrich). Cell extracts were collected at 0, 4, and 24 h following stimulation.</p>	<p>Derivatized adipocyte extracts were analyzed using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC coupled to a Bruker timsMetabo mass spectrometer. A 2 µL injection volume was used for all analyses. Chromatographic separation was achieved using an 11-min reversed-phase gradient on a biocrates UHPLC column equipped with an Agilent XDB-C18 UHPLC precolumn operated at 50 °C. Milli-Q water with 0.02 % formic acid was used as mobile phase A, and a mixture of Methanol and Acetonitrile (3:2, v/v) with 0.02 % formic acid as mobile phase B, using the following gradient: starting conditions were 10 % B and a flow rate of 0.8 mL min-1. Between 0.05 min and 3.30 min B was increased to 35 %, until 7.50 min B was increased to 75 %, and until 7.80 min B was increased to 95 %, which was maintained until 9.00 min. Between 9.00 and 9.10 min B was further increased to 100 % and the flow rate was increased to 1.2 mL min-1, both of which were maintained until 10.00 min. Between 10.00 and 10.10 min B was lowered to 10 % and the flow rate to 1.0 mL min-1, which was further lowered to 0.8 mL min-1 until 10.80 min. These settings were maintained until the end of the run at 11.00 min.</p>	<p>All data was processed in MetaboScape® 2026b for automatic mass and mobility calibration, feature detection, and alignment using the T-ReX® 4D algorithm. Feature extraction included de-adducting, de-isotoping, and feature grouping. Automated annotation was performed using a CCS- and RT-anchored target list and an in silico derivatization workflow, as described in detail in 'In silico derivatization and CCS value prediction improve interoperable semi-untargeted stable-isotope labeling analysis' (Anagho-Mattanovich et al. 2026). The following narrow and wide tolerances and scorings were applied for compound annotation: m/z 1.5 mDa and 2 mDa, retention time 0.1 min and 0.15 min, mSigma 25 and 750, MS/MS score 750 and 250, CCS 2.0 and 5.0 %.</p>	<p>Cells were quenched in ice-cold 90% methanol. Culture medium was transferred to tubes, wells were rinsed with 1 mL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and 500 µL methanol was added. Cells were scraped, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and subjected to three freeze-thaw-vortex cycles. For protein precipitation, samples were incubated on ice for 1 h and subsequently centrifuged (16,000 g, 15 min, 4 °C). Supernatants were collected and stored at -80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed in negative ion mode using a VIP-HESI source operated under the following conditions: end plate offset 500 V, capillary 4500 V, nebulizer 2.0 bar, dry gas 10.0 L/min, dry temperature 220 °C, probe gas temperature 450 °C, probe gas flow 4.0 L min-1. The measurement range was 20-1300 m/z, and the instrument was used in TIMS-PASEF model with a ramp time of 100 ms, a mobility range of 0.45-1.45 1/K0 and an MS/MS collision energy of 35 eV. The transfer parameters were the following: deflection delta -70 V, funnel 1 RF 500 Vpp, funnel 2 RF 250 Vpp, multipole RF 200 Vpp, collision energy 10 eV, collision RF 1200 V, quadrupole low mass 60 m/z, AIP MS Profile: 600-0vpp slope 20Vpp/us, storage time ramping: 3.7 at 0.7 1/K0-14.0 at 1.3 1/K0. AIP MS/MS profile: 600-0vpp slope 20Vpp/us, storage time ramping: 3.0 at 0.7 1/K0-3.0 at 1.3 1/K0. The TIMS offsets were the following: Δt1 20 V, Δt2 120 V, Δt3 -60 V, Δt4 -50 V, Δt5 0 V, Δt6 -20 V, collision cell in -140 V, ICC target intensity 30 M.</p><p>Automatic internal mass calibration was performed using sodium formate, and automatic internal mobility calibration using Agilent ESI-L low concentration Tunemix.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based on the biocrates MxQuant kit and a previously recorded CCS library that is described in the manuscript associated with this study.</p>

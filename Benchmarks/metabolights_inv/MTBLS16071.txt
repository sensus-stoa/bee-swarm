Study Identifier	MTBLS16071
Study Title	The feces metabonomics of HBV group, ETV group and EI group
Study Description	<p>We analyzed the gut fecal metabolome to determine the role of related metabolites in virological and intrahepatic immune responses to ETV and EI. Fecal samples were collected from experimental subjects receiving ETV or EI treatment, followed by untargeted metabolomic profiling using LC-MS/MS. Differential metabolites were screened and annotated, and enrichment analysis of metabolic pathways was performed. Correlation analysis was further conducted between altered fecal metabolites, serum viral load indicators, and hepatic immune cell phenotypes. This work aims to reveal whether gut metabolic signatures can mediate or reflect the virological control and intrahepatic immune regulation triggered by ETV and EI, providing new metabolic clues for understanding the differences in antiviral and immunomodulatory effects between the two interventions.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected from mice receiving ETV or EI treatment at designated time points. All fecal pellets were immediately harvested, placed into sterile cryovials, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until metabolite extraction to avoid metabolite degradation.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Flex UHPLC System. Parameter Value[Chromatography Instrument]: Thermo Scientific Vanquish Flex UHPLC System Parameter Value[Autosampler model]: Thermo Scientific Vanquish Autosampler Parameter Value[Column model]: Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 Column, 100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm Parameter Value[Column type]: reverse phase Parameter Value[Guard column]: Waters ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 VanGuard Pre-column, 2.1 mm × 5 mm, 1.8 µm The mobile phase consisted of aqueous phase A and organic phase B (acetonitrile). Gradient elution was applied at a constant flow rate. Column temperature was maintained at 40 °C and autosampler temperature was set at 4 °C.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were processed using commercial metabolomics software for peak picking, peak alignment, retention time correction, feature filtering and normalization. Peak features present in QC samples were retained for further analysis. Missing values were imputed appropriately. Multivariate statistical analysis including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), together with univariate statistical tests, were performed to screen significantly differential metabolites.</p>	<p>Fecal samples were thawed on ice. Each sample was homogenized with pre-cooled extraction solvent containing methanol and water. After thorough vortexing and centrifugation, the supernatant was transferred to a new tube. The supernatant was dried under vacuum, reconstituted, and centrifuged again. The final supernatant was transferred for LC-MS/MS untargeted metabolomic analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all sample supernatants to monitor instrument stability during data acquisition.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was carried out on a high-resolution mass spectrometer coupled to the UHPLC system. Parameter Value[Scan polarity]: negative Parameter Value[Scan m/z range]: 70–1050 m/z Parameter Value[Instrument]: Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 Parameter Value[Ion source]: electrospray ionization (ESI) Parameter Value[Mass analyzer]: Orbitrap The electrospray ion source was operated in negative ion mode. Full scan MS spectra were acquired, and data-dependent MS/MS spectra were obtained for metabolite fragmentation. Source spray voltage, sheath gas, auxiliary gas and capillary temperature were optimized for negative ionization.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation and identification were achieved by matching accurate precursor mass, MS/MS fragmentation spectra and retention time against public metabolite databases including HMDB, mzCloud and Metlin. Metabolites were annotated at different confidence levels according to MSI (Metabolomics Standards Initiative) standards. Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using KEGG and Metascape to identify perturbed metabolic pathways associated with ETV and EI treatment.</p>

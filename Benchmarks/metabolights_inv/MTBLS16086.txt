Study Title	Integrated Metagenomic and Metabolomic Analysis Reveals Microbial Succession and Metabolic Profiles of Huang Suan Co-Fermented with Post-Flowering Young Rapeseed Pods and Allium chrysanthum Regel
Study Description	Huang Suan is a traditional fermented vegetable from Guizhou prepared from young rapeseed pods and Allium chrysanthum. This study integrated metagenomics and metabolomics to investigate the microbial succession and metabolic remodeling during fermentation. Metagenomic analysis revealed that Latilactobacillus sakei, Companilactobacillus alimentarius, and Lactiplantibacillus plantarum became the dominant taxa, accompanied by rapid acidification. The decline of opportunistic pathogens co-occurred with low pH, ethanol stress from the added baijiu, and competitive exclusion. Metabolomic profiling showed significant accumulation of flavonoid aglycones (e.g., quercetin, isorhamnetin) and specific amino acids, alongside enhanced in vitro antioxidant activities. Multi-omics association analyses indicated that dominant LAB were associated with overall metabolite categories, while low-abundance taxa co-varied with specific compounds. Moreover, metagenomic data suggested a genetic potential for cell wall degradation and flavonoid deglycosylation via key CAZymes (e.g., CE8, GH1). These findings provide a descriptive foundation for understanding this novel fermentation system.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Huang Suan samples were collected on days 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20 during spontaneous fermentation at 20 °C. At each time point, three independent fermentation bottles were randomly selected and subjected to destructive sampling. Samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for 15 min and stored at −80 °C until further analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was carried out on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 A, 1.8 um, 2.1 mm x 100 mm) maintained at 40 C. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 2 uL. Mobile phase A was 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in water, and phase B was 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient elution was: 0-1 min, 5% B; 1-4.7 min, 5%-95% B; 4.7-6 min, 95% B; 6-6.1 min, 95%-5% B; 6.1-8.5 min, 5% B.</p>	<p>Raw metabolomic data were processed using MS-DIAL (v4.9.221218) and Compound Discoverer 3.3 for peak extraction, alignment, retention time correction, and normalization. Features with missing values in &gt;50% of the samples within any group were excluded. Remaining missing values were imputed using half of the minimum positive value.</p>	<p>40 mg of ground sample was extracted with 300 uL of pre-chilled methanol-acetonitrile-water (2:2:1, v/v/v) containing 5 ppm L-2-chlorophenylalanine as the internal standard. The mixture was vortexed and ultrasonicated at 55 Hz for 60 s (repeated once). After incubating at -20 C for 30 min to precipitate proteins, the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4 C for 10 min. The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 um membrane. Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes (10-20 uL) of filtrate from each sample.n</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer. Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) was conducted in alternating positive and negative ion modes. HESI source parameters: spray voltage +/-3.5 kV/-3.0 kV; sheath gas 40 arb; auxiliary gas 10 arb; capillary temperature 320 C; auxiliary gas temperature 300 C. Full MS scans were acquired at 60,000 resolution over m/z 70-1000. The top four precursor ions were selected for MS/MS at 15,000 resolution using HCD at a normalized collision energy of 30%.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was achieved by matching retention times, accurate masses, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against in-house and public databases (mzCloud, HMDB, Metlin). The identification confidence was evaluated using Dot_product (forward match score), Reverse_dot_product (reverse match score), and Total_score. To ensure accuracy, only metabolites with Total_score &gt;= 60 were retained. According to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI), these metabolites were annotated as putative (MSI Level 2 or 3).</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS16093
Study Title	Targeted Metabolomic Profiling of Porcine Ovarian Granulosa Cells Following E4F1 and MPC1 Knockdown
Study Description	This study aimed to investigate the roles of E4F1 and MPC1 in regulating metabolic homeostasis in porcine ovarian granulosa cells using targeted metabolomic analysis. Primary porcine ovarian granulosa cells were divided into three experimental groups: negative control (si-NC), E4F1 knockdown (si-E4F1), and MPC1 knockdown (si-MPC1). Targeted metabolomic profiling was performed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) to characterize metabolic alterations following gene silencing. The analysis focused on metabolites involved in glycolysis, the pentose phosphate pathway, and the tricarboxylic acid cycle. The dataset provides insights into the metabolic changes associated with E4F1 and MPC1 depletion and their potential roles in regulating mitochondrial energy metabolism and metabolic homeostasis in porcine ovarian granulosa cells.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Primary porcine ovarian granulosa cells were divided into three experimental groups: negative control (si-NC), E4F1 knockdown (si-E4F1), and MPC1 knockdown (si-MPC1).</p>	<p>The LC/MS platform is based on the Shimadzu Nexera X2 LC-30AD system, equipped with a Kinetex F5 column (2.6 μm, 3 × 100 mm, Phenomenex) and a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (QTRAP 6500+, AB SCIEX). Metabolites are detected in both electrospray negative and positive ion modes. Five microliters of sample are injected sequentially with LC autosampler. The Kinetex F5 column is maintained at 30°C with a flow rate of 200 μL/min. A gradient was used to separate the compounds consisted of 10 mM ammonium acetate (solvent A) and 100% acetonitrile (solvent B). The gradient started at 0% solvent B for 3 min and increasing linearly to 95% solvent B over 7 min with a 2 min hold before returning the starting mixture during 1min and re-equilibrating for 1 min. QC samples were injected every six or eight samples during acquisition.</p>	<p>MultiQuant 3.0.3 software was used to extract the original MRM data of CC100 KIT metabolites and obtain the peak area of each metabolite for quantification from different samples.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Extract metabolites from cells using 1 mL of pre-chilled 80% methanol, sonicate in an ice bath for 20 min, and allow to stand at -20°C for at least 1 h. Centrifuge at 16,000 g for 20 min at 4°C. Collect the supernatant and concentrate to dryness under vacuum.</p><p>The reconstitution solvent is 50% methanol, and the typical reconstitution volume is 100 μL. The solution is then transferred to a 96-well plate for LC-MS analysis.</p><p>Additionally, to ensure the quality of metabolomics data, a quality control (QC) sample is prepared by pooling equal aliquots of all samples and is used for data normalization. The preparation and analysis of QC samples are identical to those of experimental samples.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Negative ion mode: Source Temperature 500℃, Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1):55, Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2): 60, Curtain Gas (CUR):35, Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF) :-4500V;Positive ion mode: Source Temperature 500℃, Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1):55, Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2):60, Curtain Gas (CUR):35, Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF) :5500V.The mass spectrometer was operated with a dwell time of 3 ms. To establish the metabolite MRM library, each metabolite standard (1 μg/mL) is first analyzed by LC-MS/MS to obtain the optimal MRM transition parameters. Then, the corresponding MRM(Q1/Q3) transitions are measured individually to determine the retention times of 100 central carbon-related metabolites. Typical precursor-product ion pairs for targeted central carbon metabolites are listed in the supplementary information table. A series of diluted standards containing 100 central carbon-related metabolites and internal standards are prepared for LC-MS analysis. Calibration curves are plotted using the peak areas and corresponding concentrations of each standard metabolite and internal standard in the diluted standards, using the least squares method. Calibration curves and correlation coefficients (R2) are shown in Supplementary Table 1. Calibration curves with correlation coefficients (R2) greater than 0.99 are considered acceptable. The quantitative concentration of each metabolite is calculated according to the calibration curve.</p>	<p>MultiQuant 3.0.3 software was used to extract the original MRM data of CC100 KIT metabolites and obtain the peak area of each metabolite for quantification from different samples.&nbsp;</p>

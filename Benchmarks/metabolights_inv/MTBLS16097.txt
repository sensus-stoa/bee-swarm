Study Identifier	MTBLS16097
Study Title	Metabolism-Guided Lattice Strain Engineering for Eradicating Implant Infections via Bacterial Energy Reprogramming
Study Description	<p>Bone implant infections are exacerbated by bacterial biofilm formation, where dynamic metabolic transitions create therapeutic vulnerabilities often missed by conventional static or dynamic therapies. Here, we present a metabolism-guided strategy via lattice strain engineering to reprogram bacterial energetics for full-cycle infection eradication. By constructing a compressed lattice in BaTiO3 through Sr-doping and vacancy engineering (BST-epsilon), we establish a persistent electron sink that forcibly extracts electrons from the bacterial respiratory chain. This intervention arrests early-stage proliferation by switching energy production from oxidative phosphorylation to inefficient glycolysis, a mechanism validated by multi-omics analysis showing drastic ATP depletion. Subsequently, ultrasound activation leverages the strain-induced flexoelectric effect to optimize band bending, lowering the water dissociation barrier to generate ROS and O2. The released oxygen revitalizes dormant bacteria within mature biofilms, rendering them susceptible to oxidative damage and disrupting membrane integrity. Concurrently, the sustained release of Sr2+ reverses infection-induced osteolysis by modulating the OPG/RANKL pathway. In rat models, this metabolism-guided platform achieves complete bacterial elimination and enhances osseointegration. This work establishes lattice strain engineering as a universal strategy to bridge static metabolic interference with dynamic catalytic therapy, offering a new paradigm for the full-cycle management of implant infections.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>S. aureus grown on PH-BST-ε2 scaffolds were collected both before and after US treatment. Cells in the exponential phase were obtained by cryo-centrifugation and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. For the transcriptomic workflow, three independent samples were prepared for the three groups.</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) coupled to a Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Separation was achieved on an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 μm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA) at 40 °C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 0.1% formic acid in water and (B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile, at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min with an injection volume of 3 μL. The gradient was: 0–1 min, 2% B; 1–9 min, 2–50% B; 9–12 min, 50–98% B; 12–14 min, 98% B; 14–14.1 min, 98–2% B; 14.1–16 min, 2% B. Mass spectra were acquired in positive and negative ESI modes (m/z 70–1050) at a resolution of 70,000.</p>	<p>The metabolomic workflow followed the overall strategy used for the transcriptome experiments. Six biological replicates per group were analyzed by LC–MS, carried out on a Vanquish Horizon system coupled to a Q-Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, USA), with all measurements completed by Majorbio Bio-pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Subsequent data processing and bioinformatic interpretation were performed through the Majorbio Cloud Platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com).</p>	<p>Frozen samples were thawed on ice, and 50 mg of each sample was accurately weighed into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube. A 400 μL aliquot of pre-cooled methanol/water (4:1, v/v) extraction solvent containing internal standards was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 30 min, and then incubated at 20 °C for 1 h to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm (4 °C) for 15 min, the supernatant was transferred to a new tube and dried under nitrogen. The dried extract was reconstituted in 100 μL of acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v), vortexed, and centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was transferred to an autosampler vial for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Q Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The instrument was operated in both positive and negative ion modes over an m/z range of 70–1050 at a mass resolution of 70,000. The key parameters were: spray voltage, 3.5 kV (positive) and 3.2 kV (negative); capillary temperature, 320 °C; sheath gas, 35 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas, 10 arbitrary units; probe heater temperature, 350 °C. Full-scan MS data were acquired in profile mode.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed and peak-aligned using MS-DIAL software (v4.9), including peak detection, retention time alignment, and isotope/peak picking. Metabolites were identified by matching accurate mass (mass error &lt; 10 ppm), retention time, and MS/MS fragmentation spectra against public databases including HMDB, MoNA, and mzCloud. Features with a match score above 0.7 were considered putatively annotated. Data normalization and statistical analysis were performed with R (v4.1.2).</p>

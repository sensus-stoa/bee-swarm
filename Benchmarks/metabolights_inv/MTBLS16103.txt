Study Identifier	MTBLS16103
Study Title	Lipidomics of human corneal epithelial cells
Study Description	Prolonged blue-light (BL) exposure from digital devices induces oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, and apoptosis in human corneal epithelial cells (HCECs), yet effective multilevel protective strategies remain limited. Here, we developed a hierarchically assembled composite ocular delivery system, PDNs-SS31@Rhein (P-S@R), by embedding SS-31-loaded Panax notoginseng-derived nanovesicles (PDNs-SS31) within a Rhein/hyaluronic acid/Ca2+ composite hydrogel (Rhein gel). Lipidomic and functional analyses revealed that PDNs were efficiently internalized by HCECs via lipid raft-mediated endocytosis, thereby attenuating BL-induced intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and lipid peroxidation. Loading the mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide SS-31 into PDNs (PDNs-SS31) significantly enhanced intracellular peptide delivery and mitochondrial targeting compared with free SS-31, thereby conferring superior mitochondrial protection. Meanwhile, the Rhein gel provided effective BL filtration and anti-inflammatory activity that was associated with inhibition of the NF-κB pathway. In BL-injured HCECs, P-S@R markedly attenuated the loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, the accumulation of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, lipid peroxidation, and inflammatory cytokine release, outperforming Rhein gel or PDNs-SS31 alone. These protective effects were closely associated with the restoration of PI3K-Akt/mTOR signaling and the concomitant suppression of ASK1 expression, together with reduced downstream JNK/caspase-3 signaling. The protective effects were further confirmed in a mouse model of BL-induced corneal injury, in which P-S@R treatment reduced corneal fluorescein staining and alleviated central corneal edema. Collectively, P-S@R integrates optical shielding, mitochondria-targeted repair, and anti-inflammatory modulation, offering a promising multicomponent strategy against BL-induced ocular surface injury.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cells were harvested by trypsinization, centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 minutes, and stored in -80℃.</p>	<p>Lipid separation was performed using&nbsp;a Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system equipped with an Accucore C30 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 2.6 μm; Thermo Fisher Scientific).&nbsp;Mobile phase A consisted of 10 mM ammonium acetate in acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of 2 mM ammonium acetate in acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (10:88:2, v/v/v) containing 0.02% formic acid. The injection volume was 3 μL, the flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was 40 °C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Peak intensities were normalized, and features with a relative standard deviation greater than 30% among QC samples were removed.&nbsp;Multivariate analyses comprised PCA and OPLS-DA using the R package ropls (version 1.6.2). Differential lipids were defined as those with OPLS-DA VIP &gt; 1 and Student's t-test P &lt; 0.05,&nbsp;with P values adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. Differential lipids were annotated to KEGG pathways, and pathway enrichment was evaluated using Fisher’s exact test (Python package scipy.stats) .</p>	<p>Fresh Panax notoginseng juice and purified PDNs (n = 4) were used for lipid extraction. Briefly, 200 μL of each sample was mixed with 80 μL of methanol and 400 μL of MTBE, sonicated at 5 °C and 40 kHz for 30 min, and incubated at −20 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation at 13,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, 350 μL of the upper organic phase was collected, dried under nitrogen, and reconstituted in 100 μL of isopropanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). </p>	<p>Mass spectra were acquired in positive- and negative-ion modes using&nbsp;a Q Exactive HF-X Orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization source. Data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition mode over an m/z range of 200-2000. The spray voltages were +3000 V and −3000 V in positive- and negative-ion modes, respectively.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using&nbsp;LipidSearch for peak detection, integration, retention-time correction, alignment, and lipid identification based on MS and MS/MS information.&nbsp;</p>

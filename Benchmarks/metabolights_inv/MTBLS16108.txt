Study Protocol Description	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2527'>FA with a purity greater than 98% was evaluated in AGS and MKN-45 gastric cancer cells using CCK-8, EdU incorporation, colony formation, flow-cytometric, wound-healing, and Transwell assays. RNA sequencing and untargeted metabolomic profiling were performed in AGS cells.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2980'>Chromatographic separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) maintained at 45 °C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B), delivered at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min. The injection volume was 5 μL. MS data were acquired using a HESI source in both positive and negative ion modes. The data acquisition mode was Data-Dependent Acquisition (DDA) with a Full MS/dd-MS2 (Top 10) scan type.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='3136'>Prior to pattern recognition, the raw data were processed using the metabolomics software XCMS (v4.5.1) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, and retention time alignment. Metabolite identification was performed based on multiple dimensions, including retention time (RT), accurate mass, MS/MS fragmentation, and isotopic distribution. The identification was conducted by searching against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), LipidMaps (v2.3), METLIN database, and an in-house local database (LuMet-Animal 3.0).</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='3136'>LuMet-Animal 3.0 is an LC-MS/MS database established through a hardware-standardized methodology and based on authentic standards. The database comprehensively covers more than 10,000 common and important metabolites, including over 2,000 compounds verified with authentic standards (containing three-dimensional identification information: RT, MS1, and MS2). It mainly comprises 13 major categories of metabolites, such as amino acids and their derivatives, organic acids and their derivatives, carbohydrates and their derivatives, organic heterocyclics, nucleosides and their derivatives, indoles and their derivatives, steroids and their derivatives, bile acids and their derivatives, acylcarnitines, lysophospholipids, benzenoids, and exposome-related compounds. It covers dozens of important metabolic pathways, including glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, the TCA cycle, phenylalanine metabolism, tryptophan metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, steroid hormone biosynthesis, primary bile acid biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Transfer and Resuspend: The samples were sequentially transferred into a 1.5 mL EP tube using a total of 600 μL of pre-cooled methanol-water (V:V=4:1) containing mixed internal standards, and thoroughly resuspended by pipetting.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Ultrasonic Homogenization: The mixture was subjected to ultrasonic homogenization in an ice bath (1620 W, 10 min; pulsed on for 6 s and off for 4 s).</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Ultrasonic Extraction and Cold Incubation: The samples were then ultrasonicated in an ice-water bath for 20 min, followed by overnight incubation at -40 °C.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Centrifugation and Drying: After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, 400 μL of the supernatant was transferred into an LC-MS autosampler vial and evaporated to dryness.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Reconstitution: The dried extracts were reconstituted in 300 μL of methanol-water (V:V=1:4), vortexed for 1 min, ultrasonicated for 10 min, and incubated overnight at -40 °C.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Final Centrifugation: The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C. Subsequently, 150 μL of the supernatant was transferred into an LC-MS autosampler vial equipped with a spring-loaded insert for LC-MS analysis.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>QC Preparation: Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by mixing equal volumes of the extracted solutions from all samples.</p><p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='2912'>Note: All extraction reagents were pre-cooled at 4 °C prior to use.</p>	<p _msttexthash='32273813' _msthash='3058'>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source in both positive and negative ion modes. The spray voltages were set at 3800 V for positive mode and -3200 V for negative mode. The capillary temperature and auxiliary gas heater temperature were both maintained at 320 °C and 350 °C, respectively. The sheath gas and auxiliary gas flow rates were set at 35 and 8 Arb, respectively. The S-lens RF level was 50. Data were acquired in Full MS/dd-MS2 (Top 10) mode with a mass range of 70–1050 m/z. The resolutions for Full MS and MS/MS were set at 60,000 and 15,000, respectively. Normalized collision energies (NCE) were set at 10, 20, and 40 for stepped NCE fragmentation.</p>	<p>Prior to pattern recognition, the raw data were processed using the metabolomics software XCMS (v4.5.1) for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, and retention time alignment. Metabolite identification was performed based on multiple dimensions, including retention time (RT), accurate mass, MS/MS fragmentation, and isotopic distribution. The identification was conducted by searching against the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), LipidMaps (v2.3), METLIN database, and an in-house local database (LuMet-Animal 3.0).</p><p>LuMet-Animal 3.0 is an LC-MS/MS database established through a hardware-standardized methodology and based on authentic standards. The database comprehensively covers more than 10,000 common and important metabolites, including over 2,000 compounds verified with authentic standards (containing three-dimensional identification information: RT, MS1, and MS2). It mainly comprises 13 major categories of metabolites, such as amino acids and their derivatives, organic acids and their derivatives, carbohydrates and their derivatives, organic heterocyclics, nucleosides and their derivatives, indoles and their derivatives, steroids and their derivatives, bile acids and their derivatives, acylcarnitines, lysophospholipids, benzenoids, and exposome-related compounds. It covers dozens of important metabolic pathways, including glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, the TCA cycle, phenylalanine metabolism, tryptophan metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, steroid hormone biosynthesis, primary bile acid biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism.</p>

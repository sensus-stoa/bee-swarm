Study Identifier	MTBLS16133
Study Title	Bile Acid Metabolomic Profiling of a Three-Member Synthetic Microbial Consortium
Study Description	This study investigated bile acid metabolism by a defined three-member synthetic microbial consortium consisting of Clostridium scindens, Eubacterium limosum, and Eggerthella lenta. Four experimental groups were established: G1, a sterile control; G2, C. scindens alone; G3, C. scindens and E. limosum; and G4, all three bacterial species. Glycocholic acid (GCA) and taurocholic acid (TCA) were supplied as bile acid substrates. Samples were collected from independent cultures at 36 and 60 hours, with three biological replicates per group and time point (24 cultures in total). Mass spectrometry data were generated to characterize bile acid transformation across the microbial combinations, with particular interest in deconjugation, 7α-dehydroxylation, and downstream bile acid modifications.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples were obtained from an in vitro synthetic microbial consortium experiment investigating bile acid transformation. Four experimental groups were established: G1, a sterile medium control; G2, Clostridium scindens; G3, Clostridium scindens and Eubacterium limosum; and G4, Clostridium scindens, Eubacterium limosum, and Eggerthella lenta. Glycocholic acid (GCA) and taurocholic acid (TCA) were supplied as bile acid substrates.</p><p>Samples were collected at 36 and 60 h from independent culture vessels, with three biological replicates per group and time point, resulting in 24 independent cultures. Liquid samples were collected for targeted bile acid metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a SCIEX ExionLC AD ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system equipped with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm).</p><p>Mobile phase A consisted of ultrapure water containing 0.01% acetic acid and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate, while mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile containing 0.01% acetic acid. The flow rate was 0.35 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40°C, and the injection volume was 3 µL.</p><p>The gradient program was as follows: 0 min, 5% B; 1 min, 40% B; 7 min, 50% B; 12 min, 75% B; and 14 min, 95% B. The mobile phase was maintained at 95% B until 16 min, followed by re-equilibration to 5% B at 17.5 min.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired using Analyst software version 1.6.3 (SCIEX). Chromatographic peak integration and quantitative data processing were performed using MultiQuant software version 3.0.3 (SCIEX). Targeted bile acid signals were processed based on scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions, with internal standards included for quantitative analysis.</p>	<p>An aliquot of 50 µL of each liquid sample was mixed with 2.5 µL of an internal standard mixture (10 µg/mL) and 197.5 µL of methanol/acetonitrile (2:8, v/v). The mixture was vortexed at 2,500 rpm for 10 min and incubated at −20°C for 10 min to facilitate protein precipitation.</p><p>Samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatants were passed through a protein precipitation plate prior to LC-MS/MS analysis. HPLC-grade acetonitrile and methanol were obtained from Merck, and analytical standards were obtained from CNW and IsoReag.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted using a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) Turbo Ion-Spray source, operating in negative ion mode.</p><p>The ion source temperature was set to 550°C, the ion spray voltage was −4,500 V, and the curtain gas pressure was 35 psi. Bile acids were monitored using scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM). Compound-specific declustering potentials (DP) and collision energies (CE) were optimized for individual MRM transitions.</p><p>Data acquisition was performed using Analyst software version 1.6.3 (SCIEX), and metabolite quantification was conducted using MultiQuant software version 3.0.3 (SCIEX). MRM transitions were scheduled according to the retention times of the target metabolites.</p>	<p>Targeted bile acid analysis was performed using scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) on a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ LC-MS/MS system. Analytical standards were obtained from CNW and IsoReag. Compound-specific MRM transitions were monitored using optimized declustering potentials and collision energies. Chromatographic peak integration and metabolite quantification were performed using MultiQuant software version 3.0.3 (SCIEX), with internal standards included in sample preparation.</p>

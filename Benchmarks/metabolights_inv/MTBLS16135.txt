Study Title	Hypsizygus marmoreus and β-Muricholic Acid Attenuate High-Fat-Diet-Associated Hepatic Steatosis: Integrated Mycobiome, Metabolomic, and Transcriptomic Analyses
Study Description	<p>Background: Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is an increasingly important health concern in children and adolescents. Gut microbial dysbiosis and microbe-derived metabolites may contribute to hepatic metabolic dysfunction, but the potential roles of the edible mushroom Hypsizygus marmoreus and its metabolites remain incompletely understood. Objective: To characterize gut fungal differences associated with MAFLD, evaluate the effects of H. marmoreus in a high-fat-diet mouse model, and investigate beta-muricholic acid (beta-MCA) as a candidate metabolite associated with these effects. Methods: Fecal samples from 31 participants with MAFLD and 16 healthy controls were analyzed using fungal internal transcribed spacer sequencing. Fungal diversity and differential taxa were evaluated using community diversity analyses and LEfSe. The effects of H. marmoreus intervention were assessed in high-fat-diet-fed mice by liver histology, organ and adipose tissue weights, serum aminotransferases, and lipid profiles. Untargeted metabolomic analyses of mouse feces and liver, together with clinical plasma metabolomics, were used to prioritize candidate metabolites. beta-MCA was subsequently evaluated in a mouse intervention, and liver transcriptomic data were analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes and enriched pathways. Results: Fungal community structure differed between participants with MAFLD and healthy controls (Bray-Curtis-based PERMANOVA, P = 0.016). LEfSe analysis suggested enrichment of Hypsizygus-related taxa and the family Lyophyllaceae in healthy controls. In high-fat-diet-fed mice, H. marmoreus intervention was associated with reduced hepatic steatosis, lower liver and epididymal fat weights, decreased serum AST and ALT levels, and an improved lipid profile. beta-MCA intervention showed a similar pattern of phenotypic improvement. Liver transcriptomic analysis identified 295 upregulated and 439 downregulated genes using the reported thresholds of |log2 fold change| &gt;= 1 and nominal P &lt; 0.05. Pathway enrichment highlighted PI3K-AKT signaling and the biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids. These findings suggest that beta-MCA-associated changes in lipid metabolism and signaling may contribute to the observed phenotype; however, the proposed involvement of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling and autophagy requires direct experimental validation. Conclusions: The findings support further investigation of H. marmoreus and beta-MCA as potential modulators of diet-associated hepatic steatosis. The human fungal findings are associative, and additional targeted metabolomic, molecular, and functional experiments are needed to establish the causal relationship between beta-MCA, signaling pathways, autophagy, and hepatic protection.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples (25 mg±1 mg) were taken, mixed with beads and 500 μL of extraction solution (MeOH:ACN:H2O, 2:2:1 (v/v)). The extraction solution contain deuterated internal standards. The mixed solution were vortexed for 30 s.</p><p><br></p>	<p>For polar metabolites, LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted of (A) water containing 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonium hydroxide (pH 9.75) and (B) acetonitrile. The auto-sampler temperature was 4 ℃, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed with an in-house program.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Then the mixed samples were homogenized (35 Hz,4 min) and sonicated for 5 min in 4 ℃ water bath, the step repeat for three times. The samples were incubated for 1 h at -40 ℃ to precipitate proteins. Then the samples ware centrifuged at 12000 rpm (RCF=13800(×g), R= 8.6cm) for 15 min at 4 ℃. The supernatant was transferred to a fresh glass vial for analysis.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the full scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following: sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary temperature 320 ℃, full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000, collision energy: SNCE 20/30/40, spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV (negative), respectively.</p><p><br></p>	<p>which was developed using R and based on XCMS, for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. The R package and the BiotreeDB(V3.0) were applied in metabolite identification. Reference: Zhou, Z., Luo, M., Zhang, H., Yin, Y., Cai, Y., &amp; Zhu, Z. J. (2022). Metabolite annotation from knowns to unknowns through knowledge-guided multi-layer metabolic networking. Nature communications, 13(1), 6656.</p>

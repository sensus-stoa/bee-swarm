Study Title	Multi-Omics Reveals Divergent Gut Microbiome-Metabolome Associations Between High-Altitude Snowcock and Low-Altitude Pheasant
Study Description	The gut microecological mechanisms underlying high-altitude adaptation remain poorly understood. The Snowcock (Tetraogallus tibetanus), a high-altitude bird endemic to the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau, and the low-altitude Pheasant exhibit striking contrasts in altitude and diet, providing an ideal comparative model. Fecal samples from six Snowcocks and six Pheasants were analyzed using 16S rRNA gene sequencing and UPLC-MS/MS untargeted metabolomics. Multi-omics integration was performed via Procrustes analysis, correlation networks, and Random Forest classification. Snowcocks displayed significantly lower alpha diversity and distinct beta diversity clustering compared with Pheasants. Their microbiota was dominated by Actinobacteriota and enriched in cold-tolerant genera such as Psychrobacillus, whereas the Pheasant microbiota showed higher diversity with prevalent Firmicutes and Bacteroidota. Metabolomics revealed that differential metabolites enriched in Pheasants were predominantly mapped to amino acid metabolism and terpenoid biosynthesis pathways, while those enriched in Snowcocks were linked to glycerophospholipid metabolism and antibiotic intermediate-related pathways. Multi-omics integration demonstrated a highly coordinated microbiome–metabolome association, and the Random Forest model achieved perfect discrimination between the two species. The gut microecology and metabolic profiles of Snowcocks and Pheasants show systematic divergence. The simplified microbiota enriched with psychrophilic genera and the specific glycerophospholipid metabolic signatures in Snowcocks likely represent key microecological adaptations to the harsh plateau environment, providing multi-omics insights into high-altitude adaptation in wild birds

Study Protocol Description	<p>The researchers used non-invasive collection methods to collect six fresh faecal samples from wild snowcock (Tsotsin County, Tibet, 30°58′15″N, 84°51′26″E, 4,820 m above sea level) and pheasants (Tianzhu County, Gansu, 36°40′12″N, 102°16′10″E, 2,640 m above sea level).</p>	<p>The main instrument platforms used are: 1. Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography: Waters UPLC Acquity I-Class PLUS</p><p>2. High resolution mass spectrometry: Waters UPLC Xevo G2-XS QTof</p><p>3. Column: Acquity UPLC HSS T3 1.8um 2.1*100mm, Waters.</p>	<p>The raw data collected with MassLynx V4.2 were processed by Progenesis QI software to do peak extraction, peak alignment and other data processing operations, and identified based on the online METLIN database of Progenesis QI software and the self-built library of Bemac, while the theoretical fragments were identified, and the deviations of the mass counts were all within 100ppm.</p>	<p>Six fresh faecal samples were collected. Strict aseptic practices were followed throughout the experiment: sterile gloves, sampling spoons and freezing tubes were used. The samples were stored at 4°C, transported to the laboratory within 24 hours, dispensed in a biosafety cabinet and stored at -80°C until subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>The Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof High Resolution Mass Spectrometer is capable of primary and secondary mass spectrometry data acquisition in MSe mode under the control of acquisition software (MassLynx V4.2, Waters). In each data acquisition cycle, dual-channel data acquisition can be performed for both low and high collision energies. The low collision energy is 2 V, the high collision energy interval is 10~40 V, and the scanning frequency is 0.2 s. The parameters of the ESI ion source are as follows: capillary voltage: 2000 V (positive ion mode) or -1500 V (negative ion mode); conical bore voltage: 30 V; ion source temperature: 150°C; desolvent gas temperature: 500°C; backblast gas flow rate: 50 L/h; desolvent gas flow rate: 800 L/h. The ion source temperature is 150°C; desolvent gas temperature is 500°C; backblast gas flow rate: 50 L/h; desolvent gas flow rate: 800 L/h. Flow rate: 800L/h.</p>	<p>At its core is broadly targeted metabolomics (broad targeting). Rather than relying on a single technology, it is a set of composite strategies integrating the advantages of off-target discovery and on-target quantification, aiming to achieve both high-throughput coverage and precise qualitative quantification.</p>

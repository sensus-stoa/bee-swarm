Study Title	Multi-omics reveals response strategies and physiological thresholds of Skeletonema subsalsum to aquatic salinization
Study Description	<p>Climate change and anthropogenic activities are accelerating salinization in freshwater and brackish ecosystems worldwide. This change poses severe threats to aquatic biodiversity, community structure, and ecosystem functioning. As foundational primary producers, euryhaline algae may gain competitive advantages under rising salinity, potentially restructuring phytoplankton communities and altering ecosystem productivity. Diatoms drive aquatic primary production and biogeochemical cycles. Salinity-sensitive euryhaline diatoms like Skeletonema subsalsum, which are growing prevalent, act as key models to assess salinization hazards. Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying salinity adaptation in S. subsalsum is therefore critical for revealing ecological responses to salinity changes. Here, we employed S. subsalsum to examine responses across an environmentally relevant salinity gradient (0-12). By integrating morphological and physiological measurements with transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic analyses, we have revealed the response mechanism and identified candidate indicators and physiological thresholds. Exposure to elevated salinity significantly altered cell morphology, growth rates, proline accumulation, and key respiratory and antioxidant enzyme activities. Multi-omics integration revealed a coordinated energy-reallocation network involving proline- and taurine-mediated osmotic adjustment, cysteine-related redox regulation, and enhanced mitochondrial energy production. Morphological plasticity may provide additional buffering against salinity. Notably, cross-level responses converged at salinity 6, indicating a transition point in the adaptive response to salinity. The correlation analysis further identified easily measurable indicators for early detection of salinity, such as height/diameter ratio, proline content, and COX activity. These findings reveal mechanisms underlying diatom salinity adaptation and identify candidate indicators and thresholds for risk assessment, informing predictions of species adaptation and ecosystem stability in salinizing ecosystems.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Skeletonema subsalsum diatom cultures were cultivated under different salinity treatments. Cells were harvested at the set sampling time by centrifugation, washed quickly, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at 80&nbsp;until metabolite extraction. Three biological replicates were set for each salinity group.</p>	<p>The separation was performed on UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 2.6 μm). Mobile phase A: 0.1% formic acid in water; Mobile phase B: acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, column temperature was 40&nbsp;with gradient elution program.</p>	<p>Raw .raw files were converted to mzML format for deposition, and peak picking and alignment were processed by the metabolomics bioinformatics pipeline.</p>	<p>The frozen algal samples were ground with liquid nitrogen. Metabolites were extracted using methanol-acetonitrile-water solvent mixture. Quality control (QC) pooled samples and solvent blank samples were prepared and tested alongside experimental samples for system stability evaluation.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry detection was performed on Q Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with ESI ion source. Both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI) ionization modes were acquired. The scan range was m/z 60–1000. Spray voltage, capillary temperature, sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set according to standard untargeted metabolomics parameters.</p>	<p>Metabolite annotation was performed by matching accurate mass and MS/MS spectra against mzCloud, MassBank and HMDB databases. Mass tolerance was set for precursor and fragment ions.</p>

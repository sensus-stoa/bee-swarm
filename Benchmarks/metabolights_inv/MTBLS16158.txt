Study Protocol Description	<p>Human VSMCs with lentivirus-mediated IGFBP2 overexpression and control hVSMCs were cultured in calcification medium for 7 days, with medium renewal every 2 days. At harvest, cells were washed three times with prechilled calcium- and phosphate-free PBS, followed by trypsinization using 0.01% trypsin for cell collection. The cell pellets were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and then stored at −80 °C.</p>	<p>The HPIC separation was carried out using an Thermo Scientific Dionex ICS-6000 HPIC</p><p>System (Thermo Scientific), equipped with Dionex IonPac AS11-HC (2× 250 mm) and AG11-</p><p>HC (2 mm×50 mm) columns. The mobile phase A was 100 mM NaOH in water, the mobile</p><p>D was ultrapure water . Another pumping system was used to supply the solvent (2 mM acetic</p><p>acid in methanol) and solvent mixed with effluent before entering ESI (flow rate of 0.15</p><p>mL/min). The column temperature was set at 30 ℃. The auto-sampler temperature was set at</p><p>4 ℃ and the injection volume was 5&nbsp;μL.</p>	<p>Calibration solutions were subjected to HPIC-MRM-MS analysis using the methods</p><p>described above. Figure S1-Calibration summarizes the results for the calibration curves</p><p>where y is the peak areas of analyte, and x is the concentration (nmol/L) for analyte. Least</p><p>squares method was used for the regression fitting. 1/x weighting was applied in the curve</p><p>fitting since it provided highest accuracy and correlation coefficient (R2). The level was</p><p>excluded from the calibration if the accuracy of calibration was not within 80–120%. The calibration standard solution was diluted stepwise, with a dilution factor of 2. These</p><p>standard solutions were subjected to HPIC-MRM-MS analysis. The signal-to-noise ratios</p><p>(S/N) were used to determine the lower limits of detection (LLODs) and lower limits of</p><p>quantitation (LLOQs). The LLODs and LLOQs were defined as the analyte concentrations</p><p>that led to peaks with signal-to-noise ratios (S/N) of 3 and 10, respectively, according to</p><p>the US FDA guideline for bioanalytical method validation.</p>	<p>After adding 500 μL precooled MeOH/H2O (3/1, v/v), the samples were vortexed for 30</p><p>s. The samples were precooled in dry ice, repeated freeze-thaw three times in liquid</p><p>nitrogen, followed by vortexed for 30s and sonicated for 15 min in ice-water bath. After</p><p>incubating at -40 ℃ for 1 h, the samples were centrifuged at 12000 rpm (RCF=13800(×g),</p><p>R= 8.6cm) and 4 ℃ for 15 min. A 400&nbsp;μL aliquot of the clear supernatant were collected</p><p>and dried by spin. Then the samples were reconstituted with 200 μL of purified water.</p><p>Reconstituted samples were vortexed before filtration through the centrifuge tube filter,</p><p>and were subsequently transferred to inserts in injection vials for HPIC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>An AB SCIEX 6500 QTRAP+ triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (AB Sciex), equipped</p><p>with an electrospray ionization (ESI) interface, was applied for assay development. Typical</p><p>ion source parameters were: IonSpray Voltage = -4500 V, temperature = 450 ℃, Ion</p><p>Source Gas 1 = 45 psi, Ion Source Gas 2 = 45 psi, Curtain Gas = 30 psi.</p><p>The MRM parameters for each of the targeted analytes were optimized using flow injection</p><p>analysis, by injecting the standard solutions of the individual analytes, into the API source</p><p>of the mass spectrometer. Several most sensitive transitions were used in the MRM scan</p><p>mode to optimize the collision energy for each Q1/Q3 pair&nbsp;(Table S2). Among the</p><p>optimized MRM transitions per analyte, the Q1/Q3 pairs that showed the highest</p><p>sensitivity and selectivity were selected as ‘quantifier’ for quantitative monitoring. The</p><p>additional transitions acted as ‘qualifier’ for the purpose of verifying the identity of the</p><p>target analytes.</p>	<p>Table S3&nbsp;lists the resulting lower-limits of detection and quantitation (LLODs and</p><p>LLOQs), and the LLODs ranged from 0.13 to 148.31 nmol/L, the LLOQs ranged from</p><p>0.26 to 296.62 nmol/L for the target analyte. Correlation coefficients (R2) of regression</p><p>fitting were above 0.9920 for all the analytes, indicating a good quantitative relationship</p><p>between the MS responses and the analyte concentrations, which was satisfying for</p><p>targeted metabolomics analysis&nbsp;(Table S3).</p><p>Table S4&nbsp;lists the analytical recoveries and relative standard deviations of the QC samples,</p><p>with 5 technical replicates. The recoveries determined were 83.9 %-115.3 % for all the</p><p>analytes, with all the RSDs below 7.3 % (n =5).</p><p><br></p><p>The analysis metrics indicated that the method allowed accurate quantitation of the targeted</p><p>metabolites in the biological sample, in the concentration range described as above.The quantification results were shown in&nbsp;Table S5. The final concentration (CF,&nbsp;nmol/L)</p><p>equals the calculated concentration (CC,&nbsp;nmol/L) multiplied by the dilution factor (Dil). The</p><p>metabolite concentration (CM,&nbsp;nmol/10^7 cell) equals the final concentration (CF,&nbsp;nmol/L)</p><p>multiplied by the final volume (V,&nbsp;μL)&nbsp;the sample experiment concentration factor (CF),&nbsp;divided</p><p>by the&nbsp;total number of cells in the sample. NA means that the targeted metabolites were not</p><p>detectable in the corresponding samples</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS16161
Study Title	TREM2-Positive Macrophages and Renal Ceramide Dysregulation in Diabetic Kidney Disease
Study Description	This study investigated the effects of TREM2 deficiency and high-fat diet/streptozotocin (HFD/STZ)-induced diabetes on renal lipid metabolism in mice. Kidney tissue samples were collected from four experimental groups, with five biological replicates per group (20 samples in total): (A) TREM2 knockout mice maintained on a normal diet, (B) TREM2 knockout mice subjected to HFD/STZ treatment, (C) wild-type control mice maintained on a normal diet, and (D) wild-type mice subjected to HFD/STZ treatment. Untargeted lipidomic profiling was performed using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS) on a Vanquish UHPLC system and an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining equal aliquots of experimental samples and analyzed repeatedly to assess analytical stability and reproducibility. Mass spectrometry data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed using XCMS for peak detection, retention time alignment, and peak integration. Lipid annotation was performed using an in-house R-based workflow and the LipidBlast 2022 database. Following data preprocessing, 3,585 annotated lipids were retained for analysis. Multivariate statistical analyses, including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), were performed to investigate lipidomic differences between experimental groups. This dataset provides a resource for investigating renal lipid remodeling, particularly alterations in ceramide and sphingolipid metabolism, associated with TREM2 deficiency and diabetic kidney disease.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Kidney tissue samples were obtained from wild-type C57BL/6J mice and TREM2 knockout mice on a C57BL/6J genetic background. A total of 20 kidney tissue samples were collected from four experimental groups, with five biological replicates per group: Group A, TREM2 knockout mice maintained on a normal diet; Group B, TREM2 knockout mice subjected to high-fat diet and streptozotocin (HFD/STZ)-induced diabetes; Group C, wild-type control mice maintained on a normal diet; and Group D, wild-type mice subjected to HFD/STZ-induced diabetes. All tissue samples were stored at -80 °C upon receipt at the analytical laboratory until lipid extraction and LC-MS/MS analysis. Approximately 25 mg of kidney tissue was used for lipidomic analysis from each sample.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation of lipids was performed using a Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a Phenomenex Kinetex C18 reversed-phase column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 2.6 µm).</p><p>The mobile phase A consisted of water/acetonitrile (40:60, v/v) containing 10 mmol/L ammonium formate, as specified in the main experimental methods section of the analytical report. The mobile phase B consisted of isopropanol/acetonitrile (90:10, v/v) containing 10 mmol/L ammonium formate. The injection volume was 2 µL.</p><p>The UHPLC system was coupled to a Thermo Fisher Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 120 high-resolution mass spectrometer. Chromatographic analyses were performed for both positive and negative ionization modes.</p>	<p>Raw mass spectrometry data acquired in positive and negative ionization modes were converted to mzXML format using the msconvert tool from ProteoWizard. The converted files were processed using the XCMS software package with the CentWave algorithm for peak detection, feature extraction, retention time correction, peak integration, and chromatographic alignment. The minfrac parameter was set to 0.5, and the cutoff parameter for annotation was set to 0.3.</p><p>Data preprocessing included feature filtering based on relative standard deviation (RSD) and missing-value frequency. Features with poor reproducibility or excessive missing values were removed. Missing values were imputed using half of the minimum observed value, and peak intensities were normalized using the total ion current (TIC) normalization method.</p><p>After preprocessing, 36,313 features and 3,585 annotated lipid metabolites were retained. The processed data were further analyzed using principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) implemented in SIMCA software (version 18.0.1). OPLS-DA model robustness was evaluated using permutation testing.</p>	<p>Approximately 25 mg of kidney tissue was weighed on dry ice and transferred into a microcentrifuge tube containing 200 µL of ultrapure water. Steel beads were added, and the samples were homogenized at 35 Hz for 4 min, followed by sonication in an ice-water bath for 5 min. This homogenization and sonication procedure was repeated three times.</p><p>Subsequently, 480 µL of extraction solvent (methyl tert-butyl ether/methanol, MTBE:MeOH = 5:1, v/v), precooled to -40 °C and containing isotopically labeled internal standards, was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min, and incubated at -40 °C for 1 h. Samples were then centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and 300 µL of the supernatant was collected and evaporated to dryness under vacuum.</p><p>Pooled quality control (QC) samples were prepared by combining equal-volume aliquots from the experimental samples to evaluate analytical reproducibility and instrument stability. QC samples were analyzed in both positive and negative electrospray ionization modes. Blank samples were also included during LC-MS analysis to assess potential carryover and background interference.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 120 high-resolution mass spectrometer coupled to a Vanquish UHPLC system. Data were acquired using an electrospray ionization (ESI) source in both positive (ESI+) and negative (ESI-) ionization modes under the control of Xcalibur software (version 4.4, Thermo Fisher Scientific).</p><p>The spray voltage was set to +3.8 kV in positive ionization mode and -3.4 kV in negative ionization mode. The capillary temperature was maintained at 320 °C for both modes. The sheath gas flow rate was set to 30 arbitrary units (Arb), and the auxiliary gas flow rate was set to 10 Arb.</p><p>The full MS resolution was set to 60,000, while the MS/MS resolution was set to 15,000. Tandem mass spectra were acquired using normalized collision energies (NCE) of 15, 30, and 45. Full-scan MS and MS/MS data were collected for lipid profiling and subsequent lipid annotation in both ionization modes.</p>	<p>Lipid identification and annotation were performed using an in-house R-based computational workflow integrated with XCMS and the LipidBlast 2022 spectral database. Mass spectrometric features detected in positive and negative ionization modes were annotated through spectral matching against reference lipid spectra.</p><p>Lipid identities were primarily assigned based on available tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) fragmentation information and database matching. MS/MS-supported annotations were prioritized over assignments based solely on precursor mass.</p><p>Following data processing and lipid annotation, a total of 3,585 lipid metabolites were annotated in the kidney tissue lipidomics dataset. The annotated lipid species were subsequently used for lipid classification, comparative statistical analysis, and investigation of lipid metabolic alterations among the experimental groups.</p>

Study Title	Untargeted LC-MS metabolomics and 13C isotope tracing of Saccharomyces cerevisiae responses to Weissella confusa-conditioned medium
Study Description	This study contains untargeted LC-MS metabolomics and 13C isotope-tracing datasets generated to characterize extracellular metabolic remodeling during the interaction between Weissella confusa AR1038 and Saccharomyces cerevisiae F23. Metabolomic profiling was performed on AR1038-conditioned medium (CA), the corresponding supernatant after subsequent F23 cultivation (CAF), and F23-cultivated YPDM supernatant (SF). In parallel, 13C isotope tracing using [U-13C6]-glucose was used to examine the utilization of AR1038-derived extracellular metabolites by F23. The datasets include metabolite annotations, relative abundance profiles, and isotope-labeling information supporting the analysis of amino acid remodeling, particularly arginine depletion and ornithine-associated metabolic responses.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Unlabeled extracellular metabolomics samples were generated from the AR1038–F23 sequential cultivation system. AR1038-conditioned medium (CA) was obtained after cultivation of Weissella confusa AR1038 in YPDM followed by removal of bacterial cells by sterile filtration. Saccharomyces cerevisiae F23 was subsequently cultivated in CA, and the resulting culture supernatant was collected as CAF. A reference group was generated by cultivating F23 in fresh YPDM, and the corresponding culture supernatant was collected as SF. Samples were immediately frozen after collection and stored at −80°C until metabolomic analysis. Three independent biological replicates were prepared for each experimental group.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid for positive-ion mode or 5 mM ammonium acetate for negative-ion mode, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The gradient was 0–1.0 min, 1% B; 1.0–8.0 min, 1%–99% B; 8.0–10.0 min, 99% B; 10.0–10.1 min, 99%–1% B; and 10.1–12.0 min, 1% B. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min, the column temperature was 35°C, the autosampler temperature was 4°C, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS data were converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard. The converted data were processed using an in-house R-based workflow incorporating XCMS for peak detection, peak extraction, retention-time alignment, and peak integration. Positive- and negative-ion datasets were processed separately and subsequently combined for downstream metabolomic analysis. Pooled QC samples were included to monitor analytical performance throughout the LC-MS sequence.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction, 100 μL of culture supernatant was transferred to a microcentrifuge tube and mixed with 300 μL of cold methanol containing internal standards. Samples were vortexed for 30 s, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min, and incubated at −40°C for 1 h to precipitate proteins. Samples were then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was transferred to a fresh glass vial for LC-MS analysis. Quality-control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of supernatants from all study samples and were analyzed together with the experimental samples.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric data were acquired using an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) controlled by Xcalibur software (version 4.4). The sheath gas flow rate was 45 Arb and the auxiliary gas flow rate was 15 Arb. The capillary temperature was 350°C. Full-MS resolution was 60,000 and MS/MS resolution was 15,000. Normalized collision energies were 11, 32, and 64. Spray voltages were 4.0 kV in positive-ion mode and −3.8 kV in negative-ion mode.</p>	<p>Metabolite features were annotated by comparison of the acquired mass-spectrometric information with the in-house BiotreeDB MS/MS spectral database (version 2.1). Metabolite annotations were assigned using the spectral-matching algorithm implemented in the analytical workflow, with an annotation-score cutoff greater than 0.6. The resulting annotation table, including metabolite names, mass-to-charge ratios, retention times, ionization modes, and MS/MS matching scores, was used for downstream statistical and pathway analyses.</p>

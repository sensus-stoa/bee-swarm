Study Title	LaCCoAOMT-mediated phenylpropanoid remodeling contrib-utes to root-knot nematode resistance in sponge gourd
Study Description	<p>Sponge gourd (Luffa aegyptiaca L.), a nutrient-rich cucurbit widely cultivated across Asia and Africa, possesses high edible, medicinal, and industrial value. However, its production is increasingly threatened by the root-knot nematode Meloidogyne incognita. In this study, we evaluated 39 sponge gourd accessions for resistance to M. incognita and selected CQ6 (highly resistant) and CQ1 (susceptible) as contrasting genotypes for subsequent analyses. To uncover the molecular basis of resistance, we performed integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses on these contrasting genotypes. Our results highlighted the phenylpropanoid pathway as a key defense-related metabolic route, with L-phenylalanine playing a central role. Among lignin biosynthesis genes, caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase (LaCCoAOMT) exhibited significant induction upon nematode infection. Subcellular localization showed that GFP:LaCCoAOMT is distributed in the plasma membrane and nucleus. Functional analysis using virus-induced gene silencing revealed that silencing LaCCoAOMT enhanced nematode infection, accompanied by reduced CCoAOMT activity and lignin accumulation, as well as impaired plant growth under nematode stress. These findings demonstrate that LaCCoAOMT-mediated lignin deposition is essential for M. incognita resistance in sponge gourd. This study provides valuable insights into the lignin-based defense mechanisms in sponge gourd and lays a theoretical foundation for breeding nematode-resistant cultivars.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Sponge gourd (L. aegyptiaca) varieties were bred at the Chongqing Academy of Agricultural Sciences and cultivated in a controlled-environment greenhouse under long-day conditions (28°C day/22°C night, 16 h light/8 h dark photoperiod). N. ben-thamiana plants were also grown in the same greenhouse under identical photoperiod and environmental settings, which are known to support healthy vegetative develop-ment and facilitate transient expression assays.</p>	<p>To monitor the stability and repeatability of the LC-MS system, a pooled quality con-trol (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal volumes of extracts from all individu-al samples. QC samples and blank samples were injected at regular intervals through-out the analytical run to ensure data reliability. LC-MS/MS analysis was carried out on a Thermo ScientificTM UHPLC system coupled with a Q ExactiveTM HF-X Hybrid Quadrupole-OrbitrapTM mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Samples were analyzed using hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) columns under both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes. Chromato-graphic separation was performed with optimized mobile phases and gradients tai-lored for polar metabolite detection. MS acquisition parameters were set to enable full-scan detection and data-dependent MS/MS fragmentation to ensure accurate me-tabolite identification.</p>	<p>Raw LC-MS/MS data were processed using Progenesis QI software (Nonlinear Dynamics, Newcastle, UK) for peak detection, retention time alignment, peak area ex-traction, and deconvolution. Metabolite identification was achieved by comparing ac-curate mass measurements, MS/MS fragmentation spectra, and retention times against multiple databases, including the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), METLIN, and a self-built standard compound library provided by Majorbio Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All processed data were uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform for statistical analysis. Principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal projections to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were performed using the R sta-tistical environment. Metabolites with a variable importance in projection (VIP) score &gt; 1 and p-value &lt; 0.05 (based on Student’s t-test) were considered significantly differen-tially accumulated metabolites (DAMs).</p>	<p>Metabolomic analysis was performed using non-targeted liquid chromatog-raphy-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Following the same sample pretreat-ment as described above, 50 mg of frozen root tissue from each sample was placed in a 2.0 mL centrifuge tube and ground thoroughly using a tissue grinder under liquid ni-trogen conditions. Metabolites were extracted by adding 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1, v/v), followed by vortexing and centrifugation.</p>	<p>To monitor the stability and repeatability of the LC-MS system, a pooled quality con-trol (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal volumes of extracts from all individu-al samples.

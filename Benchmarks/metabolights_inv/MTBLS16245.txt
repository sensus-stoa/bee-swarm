Study Identifier	MTBLS16245
Study Title	Phenomet - Milk and Plasma analysis (LC-HRMS)
Study Description	Milk and plasma samples were collected from 50 goats to characterize their metabolomic profiles and identify potential biomarkers associated with subclinical mastitis. Metabolomic analyses were performed to investigate metabolic differences between goats with subclinical mastitis and healthy control animals, as well as temporal changes in metabolic profiles across four sampling time points. This study aims to improve the understanding of the metabolic alterations associated with subclinical mastitis and to identify candidate biomarkers for its detection in goats.

Study Protocol Description	<p>plasma and milk were collected on 50 Alpine goats from a French commercial farm without knowing SCM or CTRL group. Blood samples were collected on heparin tubes and then centrifuged at 15 000g for 15 minutes at 4°C to collect plasma supernatant. Milk samples were collected in sterilized tubes through manual milking.</p><p>Samples were collected once a week for 4 weeks, which means 4 samples per matrix. For each matrix, samples were collected and stored at -80°C until analysis was performed.</p>	<p><strong>Chromatographic System</strong></p><p>UHPLC Ultimate 3000, from Dionex</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Chromatography Reversed-Phase (+/-)</strong></p><p>Column: Kinetex XB-C18 1.7 µm x 2.1 mm x 150 mm - 100A (Phenomenex) with a precolumn SecurityGuard ULTRA Cartridges UPLC C18 2.1 mm ID Column (Phenomenex)</p><p>Temperature: 55 °C</p><p>Phase A:</p><p>200 mL + 5.5 mL * [Number of injections]</p><p>Water + 0.1% Formic acid</p><p>Phase B:</p><p>200 mL + 3.8 mL * [Number of injections]</p><p>Acetonitrile + 0.1% Formic acid</p>	<p>'.raw' files are converted into '.mzML' files on MSconvert, with the filter 'PeakPicking : MSlevels 1-2'</p>	<p>Plasma</p><p>Metabolites were extracted from 200 μL of plasma by adding 200 µL of methanol (MeOH) and 200 µL of chloroform (CHCl3) (Martias, Gatien, et al. 2021). Samples were vortexed after the addition of each chemical solvent, kept at -20°C for 20 minutes, and then centrifuged (15000g, 4°C, 10 min). 300 µL of supernatant were collected and divided into two aliquots: one for the NMR analysis and another for MS analysis. Each aliquot was then evaporated in a SpeedVac at 35°C for 2 hours (ThermoFisher, Villebon sur Yvette, France).</p><p>For the MS analysis, dried residues were reconstituted in 150 µL of ACN / H2O (1 : 9) and then vortexed and centrifuged (5000g, 4°C, 20 min). The supernatant was collected and divided into two samples, one for the RP-LC analysis and one for the HILIC analysis.</p><p>For the RP-LC analysis, 75 µL of supernatant was evaporated in a SpeedVac (35°C, 30 min) and added with 75 µL of MeOH / H2O (1 : 9), vortexed and transferred to 96 well plate.</p><p>For the HILIC analysis, 75 µL of supernatant was transferred into “insert + vial” for further analyses.</p><p><br></p><p>Milk</p><p>Milk samples were extracted by using 100 µL of milk added with 300 µL of water H2O, 400 µL of MeOH, and 600 µL of CHCl3 (Martias, Gatien, et al. 2021).&nbsp;Samples were vortexed after the addition of each chemical solvent, kept at -20°C for 20 minutes, and then centrifuged (15000g, 4°C, 10 min). 700 µL of supernatant were collected and divided into two aliquots: one for the NMR analysis and one for the MS analysis. Then both aliquots were evaporated in a SpeedVac at 35°C for 2 hours (ThermoFisher, Villebon sur Yvette, France).</p><p>For the MS analysis, the dried residues were reconstituted with 150 µL of acetonitrile (ACN) / H2O (8 : 2), vortexed, and centrifuged (5000g, 4°C, 20 min). The supernatant was collected and divided in two aliquots, one for the RP-LC analysis and the other for the HILIC analysis.</p><p>For the RP-LC analysis, 75 µL of supernatant were evaporated in a SpeedVac (35°C, 30 min) and added with 75 µL of MeOH / H2O (1 : 9), vortexed and transferred to 96 well plate.</p><p>For the HILIC analysis, 75 µL of supernatants were transferred into « insert + vial) for further analyses.</p><p><br></p><p>Quality Control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling small aliquots from all samples within each matrix and were processed the same way as other samples.&nbsp;</p>	<p>MS Parameters :</p><p>Scan Range: 58-870 m/z</p><p>Resolution MS1: 70,000</p><p>Resolution MS2: 15,000</p><p>Polarity: POS or NEG</p><p>Microscans: 1</p><p>Lock mass: None</p><p>AGC Target: 1E6</p><p>Maximum Inject Time: 250</p><p>Sheath Gas Flow Rate: 50</p><p>Aux Gas Flow rate: 15</p><p>Sweep Gas flow Rate: 1</p><p>Spray Voltage (kV): +/-2.5</p><p>Capillary Temperature (°C): 325</p><p>S-lens RF level: 60</p><p>Aux gas Heater temperature (°C): 400</p>	<p>Data have been proprocessed and processed using XCMS and CAMERA packages through Workflow4Metabolomics (https://workflow4metabolomics.usegalaxy.fr/).</p><p><br></p><p>A library of 495 standards metabolites (Mass Spectroscopy Metabolite Library, IROA technologies, USA) was used for the construction of an in-house metabolite database. Targeted molecules from our database were selected and integrated into Xcalibur 2.2 (Thermo Fisher, USA) as previously described (Bitar et al. 2018; Dieme et al. 2017). Each peak area was normalized to the total of peak areas of interest. Metabolites with a coefficient of variation (CV) &gt;30% in quality control (QC) were excluded. QCs were analyzed at the beginning, every 10 samples, and at the end of the run.</p><p>RP-MS and HILIC-MS data tables were merged for each matrix and each time point. When both platforms detected a metabolite, redundancy was corrected by deleting the metabolite with the highest CV to keep the most reproducible signal.&nbsp;</p>

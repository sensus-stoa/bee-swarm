Study Identifier	MTBLS16248
Study Title	Phenomet - Milk and Plasma analysis (NMR)
Study Description	Milk and plasma samples were collected from 50 goats to characterize their metabolomic profiles and identify potential biomarkers associated with subclinical mastitis. Metabolomic analyses were performed to investigate metabolic differences between goats with subclinical mastitis and healthy control animals, as well as temporal changes in metabolic profiles across four sampling time points. This study aims to improve the understanding of the metabolic alterations associated with subclinical mastitis and to identify candidate biomarkers for its detection in goats.

Study Protocol Description	<p>plasma and milk were collected on 50 Alpine goats from a French commercial farm without knowing SCM or CTRL group. Blood samples were collected on heparin tubes and then centrifuged at 15 000g for 15 minutes at 4°C to collect plasma supernatant. Milk samples were collected in sterilized tubes through manual milking. </p><p>Samples were collected once a week for 4 weeks, which means 4 samples per matrix. For each matrix, samples were collected and stored at -80°C until analysis was performed.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Data are not converted nor transformed for analysis.</p>	<p><strong>Plasma</strong></p><p>Metabolites were extracted from 200 μL of plasma by adding 200 µL of methanol (MeOH) and 200 µL of chloroform (CHCl3) (Martias, Gatien, et al. 2021). Samples were vortexed after the addition of each chemical solvent, kept at -20°C for 20 minutes, and then centrifuged (15000g, 4°C, 10 min). 300 µL of supernatant were collected and divided into two aliquots: one for the NMR analysis and another for MS analysis. Each aliquot was then evaporated in a SpeedVac at 35°C for 2 hours (ThermoFisher, Villebon sur Yvette, France).&nbsp;</p><p>the dried residues were reconstituted by adding 200 µL of a deuterated buffer (0.2 M potassium phosphate buffered deuterium oxide (pH = 7.44 ± 0.5) and 10 μL of deuterium oxide (D2O) with external reference [3-trimethylsilylpropionic acid (TSP) at 3.2 mM]) and then transferred into a 3mm NMR tube.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Milk </strong></p><p>Milk samples were extracted by using 100 µL of milk added with 300 µL of water H2O, 400 µL of MeOH, and 600 µL of CHCl3 (Martias, Gatien, et al. 2021). &nbsp;Samples were vortexed after the addition of each chemical solvent, kept at -20°C for 20 minutes, and then centrifuged (15000g, 4°C, 10 min). 700 µL of supernatant were collected and divided into two aliquots: one for the NMR analysis and one for the MS analysis. Then both aliquots were evaporated in a SpeedVac at 35°C for 2 hours&nbsp;</p><p>the dried residues were reconstituted by adding 200 µL of a deuterated buffer (0.2 M potassium phosphate buffered deuterium oxide (pH = 7.44 ± 0.5) and 10 μL of deuterium oxide (D2O) with external reference [3-trimethylsilylpropionic acid (TSP) at 3.2 mM]) and then transferred into a 3 mm NMR tube.</p>	<p>NMR signals are identified using ChenomX and HMDB.</p><p>Signals are integrated using ACP (v2), from Bruker.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p><p>Data Filtration &amp; Quality Management (on table file)</p><p><br></p><p>To assure the quality of the acquisition, based on '0.4 Hz &lt; HW1/2(TSP) &lt; 1 Hz' samples can be re-run</p><p>Signals with a CV &gt; 30 % in QC are removed</p><p>For metabolite with several signals, the consistency of the results is verified, and the concentration reported will be the average concentration</p><p>For glucose, both alpha- and beta- are present in a biological sample. All their signals are superposed except for one specific signal (the proximity with the water signal can induce an underestimation), concerning the anomeric carbon (alpha &amp; beta), allowing their quantification. The total glucose concentration could be quantified with another signal</p><p>If applicable, concentrations need to be normalized following the mass of the sample OR to the number of cells</p><p>The analysis is validated by observing the distribution of QCs among the samples using Principal</p><p>Component Analysis (PCA) with log-transformed and UV scaling normalization.</p>	<p>7.4 &lt; pH &lt; 7.5</p><p>[TSP] in tube = 0.152 mM</p><p>NMR tube = 3 mm diameter tube</p><p>Temperature of analysis = 298 K</p>	<p>Before Sequences:</p><p>lock</p><p>antenna tune</p><p>shim</p><p>saturation frequence of water</p><p>P1</p><p><br></p><p>Acquisition Parameters:</p><p>pulse: noesypr1d</p><p>TD = 64 K</p><p>DS = 8</p><p>NS = 64</p><p>SW = 12 ppm</p><p>AQ = 4.56 s</p><p>RG = 57</p><p>P1 = 7.85 us</p><p>PLdB = -9.33 dB</p><p>T° = 298K</p><p><br></p><p>Processing Parameters:</p><p>SI = 64k</p><p>Lb = 0.2 Hz</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS16250
Study Title	Bile acid profiling by biological nanopores
Study Description	This study provides liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) data for the analysis of bile acids in reference standards, commercially obtained animal bile samples, and human fecal samples collected from 12 volunteers. The measurements were performed to characterize bile acid compositions in biological matrices and to provide an independent analytical reference for evaluating a biological nanopore-based bile acid profiling method. The dataset includes LC–MS/MS raw data and associated chromatographic information for the analyzed samples.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human fecal samples were voluntarily provided by 12 members of the research group who provided informed consent. The participants ranged in age from 23 to 28 years and included 7 male and 5 female volunteers. Demographic information, including age and sex, was recorded for each participant. Fresh fecal samples were stored at −80 °C immediately after collection and subsequently lyophilized for 24 h before further processing.</p><p><br></p><p>Animal bile samples included chicken, sheep, bovine, and pig bile. Fresh chicken and sheep bile were aspirated directly from the gallbladders using a syringe, whereas bovine and pig bile samples were obtained commercially.</p><p><br></p><p>Bile acid reference standards were used for compound identification and calibration curve preparation. The standards were prepared by dilution and analyzed without derivatization.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Flex UHPLC system equipped with a Thermo Scientific Hypersil GOLD VANQUISH C18 column (1.9 μm, 100 × 2.1 mm).</p><p><br></p><p>The flow rate was set to 0.30 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 35 °C. The autosampler temperature was maintained at 10 °C. Mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% (v/v) formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile.</p><p><br></p><p>The gradient elution program was as follows: 40% B from 0 to 1 min, 95% B from 5 to 8 min, and 40% B from 9 to 10 min. The injection volume was 10 μL.</p>	<p>Raw LC–MS data were analyzed using Xcalibur Qual Browser software (version 2.0.3, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The acquired full-scan and MS/MS data were examined for chromatographic features and mass spectral information. Bile acid reference standards were used to support the analysis of chromatographic and mass spectrometric data.</p>	<p>For fecal sample preparation, lyophilized feces were ground into a powder and suspended in methanol at 5 mg/mL. The suspension was thoroughly homogenized and vortexed on ice. The supernatant was collected and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter. For LC–MS/MS analysis, the filtered fecal extracts were further diluted 100-fold or 10,000-fold with methanol according to the bile acid concentrations in individual samples to ensure that the target analytes fell within the linear range of the calibration curves.</p><p><br></p><p>For animal bile samples, native bile was serially diluted 1,000,000-fold with methanol through three successive 100-fold dilutions. The diluted samples were subsequently filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter before injection.</p><p><br></p><p>Bile acid reference standards were diluted to the required concentrations with the appropriate solvent and analyzed directly without derivatization.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed using a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer coupled to the UHPLC system and equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source.</p><p><br></p><p>The instrument was operated in negative-ion mode with a spray voltage of 3.0 kV. The sheath gas, auxiliary gas, and sweep gas were set to 40, 10, and 0 arbitrary units, respectively. The ion transfer tube temperature was 320 °C, the vaporizer temperature was 350 °C, and the RF lens was set to 70%.</p><p><br></p><p>Full-scan mass spectra were acquired over an m/z range of 70–700 at a resolution of 60,000 in profile mode. Data-dependent MS/MS acquisition was performed with up to 12 dependent scans per cycle. Precursor ions with an intensity of at least 10,000 were isolated using a 2 m/z isolation window and fragmented by higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with stepped normalized collision energies of 10%, 35%, and 70%. MS/MS spectra were acquired at a resolution of 15,000.</p><p><br></p><p>Dynamic exclusion was applied after one selection for 5 s using a ±10 ppm mass window, and isotope exclusion was enabled.</p>	<p>Bile acid identification was supported by the analysis of reference standards using the same LC–MS/MS method. Chromatographic retention times and mass spectral information, including precursor ion masses and MS/MS fragmentation patterns, were used to support metabolite assignments.</p><p><br></p><p>Reference standards were analyzed without derivatization. Representative LC–MS/MS chromatograms of the bile acid reference standards are provided in Supplementary Figure 59. Xcalibur Qual Browser software (version 2.0.3, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for examination of the acquired mass spectrometric data.</p>

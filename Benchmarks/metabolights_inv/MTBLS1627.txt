Study Title	Bioaccumulation of therapeutic drugs by human gut bacteria (E.coli;C.saccharolyticum;S.salivarius;E.rectale;L.plantarum;L.lactis assays)
Study Description	<p>Bacteria in the gut can modulate the availability and efficacy of therapeutic drugs. Interactions at this level have only recently started to being systematically mapped and the main underlying mechanism proposed is chemical transformation of drugs by microbes (biotransformation). Here, we investigated the depletion of 15 structurally diverse drugs by 25 representative gut bacterial strains. This revealed 70 bacteria-drug interactions, 29 of which had not been reported before.</p><p>Over half of the new interactions can be ascribed to bioaccumulation, that is bacteria storing the drug without chemically modifying it, and in most cases without their growth being affected. As a case in point, we studied the molecular basis of bioaccumulation of the widely used antidepressant duloxetine by using click-chemistry, thermal proteome profiling and metabolomics. We find that duloxetine binds to several metabolic enzymes and changes metabolite secretion of the respective bacteria. When tested in a defined microbial community of accumulators and non-accumulators, duloxetine markedly altered the community composition through metabolic cross-feeding. We further validated our findings in an animal model, showing that bioaccumulating bacteria attenuate the behavioral response of Caenorhabditis elegans to duloxetine.</p><p>Taken together, bioaccumulation by gut bacteria might be a common mechanism that alters drug availability and bacterial metabolism, with implications for microbiota composition, pharmacokinetics, side effects and drug responses, likely in an individual manner.</p><p><br></p><p>Linked studies;</p><p><strong>UPLC-MS/MS</strong> assays of <em>Bifidobacterium animalis subsp</em>. lactis are reported in<strong> Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Growth conditions </strong></p><p>All bacteria were grown as liquid cultures in Gut Microbiota Medium (GMM). Cultivations were carried out in a Vinyl Anaerobic Chamber (COY, USA) at 37 °C with oxygen below 20 ppm, 15% carbon dioxide and 1.8-2% hydrogen. Main gas in the anaerobic chamber was nitrogen. All experimental cultures were started from the second passage culture after inoculation from a glycerol or DMSO stock. All media, buffer, glass and plasticware used in the study were exposed to the anaerobic conditions at least 12 h prior to their usage.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Assay 1, and Assay 3</strong></p><p>Overnight bacterial cell cultures, treated with the drug at the indicated concentration or DMSO (solvent control), were centrifuged (10 min, 4000 rpm, 4 °C). The supernatant was stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p><p>Experiments were performed in 4 biological replicates, with additional technical replicate for Assay 3.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Assay 2</strong></p><p>The supernatant of S. salivarius cultures treated with 50 µM duloxetine or equivalent amount of DMSO (solvent control) were prepared by centrifugation (10 min, 4000 rpm, 4 °C) followed by filtration with a Millipore 0.22 µm filter. To additionally account for the duloxetine’s impact on bacterial growth, duloxetine was added to the supernatant from the untreated control to 50 µM concentration. The resulting conditioned media or spent media control were inoculated with E. rectale at OD578 of 0.01 and the growth was measured by following OD. The supernatant samples collected at indicated time points during the growth of S. salivarius and E. rectale were used for metabolomics analysis.</p><p>Experiments were performed in biological triplicates.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Assay 4</strong></p><p>Same as Assay3; plus spiked in standards for UDP, linolenic acid and glycocholic acid before mass-spectrometry analysis.</p>	<p>Liquid samples were prepared for both LCMS and LCMS/MS by adding equal volume of ice cold (90:10) acetonitrile: 5mM ammonium acetate, pH 9 to samples and incubating for at least 1 h at -20 °C in 96 deep well plate. After incubation, the samples were centrifuged at 4500 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. Then, 20 µl aliquoted in the new 96 deep well plate, closed with temperature sensitive seals and stored at -80 °C for LCMS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was achieved using Aglient InfinityLab Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z column, 2.1 mm × 150 mm, 2.7 μm column attached to Agilent 1290 Infinity LC system coupled with 6550 iFunnel Q-TOF LC/MS. Column temperature was maintained at 45 °C and following mobile phase were used. Mobile phase A (5 mM Ammonium acetate, pH 9, 250&nbsp;mM InfinityLab Deactivator) and Mobile Phase B (5 mM Ammonium acetate in acetonitrile:water 85:15 (v/v), pH, 9, 250&nbsp;mM InfinityLab Deactivator) and following mobile phase conditions were used. Sample (5 ml) was injected at&nbsp;96% B and then maintained the same condition for 2 min, followed by linear gradient upto 88% B for 3.5 min and then maintained at same condition for 3 min, then linear gradient to 86% B in 0.5 min and maintained at 86% for 5 min, then linear gradient to 82% B over 3 min, then linear gradient to 65% B over 5 min and maintained for 1 min, then bring it back to 98% B over 0.5 min and equilibrated for 5 min before injecting next sample.</p>	<p>The Agilent LCMS 6550 qTOF instrument was operated in negative scanning mode (60–1600 m/z) with the following source parameters: VCap, 3,500 V; nozzle voltage, 0 V; gas temperature, 225 °C; drying gas 13 l/min; nebulizer, 35 psi; sheath gas temperature 350 °C; sheath gas flow 12 l/min, fragmentor, 125 V and skimmer, 45 V. Online mass calibration was performed using a second ionization source and a constant flow (1 ml/min) of reference solution (119.0363 and 1033.9881 m/z).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>LC–MS/MS was performed using the same chromatographic separation conditions and source parameters described above in Agilent 6546 LC/Q-TOF and 7.5 µl sample was injected. The auto-MS/MS with iterative mode of the instrument was selected and a preferred inclusion list for parent ions with 100 p.p.m. mass tolerance and 0.5 min retention time tolerance, iso width set to ‘narrow width’ and collision energy to either 15, 20 or 40 eV was included.</p>	<p>No data transformation was supplied for this study. </p>	<p>The MassHunter Qualitative Analysis Software (Agilent, version 10.0) was used for both LCMS untargeted and LCMS/MS Molecular feature extractions. The following tolerance level were consided for feature extraction, peak filter of absolute height should be 5000 counts, limit assigned charge states to a range of at least 1, only H- charge molecules were included and compound quality score should be greater than 80%. For peak alignment and identification were carried out using Mass Profiler Professional (Agilent, version 15.1) with standard parameters like allowing tolerances for mass of 2 mDa or 20 ppm and for retention time of 0.3 min or 2% are used to search against Metlin PCDL B.08.0 metabolite and peptide database/library.</p><p>See supplementary files for further results.</p>

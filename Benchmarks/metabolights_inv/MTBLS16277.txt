INVESTIGATION
Investigation Identifier	MTBLS16277
Investigation Title	Metabolomic datasets of Enterococcus faecalis isolated from 5-FU treated cancer patients
Investigation Description	Goals: 5-Fluorouracil (5FU) chemotherapy enriches 5FU-tolerant Enterococcus faecalis lineages in the gut, but the metabolic basis of tolerance is unknown. This study compared intracellular metabolomes of 5FU-sensitive and 5FU-tolerant E. faecalis clinical isolates. Study design: 12 E. faecalis isolates cultured from fecal samples of 5FU-treated cancer patients were grouped by 5FU MIC into low-MIC 5FU-sensitive (Efs-LM; n = 6) and high-MIC 5FU-tolerant (Efs-HM; n = 6) groups (sample names Efs-LM-xxS and Efs-HM-xxR, where S = sensitive, R = resistant/tolerant, and G/H+number is the source isolate code). Pooled quality-control samples (QC_01-QC_04), an extraction blank (Efs_blank) and a pooled data-dependent MS/MS spectral-library sample (ddMS2_POS6) were analyzed alongside study samples. Methods: strains were grown in tryptic soy broth to stationary phase, washed, normalized to OD600 = 1.0, lysed with lysozyme and sonication, extracted with methanol/acetonitrile and reconstituted in methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v). Untargeted LC-MS in positive ion mode used a Thermo Vanquish UHPLC with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide HILIC column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.7 um) at 0.5 mL/min and 25 C, coupled to a Thermo Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap mass spectrometer (full MS resolution 70,000); data-dependent MS/MS on the pooled library sample supported annotation. Data were processed with Compound Discoverer and annotated with MetDNA. Results: 367 metabolites were quantified across the 12 study samples. Efs-HM differed broadly in pathways linked to wall teichoic acid (WTA) biosynthesis, including pentose and glucuronate interconversions, the pentose phosphate pathway, pyrimidine metabolism and amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism. The WTA precursor CDP-ribitol was more than 250-fold lower in Efs-HM, which lack tarI, while ribulose-5-phosphate, ribose, ribose-1-phosphate, nicotinamide, NMN, NAD+ and NADP+ were elevated, and uracil was lower with dihydrouracil higher. Conclusions: depletion of CDP-ribitol and accumulation of pentose-phosphate and NADP(H) intermediates indicate reduced NADPH drain and greater redox capacity in 5FU-tolerant lineages; altered pyrimidine turnover mirrors increased PreT/A-associated 5FU-catabolic activity. These data identify cell-envelope precursor and redox metabolism as candidate determinants of 5FU tolerance in gut E. faecalis.

Study Protocol Description	All study samples are intracellular metabolite extracts of Enterococcus faecalis laboratory cultures derived from fecal isolates of cancer patients treated with 5-fluorouracil (5FU); no human fecal material was directly extracted. Isolates were recovered from fecal samples on Enterococcus-selective agar and identified as E. faecalis, then cultured in tryptic soy broth as described in Treatment before extraction. Study design table definitions and acronyms: Sample name = Efs-LM-xxS or Efs-HM-xxR, where Efs = E. faecalis; LM = low 5FU minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC, 5FU-sensitive); HM = high 5FU MIC (5FU-tolerant); trailing S = sensitive phenotype; trailing R = resistant (tolerant) phenotype; G or H followed by a number = source isolate/patient code (e.g., Efs-LM-G1S is the 5FU-sensitive isolate G1; Efs-HM-G2R is the 5FU-tolerant isolate G2). Non-study samples: QC_01-QC_04 = pooled quality-control samples (equal-volume pool of all study extracts); Efs_blank = extraction blank (reagents only, no biomass); ddMS2_POS6 = pooled sample analyzed by data-dependent MS/MS in positive ion mode for spectral library construction. Sample source column values: Fecal = intracellular extract of an E. faecalis culture from a fecal isolate; MIX = pooled QC; blank = extraction blank; ddMS2 = pooled spectral-library sample. Treatment column values encode strain group and sample role (not an experimental drug treatment of the cultures): Control = Efs-LM (5FU-sensitive, low-MIC) group; 5FU tolerance = Efs-HM (5FU-tolerant, high-MIC) group; QC, Blank, ddMS2 = quality-control, extraction-blank and spectral-library samples. Strain grouping (study design Treatment factor): the 12 E. faecalis isolates were phenotyped by 5FU broth microdilution MIC before this study. The Treatment column of the study design table encodes this strain group rather than an experimental drug exposure of the cultures: Control = Efs-LM, isolates with low 5FU MIC (5FU-sensitive); 5FU tolerance = Efs-HM, isolates with high 5FU MIC (5FU-tolerant); QC, Blank and ddMS2 rows are pooled quality-control, extraction-blank and spectral-library samples. Sample name acronyms: Efs = Enterococcus faecalis; LM/HM = low/high 5FU MIC; S/R = sensitive/resistant (tolerant) phenotype; G/H + number = source isolate code. Culture and extraction were performed identically for both groups: E. faecalis strains were inoculated into tryptic soy broth (TSB) and cultured overnight at 37 C with shaking at 220 rpm to reach the stationary phase. Bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation at 7,000 x g for 3 min. The pellet was washed once and resuspended in sterile saline, followed by a second centrifugation under the same conditions. The washed cell pellet was resuspended in ultrapure water, and the optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was adjusted to 1.0. A 10 mL aliquot of this adjusted suspension was centrifuged at 7,000 x g for 3 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in 400 uL of ultrapure water. For cell lysis, lysozyme was added to the suspension, followed by incubation at 37 C for 30 min. The lysate was then subjected to ultrasonic disruption (100 W, 5 min). Subsequently, 800 uL of methanol and 800 uL of acetonitrile were added to the sample. After thorough mixing, the solution was centrifuged at 12,000 x g. The resulting supernatant was collected and lyophilized. The dried material was reconstituted in a resuspension solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water, 2:2:1, v/v/v) with the aid of sonication for 15 min. The final sample was obtained by centrifugation to collect the supernatant for subsequent LC-MS analysis.	Sample preparation: E. faecalis strains were inoculated into tryptic soy broth (TSB) and cultured overnight at 37 C with shaking at 220 rpm to reach the stationary phase. Bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation at 7,000 x g for 3 min, washed once and resuspended in sterile saline, followed by a second centrifugation under the same conditions. The washed cell pellet was resuspended in ultrapure water, and the optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was adjusted to 1.0. A 10 mL aliquot of this adjusted suspension was centrifuged at 7,000 x g for 3 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in 400 uL of ultrapure water. For cell lysis, lysozyme was added to the suspension, followed by incubation at 37 C for 30 min. The lysate was then subjected to ultrasonic disruption (100 W, 5 min). Subsequently, 800 uL of methanol and 800 uL of acetonitrile were added to the sample. After thorough mixing, the solution was centrifuged at 12,000 x g. The resulting supernatant was collected and lyophilized. The dried material was reconstituted in methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v) with sonication for 15 min, and the clarified supernatant was transferred to LC-MS vials. Quality control: pooled QC samples (QC_01-QC_04) were prepared by combining equal aliquots of all 12 study extracts and were injected at the start of the analytical sequence and periodically during the run to monitor instrument stability, retention-time reproducibility and feature-intensity drift. An extraction blank (Efs_blank; all reagents, no biomass) was processed and injected to identify background features. A pooled sample (ddMS2_POS6) was analyzed by data-dependent MS/MS in positive ion mode to build a spectral library for metabolite annotation. QC injections were used for system-suitability evaluation and feature filtering during Compound Discoverer/MetDNA processing; QC raw data files (QC_01.raw-QC_04.raw) are included in the deposited archive Efaecalis_5FU_metabolomics_raw.zip together with Efs_blank.raw and ddMS2_POS6.raw. Metabolite identification: untargeted features were processed with Compound Discoverer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and annotated with MetDNA using the data-dependent MS/MS library.	HILIC using Thermo Vanquish UHPLC with Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.7 um); mobile phase A: Water, B: Acetonitrile; gradient: 0.0 min 5% A / 95% B; 0.5 min 5% A; 7.0 min 35% A / 65% B; 8.0 min 60% A / 40% B; 9.0 min 60% A; 9.1 min 5% A / 95% B; 12.0 min 5% A (re-equilibration to 12 min); flow rate: 0.5 mL/min; column temperature: 25.	ESI on Thermo Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap in POSITIVE ion mode; full-scan resolution 70,000; Data were acquired on a Thermo Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to a Thermo Vanquish UHPLC, positive ESI. Full MS scans at 70,000 resolution (AGC target 1e6, maximum IT 100 ms); data-dependent MS/MS on the pooled library sample ddMS2_POS6 for spectral library construction. The .raw files are Thermo Xcalibur format generated by the Q Exactive Plus (Orbitrap); no SCIEX QTrap data are included in this deposit (5FU quantification in stool samples reported elsewhere was performed separately on a SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ and is not part of this study).	Peak picking and feature annotation were performed with Compound Discoverer and MetDNA (MS/MS spectral-library matching). Metabolite abundances were exported per sample.	Putative metabolite annotation by MetDNA using data-dependent MS/MS (ddMS2_POS6) spectral library matching; annotation reliability level 3 (putatively annotated compound).

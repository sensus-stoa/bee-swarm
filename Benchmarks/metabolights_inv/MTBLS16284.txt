Study Identifier	MTBLS16284
Study Title	Salivary metabolomic profiles across periodontitis stages in pregnant women: a cross-sectional study
Study Description	This cross-sectional study investigated salivary metabolomic profiles in 64 pregnant women classified as having stage I periodontitis (G1, n = 16), stage II periodontitis (G2, n = 14), stage III periodontitis (G3, n = 11), or no periodontitis (G4, n = 23). All participants in G4 had gingivitis. Unstimulated whole-saliva samples were individually analyzed using proton nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and relative intensities were obtained for 42 metabolites. Statistical analyses included univariate comparisons with Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction, PLS-DA, sPLS-DA, and exploratory univariate ROC analyses. Additional internal validation used nested stratified five-fold cross-validation with preprocessing and component selection within training partitions. An exploratory secondary analysis excluded G1. The four groups showed substantial overlap, with nested cross-validated accuracies of 0.391 for PLS-DA and 0.344 for sPLS-DA. Nine metabolites differed significantly among groups after FDR correction. The findings support an exploratory interpretation of the salivary metabolic differences associated with periodontitis during pregnancy. This dataset comprises individual one-dimensional NMR acquisitions and their associated processed spectra. Metabolite intensity tables and statistical analysis code are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23902174.

Study Protocol Description	Unstimulated whole saliva was collected from 64 pregnant women before periodontal examination. Participants were instructed to avoid food, drinks, oral hygiene, chewing gum and physical exercise for at least 1 hour before collection. Samples were collected between 09:00 and 11:00 after 15 minutes of seated rest. Participants rinsed with 5 mL deionized water and expectorated the rinse. Saliva was collected for 10 minutes by spontaneous expectoration into a sterile 50-mL polypropylene tube maintained on ice. Groups were G1, stage I periodontitis (n=16); G2, stage II periodontitis (n=14); G3, stage III periodontitis (n=11); and G4, no periodontitis (n=23). All G4 participants had gingivitis. Each shared sample code represents one participant and one individual saliva sample.	Spectra were processed in TopSpin 4.0.3 with exponential line broadening of 0.3 Hz before Fourier transformation. Automatic phase correction was followed by visual inspection and manual phase/baseline correction when required. Spectra were exported to MATLAB R2018b for peak alignment by fast Fourier transform and probabilistic quotient normalization (PQN), using the custom toolbox described by Clendinen et al. (2014). The residual water region (4.2-5.1 ppm) and the TSP signal were excluded before preprocessing. Selected metabolite multiplet regions were integrated. The MAF contains the received laboratory-processed metabolite table rescaled to [0,1], copied without additional transformation or Pareto scaling. These are relative intensities, not absolute concentrations. Pareto scaling was applied separately for the downstream multivariate analyses. Analysis code, settings, clinical variables and the source metabolite CSV are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23902174.	Immediately after collection, saliva was centrifuged at 15000 x g for 10 minutes at 4 degrees C to remove cells and debris. Supernatants were transferred to clean tubes and stored at -80 degrees C. Each aliquot was thawed once at room temperature before preparation. The saliva supernatant was analyzed without organic-solvent extraction.	Forty-two metabolites were putatively assigned by comparison of chemical shifts, signal multiplicities, 1D spectral patterns and 2D TOCSY/HSQC correlations with the Human Metabolome Database, the Saliva Metabolome Database and published 1H-NMR spectra of saliva, as described in the manuscript. Spectral regions containing at least an isolated part of a metabolite multiplet were selected for peak integration. The MAF preserves the reported names and relative intensities. Unprovided metabolite-specific accessions, chemical shifts and multiplicities have not been inferred from database reference spectra.	For each sample, 540 microliters saliva supernatant was mixed with 60 microliters NMR phosphate buffer in a 5-mm-outer-diameter glass NMR tube (600 microliters total). The buffer contained 0.20 M Na2HPO4, 44 mM NaH2PO4 and 0.50 mM sodium trimethylsilyl-[2,2,3,3-2H4]-propionate (TSP-d4) in D2O (99.9 atom% D), adjusted to pH 7.4. These added reagents were diluted tenfold in the final mixture: nominal added phosphate 24.4 mM and TSP-d4 0.05 mM, with approximately 10% v/v D2O. D2O provided the field-frequency lock and TSP the chemical-shift reference. The reported pH 7.4 refers to the preparation buffer; a measured final sample pH is not reported in the available preparation documentation. Acquisition temperature was 298 K.	Individual 1D 1H spectra were acquired on a Bruker AVANCE NEO 600-MHz spectrometer (INSTRUM: AvNeo600) with a 5-mm TCI Prodigy cryogenic probe with z-axis gradients (PROBHD: Z129773_0026 (CPP TCI 600S3 H&F-C/N-D-05 Z)). Proton resonance frequency was 600.2 MHz, corresponding to approximately 14.1 T; temperature was 298 K. A Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill sequence with water presaturation was used (PULPROG: K_cpmgpr1d). Spectra were acquired with 32 scans, 65536 data points, spectral width 20.8 ppm, acquisition time 2.62 s and interscan delay 4 s. TopSpin version 4.0.3 was used for acquisition/initial processing.	<p>Each participant has one individual 1D NMR dataset. The NMR Assay Name and Sample Name use the shared code S001-S064. Acquisition parameters and raw FIDs are referenced in FILES/RAW_FILES/Sxxx.zip. Processed real and imaginary spectra are referenced separately in FILES/DERIVED_FILES/Sxxx_processed.zip. Each processed-data archive contains byte-identical copies of the acqu and acqus files and the pdata/1/ directory from the corresponding raw-data archive, including the spectra and processing parameters. No spectral reprocessing was performed when preparing these separate archives.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected before euthanasia and preserved in sterile cryopreservation tubes and frozen in liquid nitrogen.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a ZORBAX Extend-C18 UHPLC column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Agilent Technologies) maintained at 25 °C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B), with a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0.01–2 min, 5–36% B; 2–17 min, 36–95% B; 17–19 min, 95% B; 19–21 min, 95–5% B; and 21–23 min, 5% B. The total run time was 23.01 min, and the injection volume was 1 μL.</p>	<p>Database Construction: The mass spectrometry data of differential metabolites screened using MetaboAnalyst 6.0 were submitted to the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB; https://hmdb.ca/spectra/ms/search) for spectral matching. The corresponding metabolite names, molecular formulas, molecular weights, and molecular structure files (MOL format) were retrieved to construct a database of differential metabolites.</p><p>Compound Identification: The acquired mass spectrometry data were imported into PeakView 1.2 software for compound identification based on accurate precursor ion masses. Metabolites were identified by integrating accurate mass measurements, isotopic pattern matching, and comparisons of MS/MS fragmentation patterns with theoretical fragmentation patterns derived from chemical structures. Compounds with matching scores exceeding 80% were considered putatively identified.</p><p>Data Preprocessing: The raw mass spectrometry data were subsequently processed using MarkerView 1.2 software, including noise reduction, peak extraction, peak alignment, peak identification, and peak area normalization. The processed data were then imported into SIMCA 14.1 software for multivariate statistical analysis.</p><p>Multivariate Statistical Analysis and Model Validation: Principal component analysis (PCA) was initially performed using SIMCA 14.1 to explore the overall metabolic profiles and clustering patterns among different groups. Subsequently, orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was conducted to enhance the discrimination between groups and identify metabolites contributing to group separation. The robustness of the OPLS-DA model and the potential risk of overfitting were evaluated using a permutation test with 200 iterations.</p><p>Differential Metabolite Screening and Pathway Enrichment Analysis: Metabolic features with variable importance in projection (VIP) scores greater than 1 were selected based on the OPLS-DA results. The corresponding normalized peak intensities were extracted from MarkerView 1.2 and imported into MetaboAnalyst 6.0 (https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/MetaboAnalyst/home.xhtml) for further statistical analysis. Student's t-test was performed to assess differences in metabolite abundance between groups. Differential metabolites were screened according to the following criteria: VIP &gt; 1, |log2(fold change)| &gt; 1, P &lt; 0.05, and false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05. Volcano plots were generated to visualize the differential metabolites. Finally, the identified differential metabolites were subjected to metabolic pathway enrichment analysis using MetaboAnalyst 6.0.</p>	<p>All the fecal samples (50 mg) were homogenized by adding 1 mL of methanol for 2 min at 4℃. All samples were centrifuged for 10 min at 13000 rpm and the supernatant was collected. The sample was diluted five times with methanol and then analyzed by UPLC-Q-TOF/MS, and the injection volume of each sample was 5 μL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed in both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes with dynamic background subtraction enabled. The total cycle time was set to 840 ms, comprising one TOF MS scan (200 ms) and eight TOF MS/MS scans (80 ms each). An automated calibration system (calibration delivery system, CDS) was employed to perform mass calibration after every six sample injections.</p><p>For TOF MS acquisition, the mass scan range was set to m/z 100–1500. The ion spray voltage floating (ISVF) was set to 5500 V and −4500 V, the declustering potential (DP) to 60 V and −60 V, and the collision energy (CE) to 10 V and −10 V in positive and negative ionization modes, respectively. Both the nebulizer gas (Gas 1) and heater gas (Gas 2) pressures were maintained at 55 psi, while the curtain gas (CUR) pressure was set to 35 psi. The ion source temperature was maintained at 550 °C.</p><p>TOF MS/MS data were acquired in high-sensitivity mode over a mass scan range of m/z 50–1000. The DP was set to 60 V and −60 V, and the CE was set to 40 V and −40 V in positive and negative ionization modes, respectively. The collision energy spread (CES) was set to 10 V.</p>	<p>Database Construction: The mass spectrometry data of differential metabolites screened using MetaboAnalyst 6.0 were submitted to the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB; https://hmdb.ca/spectra/ms/search) for spectral matching. The corresponding metabolite names, molecular formulas, molecular weights, and molecular structure files (MOL format) were retrieved to construct a database of differential metabolites.</p><p>Compound Identification: The acquired mass spectrometry data were imported into PeakView 1.2 software for compound identification based on accurate precursor ion masses. Metabolites were identified by integrating accurate mass measurements, isotopic pattern matching, and comparisons of MS/MS fragmentation patterns with theoretical fragmentation patterns derived from chemical structures. Compounds with matching scores exceeding 80% were considered putatively identified.</p><p>Data Preprocessing: The raw mass spectrometry data were subsequently processed using MarkerView 1.2 software, including noise reduction, peak extraction, peak alignment, peak identification, and peak area normalization. The processed data were then imported into SIMCA 14.1 software for multivariate statistical analysis.</p><p>Multivariate Statistical Analysis and Model Validation: Principal component analysis (PCA) was initially performed using SIMCA 14.1 to explore the overall metabolic profiles and clustering patterns among different groups. Subsequently, orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was conducted to enhance the discrimination between groups and identify metabolites contributing to group separation. The robustness of the OPLS-DA model and the potential risk of overfitting were evaluated using a permutation test with 200 iterations.</p><p>Differential Metabolite Screening and Pathway Enrichment Analysis: Metabolic features with variable importance in projection (VIP) scores greater than 1 were selected based on the OPLS-DA results. The corresponding normalized peak intensities were extracted from MarkerView 1.2 and imported into MetaboAnalyst 6.0 (https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/MetaboAnalyst/home.xhtml) for further statistical analysis. Student's t-test was performed to assess differences in metabolite abundance between groups. Differential metabolites were screened according to the following criteria: VIP &gt; 1, |log2(fold change)| &gt; 1, P &lt; 0.05, and false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05. Volcano plots were generated to visualize the differential metabolites. Finally, the identified differential metabolites were subjected to metabolic pathway enrichment analysis using MetaboAnalyst 6.0.</p>

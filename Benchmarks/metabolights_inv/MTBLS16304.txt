Study Title	An integrated genomic, transcriptomic, and metabolomic resource for biosynthetic gene cluster characterization in Myxococcus xanthus
Study Description	<p>Myxococcus xanthus is a model bacterium with a complex multicellular life cycle and a large genome carrying numerous biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) that encode diverse secondary metabolites. Here, we present an integrated genomic, transcriptomic, and metabolomic dataset for M. xanthus DK1622 designed to support comprehensive BGC characterization. Using a hybrid sequencing strategy combining long and short reads, we generated a complete chromosome assembly with functional and metabolic pathway annotations. Furthermore, we profiled global gene expression by RNA-Seq and matched it to the metabolomic data across three solid cultivation media varying in nutrient composition (CTT Casitone-Tris medium, amino acid rich medium, and glycerol-complex nitrogen mediumI) at 24, 48, and 72 hours of growth. Technical validation confirmed high genome completeness and sequence accuracy, alongside strong transcriptomic and metabolomic replicate reproducibility across media and time points. All raw and processed multi-omic records are publicly available. This resource enables systematic correlation of BGC transcriptional dynamics with metabolite production and facilitates natural product discovery in myxobacteria.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples of <em>Myxococcus xanthus</em> were cultivated in CTT, INA5 and V6 media. Media were harvested by spinning bacteria down to form debris. 500 µL&nbsp;of media samples were transferred to new tubes and shared for metabolomic analyses.</p>	<p>Metabolite separation was achieved using ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 (130Å, 1.7 µm, 2.1 mm, 100 mm) column. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in MQ water and mobile phase B of&nbsp;LC-MS grade acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The gradient started and was maintained at 1% for 3.0 min. Next, it increased to 40% B till 13.0 min. It ramped to 100% B till 15.0 min and was kept constant for next 3 min. At 18.01 the gradient changed back to initial conditions. The total run time was 20.0 min, flow was maintained at 0.4 mL/min throughout the whole gradient, and column oven was kept at 45°C.</p>	<p>The raw data files were not additionally transformed due to a fact the Compound Discoverer allows for straight-forwards upload of Thermo compatible raw files. The mzXML files for MetaboLights databased were converted from RAW files using MSConvertGUI Version: 3.0.24225-374f83c</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction started with mixing 50 µL of sample with 250 µL of ice-cold extraction solvent (acetonitrile:iso-proponol:methanol, 45%/45%/10%, v/v/v) containing 3 internal standards (phenylalanine-d8, tryptophan-d5 and methionine-d4). Samples were then vortexed for 10 min at 1200 RPM, 8°C using Eppendorf ThermoMixer® C. They were then centrifuged for 10 min at 14 000 RPM, 4°C. Remaining supernatant was transferred to new polypropylene Eppendorf LoBind tubes and evaporated to dryness at 40°C using gentle nitrogen flow. Dry residues were reconstituted in 10% methanol solution and subjected to LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were acquired using Data-Dependent Acquisition mode (DDA) in both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes. Both tune and DDA parameters were similar to those used in previous analyses [PMID: 41423679] with slight modifications. Briefly: the capillary voltage was 4.0 and 3.5 in positive and negative ESI modes respectively, the S-lens RF 35.0 [a.u.], heated capillary 350°C and capillary transfer tube 320°C. Ions were searched across 120 to 1500 m/z scan range, with TopN = 8, MS1 resolution 70,00 and AGC Target 1.0×106. Normalized stepped collision energy was used of 20, 30 and 40 to collect fragmentation spectra.</p>	<p>Acquired RAW files were uploaded to Compound Discoverer 3.4 (Thermo Scientific). An in-house workflow previously described in [PMID: 41423679] was used with several modifications to adjust for bacterial media. The workflow tree consisted of: Select Spectra node, Align Retention Times node (adaptive curve, maximum time shift 2.0 min, mass tolerance 5.0 ppm), Detect Features node (mass tolerance 7.5 ppm, min. peak intensity of 1.0×106 and average peak width of 0.2 min), Group Features node (mass tolerance 7.5 ppm, RT tolerance 0.5 min and peak rating threshold of 6.0), Assemble Compounds node (mass tolerance 7.5 ppm, base ions of [M+H]+, [M+Na]+1 and [M-H]-1), Fill Gaps (mass tolerance 7.5 ppm and S/N threshold of 1.5), Mark Background (max. sample/blank 5) and Normalize Areas (Median Absolute Deviation). ChemSpider, Mass Lists, mzCloud and BioCyc pathways (for Myxococcus xanthus) databases were selected for compound identification. FISh and mzLogic scoring algorithms were applied to rank best candidates based on chemical composition and spectral fit.</p>

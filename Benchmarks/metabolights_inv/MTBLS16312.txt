Study Identifier	MTBLS16312
Study Title	Effects of gelatin/chitosan/Zanthoxylum bungeanum essential oil composite coating on microbial composition and metabolite profiles in refrigerated mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi)
Study Description	Our previous study demonstrated that a gelatin/chitosan/Zanthoxylum bungeanum essential oil (GE/CS/ZEO) composite film effectively delayed spoilage in refrigerated mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi). However, the underlying microbial and metabolic mechanisms responsible for its preservative efficacy remain unclear. In this study, high-throughput sequencing coupled with untargeted metabolomics was employed to characterize the core microbiota and metabolic profiles of mandarin fish treated with the GE/CS/ZEO composite film during refrigerated storage. The results showed that the GE/CS/ZEO composite film significantly suppressed the relative abundances of Acinetobacter and Pseudomonas in mandarin fish fillets. At the later stage of refrigerated storage, the predominant genera in the TM-15 group were Acinetobacter, Aeromonas, Vagococcus, and Shewanella. Untargeted metabolomic analysis yielded 2449 metabolic features, among which the differential metabolites were predominantly glycerophospholipids, bitter-tasting amino acids and their derivatives, amines, nucleotides, and coenzymes. These differential metabolites were significantly enriched in 17 metabolic pathways (P < 0.05), with purine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, and tyrosine metabolism emerging as key metabolic pathways associated with quality deterioration in mandarin fish during refrigerated storage. Correlation analysis further revealed that Acinetobacter and Chryseobacterium were closely associated with tryptophan metabolism, whereas treatment with the GE/CS/ZEO composite film markedly reshaped the metabolic associations of Aeromonas, resulting in significant correlations with nicotinamide and inosine. Collectively, these findings provide mechanistic insights into the preservative action of the GE/CS/ZEO composite film and highlight the critical roles of microbiota–metabolite interactions in regulating spoilage-related metabolic changes, thereby providing a theoretical basis for the development and application of edible active films for the preservation of refrigerated fish. The repository MAF files were independently recalculated from the original RAW data with ProteoWizard/OpenMS and contain unidentified LC-MS features (MSI level 4), rather than the original Progenesis annotation results.

Study Protocol Description	Fresh mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi) were eviscerated, rinsed, and dorsal muscle was cut into 2 cm cubes. Fillets were randomly allocated to baseline, control, and GE/CS/ZEO coating groups. Treated fillets were immersed in coating solution for 3 min, air-dried, bagged, and stored at 4 degrees C; controls were immersed in sterile water and stored in the same way. Muscle samples were collected on days 0, 9, and 15.	Reverse-phase UPLC was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class PLUS with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 by 100 mm, 1.8 micrometre). Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water and B was 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The reported gradient was 5% B at 0 to 5.5 min, 50% B at 5.5 to 9 min, and 95% B at 9 to 12 min. Flow rate was 400 microlitre/min and injection volume was 2 microlitre.	For the repository MAF files, the original Waters RAW datasets were independently reprocessed with ProteoWizard msconvert 4.0.26308-0d93cd2 and OpenMS 3.6.0. Vendor centroiding was applied and analytical MS1 function 1 was used, excluding lock-mass reference function 3. MassTraceDetection used 10 ppm mass error, absolute intensity threshold 100, S/N 3, minimum trace length 5 s and chromatographic area quantification. ElutionPeakDetection used expected FWHM 5 s and allowed widths 3-60 s. FeatureFindingMetabo retained single mass traces and reported monoisotopic chromatographic peak areas. Each polarity was aligned separately to the QC injection with the most detected features, using PoseClustering with 10 ppm and 30 s landmark tolerances, and grouped with QT at 10 ppm and 10 s aligned retention-time tolerances. A feature was retained when detected in at least 3 of 6 injections in any one biological group or in at least 3 of 6 pooled QC injections. Undetected areas are empty; no zero replacement, normalization or abundance imputation was applied. Retention times in the MAF files are in seconds. These repository feature tables were recalculated from the raw data and are not the original MassLynx/Progenesis analysis tables.	Fifty mg of internal fish muscle was extracted with 1000 microlitre methanol/acetonitrile/water (2:2:1, v/v/v) containing 20 mg/L L-2-chlorophenylalanine. After vortexing, ice-water sonication for 10 min, incubation at -20 degrees C for 1 h, and centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 15 min at 4 degrees C, 500 microlitre supernatant was dried and reconstituted in 160 microlitre acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). After a second sonication and centrifugation, 120 microlitre supernatant was transferred to an autosampler vial. Pooled quality-control material was prepared from 10 microlitre of each sample supernatant.	A Waters Xevo G2XS QTOF with electrospray ionization acquired positive- and negative-ion LC-MS data over m/z 50 to 1200. The capillary voltage was 2500 V, sampling cone voltage 30 V, source temperature 100 degrees C, desolvation temperature 500 degrees C, cone gas flow 50 L/h, and desolvation gas flow 800 L/h.	The submitted MAF files contain unidentified LC-MS features (MSI level 4), described by measured m/z, aligned retention time in seconds, polarity-signed charge magnitude where estimated, and monoisotopic chromatographic peak area per injection. No structural identities, spectral-library matches or database identifiers are asserted for these recalculated features. The original study reports putative annotation with Progenesis QI; those annotation tables were not available among the supplied files and are not reproduced here.

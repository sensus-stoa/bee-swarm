Study Identifier	MTBLS16348
Study Title	In utero exposure to high temperature-humidity index and its association with neonatal plasma metabolites in female dairy calves
Study Description	Early-life environmental conditions during gestation can influence fetal development and induce long-term physiological adaptations. Prenatal heat stress has been associated with impaired health, survival, and productive performance in dairy cattle offspring. Metabolomics can reveal whether such exposures are reflected in the neonatal metabolome, offering insight into biological pathways linked to future performance and resilience. Plasma samples from 164 neonatal Holstein-Friesian calves from three Belgian dairy farms were analyzed using UHPLC-HRMS metabolomics. Prenatal factors (gestational exposure to elevated THI, maternal parity and season of birth) and later-life outcomes (age at removal from the herd, reproductive and productive parameters) were linked to the neonatal metabolome using partial Spearman correlations adjusted for herd and age at sampling. Exposure to elevated THI during mid- and late gestation was associated with 27 and 55 molecular features, respectively (FDR-adjusted p < 0.05, |ρ| > 0.3). Putatively annotated features were mainly eicosanoid-like, with opposite directions of association for mid- and late-gestational THI. Glutathione- and arachidonic acid metabolism were enriched for both trimesters. These observational findings indicate that gestational thermal conditions are associated with measurable differences in the neonatal plasma metabolome of dairy calves, with exploratory evidence implicating lipid mediator metabolism alterations.

Study Protocol Description	<p>This longitudinal observational study was conducted on three commercial dairy farms in Flanders, Belgium (herd codes JL, KS and LH). All singleton purebred female Holstein-Friesian calves born after a gestation of 265 to 295 days between August 2017 and November 2018 were enrolled; each dam contributed at most one calf, giving 164 calves. Colostrum and milk feeding were standardised across farms (4 L colostrum within 12 h after birth, followed by commercial milk replacer, 2 L three times a day). Calves were blood sampled at 10 days of age or younger by jugular venipuncture under manual restraint, without anaesthesia, into 10 mL BD Vacutainer tubes containing K2EDTA (Becton Dickinson, Erembodegem, Belgium). Samples were kept refrigerated during transport, centrifuged (1,500-2,000 x g, 10-15 min, 4 °C), and the plasma was aliquoted into 1 mL cryovials, frozen within 2 h of collection and stored at -30 °C without prior thawing until analysis (median storage 3.4 years, range 2.8-4.0 years). Mean temperature-humidity index (THI) during mid- and late gestation was calculated per calf from daily weather data (Royal Observatory of Belgium; Royal Meteorological Institute) as THI = 0.8 x T + RH x (T - 14.4) + 46.4, with T the daily maximum temperature (°C) and RH the relative humidity (0-1). The study was approved by the Ethical Committee of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Ghent University (EC 2017/87).</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Dionex UltiMate 3000 XRS UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA, USA) using an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column (1.8 um, 150 x 2.1 mm; Waters, Manchester, UK), according to De Paepe et al. (2018). Run time was 18 min. All samples were analysed in a single analytical batch (30 August 2021) in randomised order, with pooled QC samples injected at regular intervals throughout the sequence to monitor instrument stability.</p>	<p>Raw data (Thermo .raw) were processed with Compound Discoverer 3.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for peak picking, alignment and grouping of features (m/z and retention time), using a peak intensity threshold of 500,000 a.u., a mass tolerance of 5 ppm and a retention time tolerance of 0.4 min. Normalisation strategies were compared with the Metanorm package (https://github.com/UGent-LIMET) based on QC clustering in PCA, explained variance, cumulative RSD plots and the number of features retained after a CV &lt; 30% filter in the QC samples. QC-RSC normalisation, excluding two QC outliers, was selected. Data were log-transformed and Pareto-scaled before statistical analysis. The peak areas reported in the metabolite assignment file are the un-normalised Compound Discoverer areas.</p>	<p>Plasma samples were extracted as described by De Paepe et al. (2018, Anal Chim Acta 1033:108-118, doi:10.1016/j.aca.2018.06.065). Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling aliquots of the biological extracts (n = 16 QC injections). Solvent blanks (n = 4) were included in the sequence.</p>	<p>Detection was performed on a Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA, USA) with a heated electrospray ionisation source, operated in full-scan mode with polarity switching (alternating positive and negative ionisation within one injection). The scan range was m/z 53.4-800 at a resolution of 100,000 FWHM. </p>	<p>Features are reported with their Compound Discoverer feature number (Feature_n), m/z, retention time (min) and reference ion. For features of interest, putative identification was performed by matching measured m/z values (mass error &lt; 5 ppm) to the in-house LIMET database, with retention time comparison (± 0.1 min) against in-house reference standards where available and evaluation of the isotopic pattern (12C/13C ratio). Candidate identities were further evaluated against the Livestock Metabolome Database and the Bovine Metabolome Database. Annotations correspond to MSI levels 2-4. Significant feature lists were manually inspected for isotopes, adducts and in-source fragments.</p>

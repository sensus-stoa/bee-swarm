Study Description	This case-control study investigated children with functional dyspepsia (FD) and healthy controls. Fecal samples were analyzed using untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics to profile intestinal metabolites. Integrative analysis revealed a diet-microbiota-metabolite dysregulation axis associated with gastrointestinal symptoms.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human fecal samples were collected from 74 subjects, divided into two groups (FD and CON). Samples were collected and stored according to standard clinical protocols prior to metabolomic analysis.</p><p><br></p>	<p>LC separation was performed on a Thermo Ultimate 3000 UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, the column temperature was 40°C, and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p><p>For positive ion mode, the mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B). The gradient elution was: 0-1 min, 10% B; 1-7.5 min, 10-98% B; 7.5-10 min, 98% B; 10-10.1 min, 98-10% B; 10.1-12 min, 10% B.</p><p>For negative ion mode, the mobile phases consisted of 5 mM ammonium formate in water (C) and acetonitrile (D). The gradient elution was: 0-1 min, 10% D; 1-7.5 min, 10-98% D; 7.5-10 min, 98% D; 10-10.1 min, 98-10% D; 10.1-12 min, 10% D.</p>	<p>Raw data were converted to mzXML format using MSConvert in ProteoWizard (v3.0.8789). Peak detection, filtration, and alignment were performed using the R XCMS package (v3.12.0) with the following parameters: bw = 2, ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzwid = 0.015, mzdiff = 0.01, method = 'centWave'. A QC-based LOESS signal correction method was applied to eliminate systematic errors. Features with a coefficient of variation (CV/RSD) &gt; 30% in QC samples were filtered out before subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>Samples were precisely weighed into 2 mL centrifuge tubes with steel beads. 600 μL of methanol:water (4:1, v/v) containing 2-chloro-L-phenylalanine (internal standard) was added, and the samples were ground in a tissue grinder for 120 s (60 Hz, 4°C). After ice-bath ultrasonication for 10 min, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane and transferred to a vial for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a Thermo Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes. The spray voltages were 3.50 kV (positive) and -2.50 kV (negative). The sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set at 40 arb and 10 arb, respectively, with a capillary temperature of 325°C.</p><p>Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 70,000 with an m/z range of 81-1000. MS/MS data were acquired using Higher-energy Collisional Dissociation (HCD) with a normalized collision energy of 30 eV, at a resolution of 17,500. The top 10 most intense precursor ions were selected for fragmentation, and dynamic exclusion was applied to avoid redundant MS/MS information.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by matching accurate mass and MS/MS spectra against public databases, including HMDB, MassBank, LipidMaps, mzCloud, KEGG, and an in-house standard metabolite database. The mass tolerance for matching was set to &lt; 30 ppm. For MS/MS identification, the fragment ions of metabolites with MS/MS spectra in the quantitative list were compared and matched with the database records.</p>

Study Protocol Type Term Source REF		CHMO	OBI	EFO	CHMO	MI
Study Protocol Description	<p>Fecal samples were collected with standardized kits 1-2 weeks before radiotherapy (baseline) and 1-2 weeks after radiotherapy from patients with rectal cancer undergoing total neoadjuvant therapy, and stored at −80 °C until analysis. A subset of these samples were used for targeted metabolomics.</p>	<p>Bile acids: Chromatographic separation was performed on an ACE Excel C18-Amide column (1.7 um, 2.1 mm x 75 mm) maintained at 45 °C. Mobile phase A was water with 0.01% (v/v) formic acid and mobile phase B was acetonitrile/methanol (90/10, v/v). The flow rate was 0.65 mL/min, the injection volume was 1 uL, and the run time was 10.95 min per sample.</p><p>Tryptophan metabolites: Chromatographic separation was performed on an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 um, 2.1 mm x 150 mm; Waters) maintained at 40 °C. Mobile phase A was water with 0.1% (v/v) formic acid and mobile phase B was acetonitrile with 0.1% (v/v) formic acid. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min, the injection volume was 1 uL, and the run time was 12 min per sample.</p>	<p>Calibration curves were constructed for each metabolite detected in feces, and linearity was confirmed (correlation coefficient r &gt; 0.999). Metabolite concentrations (umol/g feces) were calculated from the corresponding calibration curves.</p>	<p>Fecal samples (50 mg) were mixed with 50% methanol, vortexed thoroughly, and centrifuged. The supernatant was filtered and transferred to a 96-well plate for LC-MS/MS analysis. Extracts from samples showing detector saturation were diluted 64-fold and re-analyzed.</p>	<p>Analyses were performed on a Shimadzu LCMS-8050 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. Bile acids were measured in negative ion mode and tryptophan metabolites in positive ion mode. MRM transitions and retention times for each analyte are listed in the metabolite assignment files.</p>	<p>Targeted analysis of 16 bile acids and 18 tryptophan-related metabolites. Metabolites were identified by matching retention time and MRM transition.</p>

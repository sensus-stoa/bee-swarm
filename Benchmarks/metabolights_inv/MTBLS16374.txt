Study Title	The TREM2-SCD2 axis drives microglial phagocytic polarization after subarachnoid hemorrhage by modulating lipid dysfunction_TREM2 Activation
Study Description	Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) is a devastating subtype of hemorrhagic stroke characterized by high mortality and morbidity rates. Its complex underlying pathological mechanisms remain poorly understood, leading to limited clinical therapeutic interventions and persistent challenges in ameliorating poor patient prognoses. Through a retrospective analysis of 200 SAH patients who underwent endovascular interventional therapy, our team discovered that the temporal patterns of hematoma clearance correlate closely with dynamic alterations in microglial phagocytic function, accompanied by striking dysregulation in lipid metabolism. Utilizing multi-omics profiling, we identified TREM2 as a pivotal determinant of microglial functional shifts. Subsequent experimental validation delineated the molecular mechanism by which TREM2 modulates microglial lipid metabolic dysfunction via the SCD2-MUFA signaling axis. Finally, by conducting free fatty acidomic analysis on the cerebrospinal fluid of SAH patients and establishing a positive correlation between monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs) and hematoma resolution efficacy, we corroborate MUFA as a promising therapeutic target for SAH treatment

Study Protocol Description	<p>Primary microglia were isolated and cultured in vitro and then stimulated with oxyhemoglobin (OxyHb) for 24 h to mimic subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH). Cells were treated with an agonistic anti-TREM2 antibody (MAB17291, R&amp;D Systems), harvested by trypsinization, and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent analysis.</p>	<p>A sample volume of 1 μL was injected under splitless mode with helium as the carrier gas. Chromatographic separation was performed on a DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm) at a constant column flow rate of 1 mL/min. The oven temperature program was set as follows: initial temperature of 40 °C held for 2 min, increased to 200 °C at 30 °C/min and held for 1 min, then raised to 240 °C at 10 °C/min and held for 1 min, followed by an increase to 285 °C at 5 °C/min and held for 3 min. The transfer line temperature was set at 240 °C, the ion source temperature was 230 °C, the quadrupole temperature was 150 °C, and the electron energy was 70 eV.</p>	<p>Selected ion monitoring (SIM) was adopted as the mass spectrometry scanning mode for fatty acid detection, as illustrated in the figure below. After ionization of sample molecules, only the predefined target ions pass through the first quadrupole (MS1), travel through the second quadrupole (MS2) without collision, and finally reach the detector for acquisition, whereas other ions are blocked and cannot reach the detector. The selected ion monitoring mode can effectively eliminate matrix interference and achieve high sensitivity.</p>	<p>Samples were taken out from the −80 °C freezer and thawed on ice (all subsequent operations were performed on ice).</p><p>After thawing, 150 μL of methanol, 200 μL of methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE), and 50 μL of 36% phosphoric acid solution were added to each sample.</p><p>The mixture was vortexed for 3 min, immersed in liquid nitrogen for 2 min, thawed on ice for 5 min, and vortexed again for 3 min; this freeze-thaw-vortex cycle was repeated twice.</p><p>Centrifugation was performed at 4 °C and 12000 rpm for 5 min.</p><p>An aliquot of 200 μL supernatant was transferred and dried under nitrogen using a nitrogen evaporator, followed by the addition of 300 μL of 15% boron trifluoride-methanol solution.</p><p>After vortexing for 3 min, the mixture was incubated in an oven at 60 °C for 30 min.</p><p>After cooling to room temperature, 500 μL of n-hexane and 200 μL of saturated sodium chloride solution were added accurately.</p><p>The mixture was vortexed for 3 min and centrifuged at 4 °C and 12000 rpm for 5 min. Then, 100 μL of the n-hexane layer was transferred for GC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>A sample volume of 1 μL was injected under splitless mode with helium as the carrier gas. Chromatographic separation was performed on a DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm) at a constant column flow rate of 1 mL/min. The oven temperature program was set as follows: initial temperature of 40 °C held for 2 min, increased to 200 °C at 30 °C/min and held for 1 min, then raised to 240 °C at 10 °C/min and held for 1 min, followed by an increase to 285 °C at 5 °C/min and held for 3 min. The transfer line temperature was set at 240 °C, the ion source temperature was 230 °C, the quadrupole temperature was 150 °C, and the electron energy was 70 eV.</p>	<p>Selected ion monitoring (SIM) was adopted as the mass spectrometry scanning mode for fatty acid detection, as illustrated in the figure below.

Study Title	PRDM15 is a master regulator of metabolism essential to sustain B-cell lymphomagenesis
Study Description	PRDM family members are sequence-specific transcriptional regulators involved in cell identity and fate determination and are often dysregulated in cancer. The PRDM15 gene is of particular interest, given its relatively low expression in adult tissues and its overexpression in follicular lymphomas (FL). Despite its well characterized role in stem cell biology and during early development, the role of PRDM15 in cancer remains obscure. Herein, we demonstrate that while PRDM15 is largely dispensable for mouse adult somatic cell homeostasis in vivo, it plays an essential role in B cell lymphomagenesis. Mechanistically, PRDM15 regulates a transcriptional program pertinent to the activity of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway and glycolysis in B cell lymphomas. Abrogation of PRDM15 induces a metabolic crisis and selective death of lymphoma cells. Collectively, our data demonstrate that PRDM15 fuels the metabolic requirement of B cell lymphomas and validate it as an attractive and previously unrecognized target in oncology.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p>Using 3 independent Prdm15F/F;CreER;Eµ-Myc tumor cell lines, PRDM15 depletion was induced by treatment for 1 day with OHT (50 nM) or EtOH as vehicle control. The end of the induction period is considered as day 0. 1 day post-PRDM15 KO induction, WT and PRDM15 KO cells were resuspended in fresh media at 1 million cells/mL for an additional 6 h, then collected on ice for steady state GC-MS analysis. 13C5-glutamine stable isotope tracer analyses were performed as in [Hulea et al., 2018]. 1 day post-PRDM15 KO induction, cells were resuspended in glutamine-free fresh media supplemented with 6 mM 12C-glutamine for 2 h to equilibrate the cells. Then, cells were spun and resuspended in glutamine-free fresh media, supplemented with 6 mM 13C5-glutamine (CLM-1822-H-PK; Sigma), for 30, 60 and 180 min, then collected on ice for GC-MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>Using 3 independent Prdm15F/F;CreER;Eµ-Myc tumor cell lines, PRDM15 depletion was induced by treatment for 1 day with OHT (50nM) or EtOH as vehicle control. The end of the induction period is considered as day 0.</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p>For GC-MS analysis, cells were rinsed 3x times with 4 °C saline solution (9 g/L NaCl) and quenched with 600 µL 80% MEOH (&lt;20 °C). Membrane disruption was carried by sonication at 4 °C (10 min, 30 s on, 30 s off, high setting, Diagenode Bioruptor). Extracts were cleared by centrifugation (14,000 rpm, 4 °C) and supernatants were dried in a cold trap (Labconco) overnight at -4 °C. Pellets were solubilized in 30 µL pyridine containing methoxyamine-HCl (10 mg/mL, Sigma) by sonication and vortex, centrifuged and pellets were discarded. Samples were incubated for 30 min at 70 °C (methoximation), and then were derivatized with MTBSTFA (70 µL, Sigma) at 70 °C for 1 h. In total 1 µL was injected per sample for GC-MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>1 day post-PRDM15 KO induction, WT and PRDM15 KO cells were collected on ice, washed with 3 mL of ice-cold 150 mM ammonium formate solution (pH: 7.4) 3x times, followed by addition of 230 µL of 50% MeOH/50% water solution (pre-chilled to -20 °C). 6 pre-washed 1.4 mm ceramic beads were added to tubes. This was followed by addition of 220 µL of -20 °C acetonitrile (ACN) and a 10 s vortex. The samples were loaded into pre-chilled bead beater racks (TissueLyser II – Qiagen) and beaten for 2 min at 30 Hz. 600 µL of 4 °C dichloromethane and 300 µL pre-chilled (4 °C) LC-MS water was added. The samples were vortexed for 1 min and allowed to partition on ice for 10 min. The samples were then centrifuged for 10 min at 1750 RCF and 4 °C. The upper aqueous phase was transferred to a new Eppendorf tube on dry ice. Samples were dried at -4 °C using a temperature-controlled vacuum centrifuge (Labconco, Kansas City MO, USA). Samples were resuspended in 30 µL cold LC-MS grade water and clarified by centrifugation 10 min, 14,000 rpm at 1 °C. Supernatants were transferred to LC-MS vials containing 200 µL Teflon inserts.&nbsp;A volume of 5 µL was injected for each LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS: </strong></p><p>GC-MS instrumentation and software were all from Agilent. GC-MS methods and mass isotopomer distribution analyses are as previously described [Gravel et al., 2016; Morita et al., 2013].</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>Chromatography was achieved using a 1290 Infinity ultra-performance LC system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) consisting of vacuum degasser, autosampler and a binary pump.</p><p><br></p><p><em>Nucleotides (Shsg): </em>Nucleotide separation was achieved through the use of a Scherzo SM-C18 column 3μm, 3.0×150mm (Imtakt Corp, JAPAN) maintained at 10°C. The chromatographic gradient started at 100% mobile phase A (5 mM ammonium acetate in water) with a 5 min gradient to 100% B (200 mM ammonium acetate in 20% ACN / 80% water) at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min. This was followed by a 5 min hold time at 100% mobile phase B and a subsequent re-equilibration time (6 min) before next injection. A sample volume of 5µL of sample was injected for analysis. Sample temperature was maintained at 4oC before injection.&nbsp;Eluting nucleotides were detected using an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in positive ionisation mode.&nbsp;Dynamic multiple reaction monitoring (dMRM) were optimized on authentic standards for each targeted metabolite. Gas temperature and flow were set at 350°C and 10 l/min respectively, nebulizer pressure was set at 40 psi and capillary voltage was set at 3500V.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><em>Glycolysis, Pentose Phosphate Pathway and Citric Acid Cycle intermediates (Shn).</em> Metabolite separation was achieved through the use of a Scherzo SM-C18 column 3 μm, 3.0×150mm (Imtakt Corp, JAPAN) maintained at 10°C. The chromatographic gradient started at 100% mobile phase A (100mM formic acid in water) for 2 min post sample injection.&nbsp;The hold was followed with a 6 min gradient to 80% mobile phase B (200mM ammonium formate in 30% CAN pH=8)) at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min. This was followed by a 5 min hold time at 100% mobile phase B and a subsequent re-equilibration time (6 min) before next injection. A sample volume of 5 uL of sample was injected for analysis. Sample temperature was maintained at 4oC before injection. Eluting compounds were detected by ESI in both positive and negative ionization mode and dMRM optimized on authentic standards.&nbsp;ESI source conditions were the same as for the nucleotide’s analysis. Amino acid and amino acid derivatives.</p><p><br></p><p><em>Amino acid and amino acid derivatives (Sha):</em> Metabolite separation was achieved through the use of a Scherzo SM-C18 column 3μm, 3.0×150mm (Imtakt Corp, JAPAN) maintained at 10°C. The chromatographic gradient started at 100% mobile phase A (0.2% formic acid in water) for 2 min post sample injection.&nbsp;The hold was followed with a 6 min gradient to 80% mobile phase B (0.2% formic acid in methanol) at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min. This was followed by a 5 min hold time at 100% mobile phase B and a subsequent re-equilibration time (6 min) before next injection. A sample volume of 5uL of sample was injected for analysis. Sample temperature was maintained at 4oC before injection.</p><p>Eluting compounds were detected by ESI in both positive and negative ionization mode and dMRM as optimized on authentic standards.&nbsp;ESI source conditions were the same as for the nucleotides analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p>GC-MS instrumentation and software were all from Agilent. GC-MS methods and mass isotopomer distribution analyses are as previously described [Gravel et al., 2016; Morita et al., 2013].</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>All semi-quantitative LC-MS/MS analyses were carried out on Agilent 6430 Triple Quadrupole (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA).</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p>Data analyses were performed using the Chemstation and MassHunter software (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, USA). For steady state values, data were collected from 3 independent tumors, each consisting of 3 technical replicates. For isotope tracing experiments, data were collected from 3 independent tumors.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>Relative concentrations were determined by integrating area under the curve for the quantifying dMRM transition and compared to external calibration curves.

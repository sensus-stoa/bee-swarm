Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples from 70 NSCLC patients were analyzed using 16S rRNA sequencing to compare intratumoral bacterial profiles between LN-negative and LN-positive cases. Moreover, a combination of in vitro experiments, patient-derived organoids, the mouse popliteal lymphatic metastasis model, and the lung orthotopic xenograft model was used to evaluate the impact of intratumoral bacteria on lymphatic metastasis. Furthermore, metabolome profiling, affinity pull-down assays, and bio-layer interferometry were conducted to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.</p>	<p>All samples were for three LC/MS methods. One alipuot was analyzed using positive ion conditions</p><p>and was eluted from T3 column (Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 1.8 µm 2.1 mm * 100 mm) using</p><p>0.1 % formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1 % formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B in the following</p><p>gradient: 5 to 20 % in 2 min, increased to 60 % in the following 3 mins, increased to 99 % in 1 min and held</p><p>for 1.5 min, then come back to 5 % mobile phase B witnin 0.1 min, held for 2.4 min. The analytical conditions</p><p>were as follows, column temperature, 40 °C; flow rate, 0.4 mL/min; injection volume, 2 μL or 5μL; Another</p><p>alipuot was using negative ion conditions and was the same as the elution gradient of positive mode. The</p><p>third alipuot was analyzed via negative ionization and was eluted from a HILIC column (Waters ACQUITY</p><p>UPLC BEH HILIC Column 1 mm * 150mm, 1.7µm) using 80 % ACN, 10 % water, and 10 % MeOH with</p><p>20 mM ammonium formate, pH 10.6 as solvent A and 40 % ACN and 60 % water with 20 mM ammonium</p><p>formate as solvent B in the following gradient, 5 to 20 % B in 2 min, 20 to 70 % B in 1.5 min ,70 to 95 % B</p><p>in 3 min, hold at 95 % B for 1 min, then rapidly return to starting conditions.</p>	<p>A specific set of</p><p>MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the metabolites eluted within this period.</p><p>Unsupervised PCA (principal component analysis) was performed by statistics function prcomp within</p><p>R (www.r-project.org). The data was unit variance scaled before unsupervised PCA.</p><p>Hierarchical Cluster Analysis and Pearson Correlation Coefficients</p><p>The HCA (hierarchical cluster analysis) results of samples and metabolites were presented as heatmaps</p><p>with dendrograms, while pearson correlation coefficients (PCC) between samples were caculated by the cor</p><p>function in R and presented as only heatmaps. Both HCA and PCC were carried out by R package Complex-</p><p>Heatmap. For HCA, normalized signal intensities of metabolites (unit variance scaling) are visualized as a</p><p>color spectrum.</p><p>Differential metabolites selected</p><p>For two-group analysis, differential metabolites were determined by VIP (VIP &gt; 1) and P-value (P-value</p><p>&lt; 0.05, Student’s t test). VIP values were extracted from OPLS-DA result, which also contain score plots and</p><p>permutation plots, was generated using R package MetaboAnalystR. The data was log transform (log2) and</p><p>mean centering before OPLS-DA.</p>	<p>The sample stored at -80 °C refrigerator was thawed on ice and vortexed for 10 s. A 150 μL extract</p><p>solution (ACN : Methanol = 1:4, V/V) containing internal standard was added into 50 μL sample. Then</p><p>the sample was vortex for 3 min and centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 10 min (4 °C). A 150 μL aliquots of the</p><p>supernatant was colleted and placed in -20 °C for 30 min, and then centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 3 min (4 °C).</p><p>120 μL aliquots of supernatant were transferred for LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>The data acquisition was operated using the information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode using Ana-</p><p>lyst TF 1.7.1 Software (Sciex, Concord, ON, Canada). The source parameters were set as follows: ion source</p><p>gas 1 (GAS1),50 psi; ion source gas 2 (GAS2), 50 psi; curtain gas (CUR), 25 psi; temperature(TEM), 550</p><p>°C; declustering potential (DP), 60 V, or−60 V in positive or negative modes, respectively; and ion spray</p><p>voltagefloating (ISVF), 5000 V or−4000 V in positive or negative modes, respectively. The TOF MS scan pa-</p><p>rameters were set as follows: mass range, 50–1000 Da; accumulation time, 200 ms; and dynamic background</p><p>50</p><p>subtract, on. The product ion scan parameters were set as follows:mass range, 25–1000 Da; accumulation</p><p>time, 40 ms; collision energy, 30 or−30 V in positive or negative modes, respectively; collision energy spread,</p><p>15; resolution, UNIT; charge state, 1 to 1; intensity, 100 cps; exclude isotopes within 4 Da; mass tolerance,</p><p>50 ppm; maximum number of candidate ions to monitor per cycle, 18.</p><p>LIT and triple quadrupole (QQQ) scans were acquired on a triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spec-</p><p>trometer (QTRAP), QTRAP® LC-MS/MS System, equipped with an ESI Turbo Ion-Spray interface, oper-</p><p>ating in positive and negative ion mode and controlled by Analyst 1.6.3 software (Sciex). The ESI source</p><p>operation parameters were as follows: source temperature 500°C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (positive),</p><p>-4500 V (negative); ion source gas I (GSI), gas II (GSII), curtain gas (CUR) were set at 50, 50, and 25.0 psi,</p><p>respectively; the collision gas (CAD) was high. Instrument tuning and mass calibration were performed with</p><p>10 and 100 μmol/L polypropylene glycol solutions in QQQ and LIT modes, respectively.&nbsp;</p>	<p>&nbsp;In order to avoid overfitting, a permutation test (200 permutations) was</p><p>performed.KEGG annotation and enrichment analysis</p><p>Identified metabolites were annotated using KEGG Compound database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/</p><p>compound/), annotated metabolites were then mapped to KEGG Pathway database (http://www.kegg.jp/</p><p>kegg/pathway.ht</p>

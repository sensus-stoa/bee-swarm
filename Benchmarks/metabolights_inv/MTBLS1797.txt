Study Identifier	MTBLS1797
Study Title	A metabolite roadmap of the wood-forming tissue in Populus tremula (UPLC-MS assay; Wood ring and extraxylary tissue)
Study Description	<p>Wood, or secondary xylem, is the product of xylogenesis, a developmental process that begins with the proliferation of cambial derivatives and ends with mature xylem fibers and vessels with lignified secondary cell walls. Fully mature xylem has undergone a series of cellular processes, including cell division, cell expansion, secondary wall formation, lignification and programmed cell death. A complex network of interactions between transcriptional regulators and signal transduction pathways controls wood formation. However, the role of metabolites during this developmental process has not been comprehensively characterized. To evaluate the role of metabolites during wood formation, we performed a high spatial resolution metabolomics study of the wood-forming zone of Populus tremula, including laser dissected aspen ray and fiber cells. We show that metabolites show specific patterns within the wood-forming zone, following the differentiation process from cell division to cell death. The data from profiled laser dissected aspen ray and fiber cells suggests that these two cell types host distinctly different metabolic processes. Furthermore, by integrating previously published transcriptomic and proteomic profiles generated from the same trees, we provide an integrative picture of molecular processes, for example, deamination of phenylalanine during lignification is of critical importance for nitrogen metabolism during wood formation.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>UPLC-MS assay of Wood ring and extraxylary tissue</strong> is reported in the current study <strong>MTBLS1797</strong>.</p><p><strong>GC-MS assay of Wood ring and extraxylary tissue</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS1796' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS1796</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>UPLC-MS assay of Wood fibre and ray cell tissue</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS1831' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS1831</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The plant material consisted of 47-year-old aspen (Populus tremula) trees growing in northern Sweden (64°21´N, 19° 47´ E). 5 trees from the same clone were sampled on July 7th 2010. Blocks (2 x 10 cm), each consisting of extraxylary tissues and a few annual rings, were collected at a height of approximately 3 m. The blocks were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until required for sectioning. The sections were obtained by centripetal tangential cryosectioning of approximately 2 x 20 mm specimens obtained from the blocks. Sampling and anatomical characterization of the tissues in each section was performed as previously described<strong>[1][2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Uggla C, Mellerowicz EJ, Sundberg B. Indole-3-acetic acid controls cambial growth in scots pine by positional signaling. Plant Physiol. 1998 May;117(1):113-21. doi:10.1104/pp.117.1.113. PMID:9576780</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Uggla C, Moritz T, Sandberg G, Sundberg B. Auxin as a positional signal in pattern formation in plants. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA. 1996 Aug 20;93(17):9282-6. doi:10.1073/pnas.93.17.9282. PMID:11607701</p>	<p>Each tangential section was extracted separately in CHCl3: MeOH: H2O (1:3:1) (all solvents used were of HPLC grade) using a Retsch bead mill at 30 Hz for 3 min as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. The sections, were extracted with 1000 µl and then centrifuged at 18,620 x g for 10 min at 4 °C. 300 µl from the supernatant were dried in a SpeedVac. All dried extracts were stored at -80 °C until required for analysis. The evaporated samples were dissolved in 6 µl methanol (containing Val-Tyr-Val and&nbsp;sulphadimethoxine) and diluted with 6 µl milliQ water (containing leucine-enkaphalin and reserpine). The final concentration of each of the internal standards was 1.25 pg/µl.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Gullberg J, Jonsson P, Nordström A, Sjöström M, Moritz T. Design of experiments: an efficient strategy to identify factors influencing extraction and derivatization of Arabidopsis thaliana samples in metabolomic studies with gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Anal Biochem. 2004 Aug 15;331(2):283-95. doi:10.1016/j.ab.2004.04.037. PMID:15265734.</p>	<p>LC-MS profiling was performed on a 1290 Infinity system from Agilent Technologies (Waldbronn, Germany), with an Agilent 6550 QTOF mass spectrometer for MS-detection. The analysis was performed as follows. First the entire sample set was analysed in positive mode. When all samples had been analysed in this way the instrument was switched to negative ion mode and a second injection was performed for each sample. For each analysis mode, 2 µl of the dissolved sample was injected onto a 2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 µm UHPLC Kinetex C18-column (Phenomenex, Torrance, CA, USA). The gradient elution buffers were A (H2O, 0.1% formic acid) and B (acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid), and the flow-rate was 500 µl/min. The initial condition was 0.1% B, and the proportion of this solvent was linearly increased to reach 20% B at 4 min, and 60% B at 6 min. From 6 to 9 min the proportion of B was linearly increased from 60% to 95% and it was held at 95% up to 13.5 min. From 13.5 to 14.5 min the column was returned to its initial conditions (0.1% B), and it was equilibrated for 4.5 min before injection of the next sample.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The Mass Feature Extraction (MFE), alignment, sample grouping and peak finding of the generated files were performed using Profinder 8.0 (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA).&nbsp;</p>	<p>The auto MSMS analysis was performed in the extracts using collision energy from 10 to 30 V. The MSMS networking method<strong>[1]</strong> was used to cluster metabolites, whose MSMS spectra produced similar fragments.</p><p>The following files derived from the auto MSMS analysis is provided in this study:</p><p>In Positive mode: Phloem_1mzdata.xml Phloem_2mzdata.xml Phloem_3mzdata.xml Cambiummzdata.xml Exp_zonemzdata.xml Xyl_1mzdata.xml Xyl_2mzdata.xml Xyl_3mzdata.xml Xyl_4mzdata.xml matryl1mzdata.xml matryl2mzdata.xml</p><p>In negative mode: Phloem_2_negmzdata.xml Phloem_3_negmzdata.xml Cambium_negmzdata.xml exp_zone_negmzdata.xml Xyl_neg_1mzdata.xml Xyl_neg_2mzdata.xml Xyl_neg_3mzdata.xml Xyl_neg_4mzdata.xml Xyl_neg_5mzdata.xml Xyl_neg_6mzdata.xml matryl1_negmzdata.xml matryl2_negmzdata.xml</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Wang M, Carver JJ, Phelan VV, Sanchez LM, Garg N, Peng Y, Nguyen DD, Watrous J, Kapono CA, Luzzatto-Knaan T. et al. Sharing and community curation of mass spectrometry data with Global Natural Products Social Molecular Networking. Nat Biotechnol. 2016 Aug 9;34(8):828-837. doi:10.1038/nbt.3597. PMID:27504778</p>	<p>The metabolites were detected with an Agilent 6560 QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with a jet stream electrospray source operating in positive or negative ion mode. The capillary voltage was set at 4 kV in positive ion mode and 3.5 kV in negative ion mode; all other settings were kept constant between the two modes. The jet-stream gas temperature was 150 °C with a gas flow of 16 l/min, sheath gas temperature of 350 °C, and sheath gas flow of 11 l/min. The nebulizer pressure was set to 35 psi. The fragmentor voltage was 380 V and the collision energy was set at 0 V. The mass range of the TOF was set to 70-1700 m/z with a scan rate of 4 scans/s. Purine (2 µM) and HP-0921&nbsp;(Hexakis(1H, 1H, 3H-tetrafluoropropoxy)phosphazine ) (2.5 µM),&nbsp;both purchased from Agilent Technologies (Santa Clara, CA, USA),&nbsp;were used as reference masses and infused using an isocratic pump with a 1.9 ml/min flow rate and a 1:100 split.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS1831
Study Title	A metabolite roadmap of the wood-forming tissue in Populus tremula (UPLC-MS assay; Wood fibre and ray cell tissue)
Study Description	<p>Wood, or secondary xylem, is the product of xylogenesis, a developmental process that begins with the proliferation of cambial derivatives and ends with mature xylem fibers and vessels with lignified secondary cell walls. Fully mature xylem has undergone a series of cellular processes, including cell division, cell expansion, secondary wall formation, lignification and programmed cell death. A complex network of interactions between transcriptional regulators and signal transduction pathways controls wood formation. However, the role of metabolites during this developmental process has not been comprehensively characterized. To evaluate the role of metabolites during wood formation, we performed a high spatial resolution metabolomics study of the wood-forming zone of Populus tremula, including laser dissected aspen ray and fiber cells. We show that metabolites show specific patterns within the wood-forming zone, following the differentiation process from cell division to cell death. The data from profiled laser dissected aspen ray and fiber cells suggests that these two cell types host distinctly different metabolic processes. Furthermore, by integrating previously published transcriptomic and proteomic profiles generated from the same trees, we provide an integrative picture of molecular processes, for example, deamination of phenylalanine during lignification is of critical importance for nitrogen metabolism during wood formation.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>UPLC-MS assay of Wood fibre and ray cell tissue</strong> is reported in the current study <strong>MTBLS1831</strong>.</p><p><strong>UPLC-MS assay of Wood ring and extraxylary tissue</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS1797' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS1797</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>GC-MS assay of Wood ring and extraxylary tissue</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS1796' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS1796</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The plant material consisted of 47-year-old aspen (Populus tremula) trees growing in northern Sweden (64°21´N, 19° 47´ E). Fibers and ray cell samples were isolated from 40 μm thick radial sections prepared on cryomicrotome (Leica CM3050 S) from a small wood block (approximately 3 cm x 1 cm x 3 cm) containing phloem, cambium and a few annual growth rings. Cryosections were mounted on glass microscope slides and fixed in a drop of 80% ethanol. After that they were dehydrated in a freeze-dryer chamber (Scanvac CoolSafe) overnight and glass sandwiches were opened under a laminar flow cabinet to attach the dried sections to the slides with strips of tape. Then sections were again closed in a glass sandwich and placed in a portable desiccator chamber until laser capture microdissection (LCM)<strong>[1]</strong>. Fibers and rays were dissected at room temperature using a PALM MicroBeam System (Carl Zeiss AB) and collected into separate adhesive caps of the collecting tubes. Each tube and microscope slide was used for no longer than 1.5 h and then snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. All dissected material was stored at -80 ºC until metabolomic analyses.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Blokhina O, Valerio C, Sokołowska K, Zhao L, Kärkönen A, Niittylä T, Fagerstedt K. Laser Capture Microdissection Protocol for Xylem Tissues of Woody Plants. Front Plant Sci. 2017 Jan 4;7:1965. doi:10.3389/fpls.2016.01965. PMID:28101088</p>	<p>The ray and fibre cells were extracted in 300 ul of CHCl3: MeOH: H2O (1:3:1) containing leucine-enkaphalin, sulphadimethoxine and reserpine (all from Sigma-Aldrich Co. ,St Louis, MO, USA) and&nbsp;Val-Tyr-Val ( Bachem AG, Bubendorf, Switzerland) using a Retsch bead mill at 30 Hz for 3 min as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. The sections, were extracted with 1000 µl and then centrifuged at 18,620 x g for 10 min at 4 °C. 300 µl from the supernatant were dried in a SpeedVac. All dried extracts were stored at -80 °C until required for analysis. The evaporated extracts were dissolved in&nbsp;methanol: water (50%).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Gullberg J, Jonsson P, Nordström A, Sjöström M, Moritz T. Design of experiments: an efficient strategy to identify factors influencing extraction and derivatization of Arabidopsis thaliana samples in metabolomic studies with gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Anal Biochem. 2004 Aug 15;331(2):283-95. doi:10.1016/j.ab.2004.04.037. PMID:15265734.</p>	<p>LC-MS profiling was performed on a 1290 Infinity system from Agilent Technologies (Waldbronn, Germany), with an Agilent 6550 QTOF mass spectrometer for MS-detection. The analysis was performed as follows. For each analysis, 2 µl of the dissolved sample was injected onto a Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 x 50 mm, 1.8 µm C18) at 40 °C. The gradient elution was A (H2O, 0.1 % formic acid) and B (75/25 acetonitrile:2-propanol, 0.1 % formic acid) as follow: 0.1 - 10 % B over 2 min, B was increased to 99 % over 5 min and held at 99 % for 2 min; returning to 0.1 % for 0.3 - 0.9 min and the flow-rate was 0,5 ml/min.</p>	<p>The metabolites were detected with an Agilent 6560 QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with a jet stream electrospray source operating in negative ion mode. A reference interface was connected for accurate mass measurements; the gas temperature was set to 300 °C, the drying gas flow to 8 l/min and the nebulizer pressure 40 psig. The sheath gas temp was set to 350 °C and the sheath gas flow 11 l/min. The capillary voltage was set to 4000 V and the nozzle voltage was 0 V. The fragmentor voltage was 100 V, the skimmer 45 V and the OCT 1 RF Vpp 750 V. The collision energy was set to 0 V. The m/z range was 70 - 1700, and data was collected in centroid mode with an acquisition rate of 4 scans/s.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Mass Feature Extraction (MFE) from the data acquired was performed using the MassHunter Qualitative Analysis software package, version B08.00 (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Extracted features were aligned and matched between samples using Profinder 8.0 (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA).&nbsp;</p>	<p>The auto MSMS analysis was performed in the extracts using collision energy from 10 to 30 V. The MSMS networking method<strong>[1]</strong> was used to cluster metabolites, whose MSMS spectra produced similar fragments.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Wang M, Carver JJ, Phelan VV, Sanchez LM, Garg N, Peng Y, Nguyen DD, Watrous J, Kapono CA, Luzzatto-Knaan T. et al. Sharing and community curation of mass spectrometry data with Global Natural Products Social Molecular Networking. Nat Biotechnol. 2016 Aug 9;34(8):828-837. doi:10.1038/nbt.3597. PMID:27504778</p>

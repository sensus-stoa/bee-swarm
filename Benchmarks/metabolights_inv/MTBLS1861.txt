Study Title	Public LC-Orbitrap-MS/MS Spectral Library for Metabolite Identification
Study Description	<p>Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)-based untargeted metabolomics studies require high-quality spectral libraries for reliable metabolite identification. We have constructed EMBL-MCF (European Molecular Biology Laboratory-Metabolomics Core Facility), an open LC-MS/MS spectral library that currently contains over 1600 fragmentation spectra from 435 authentic standards of endogenous metabolites and lipids. The unique features of the library include the presence of chromatographic profiles acquired with different LC-MS methods and coverage of different adduct ions. The library covers many biologically important metabolites with some unique metabolites and lipids as compared with other public libraries. The EMBL-MCF spectral library is created and shared using an in-house-developed web application at https://curatr.mcf.embl.de/. The library is freely available online and also integrated with other mass spectral repositories.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The list of all standards along with their vendor information and PubChem ID is available online http://curatr.mcf.embl.de/inventory/. The Mass Spectrometry Metabolite Library of Standards (MSMLS) kit with over 600 compounds was purchased from IROA Technologies (MA, USA). All other metabolite and lipid standards were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Avanti Polar Lipids, and Matreya. Compounds contained in each standard are listed within the metabolites table.</p>	<p>Multiplexed standard mixtures were prepared based on a chemical class of metabolites, suitability with LC-MS protocols, and non-overlapping molecular weights. About 1 mg/mL stock solutions were prepared in methanol/H2O (50:50; v/v) for primary metabolites and in methanol:isopropanol (50:50, v/v) for lipids. The equal volumes of stock solutions were combined to yield a mixture of 10 to 15 non-isobaric compounds at a concentration of about 10 μg/mL (standards mix). The ‘standards mix’ were then further 10x diluted in respective mobile phases to obtain a final concentration of about 1 μg/mL for each compound (injection mix). The 10μL of each ‘injection mix’ was injected into the LC-MS system. In the case of IROA MSMLS kit; standards were diluted in water-methanol (50:50; v/v) or in methanol (as recommended by the supplier; IROA Technologies) to get a final concentration of 25 μg/mL. The ‘standards mix’ and ‘injection mix’ were then generated in a similar manner to obtain the same concentrations. In some cases where mass spectrometry response was low, higher concentrations of metabolites were used.</p>	<p>Several methods were used to analyse the standards, details of which are summerized in this <a href='https://docs.google.com/document/d/10uDpOz6MEfMOJ0JgvnLCwHSWIowX4QK9gykmmqjyXDQ/edit?usp=sharing' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>table</a>. All methods used a Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System using the following chromatography conditions;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC Amide - negative </strong>(a_MTBLS1861_LC-MS_negative_Amide_hilic_metabolite_profiling.txt)</p><p>Metabolites were separated on an XBridge BEH Amide XP (2.5 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) operated with a column temperature&nbsp;of 30 ℃ and flow rate 0.26 ml/min. The mobile phase consisted of Buffer A (10 mM Ammonium acetate+0.1% NH4OH (pH 9)) and Buffer B (ACN) with gradient as follows; 0 min 85% Buffer B, 2 min 85% % Buffer B, 12 min 10% Buffer B, 14 min 10% Buffer B, 14.1 min 85% Buffer B, 16 min 85% Buffer B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>C18 - positive </strong>(a_MTBLS1861_LC-MS_positive_C18_reverse-phase_metabolite_profiling.txt)</p><p>Metabolites were separated on an Kinetex C18 100 Å (2.6 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm) operated with a column temperature&nbsp;of 30 ℃ and flow rate 0.26 ml/min. The mobile phase consisted of Buffer A (10mM Ammonium formate+0.1 % formic acid in Isopropanol:ACN (90:10)) and Buffer B (10mM Ammonium formate+0.1 % formic acid in Water:ACN (60:40)) with gradient as follows; 0 min 20% Buffer B, 0.5 min 20% % Buffer B, 3 min 50% Buffer B, 10 min 70% Buffer B, 18 min 97% Buffer B, 22 min 97% Buffer B, 22.1 min 20% Buffer B and 25 min 20% Buffer B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>C18 - negative</strong> (a_MTBLS1861_LC-MS_negative_C18_reverse-phase_metabolite_profiling.txt)</p><p>Metabolites were separated on an Ascentis Express C18 (2.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Sigma-Aldrich) operated with a column temperature&nbsp;of 30 ℃ and flow rate 0.26 ml/min. The mobile phase consisted of Buffer A (10mM Ammonium acetate in Isopropanol:ACN (09:10)) and Buffer B (10mM Ammonium acetate in Water:ACN (60:40)) with gradient as follows; 0 min 32% Buffer B, 1.5 min 32% % Buffer B, 4 min 45% Buffer B, 5 min 52% Buffer B, 8 min 58% Buffer B, 11 min 66% Buffer B, 14 min 70% Buffer B, 18 min 75% Buffer B, 21 min 97% Buffer B, 25 min 97% Buffer B, 25.1 min 32% Buffer B and 28 min 32% Buffer B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC Amino - positive </strong>(a_MTBLS1861_LC-MS_positive_Amino_hilic_metabolite_profiling.txt)</p><p>Metabolites were separated on an Luna NH2 100 Å (3 µm, 2 mm x 150 mm; Phenomenex) operated with a column temperature&nbsp;of 30 ℃ and flow rate 0.26 ml/min. The mobile phase consisted of Buffer A (10 mM Ammonium acetate+0.1% NH4OH (pH 9)) and Buffer B (ACN) with gradient as follows; 0 min 85% Buffer B, 2 min 85% % Buffer B, 12 min 10% Buffer B, 14 min 10% Buffer B, 14.1 min 85% Buffer B, 16 min 85% Buffer B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>HILIC Amino - negative </strong>(a_MTBLS1861_LC-MS_negative_Amino_hilic_metabolite_profiling.txt)</p><p>Metabolites were separated on an Luna NH2 100 Å (3 µm, 2 mm x 150 mm; Phenomenex) operated with a column temperature&nbsp;of 30 ℃ and flow rate 0.26 ml/min. The mobile phase consisted of Buffer A (10 mM Ammonium acetate+0.1% NH4OH (pH 9)) and Buffer B (ACN) with gradient as follows; 0 min 85% Buffer B, 2 min 85% % Buffer B, 12 min 10% Buffer B, 14 min 10% Buffer B, 14.1 min 85% Buffer B, 16 min 85% Buffer B.</p>	<p>Molecules were detected with HRMS full scan at the mass resolving power R=70000 in the mass range of 60-900 m/z (for small molecules) or 150 to 1500 m/z (for lipids).&nbsp; The data-dependent (DDA) MS/MS scans for 10 most intense (TOP10)were obtained along with full scans using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) of normalized collision energies (NCE) of 20, 40 and 60 units at the mass resolving power R=17500. The MS parameters in the Tune software (Thermo Scientific) were set as; spray voltage of 4.2 kV (for negative mode 3.5 kV), sheath gas 35, and auxiliary gas 5 units, S-Lens 65 eV,&nbsp; capillary temperature&nbsp; 350oC&nbsp; and vaporization temperature of auxiliary gas was set at 300oC. </p>	<p>LC-MS/MS data was acquired using Thermo Xcalibur software (version 3.1, Thermo Electron Corporation). Our in-house developed web application ‘curatr’ was used for curation, cataloging, and sharing the mass spectral library online.1</p><p>The overall workflow for creating the spectral library is described in Figure 1 of the manuscript. All acquired LC-MS/MS raw files were converted to centroid mzML format29 using MSConvert software (ProteoWizard version 3).30 A mzML file of standard mixtures was then uploaded on ‘curatr’ web-application.1 The metadata and other relevant parameters are listed in Supplementary Methods. The detailed video protocol for browsing, download, curation using ‘curatr’ web application is available on the website.&nbsp;</p><p>The curation of the LC-MS/MS spectra is a key part of this work and was performed by expert mass spectrometrists using online visualization and curation features ‘curatr’ web application. Curatr automates the process of extracting the possible chromatograms and fragmentation spectra for a metabolite taking relevant adducts into account and presents the user with several spectra to choose from, along with quality metrics criteria and metadata information (Figure 1). As shown in Figure 1; the first step of curation was an evaluation of the quality of extracted ion chromatogram (XIC) for optimal retention, acceptable peak shape, and peak intensity. Once the XIC profile is acceptable then each MS/MS spectra across the peak were checked for intensity (&gt; 104 units), presence of precursor fraction (&gt; 0.1), and fragmentation pattern consistency across chromatographic elution profile. Next, the spectra were interpreted manually for their fragmentation patterns. Based on these criteria each MS/MS spectra is either accepted or rejected. The accepted spectra are then stored with the molecule along with its metadata. The rejected spectra are then discarded from the database. A convenient feature of ‘curatr’ is that it automatically catalogs and displays multiple MS/MS spectra curated for the same standard using different LC-MS protocols systematically in a single browser window. For each curated MS/MS spectra, the method details and overlay precursor XIC profiles are displayed in a browser window. For example; https://curatr.mcf.embl.de/inventory/inventory132/</p>	<p>The metabolite identification was performed with authentic standards and curated manually using online web application. The list of identified metabolites and their MS/MS spectra along with LC chromatogram (XIC) can be accessed freely online https://curatr.mcf.embl.de/molecule/</p><p><br></p>

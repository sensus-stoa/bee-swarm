Study Identifier	MTBLS1955
Study Title	Comparative Lipidomics of Different Yeast Species Associated to Drosophila suzukii
Study Description	Yeasts constitute a dietary source for the spotted wing drosophila (SWD) and produce compounds that attract these flies. The study of the chemical composition of the yeast communities associated with SWD should therefore help to understand the relationship between the biology of the insect and the yeast's metabolism. In the present study, the lipidome of five yeast species isolated from grapes infested by SWD (three <i>Hanseniaspora uvarum</i> strains, <i>Candida</i> sp., <i>Issatchenkia terricola</i>, <i>Metschnikowia pulcherrima</i> and <i>Saccharomycopsis vini</i>) and a laboratory strain of <i>Saccharomyces cerevisiae</i> was explored using an untargeted approach. Additionally, the lipid profile of two species, <i>S. cerevisiae</i> and <i>H. uvarum</i>, which were reported to elicit different responses on SWD flies based on feeding and behavioral trials, was compared with a chemical enrichment approach. Overall, 171 lipids were annotated. The yeast species could be distinguished from each other based on their lipid profile, except for the three strains of <i>H. uvarum,</i> which were very similar to each other. The chemical enrichment analysis emphasized diversities between <i>S. cerevisiae</i> and <i>H. uvarum</i>, that could not be detected based on their global lipid profile. The information concerning differences between species in their lipidome may be of interest to future entomological studies concerning the yeast-insect interaction and could help to explain the responses of SWD to diverse yeast species.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Yeasts were grown in 220 mL PDB at 25 °C for 30 h in a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask closed with cotton and aluminum foil on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm. The inoculum (0.1 mL) was prepared with a loop full of yeast cells cultivated on PDA for four days, which were transferred in a 2 mL Eppendorf tube filled with 1 mL PDB and vortexed for 10 s at 1800 rpm. Six replicates of the inoculum were prepared for each yeast.</p>	<p>Ten mL of fermentation broth were quenched in 20 mL methanol at -80 °C, centrifuged (Eppendorf Centrifuge 5810 R) at -10 °C for 5 min at 4000 rpm using a -80 °C prechilled rotor. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was freeze dried. Ten mg of freeze-dried cell pellet were weighed, and intracellular lipids were extracted using the procedure of Showalter et al. [1]. A volume of 225 µL of -20 °C methanol containing an internal standard mixture reported in the paper and 750 μL of -20 ˚C MTBE containing the internal standard cholesteryl ester 22:1 was added to the pellet. Samples were shaken for 6 min at 4 ˚C using a Thermomixer (Eppendorf) and 188 μL of milliQ water were added. Samples were vortexed, centrifuged and 350 µL of the upper layer was collected, evaporated to dryness using a SpeedVac vacuum concentrator and re-suspended in methanol:toluene (9:1, v/v) containing 50 ng mL-1 CUDA ((12-[[(cyclohexylamino)carbonyl]amino]-dodecanoic acid). Samples were vortexed, sonicated for 5 min and centrifuged before analysis. Pooled samples were used as quality control.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Showalter MR, Nonnecke EB, Linderholm AL, Cajka T, Michael RS, Lönnerdal B, Kenyon NJ, Fiehn O. Obesogenic diets alter metabolism in mice. Plos One. 2018 doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0190632</p>	<p>Chromatographic conditions were based on Showalter et al [1]. A Waters Acquity UPLC CSH C18 (100 mm length × 2.1 mm id; 1.7 μm particle size) column with a Waters Acquity VanGuard CSH C18 pre-column (5 mm × 2.1 mm id; 1.7 μm particle size) maintained at 65 ˚C was used for RPLC-QTOFMS analysis. In positive ion mode solvent A was 60:40 v/v acetonitrile:water with 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid and solvent B was 90:10 v/v isopropanol:acetonitrile with 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid. In negative ion mode solvent A was 60:40 v/v acetonitrile:water with 10 mM ammonium acetate and solvent B was 90:10 v/v isopropanol:acetonitrile with 10 mM ammonium acetate. Flow rate was set at 0.6 mL/min, with a 15 min gradient as reported in the paper.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Showalter MR, Nonnecke EB, Linderholm AL, Cajka T, Michael RS, Lönnerdal B, Kenyon NJ, Fiehn O. Obesogenic diets alter metabolism in mice. Plos One. 2018 doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0190632</p>	<p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>The instrument Impact HD QTOF (Bruker) equipped with an Ultimate 3000 UHPLC (Thermo Scientific) was used for LC-MS analysis. Five µL of re-suspended sample were injected in ESI positive ion mode, while injection volume in negative ion mode was 10 µL. The mass spectrometric conditions were as follows: m/z range, 60-1700; capillary voltage, 3500 V; nebulizer gas (nitrogen), 2.4 bar; dry gas (nitrogen), 8 L/min in positive ion mode and 12 L/min in negative ion mode; dry temperature, 325 °C in positive ion mode and 200 °C in negative ion mode. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>MS/MS</strong></p><p>For MS/MS, collision energy was set at 20 eV in positive and in negative ion mode, spectra rate was 13 Hz with 4 precursor ions per cycle. Sodium formate was used as calibrant for maintaining mass accuracy.</p>	<p>Before submission to MS-DIAL data was calibrated using DataAnalysis vendor software and transformed from Bruker.d format to .abf format using&nbsp;Reifycs Abf (Analysis Base File) Converter. MS-DIAL was used for deconvolution, peak picking, alignment and annotation as described in the publication associated with this study. Peak height values were normalized using class-based internal standards and further by log-transformation before statistical testing.</p><p><br></p>	<p>For compound identification full scan and MS/MS analysis were performed. LipidBlast was used as library for compound identification with an identification score cut-off of 85% and a retention time tolerance of 0.1 min. The level of identification of lipids was MSI2 for all compounds based on Sumner et al. [1]. The nomenclature used&nbsp;was based on Züllig et al. [2]: the bond type level was reported in case of annotation based on high resolution full scan (MS1), while double-bond positions were indicated in case of MS/MS- based annotation as described in the publication associated with this study.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Proposed minimum reporting standards for chemical analysis Chemical Analysis Working Group (CAWG) Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI). Sumner LW, Amberg A, Barrett D, Beale MH, Beger R, Daykin CA, Fan TW, Fiehn O, Goodacre R, Griffin JL, Hankemeier T, Hardy N, Harnly J, Higashi R, Kopka J, Lane AN, Lindon JC, Marriott P, Nicholls AW, Reily MD, Thaden JJ, Viant MR. Metabolomics. 2007 Sep;3(3):211-221. doi: 10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2. PMID: 24039616</p><p>[2] Lipidomics from sample preparation to data analysis: a primer. Züllig T, Trötzmüller M, Köfeler HC. Anal Bioanal Chem. 2020 Apr;412(10):2191-2209. doi: 10.1007/s00216-019-02241-y. Epub 2019 Dec 10. PMID: 31820027</p>

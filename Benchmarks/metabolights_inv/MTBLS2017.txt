Study Identifier	MTBLS2017
Study Title	Umbilical cord blood metabolome differs in relation to delivery mode, birth order and sex, maternal diet and possibly future allergy development in rural children
Study Description	Allergy is one of the most common diseases among young children yet all factors that affect development of allergy remain unclear. In a small cohort of 65 children living in the same rural area of south-west Sweden, we have previously found that maternal factors, including prenatal diet, affect childhood allergy risk, suggesting that in utero conditions may be important for allergy development. Here, we studied if metabolites in the umbilical cord blood of newborns may be related to development of childhood allergy, accounting for key perinatal factors such as mode of delivery, birth order and sex. Available umbilical cord blood plasma samples from 44 of the participants were analysed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry metabolomics; allergy was diagnosed by specialised paediatricians at ages 18 months, 3 years and 8 years and included eczema, asthma, food allergy and allergic rhinoconjunctivitis. Nineteen cord blood metabolites were related to future allergy diagnosis though there was no clear pattern of up- or downregulation of metabolic pathways. In contrast, perinatal factors birth order, sex and mode of delivery affected several energy and biosynthetic pathways, including glutamate and aspartic acid-histidine metabolism (p = 0.004) and the tricarboxylic acid cycle (p = 0.006) for birth order; branched chain amino acid metabolism (p = 0.0009) and vitamin B6 metabolism (p = 0.01) for sex; and glyoxylate and dicarboxylic acid metabolism (p = 0.005) for mode of delivery. Maternal diet was also related to some of the metabolites associated with allergy. In conclusion, the cord blood metabolome includes individual metabolites that reflect lifestyle, microbial and other factors that may be associated with future allergy diagnosis, and also reflects temporally close events/factors. Larger studies are required to confirm these associations, and perinatal factors such as birth order or siblings must be considered in future cord-blood metabolome studies.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Umbilical cord blood plasma samples from 44 participants living in the same rural area of south-west Sweden. Allergy was diagnosed by specialised paediatricians at ages 18 months, 36 months and 8 years and included eczema, asthma, food allergy and allergic rhinoconjunctivitis. Umbilical cord blood, a mix of venous and arterial blood, was collected at birth and stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p><strong>Extraction:</strong></p><p>Blood plasma (100 µL) was extracted in 900 µL of methanol:water (9:1 v/v) containing internal standards (see below), shaken in a bead shaker, centrifuged for 10 mins and incubated at -20 °C for 30 mins. Aliquots (200 µL) were removed and dried in an amber chromatography vial with insert using a vacuum centrifuge concentrator until complete dryness.</p><p>The methanol:water extraction solution contained stable isotope-labelled internal standards at a concentration of 62.5 pg/µL each internal standard: </p><p>13C5-proline, 2H4-succinic acid, 13C5,15N-glutamic acid, 1,2,3-13C3-myristic acid, 2H7-cholesterol, 13C4-α-ketoglutarate, 13C12−sucrose, 13C4-hexadecanoic acid, 13C6-glucose 2H4−butanediamine, and 2H6-salicylic acid (all from Cambridge Isotope laboratories, Tewksbury, MA).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Derivatisation:</strong></p><p>30 µL methoxyamine (15 µg/µL pyridine) was added to the dried sample in vial, vortexed for 5 mins and placed in an oven for 60 mins at 70 °C. The samples were then removed and left at room temperature overnight to complete the reaction. Samples were silylated with 30 µL MSTFA+1 % TMCS for 60 mins at room temperature. 30 µL of heptane was added before analysis by GC-MS.</p>	<p>Analysis were carried out on a Shimadzu GCMS TQ-8030 GC-MS/MS system consisting of a Shimadzu GC-2010 Plus gas chromatograph, Shimadzu TQ-8030 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer and a Shimadzu AOC-5000 Plus sample handling system (Shimadzu Europa GmbH, Duisberg, Germany). Data was acquired with Shimadzu GCMSsolutions software version 4.2. One μL of sample was injected with a split ratio of 1:4 and separation was done on a 15 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm Rxi-5Sil MS column (Restek Corporation, Bellefonte, PA). No guard column was used. Injection port temperature was set to 270 °C and oven temperature program was as follows: 70 °C for 2 min and ramped to 330 °C at 30 °C/min where it was held for 2 min. GC was operated in constant linear velocity mode set to 80 cm/sec. Septum purge flow was set to 3 mL/min. Interface and ion source temperature were 290 and 200 °C respectively. Helium was used as the GC carrier gas.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Shimadzu GCMS TQ 8040 with electron impact in positive mode, 70 eV. Data were acquired using full scan mode (m/z 50-750).</p>	<p>Data were corrected for drift using internal standard correction (PCA method).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based on their retention index, mass spectral similarity, and main mass fragment, matching against standard data from the Swedish Metabolome Centre library.</p>

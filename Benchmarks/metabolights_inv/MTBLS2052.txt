Study Identifier	MTBLS2052
Study Title	Tissue, urine and serum NMR metabolomics dataset from a 5/6 nephrectomy rat model of chronic kidney disease
Study Description	<p>Serum, urine and tissue from a rat model of chronic kidney disease (CKD) were analysed using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy-based metabolomics methods, and compared with samples from sham operated rats. Both urine and serum were sampled at multiple timepoints, and the results have been reported elsewhere [1]. The data could be useful to researchers working with human CKD or rat models of the disease. In addition, several different types of NMR spectra were recorded, including 1D NOESY, CPMG, and 2D J-resolved spectra, and the data could be useful for method comparison and algorithm development, both in terms of NMR spectroscopy and multivariate analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Hanifa MA, Skott M, Maltesen RG, Rasmussen BS, Nielsen S, Frøkiær J, Ring T &amp; Wimmer R. (2019). Tissue, urine and blood metabolite signatures of chronic kidney disease in the 5/6 nephrectomy rat model. Metabolomics<strong> </strong>15;112. doi.org/10.1007/s11306-019-1569-3.</p>

Study Protocol Description	Thirty male Wistar rats were randomised to either 5/6 nephrectomy (Nx) or sham operation. More rats were randomised to the nephrectomy group (n=17), compared to the sham operation group (n=13), as more nephrectomy rats were expected to die due to the more extensive surgical procedure. On day -7 (week -1), rats were anaesthetised with isoflurane and the two poles of the left kidney were tied off and removed. On day 0 (week 0), rats were anaesthetised and the right kidney was removed. Sham-operated rats underwent similar surgical interventions, but did not have any kidney tissue removed. Analgesia was provided with buprenorphine, subcutaneously and in the drinking water, following both operations. Urine samples were collected weekly by attaching the implanted bladder catheter to a silicone tube, the other end of which was attached to an Eppendorf tube suspended in a container of ice and water. The rats were placed in restraining cages for approximately two hours, with free access to water, whilst urine was collected. Urine was then centrifuged to remove precipitate, before aliquoting and freezing in liquid nitrogen. Blood samples were collected from the tail veins (weeks 0 and 3), or cardiac puncture at sacrifice (week 6). Samples were left to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes before being spun down. Serum was then aliquoted and snap frozen in liquid nitrogen. Tissue samples were collected after sacrifice (week 6), weighed, placed in cryo-tubes and snap frozen in liquid nitrogen. All samples were stored at -80 °C.	<p>Only tissue samples needed extraction. Samples were lyophilised and then homogenised using a Precellys bead homogeniser. 50 mg or 100 mg of the homogenised tissue was extracted using ice-cold methanol, chloroform and water in a 2:2:1.8 ratio. The hydrophilic phase was transferred to a clean vial and lyophilised.</p>	Urine and blood samples were defrosted at 4 °C. They were then vortexed briefly before centrifuging (at 14000 x g and 4 °C for 5 minutes), to remove precipitate. Samples were mixed with buffer, 9:1 urine:buffer (1.5 M KH2PO4, 2 mM NaN3, 0.1% TSP, pH* 7.4) or 1:1 serum:buffer (0.075 M NaH2PO4, 0.04% NaN3, 0.08% TSP, pH* 7.4). Because rat urine samples often suffered from precipitation after the addition of buffer, samples were re-centrifuged before transferring to an NMR tube. Lyophilised tissue extracts were dissolved in 0.1 M imidazole-d4 buffer (0.02% NaN3, 1 mM TSP, pH* 7.4).	NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker 600 MHz Avance DRX-600 equipped with a 5mm TXI probe and SampleJet sample changer (Bruker BioSpin, Rheinstetten, Germany). Serum spectra were acquired at 310 K, whilst urine and tissue spectra were aquired at 298 K.	Urine spectra were acquired with the 1D nuclear Overhauser effect spectroscopy (NOESY), Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG), skyline projection of J-resolved (pJRES), and a quantitative NOESY (qNOESY) pulse sequence (run with a long relaxation delay). The NOESY spectra were run with 128 scans, 64k data points, 20 ppm spectral width, 2.73 s acquisition time, and 4 s relaxation delay. The CPMG spectra used the same parameters, whilst the J-resolved spectra were run with 4 scans per increment, 16k data points in the direct dimension and 42 in the indirect dimension, 12 ppm spectral width in the direct dimension and 54 Hz in the indirect dimension, 1.17 s acquisition time, and 4 s relaxation delay. For serum NOESY spectra, 96k data points and a 30 ppm spectral width were used (remaining parameters were the same as for other NOESY spectra). The quantitative NOESY (qNOESY) spectra (run on urine and tissue samples) used a long relaxation delay of 27.3 s and only 64 scans (with all other parameters the same as for other NOESY spectra). NMR spectra of tissue samples were only acquired using the NOESY, pJRES and qNOESY pulse sequences, whilst NMR spectra of serum samples were only acquired using the CPMG and pJRES pulse sequence.	<p>NMR spectra were processed using TopSpin v3.1 (Bruker BioSpin, Rheinstetten, Germany). NOESY, qNOESY and CPMG spectra were processed by zero-filling to a spectral size of 128k, exponential multiplication (0.3 Hz), Fourier transformation and manual phase and baseline correction. JRES spectra were zero-filled to a spectral size of 32k in the direct dimension and 64 in the indirect dimension, followed by application of an unshifted sine bell window function, Fourier transformation, tilting and symmetrising. A skyline projection, followed by manual baseline correction, was used to produce the pJRES spectra. Urine and tissue spectra were referenced to the TSP signal, whilst serum spectra were referenced to Œ±-H1-glucose duplet at 5.24 ppm. No normalisation has been applied to the spectra, but various strategies can be used, for example: PULCON peak normalisation in serum (at -2 ppm in the serum CPMG spectra); TSP peak normalisation followed by normalisation to extracted tissue mass; or creatinine peak integral normalisation in urine.</p><p><br></p><p>Integrals were calculated using the Peak Fitting tool in MestReNova v8.1 (Mestrelab Research, Santiago de Compostela, Spain). Integrals for 12 metabolites are included in this dataset (acetate, alanine, allantoin, citrate, creatine, creatinine, dimethylglycine, formate, glucose, glycine, lactate and valine). All integrals using non-quantitative spectra (i.e. all spectra except qNOESY spectra) are presented as relative to the maximum metabolite integral in the same tissue type, and this includes all serum spectra (CPMG integration), and urine alanine, glucose, glycine and valine (JRES or pJRES integration). In addition, urine integrals are presented relative to the creatinine integral (except for creatinine itself).</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by comparing proton shifts, carbon shifts and coupling constants taken from TOCSY, HSQC and JRES spectra with the BBIOREFCODE (Bruker BioSpin) and HMDB (www.hmdb.ca) databases. Where identification or peak overlap was a problem, chemical standards were spiked into representative samples.</p>

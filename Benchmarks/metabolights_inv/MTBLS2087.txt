Study Identifier	MTBLS2087
Study Title	Clostridium butyricum MIYAIRI 588-induced protectin D1 has an anti-inflammatory effect on antibiotic-induced intestinal disorder
Study Description	Metabolites are thought as the end products in cellular regulatory processes and their levels show the strongest relationships with the phenotype. Previously, we showed that the administration of Clostridium butyricum MIYAIRI 588 (CBM 588) upregulated protectin D1, an anti-inflammatory lipid metabolite, in colon tissue under antibiotic therapy. However, how CBM 588 induces protectin D1 expression and whether the metabolite has anti-inflammatory effects on antibiotic-induced inflammation are unclear. Therefore, here, we evaluated the effect of CBM 588 on lipid metabolism and protectin D1 in gut protection from antibiotic-induced intestinal disorders. In the CBM 588 treatment group, expression levels of genes encoding lipid receptors related to the conversion of DHA to protectin D1, such as polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) receptors, G-protein coupled receptor 120 (GPR120), and 15-lipoxygenase (LOX), were increased in colon tissue. CD4+ cells producing interleukin (IL)-4, the main component of T helper type 2 (Th2) cells that can activate 15-LOX, also increased in CBM 588-treated groups even after clindamycin co-administration. In addition, similar to CBM 588, exogenously administered protectin D1 reduced inflammatory cytokines, while IL-10 and TGF-β1, works as anti-inflammatory cytokines, were increased. Our data revealed that CBM 588 activated 15-LOX to enhance protectin D1 production by increasing IL-4-producing CD4+ cell population in the intestinal tract. Additionally, CBM 588-induced protectin D1 clearly upregulated IL-10-producing CD4+ cells to control antibiotic-induced gut inflammation. We provide new insights into CBM 588-mediated lipid metabolism induction for the treatment of gut inflammatory diseases.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Twelve female ICR mice were divided into four groups (n = 3): (I) control group, (II) clindamycin (CLDM) administration group, (III) CBM 588 administration group, and (IV) combination group (CBM 588 + CLDM). CBM 588 spore powder was administered by oral gavage at 500 mg/kg/day (3.4 × 10^8 cfu/g/mouse/day) of CBM 588. CLDM was also administered by oral gavage at 40 mg/kg/day. The CBM 588 spore powder was dissolved into PBS and then mixed. Mice were administered twice/day, at 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., using 1/2 doses every time, for 4 days and kept for another 4 days. At Day 8, mice were sacrificed to analyze lipid metabolome in colon tissue.&nbsp;Mice were euthanized by an overdose of CO2 followed by cervical dislocation.&nbsp;Colon was collected and opened longitudinally, washed to remove fecal content by PBS, then homogenized in RIPA buffer (Nacalai Tesque, Kyoto, Japan). After sonication for 10 s, the suspension was centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 20 min at 4°C. Samples were then stored at -80°C until extraction.</p><p><br></p><p>A standard mix was used as a quality control (Q1). Standards (described below) were dissolved in ethanol to 50 ppb respectively.</p><p>Standards used for this test were:</p><p>[Polyunsaturated Fatty Acid LC/MS Mixture 17941, Lipoxin LC-MS Mixture 19412 SPM D-series LC-MS Mixture 18702, 10(S), 17(S)-DiHDoHE 10008128, SPM E-series LC-MS Mixture 19417] (Funakoshi, Tokyo, Japan),</p><p>[PGD2: Prostaglandin D2, Lipid Maps MS standard 10007202 , PGJ2: 15-deoxy-Δ12,14-prostaglandin J2, Lipid Maps MS standard 10007235, PGF1α: 6-keto prostaglandin F1α Lipid Maps MS standard 10007219, PGF2α: Prostaglandin F2α Lipid Maps MS standard 10007221, PGF2α: 8-iso prostaglandin F2α 16350, PGD2-d4: Prostaglandin D2-d4 10007272, PGJ2-d4: 15-deoxy-Δ12,14-prostaglandin J2-d4 318570, PGF1α-d4: 6-keto prostaglandin F1α-d4 Lipid Maps MS standard 10007274, PGF2α-d4: Prostaglandin F2α-d4 10007275] (Cayman, Ann Arbor, MI, USA), [Arachidonic Acid A0781] (TCI, Tokyo, Japan), [Heptadecanoic Acid Standard 084-08661](Wako, Osaka, Japan).</p>	<p>Frozen supernatants from mouse colon samples (stored at −80 °C) were thawed on ice to 0 °C. Immediately after thawing, a portion of the supernatant sample (20 μL) was added to a solution consisting of 80 μL of methanol, and the resulting mixture was vigorously vortexed for 1 min. The mixture was then centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 3 min, and the supernatant was collected. The supernatant was dried under reduced pressure, followed by addition of 50 µL of acetonitrile and vortexing for 1 min. The mixture was then centrifuged at 15,000 × g for 3 min, and the supernatant was collected into 300 μL vial.</p>	<p>Vanquish H system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cleveland, OH, USA) using an Acclaim RSLC120 C18 (2.2 µm 2.1 i.d. × 150 mm, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cleveland, OH, USA). Mobile phase A was prepared using MilliQ containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. After equilibration with 20% mobile phase solution B, 2 µL of the sample was injected and measured. The flow condition of the LC was eluted at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min.&nbsp;</p><p>Linear gradient condition was 0 min 80% A, 0.5 min 80% A, 15 min 2% A, 25 min 2 A, 25 min 80 % A, 32 min 80% A.</p>	<p>The orbitrap LC-MS/MS analyses were performed on a Q Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cleveland, OH, USA). The source was an ESI operated in negative mode. Settings were as follows; Spray voltage 3 kV, Vaporizer temperature 350 °C, Capillary temperature 275 °C, Sheath N2 gas flow rate 50, Aux N2 gas flow rate 15, S-lens level 50, Probe position C, +1.75.</p><p>For full MS resolution was 7.00E+4 with scan range 215-800 m/z, AGC target 1.00E+6 and maximum IT 100 ms.</p><p>For data-dependent MS/MS resolution was 1.75E+4, Isolation window (m/z) ±0.8 Da, Stepped normalized collision energy Step20/40/60, Loop count 3, AGC target 5.00E+4, Maximum IT 60 ms, Dynamic exclusion 4 s / 10 s / 30 s and Intensity threshold 8.00E+4.</p>	<p>The raw files were produced by Xcalibur Version 4.1, using the software Compound Discoverer Version 3.1, and the negative ion mode data were imported into Compound Discoverer Version 3.1 software for data preprocessing, filtering, alignment, and peak identification. </p>	<p>The identified metabolites, retention times (Rt), mass-to-charge ratios (m/z), peak area values, MS and MS/MS fragments, and pathway information were obtained for all samples. The raw files were normalized by Compound Discoverer Version 3.1 software and MS and MS/MS fragments were used to compare the MS fragment and MS/MS fragment library mzCloud and MS fragment library Chem Spider.</p>

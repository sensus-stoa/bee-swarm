STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS2096
Study Title	Differential alteration of plant functions by homologous fungal candidate effectors
Study Description	<p>Rust fungi are plant pathogens that cause epidemics which threatens the production of many important plant species, such as wheat, soy, coffee and poplar. Melampsora larici-populina (Mlp) causes the poplar rust and encodes at least 1184 candidate effectors (CEs), however their functions are poorly known. In this study, we analysed the transcriptome and metabolome of Arabidopsis constitutively expressing CEs of Mlp to discover processes targeted by these fungal proteins. For this purpose, we sequenced the transcriptome and used mass spectrometry to analyse the metabolome of Arabidopsis plants expressing one of 14 selected CEs and of a control line. We found 2299 deregulated genes in at least one of the 14 transgenic lines. Among the down-regulated genes, the KEGG pathways “MAPK signaling pathway” and “Plant-pathogen interaction” were over-represented in six and five of the 14 transgenic lines, respectively. Moreover, the genes down-regulated across the fourteen transgenic lines are related to hormone response and defense. </p><p>Regarding the metabolome, there were 680 metabolites deregulated across the 14 transgenic lines, with highly unsaturated and phenolic compounds and peptides enriched among down-regulated and up-regulated metabolites, respectively, in almost all transgenic lines. Interestingly, we found that some transgenic lines expressing CEs with no similarity in amino acid sequence had similar patterns of gene and metabolite deregulation, while plants expressing CEs from the same family deregulated different genes and metabolites. Taken together, our results indicate that the sequence of effectors may not be a good predictor of its impact in the plant.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><em>Arabidopsis thaliana</em> transgenic plants in Columbia-0 background expressing GFP alone (control) or fused to a candidate secreted effector protein of the fungus <em>Melampsora larici-populina</em> (Mlp37347, Mlp72983, Mlp102036, Mlp106078, Mlp123218, Mlp123227, Mlp123531, Mlp124256, Mlp124266, Mlp124357, Mlp124466, Mlp124497, Mlp124499, Mlp124518) previously obtained in our laboratory [1], were grown at 22 °C at 12h/12h&nbsp;light/dark cycles. Pooled aerial tissue of 2-week-old soil-grown plants, with four replicates per genotype, were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Germain H, Joly DL, Mireault C, Plourde MB, Letanneur C, Stewart D, Morency MJ, Petre B and Duplessis S. Armand SéguinInfection assays in Arabidopsis reveal candidate effectors from the poplar rust fungus that promote susceptibility to bacteria and oomycete pathogens. Molecular plant pathology (2018) 19(1), 191–200. doi.org/10.1111/mpp.12514</p>	<p>After pulverizing the tissues with a TissueLyser (30&nbsp;cycles per second for 45 seconds repeated 3 times), we added 300&nbsp;μL of distilled water to it. From the mix of tissue and water, we used 100&nbsp;μL for an extraction with 20% methanol and 100&nbsp;μL for an extraction with 80% methanol. After agitation of the samples with 1&nbsp;mL of solvent, we pooled the replicates of the same genotype together and filtered them using glass microfiber filters (Whatman GF/F CAT No. 1825-025). We evaporated the extracts with a speed vacuum at room temperature and chamber vacuum of 7.4 torr’s and resolubilized them in 2&nbsp;mL of distilled water. Then, we filtered 50&nbsp;µg of dissolved organic carbon (DOC) of each sample and resuspended it in 1 mL of 100% methanol, using PPL cartridges.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry was performed in an Orbitrap LTQ-Velos calibrated and tuned to maximize the peak at 369.1 in SRFA. The extracts were analysed by direct injection in negative mode at a resolution setting 100&nbsp;000, with accumulation time set to a maximum of 2&nbsp;s and a maximum of 5 x 10^4 ions.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry was performed in an Orbitrap LTQ-Velos calibrated and tuned to maximize the peak at 369.1 in SRFA. The extracts were analysed by direct injection in negative mode at a resolution setting 100&nbsp;000, with accumulation time set to a maximum of 500&nbsp;ms and a maximum of 1 x 10^6 ions.</p>	<p>Peaks were only considered for formula assignment if their intensity was higher than 3x the noise baseline. We assigned formulas to masses using an in-house MATLAB script described in Hawkes, et al. [1] and we allowed assignments with mass error &lt; 2&nbsp;ppm. Briefly, formulae were considered over the ranges C4-50H4-100O2-40N0-2 under the conditions O ≤ C; 0.3C ≤ H ≤ 2.2C. For each sample, the intensity of the peaks was normalised so that the sum of the intensities equalled 10&nbsp;000.</p><p><br></p><p>References:</p><p>[1] Hawkes JA, Patriarca C, Sjöberg PJR, Tranvik LJ. Jonas Bergquist Extreme isomeric complexity of dissolved organic matter found across aquatic environments. Limnology and Oceanography Letters, 3(2), 21-30 - April 2018. https://doi.org/10.1002/lol2.10064</p>	<p>We searched the molecular formulas, obtained with the in-house script, in KEGG database using the R package KEGGREST (version 1.28.0) for identification of the metabolites detected. We also used the <a href='http://motif.gla.ac.uk/Pathos/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>Pathos </a>to search for metabolites with the same M/Z (settings: negative mode, all organisms, -H+ as adduct and mass error at 3ppm).&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>

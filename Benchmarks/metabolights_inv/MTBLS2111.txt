Study Identifier	MTBLS2111
Study Title	Ultraperformance Liquid Chromatography Coupled with Quadrupole Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry-Based Metabolomics and Lipidomics Identify Biomarkers for Efficacy Evaluation of Mesalazine in a Dextran Sulfate Sodium-Induced Ulcerative Colitis Mouse Model
Study Description	This study aims to identify biomarkers for evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of mesalazine on ulcerative colitis by metabolomics and lipidomics. A dextran sulfate sodium-induced mouse model was used. The disease status was assessed by a disease activity index, the TNF-α level of colon was measured by an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and the pathological changes of colon tissue was examined by hematoxylin-eosin staining. Serum metabolomics and lipidomics analysis based on ultraperformance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry were applied to decipher the metabolic profile changes. Multivariate analysis was applied to differentiate the metabolites of controls, models, and mesalazine-treated mice. By the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, 40 differential metabolites with an area under curve (AUC) >0.80 were screened out between control and model groups. Among them, four potential biomarkers (palmitoyl glucuronide, isobutyrylglycine, PC (20:3 (5Z, 8Z, 11Z)/15:0) and L-arginine) had a signficantly reversed level of peak areas in the mesalazine group, and three of them were closely correlated with mesalazine efficacy by linear regression analysis. Furthermore, metabolic pathway analysis revealed several dysregulated pathways in colitis mice, including glycerophospholipid metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, arginine biosynthesis, etc. This study indicates that serum metabolomics is a useful approach that can noninvasively evaluate the therapeutic effect and provide unique insights into the underlying mechanism of mesalazine.

Study Protocol Description	<p>6 wk-old male <strong>C57BL/6J mice</strong> were supplied by Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co, Ltd. (Hunan, China). The animals were acclimated to the environment for a week prior to the study. With a 12 h light/dark cycle, all animals were kept under constant humidity (55 ± 5%) and room temperature. 3 experimental groups were set up by randomly assigning 10 mice to each group: <strong>(1)</strong> Control group: the mice were given normal water and were orally administrated with saline solution once a day for 7 d; <strong>(2)</strong> model group: 3% DSS was given to the mice in drinking water to establish the UC model, and the mice were orally administrated with saline solution once a day for 7 d; <strong>(3)</strong> mesalazine group: 3% DSS was given to the mice in drinking water, and the mice were orally administrated with mesalazine (200 mg/kg) once a day for 7 d. 24 h after the last dose, the mice were sacrificed. Serum samples were collected. Intestine samples from the anus to the ileocecal part were resected. All biological samples were kept at −80 °C after disposing.</p>	<p><strong>For lipidomics analysis</strong>, 150 μl methanol was added into 50 μl serum. The mixture was vortexed for 1 min. Then, the lipid metabolites in the mixed solution were extracted with 500 μl MTBE by shaking at room temperature for 1 h. Then, 125 μl H2O was added to the samples, the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, 4 °C, and then the organic phase at the upper layer was collected. The residue was extracted again using MTBE/methanol/H2O (20:6:5 v/v/v) mixture with the same procedure. The 2 parts of organic solution were mixed and evaporated by Termovap Sample Concentrator. The residue was diluted with isopropanol/methanol (1:1 v/v) mixture before injecting into the autosampler.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For the polar metabolomics analysis</strong>, 150 μl methanol was added into 50 μl serum. The mixture was vortexed for 1 min and the samples were set at −20 °C for 20 min. Next, samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, then the upper layer was collected and evaporated by Termovap Sample Concentrator. The residue was diluted with water before the UPLC/MS analysis. Each analyzed sample was pooled together with equal volume to make quality control (QC) samples.</p>	<p>An Agilent Technologies 1290 II Infinity LC system coupled with a 6545 Q-TOF mass spectrometer was applied to lipidomic and metabolomic analysis. A UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, Manchester) was used to separate the samples.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Lipidomics UPLC conditions:</strong> mobile phase A is ACN/H2O (6:4 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate. Mobile phase B 2-propanol/ACN (9:1 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate.</p><p><strong>Lipidomics gradient elution conditions:</strong> 0-0.5 min, A 70% B 30%; 0.5-2 min, A 50% B 50%; 2-15 min, A 35% B 65%; 15-19 min, A 0% B 100%; 19-22 min, A 0% B 100%; 22-22.5 min, A 70% B 30%; and 22.5-25 min, A 70% B 30%. The flow rate was 0.4 ml/min. The column temperature was 55 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Polar metabolomics UPLC conditions:</strong> mobile phase A is 100% H2O with 0.1% formic acid. Mobile phase B ACN/2-propanol (7.5:2.5 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid.</p><p><strong>Polar metabolomics gradient elution conditions:</strong> 0-0.5 min, A 70% B 30%; 0.5-2 min, A 70% B 30%; 2-8 min, A 15% B 85%; 8-11 min, A 1% B 99%; 11-13 min, A 99.9% B 0.1%; and 13-15 min, A 99.9% B 0.1%. The flow rate was 0.4 ml/min. The column temperature was 45 °C.</p>	<p>The Agilent 6545 Q-TOF mass spectrometer was run under electrospray ionization (ESI) positive and negative ion modes. The capillary, fragmentor, nozzle and skimmer voltages were 3.5 kV, 140 V, 500 V and 65 V, respectively. The source temperature was 350 °C. The flow rates of drying and sheath gases were 6 and 12 L/min, respectively. The nebulizer pressure was 45 psi. The mass data were collected in a centroid mode at the range of m/z 50-1700 using an extended dynamic range.</p>	<p>The raw data were processed by <strong>Agilent MassHunter Profinder</strong> and subsequently analyzed for peak detection, peak alignment, metabolites feature annotation, and peak intensity measurement on the <strong>XCMS Online platform</strong>. The normalized data were analyzed by the <strong>principle component analysis (PCA)</strong> and <strong>orthogonal projection to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA</strong>) by the <strong>SIMCA-P (14.1, Umetrics AB, Sweden)</strong>. The relative importance of variables was visualized and presented as <strong>VIP (variable importance in the projection)</strong> by a <strong>OPLS-DA-generated</strong> volcano plot. <strong>VIP &gt; 1</strong> was set as one of the criteria for variable screening. The <strong>fold change (FC)</strong> of the corresponding peak area of selected variables between groups was calculated, and <strong>p &lt; 0.05</strong>, which was obtained by the <strong>Student t test</strong>, was set as a threshold. The <strong>p value</strong> was further corrected by the <strong>Benjamini−Hochberg method</strong> to obtain the <strong>false discovery rate (FDR)</strong>. Molecular features with <strong>VIP ≥ 1.0</strong>, <strong>FC ≥ 1.2</strong>, and <strong>FDR &lt; 0.05</strong> were considered to be potential biomarkers.</p>	<p>The qualified molecular features were identified by using the <strong>Mass Hunter Workstation Software-qualitative analysis (Agilent Technologies, U.S.A.)</strong> allowing a 10 ppm mass error, and searching the public databases (<strong>KEGG, the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes</strong> and <strong>HMDB, the Human Metabolome Database</strong>) for data feature comparison and composite structure matching. The metabolomic and lipidomic profiles in different groups were compared by hierarchical clustering heatmap analysis. The pathway enrichment analysis was performed in <strong>MetaboAnalyst</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>The <strong>receiver operating characteristic (ROC)</strong> curve analysis was run by <strong>SPSS 19.0 (IBM, U.K.)</strong> and used to assess the differential ability of the candidate biomarkers. The correlation between DAI and biomarkers was analyzed by <strong>linear regression (GraphPad Prism 8.0, San Diego, U.S.A.)</strong>.</p>

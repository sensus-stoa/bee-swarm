Study Identifier	MTBLS2131
Study Title	A multi-omics approach to study peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor mediated regulation of lipid metabolism in Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua)
Study Description	Lipid metabolism is essential in maintaining energy homeostasis in multicellular organisms. In vertebrates, a group of nuclear receptor transcription factors named peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs, NR1C) regulate the expression of many genes involved in these processes. We have recently cloned the four Ppars in Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua), including Ppara1 and Ppara2, Pparb/d, and Pparg, and studied their tissue specific transcription and ligand activation characteristics. However, the downstream regulative role of Ppars in cod lipid metabolism is not well understood or described. In this study, activation of Atlantic cod Ppar by the fibrate drug WY-14,643 (pirinixic acid) and the performance enhancing drug GW501516 (Cardarine) were first studied using ligand-binding luciferase reporter assays in vitro. Based on the agonist activities found in vitro, juvenile Atlantic cod was injected twice over four days with WY-14,643 and GW501516 in vivo, and sampled seven days after the last injection. Using multiple omics methods, including RNA sequencing, quantitative proteomics, and lipidomics, liver and plasma samples from male cod were analyzed. The resulting multi-omics dataset provides novel insights into the systemic regulation of lipid metabolism in Atlantic cod.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Atlantic cod (<em>G. morhua</em>) were obtained from Havbruksstasjonen in Tromsø AS (Nofima, Tromsø, Norway) and maintained at the Industrial and Aquatic Laboratory (ILAB, Bergen, Norway). Juvenile Atlantic cod approximately 15 mth old were kept in 500 L tanks in 6 °C, 34 ppt seawater with a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle. The fish were fed with a commercial diet (Amber Neptune, Skretting, Stavanger, Norway) until the start of the treatment. The mean body weight ± standard deviation of the fish at the start of the experiment was 205 ± 37 g. The reproductive immaturity of the fish was confirmed at sampling. The fish were maintained and treated in accordance with the guidelines of the Norwegian Board of Biological Experiments with Living Animals and with the necessary approval (FOTS ID 11730).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>WY-14,643</strong> (4-Chloro-6-(2,3-xylidino)-2-pyrimidinylthioacetic acid, Pirinixic acid; Sigma Aldrich) and <strong>GW501516</strong> (2-[2-Methyl-4-[[[4-methyl-2-[4-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]-5-thiazolyl]methyl]thio]phenoxy]-acetic acid; Sigma Aldrich) were dissolved in 10% DMSO, 90% teleost saline (2.41 mM KCl, 133.5 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM CaCl2, 0.79 mM MgSO4, 1 mM NaHCO3, 0.5 mM Na2HPO4). The final concentrations administered were <strong>4.0 (low dose)</strong> and <strong>40 mg/kg (high dose)</strong> for <strong>WY14,64</strong>3, and <strong>0.4 (low dose) and 4.0 mg/kg (high dose)</strong> for <strong>GW501516</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>The fish were acclimatized in 150 L treatment tanks for 16 d prior to exposure and fed daily 1% of their biomass. In total, 109 fish were divided into <strong>5 different groups</strong>, with <strong>21-22 individuals in each group</strong>: <strong>Solvent control (Control)</strong>, <strong>low and high dose WY-14,643 (WY_LD and WY_HD)</strong>, and <strong>low and high dose GW501516 (GW_LD and GW_HD)</strong>. Of these, 4 fish belonging to different treatment groups died or were euthanized before sampling. The fish were injected at <strong>day 0</strong> and <strong>day 4</strong>, and sampled at <strong>day 11</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>At sampling, the fish were killed with a blow to the head, and weight, length, and sex were registered <strong>(FAIRDOM DOI)</strong>. Samples of blood, liver, bile, muscle, head kidney, and gills were collected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C. Only samples from <strong>male fish</strong> were chosen for the subsequent analyses.</p><p><br></p><p>Targeted lipidomic analysis was performed on plasma and isolated liver microsomes of <strong>8 male</strong> fish from <strong>solvent control (Control)</strong> and <strong>High-Dose</strong> groups (n = 8) at Cinta Porte’s lab at CSIC, Spain, using Flow Injection Analysis High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry (FIA-HRMS).</p>	<p>Liver samples were homogenized as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>, with some modifications. Homogenates were centrifuged at 1000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and further centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 45 min and 20,000 x g for 30 min. The obtained supernatant was centrifuged at 100,000 x g for 90 min. Microsomal pellets were then resuspended in a small volume of 100 mM phosphate buffer pH 7.4, 1 mM EDTA, 0.1 mM DTT, 0.1 mM PMSF and 20% w/v glycerol. Protein concentrations were determined by the method of Bradford, using bovine serum albumin as a standard<strong>[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Lipids of 50 µl of plasma were extracted with ethyl acetate. Samples were incubated for 30 min at room temperature and soft shaking. The organic upper layer was collected and the extraction was repeated twice. The final extract was evaporated under nitrogen. Microsomal lipids were extracted by a modification of a previously described method by Folch<strong>[3]</strong>. 200 µg protein were homogenized in ice-cold chloroform:methanol (2:1 v/v) containing 0.01% butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT). Samples were incubated for 30 min at room temperature and soft shaking; 0.88% KCl was added to the supernatant (1:4 v/v), thoroughly mixed and further incubated for 10 min. After centrifugation (2500 rpm, 10 min, 10 °C) the organic layer was collected, the extraction was repeated, and the solvent evaporated under nitrogen. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Blanco M, Rizzi J, Fernandes D, Colin N, Maceda-Veiga A, Porte C. Assessing the impact of waste water effluents on native fish species from a semi-arid region, NE Spain. Sci Total Environ. 2019 Mar 1;654:218-225. doi:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2018.11.115. PMID:30445323.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Bradford MM. A rapid and sensitive method for the quantitation of microgram quantities of protein utilizing the principle of protein-dye binding. Anal Biochem. 1976 May 7;72:248-54. doi:10.1006/abio.1976.9999. PMID:942051.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Folch J, Lees M, Stanley GHS. A simple method for the isolation and purification of total lipides from animal tissues. J Biol Chem. 1957 May;226(1):497-509. PMID:13428781.</p>	<p>The analysis of lipids was performed as previously described[1], with some modifications. Reconstituted lipid extracts were analyzed by direct injection in an Orbitrap-Exactive HCD mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with heated electrospray source (H-ESI II), a Surveyor MS Plus pump and an Accela Open AS autosampler kept at 15 ºC (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, California). The mobile phase was methanol/dichloromethane 80:20 at 50 µl/min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Vichi S, Cortés-Francisco N, Caixach J. Ultrahigh resolution mass spectrometry and accurate mass measurements for high-throughput food lipids profiling. J Mass Spectrom. 2012 Sep;47(9):1177-90. doi:10.1002/jms.3017. PMID:22972786.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were acquired on a Thermo Orbitrap-Exactive HCD mass spectrometer in full scan both in ESI+ and ESI- ionization mode. The acquisition mass range was set to m/z 200-2000 and the total analysis time was 2 min. The ultrahigh resolving power defined as R=100000 (m/z 200, FWHM) was set.</p>	<p>Univariate analysis of the lipid profiles was performed using the <strong>Metaboanalyst software (version 3.0)[1]</strong>. The missing values were first replaced using the <strong>KNN algorithm</strong>. Data were scaled by mean-centering and divided by the standard deviation of each variable<strong>[2]</strong>. Volcano plots based on fold-change values (&gt;1.5) and a significance threshold of p &lt;0.05 <strong>(Student t-test)</strong> were used to visualize the significance and magnitude of the changes in the exposed groups compared to the control group. Enrichment analysis was performed using <strong>LION/web[3]</strong>. Prior to this analysis, data were normalized, and the imputation of missing values was performed using half of the minimum value found in the dataset. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Xia J, Sinelnikov IV, Han B, Wishart DS. MetaboAnalyst 3.0--making metabolomics more meaningful. Nucleic Acids Res. 2015 Jul 1;43(W1):W251-7. doi:10.1093/nar/gkv380. PMID: 25897128.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Xia J, Wishart DS. Using MetaboAnalyst 3.0 for Comprehensive Metabolomics Data Analysis. Curr Protoc Bioinformatics. 2016 Sep 7;55:14.10.1-14.10.91. doi:10.1002/cpbi.11. PMID:27603023.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Molenaar MR, Jeucken A, Wassenaar TA, van de Lest CHA, Brouwers JF, Helms JB. LION/web: a web-based ontology enrichment tool for lipidomic data analysis. Gigascience. 2019 Jun 1;8(6):giz061. doi:10.1093/gigascience/giz061. PMID:31141612.</p>	<p>Mass peaks considered were single positive charged sodium molecular ions, <strong>[M+Na+]</strong>, including <strong>triacylglycerols (TG)</strong>, <strong>diacylglycerols (DG)</strong>, <strong>phosphatidylcholines (PC)</strong>, their <strong>plasmanyl/plasmenyl forms (PCO/PCP)</strong>, <strong>cholesteryl esters (CE)</strong>; and single negative charge <strong>[M-H+]</strong>, including <strong>phosphatidylethanolamines (PE)</strong>, their <strong>plasmanyl/plasmenyl forms (PEO/PEP)</strong>, <strong>phosphatidylserines (PS)</strong>, <strong>phosphatidylinositols (PI)</strong>, <strong>phosphatidylglycerols (PG)</strong>, and <strong>free fatty acids (FA)</strong>. Data from <strong>LIPID MAPS</strong>, exact mass, isotopic distributions, charge, adducts formed, number of rings plus double bonds (<strong>RDB=0.5-15</strong>) and elements in formula, were used for the identification of the lipid molecules with a maximum permitted mass error fixed at 5 ppm. Mass spectra were processed with <strong>Xcalibur (v2.1, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany)</strong> and lipid species were quantified with internal standards.</p>

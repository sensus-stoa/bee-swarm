Study Identifier	MTBLS2150
Study Title	Hyperhomocysteinemia-related serum metabolome alterations not normalized by short-term folic acid treatment
Study Description	<h4>Introduction</h4>Hyperhomocysteinemia (HHCys) is an independent risk factor for various diseases such as cardiovascular diseases, Alzheimer's, and cancers. Folate deficiency is one of the significant reasons for HHCys. However, it is not known whether folate deficiency with HHCys is associated with any serum metabolites.<h4>Objectives</h4>Our objective was to identify the metabolic alterations in people having folate deficiency with HHCys and check whether a short-term folic acid therapy could reverse those metabolic changes.<h4>Methods</h4>The study enrolled 34 participants aged between 18 and 40 years having folate deficiency (< 4.6 ng/mL) with HHCys (> 15 μmol/L) and 21 normal healthy individuals. A short-term intervention of oral folic acid (5 mg/day) was done in the HHCys group for 30 days. Untargeted metabolomics analysis of serum was performed in all study subjects before and after the folic acid treatment. Different univariate methods and the multivariable-adjusted linear regression models were employed to determine an association between homocysteine level and metabolite profile.<h4>Results</h4>Metabolomics analysis data showed that many metabolites involved in the biochemical pathways of lipid metabolisms such as polyunsaturated fatty acids, glycerolipids, and phospholipids were downregulated in the HHCys group. Short-term oral folic acid therapy significantly reduced their serum homocysteine level. However, the metabolic pathway alterations observed in folate-deficient HHCys-condition were unaltered even after the folic acid treatment.<h4>Conclusions</h4>Our study revealed that people who have a folic acid deficiency with HHCys have an altered metabolite profile related to lipid metabolism, which cannot be reversed by short-term folic acid therapy.

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study was approved by the institutional ethical committee of Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Biotechnology and Medical Trust Hospital and Diabetes Care Centre. All the participants were informed of the objectives of the study, and their consent to participate in the study was obtained. The serum folate level of less than 4.6 ng/mL was defined as folate deficiency in our research. The serum HCys level of higher than 15 μmol/L was defined as HHCys<strong>[1]</strong>. The study included both men and women aged between 18 and 40 yrs, BMI not greater than 25 kg/m2 , and fasting blood sugar (FBS) not higher than 100 mg/dL. People who have a history of gastrointestinal surgery, chronic diseases, smoking or regular drug use or alcoholism, vitamin, amino acid, or protein supplementation, or chronic consumption of a high protein diet or any known illness were excluded from the study. The study recruited 34 participants having folate deficiency with HHCys and 21 normal healthy individuals. Participants have been recruited from Pathanamthitta district in Kerala, India. Study participants with HHCys were supplemented with a daily oral dose of 5 mg folic acid (a vitamin supplement) for 30 days. Samples were collected at the fasting state from all study subjects during the morning to minimize the circadian component of metabolite expression. Whole blood was collected into tubes (Safelab Fluoride Oxalate Tubes) without anticoagulant for the separation of serum. The collected serum samples were used for the estimation of fasting blood glucose, cholesterol, Serum Iron, Serum ferritin, homocysteine, and serum folate. The remaining serum samples were stored at − 20 °C freezer for further analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Tinelli C, Di Pino A, Ficulle E, Marcelli S, Feligioni M. Hyperhomocysteinemia as a Risk Factor and Potential Nutraceutical Target for Certain Pathologies. Front Nutr. 2019 Apr 24;6:49. doi:10.3389/fnut.2019.00049. PMID:31069230</p>	<p>Serum samples collected in a cryovial tube were stored at -20° C&nbsp;until the metabolomics analysis. Methanol was used for the protein precipitation from the serum. The quality control (QC) samples for the metabolomics analysis were prepared by pooling an equal volume of serum samples from all the samples. Samples were centrifuged at 14,000 g at 4 °C for 20 min, and supernatants were dried at room temperature using the SpeedVac concentrator. Samples were reconstituted in 100 µL of a solvent containing 50% methanol and 0.1% formic acid<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Kumar AA, Satheesh G, Vijayakumar G, Chandran M, Prabhu PR, Simon L, Kutty VR, Kartha CC, Jaleel A. Postprandial Metabolism is Impaired in Overweight Normoglycemic Young Adults without Family History of Diabetes. Sci Rep. 2020 Jan 15;10(1):353. doi:10.1038/s41598-019-57257-2. PMID:31941993</p>	<p>An Ultra-Performance Liquid Chromatography, ACQUITY UPLC System (Waters) attached to a Quadrupole - Time of Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (UPLC-MS/MS, SYNAPT-G2, Waters) was used for the analysis. Reversed-Phase Liquid Chromatography (RPLC) technique was employed with a C18 column (ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column, 100 A°, 1.8 μm, 2.1mm X 100mm, Waters). In the RPLC Method, mobile phase consisted of aqueous (A) and organic (B) solvent components, where A was 0.1% formic acid in water and B was 0.1% formic acid in methanol. The gradient was 0 min, 0% B; 1 min, 0% B; 16 min, 100% B; 20 min, 100% B, and 22 min, 0% B. The Hydrophilic Interaction Chromatography (HILIC) method was performed with a BEH column (ACQUITY BEH HILIC column, 130 A°, 1.7 μm, 2.1mm X 150mm, Waters). For the HILIC mobile phase, A was 95% Acetonitrile in 10mM ammonium acetate and B was 50% Acetonitrile in 10mM ammonium acetate. Both A and B contained 0.1% formic acid and 0.1% acetic acid in positive ion mode and negative ion mode respectively. The HILIC positive ion gradient was 0 min, 1% B; 5 min, 20% B; 10 min, 50% B; 14 min, 95% B; 16 min, 95% B; 17 min, 1% B; and 20 min, 1% B. The injection volume was 5 µL with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min and the column temperature of 40 °C<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Kumar AA, Satheesh G, Vijayakumar G, Chandran M, Prabhu PR, Simon L, Kutty VR, Kartha CC, Jaleel A. Postprandial Metabolism is Impaired in Overweight Normoglycemic Young Adults without Family History of Diabetes. Sci Rep. 2020 Jan 15;10(1):353. doi:10.1038/s41598-019-57257-2. PMID:31941993</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer was operated in resolution mode (MSE) with electrospray ionization (ESI). The selected mass range was from 50 to 1200 m/z in full scan mode with a scan time of 0.25s and an inter-scan delay of 0.024 s. Ion source and desolvation gas (nitrogen) temperatures were kept at 112 °C and 350 °C respectively. The sampling cone and desolvation gas flow rates were 60 L/h and 875 L/h respectively. Capillary voltage was set at 2.85 kV. Sampling cone voltage was set at 40 V. The lock mass acquisition was made every 10 s by Leucine enkephalin (MW= 555.62) for accurate on-line mass calibration. Initial 10 QC samples run was used for the stabilization of the system. QC samples were acquired after every 5th sample run<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Kumar AA, Satheesh G, Vijayakumar G, Chandran M, Prabhu PR, Simon L, Kutty VR, Kartha CC, Jaleel A. Postprandial Metabolism is Impaired in Overweight Normoglycemic Young Adults without Family History of Diabetes. Sci Rep. 2020 Jan 15;10(1):353. doi:10.1038/s41598-019-57257-2. PMID:31941993</p>	<p><strong>MassLynx4.1 SCN781</strong> (Waters) was used for data acquisition and <strong>Progenesis QI</strong>. (Non-linear dynamics, Waters) was employed for data analysis<strong>[1]</strong>. <strong>MetaX </strong>software was used for the signal correction method called QC-RLSC (Quality Control-Robust Loess Signal Correction) to correct the batch influence<strong>[2]</strong>. The missing values were imputed with the k-nearest neighbor (KNN) method. The features detected in &gt;50% of the QC samples and &gt;20% of the study samples were retained. Metabolite features with a relative standard deviation of &lt; 30% in the QC samples were used for further statistical analysis<strong>[3]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Kumar AA, Satheesh G, Vijayakumar G, Chandran M, Prabhu PR, Simon L, Kutty VR, Kartha CC, Jaleel A.

Study Identifier	MTBLS2279
Study Title	Metabolomic profiling reveals the effects of early-life lactoferrin intervention on protein synthesis, energy production and antioxidative capacity in the liver of suckling piglets
Study Description	<p>The objective of this study was to reveal the effects of early life lactoferrin intervention on the liver metabolism in the suckling piglets. Sixty newborn piglets with an average initial body weight (BW) of 1.51 ± 0.05 kg were assigned to the control (CON) group and the lactoferrin (LF) group. Piglets in the LF group were orally administered LF solution (0.5 g/kg BW per day) from 1 to 7 days of age, and the piglets in the CON group were orally administrated with the same dose of physiological saline. Plasma, jejunum and liver samples were collected on day 8 and 21. The LF piglets had a decreased level of plasma urea nitrogen on day 8 and an increased level of plasma albumin on day 21. Pathway analysis of metabolomic profiles showed that LF treatment affected amino acid metabolism in the liver of piglets. Additionally, LF treatment upregulated the gene expression of proteolytic enzyme and amino acid transporters (APA, aminopeptidases A; APN, aminopeptidases N; EAAC 1, excitatory amino acid carrier 1; Pept 1, peptide transporter 1) in the jejunum, and enhanced phosphorylation levels of mTOR and p70S6K in the liver. Moreover, LF treatment upregulated the expression of β oxidation related gene (CPT 1, carnitine palmitoyl transferase-1) and affected tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle in the liver on day 21. Furthermore, LF piglets had a lower level of MDA and a higher level of GSH and GSH-px in the liver mitochondria. Overall, the early life LF intervention affected protein synthesis, energy production and antioxidative capacity in the liver of neonatal piglets.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The animal experiments in this study were conducted in accordance with the Chinese Guidelines for Animal Welfare and were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Agricultural University (Nanjing, Jiangsu Province, China). </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Animals and experimental design </strong></p><p>60 newly born <strong>piglets </strong>(Duroc × Landrace × Yorkshire) with an average initial body weight (BW) of 1.51 ± 0.05 kg were utilized in this study. 10 <strong>piglets </strong>were raised by 1 sow and <strong>piglets </strong>in 1 litter were divided into 2 groups, 1 is the <strong>control (CON)</strong> group and the other 1 is the<strong> lactoferrin (LF)</strong> group. Each group consisted of 6 <strong>replicates</strong>, and each replicate consisted of 5 newborn <strong>piglets </strong>(n = 6). At age 1 to 7 days, the piglets in the LF group were orally administered <strong>LF </strong>solution (35 mg mL−1 , Ingredia, France) 3 times per day at 09:00, 14:00 and 19:00 (0.5 g per kg BW daily), whereas the piglets in the <strong>CON </strong>group were orally administered the same dose of saline. During the suckling period (21 days), the piglets had no access to creep feed. The dose of <strong>LF </strong>used in this study was determined based on the assumption that neonatal <strong>piglets </strong>(1.5 kg BW) consume 400–700 mL of colostrum per day, and this volume of colostrum contains a mean <strong>LF </strong>concentration of 1.6 g/L, which is approximately equal to 0.5 g per kg BW<strong>[1],[2].</strong></p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample collection </strong></p><p>On days 8 and 21, 6 piglets from each group were euthanized with sodium pentobarbital (100 mg per kg BW). Blood samples (5 mL) were collected from the jugular vein, and plasma was obtained by centrifugation at 3000g for 15 min at 4 °C and stored at −80 °C until further analysis. A fraction of the fresh liver sample was snap-frozen and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Pluske JR, Williams IH, Zak LJ, Clowes EJ, Cegielski AC, Aherne FX. Feeding lactating primiparous sows to establish three divergent metabolic states: III. Milk production and pig growth. J Anim Sci. 1998 Apr;76(4):1165-71. doi: 10.2527/1998.7641165x. PMID: 9581941</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Yang TS, Wu SC, Wang SR. Serum and milk lactoferrin concentration and the correlation with some blood components in lactating sows. Res Vet Sci. 2000 Aug;69(1):95-7. doi: 10.1053/rvsc.2000.0393. PMID: 10924401</p>	<p>The liver samples were freeze-dried and ground into powder before extraction. 30 mg of each sample were added into a 5 mL glass centrifuge tube, which contained 0.3 mL of methanol/chloroform (3:1, volume [v]/v) and 20 μL of a L-2-chlorophenylalanine solution (internal standard). Subsequently, the solution was vortexed for 30 s, homogenized for 4 min, chilled for 20 min at −20 °C and then centrifuged for 15 min at 12,000 rpm (4 °C). The supernatants (0.25 mL) were then transferred into glass sampling vials. The samples were dried in a vacuum concentrator and then mixed with 80 μL of methoxy amination hydrochloride (20 mg/mL). The samples were incubated at 37 °C for 90 min after vortexed for 20 s. After incubation, 80 μL of N,O-bis[trimethylsilyl] trifluoroacetamide (BSTFA) plus 1% trimethylchlorosilane (TMCS) were added, and the mixtures were vortexed and incubated at 70°C for 30 min.&nbsp;QC sample was prepared by mixing aliquots of the all samples to be a pooled sample.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>The derivative samples (1 μL) were analyzed using an Agilent 7890B GC system (Agilent, Shanghai, China) equipped with a DB-5MS fused-silica capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; Agilent J&amp;W Scientific) and were separated by a programme warming process. Briefly, the initial oven temperature was 60 °C (held for 1 min), and then increased to 100 °C at a rate of 8°C/min. Subsequently, the temperature was increased at 10 °C/min to 170 °C. Finally, the oven temperature was increased to 305 °C at a rate of 8 °C/min and held for 4 min. Helium was used as a carrier gas at a flow rate of 3 mL/min. The temperatures of injection, MS quadrupole and ion source were 280, 150, and 230 °C, respectively.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The derivatived samples were analyzed on an Agilent 7890B gas chromatography system coupled to an Agilent 5977A MSD system (Agilent Technologies Inc.,</p><p>CA, USA). Briefly, the initial oven temperature was 60 °C (held for 1 min), and then increased to 100 °C at a rate of 8 °C/min. Subsequently, the temperature was increased at 10 °C/min to 170 °C. Finally, the oven temperature was increased to 305 °C at a rate of 8 °C/min and held for 4 min. Helium was used as a carrier gas at a flow rate of 3 mL/min. The temperatures of injection, MS quadrupole and ion source were 280, 150, and 230 °C, respectively.&nbsp;The collision energy was 70 eV. Mass spectrometric data was acquired in a full-scan mode (m/z 50-500).The QCs were injected at regular intervals throughout the analytical run to provide a set of data from which repeatability could be assessed.</p>	<p>The GC/MS data were normalized and analyzed by <strong>ChemStation </strong>(<strong>version E.02.02.1431</strong>; Agilent) and <strong>ChromaTOF </strong>(<strong>version 4.34</strong>; LECO). Normalized data were exported into the <strong>SIMCA </strong>software package (<strong>14.0</strong>; Umetrics) for principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least square-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA). PCA analysis was mainly used to visualize the trend of the samples. PLS-DA were used to determine the difference between 2 groups<strong>[1]</strong>. The R2Y parameter shows the percentage of variation explained by the models and Q2 describes the predictive ability of the models<strong>[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Yang TS, Wu SC, Wang SR. Serum and milk lactoferrin concentration and the correlation with some blood components in lactating sows. Res Vet Sci. 2000 Aug;69(1):95-7. doi: 10.1053/rvsc.2000.0393. PMID: 10924401</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Tang X, Gao J, Wang Y, Fan YM, Xu LZ, Zhao XN, Xu Q, Qian ZM. Effective protection of Terminalia catappa L. leaves from damage induced by carbon tetrachloride in liver mitochondria. J Nutr Biochem. 2006 Mar;17(3):177-82. doi: 10.1016/j.jnutbio.2005.06.008. Epub 2005 Aug 10. PMID: 16169207</p>	<p>The potential different metabolites discriminating the LF group and CON group were found by variable importance in the projection (VIP &gt;1) and Student’s t test (P &lt; 0.05) calculated by <strong>SIMCA </strong>software. Finally, metabolites with VIP values of higher than 1 and P values of lower than 0.05 were utilized to be further identified by <strong>Metabolites Biological Role</strong> (<a href='http://csbg.cnb.csic.es/mbrole2/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://csbg.cnb.csic.es/mbrole2/</a>) and performed functional enrichment analysis using <strong>MetaboAnalyst </strong>(<a href='https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/</a>).</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2288
Study Title	The Role of Annexin A1 in Candida albicans and Candida auris infections in murine neutrophils
Study Description	<p>Annexin A1 (AnxA1) is an anti-inflammatory protein present in different cells in biological systems, especially expressed in macrophages and neutrophils. Since neutrophils play an important role in infections and inflammatory processes and the AnxA1 and its relation in Candida spp. infections is not well understood, our study highlights a perspective in AnxA1 as a target protein in control the immune response during fungi infection. C57BL/6 wild-type (WT) and AnxA1 knockout (AnxA1-/-) peritoneal neutrophils were coinfected with Candida albicans or Candida auris for 4 hours. High levels of COX-2 were observed in WT and AnxA1-/- neutrophils infected with C. albicans and C. auris coinfection. In addition, AnxA1-/- neutrophils exhibited a marked increase of phosphorylated ERK, p-38 and JNK levels after coinfection with both Candida spp. Lipidomics approach shows that lack of AnxA1 produced a marked difference in lipid profile of control or coinfected neutrophil supernatants compared to WT, especially glycerophospholipids and glycerolipids. In summary, our results showed that endogenous AnxA1 regulates neutrophil response under fungal infection and its lipid membrane organization and metabolism.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Animals</strong></p><p>Male <strong>C57BL/6 wild-type </strong>(<strong>WT</strong>) and <strong>AnxA1-null </strong>(<strong>AnxA1-/-</strong>) mice 7–8 wks of age with a weight of 20–25 g were kept in cages (4 mice per cage) in temperature-controlled environment (22 to 25 °C) on a 12-h light-dark cycle and received water and food ad libitum. All animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee in Animal Experimentation of the Federal University of São Paulo-UNIFESP (CEUA agreement number: 6493130318) and by the Internal Biosafety Commission.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Isolation of murine neutrophils </strong></p><p><strong>WT </strong>and <strong>AnxA1− /− peritoneal neutrophils</strong> were obtained by performing an intraperitoneal injection with a 0.3% carrageenan solution, in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), followed by cell collection by peritoneal wash after 3 h. Differential cell counts were obtained using Diff-Quick-stained cell smears, prepared by cytocentrifugation. The obtained neutrophil populations were 84-90% pure and at least 90% viable, as assessed by trypan blue exclusion. Peritoneal cells were cultured at 37 °C, in a 5% CO2 atmosphere at 1 × 106 cells/1.5 ml conical microtubes in Opti-MEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA)</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Candida albicans and Candida auris strains and growth conditions</strong></p><p><strong><em>Candida albicans</em></strong> (ATCC 90029) and <strong><em>Candida auris</em></strong> (CBS 10913)<strong>[1]</strong> were used in this study. We cultured Candida spp. on Sabourand (SAB) agar (Difco, BD, USA), at 37 ◦C for 24 h. The yeasts were suspended in 5 ml pyrogen-free PBS to perform yeast counts. Yeasts were counted in a Neubauer Chamber and diluted 1:1 with trypan blue dye, to determine cell viability. For the in vitro assay, we used 1 × 106 cells/mL.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Phagocytosis index</strong></p><p>The amount of phagocytosis was measured using the phagocytosis index (PI)(ODA; KUBELKA; ALVIANO; TRAVASSOS, 1983; ROSSATO; SANTOS; FERREIRA; DE ALMEIDA, 2019; ROSSATO; SILVANA DOS SANTOS; FERREIRA; ROGÉRIO DE ALMEIDA, 2019).&nbsp;The PI was calculated according to the subsequent formula:</p><p>PI=((number of internalized yeast)/(number of neutrophils in the same field))×100</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Satoh K, Makimura K, Hasumi Y, Nishiyama Y, Uchida K, Yamaguchi H. Candida auris sp. nov., a novel ascomycetous yeast isolated from the external ear canal of an inpatient in a Japanese hospital. Microbiol Immunol. 2009 Jan;53(1):41-4. doi: 10.1111/j.1348-0421.2008.00083.x. Erratum in: Microbiol Immunol. 2018 Mar;62(3):205. PMID: 19161556.</p>	<p>After 4 hours of co-culture with <strong>Candida </strong>spp, <strong>WT </strong>and <strong>AnxA1-/-</strong> cell supernatants were collected and stored at -80°C until sample processing. For lipid extraction, the supernatants were randomized and resuspended in 380 µL 1:2 CHCl3:MeOH solution (Sigma Aldrich, Basel, Switzerland), followed by the addition of 125 µL CHCl3 and 125 µL deionized water. The solution was then stirred for 5 min, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 rpm for 5 min. The bottom, organic fraction, which contained the lipids, was collected and transferred to a 2-mL glass tube. These fractions were dried in a concentrator SpeedVac Savant SPD131DDA (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), for 30 min at 30 °C, and stored at -80 °C until use. Dried lipid extracts were reconstituted in 1 mL 50 µg/mL P-fluoro-DL186 phenylalanine (PFFA)&nbsp;solution, diluted in isopropanol/acetonitrile (ACN)/water (2:1:1, v/v/v), before performing liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis.</p>	<p>Data were acquired using an ACQUITY FTN liquid chromatograph, coupled to a XEVO-G2XSQTOF mass spectrometer (Waters, Milford, MA, USA), using MassLynx 4.1 software, as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, a Supelcosil LC-18 (2.1 × 100 mm, 5.0 µm, Supelco) column was used. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 10 mM ammonium acetate with 0.1% formic acid in ACN/water (60:40, v/v) and (B) 10 mM ammonium acetate with 0.1% formic acid in isopropanol/water (90:10, v/v), at a flow rate of 0.40 mL/min, with a linear gradient (in % B): 0-2.0 min: 40-43%; 2.0–2.1 min: 43-50%; 2.1-12.0 min: 50-54%; 12.0-12.1 min: 54-70%; 12.1-18 min: 70-99%; 18-18.1 min: decrease to 40% (with a further 1.8 min for column re-equilibration), resulting in a 20 min analysis. The injection volume was 1 µL, and the column was maintained at 55 °C. PFFA, which was used as an internal standard.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Franco EPD, Contesini FJ, Lima da Silva B, Alves de Piloto Fernandes AM, Wielewski Leme C, Gonçalves Cirino JP, Bueno Campos PR, de Oliveira Carvalho P. PMID: 31656110</p>	<p>For the electrospray ionization source, the parameters were set as follows: positive mode: capillary voltage of 3.5 kV, sampling cone of 40,000, source temperature of 140 °C, desolvation temperature of 550 °C, cone gas flow of 10 L/h, and desolvation gas flow of 900 L/h; negative mode: capillary voltage of 2.5 V, sampling cone of 40,000, source temperature of 140 °C, desolvation temperature of 550 °C, cone gas flow of 50 L/h, and desolvation gas flow of 900 L/h. The acquisition scan range was 50-1,700 Da, in the centroid, and lyses were performed using a data-independent acquisition (MSE ) approach. Leucine encephalin (molecular weight = 555.62; 200 pg/µl in 1:1 ACN:H2O solution) was used as the lock mass, for accurate mass measurements, and a 0.5 mM sodium formate solution was used for instrument calibration. Samples were randomly analyzed, to observe biological variations and minimize instrumental bias. To monitor the stability of the system, quality control (QC) samples were injected 15 times before the batch start and after every 8 injections.</p>	<p><strong>Raw </strong>data were processed with <strong>Progenesis QI </strong>software (Waters), for peak detection, alignment, integration, deconvolution, data filtering, ion annotation, and the MSE -based putative identification of compounds.</p>	<p><strong>Lipid Maps</strong> databases (<a href='http://www.lipidmaps.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.lipidmaps.org/</a>), <strong>LipidBlast </strong>(<a href='https://fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/projects/LipidBlast' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/projects/LipidBlast</a>), and <strong>Human Metabolome Database</strong> (<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca/metabolites' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.hmdb.ca/metabolites</a>) were used to perform identification, using the following search parameters: precursor mass error ≤ 5 ppm and fragment tolerance ≤ 10 ppm. The fragmentation score, mass accuracy, and isotope similarity were considered for the identification of molecules.&nbsp;</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2308
Study Title	Microbial community-scale metabolic modelling predicts personalized short-chain fatty acid production profiles in the human gut
Study Description	Microbially derived short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) in the human gut are tightly coupled to host metabolism, immune regulation and integrity of the intestinal epithelium. However, the production of SCFAs can vary widely between individuals consuming the same diet, with lower levels often associated with disease. A systems-scale mechanistic understanding of this heterogeneity is lacking. Here we use a microbial community-scale metabolic modelling (MCMM) approach to predict individual-specific SCFA production profiles to assess the impact of different dietary, prebiotic and probiotic inputs. We evaluate the quantitative accuracy of our MCMMs using in vitro and ex vivo data, plus published human cohort data. We find that MCMM SCFA predictions are significantly associated with blood-derived clinical chemistries, including cardiometabolic and immunological health markers, across a large human cohort. Finally, we demonstrate how MCMMs can be leveraged to design personalized dietary, prebiotic and probiotic interventions aimed at optimizing SCFA production in the gut. Our model represents an approach to direct gut microbiome engineering for precision health and nutrition.

Study Protocol Description	<p>All study participants were enrolled in the Arivale Scientific Wellness program, and provided consent and research authorization to allow the use of their anonymized, deidentified data for research purposes. Arivale, Inc. (USA) was a consumer scientific wellness company, which ceased operations in 2019. The study cohort had a mean age of 48 yr (SD=12). Among participants, ~60% were female, and 79% were White, 9% Asian, 4% Hispanic, and 2% Black. Blood samples for all assays included in these analyses were collected by trained phlebotomists at LabCorp or Quest service centers. Arivale participants were instructed to refrain from consuming alcohol, engaging in intense physical activity, or ingesting aspartame and monosodium glutamate for the 24-h period leading up to the blood draw. Additionally, they were required to commence a 12-h fasting period prior to the blood draw. Prior to transferring, samples were treated with EDTA to prevent coagulation, and frozen. Metabolomics analysis was then performed on baseline plasma samples from the Arivale cohort by <strong>Metabolon</strong> (USA).</p>	<p>Samples are prepared using the automated MicroLab STAR system from Hamilton Company. Several recovery standards are added prior to the first step in the extraction process for QC purposes. Samples are extracted with <strong>methanol </strong>under vigorous shaking for 2 min (Glen Mills GenoGrinder 2000) to precipitate protein and dissociate small molecules bound to protein or trapped in the precipitated protein matrix, followed by centrifugation to recover chemically diverse metabolites. The resulting extract is divided into <strong>5 fractions</strong>: <strong>2 </strong>for analysis by <strong>2 separate reverse phase (RP)/UPLC-MS/MS methods</strong> using <strong>positive </strong>ion mode electrospray ionization (<strong>ESI</strong>), <strong>1</strong> for analysis by <strong>RP/UPLC-MS/MS</strong> using <strong>negative </strong>ion mode <strong>ESI</strong>, <strong>1</strong> for analysis by <strong>HILIC/UPLC-MS/MS </strong>using <strong>negative </strong>ion mode <strong>ESI</strong>, and one reserved for backup. Samples are placed briefly on a TurboVap (Zymark) to remove the organic solvent and stored overnight under nitrogen. In preparation for analysis, samples are then reconstituted in solvents compatible to each of the 4 methods.</p>	<p>All methods utilize a <strong>Waters ACQUITY ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC)</strong>. Each reconstitution solvent contains a series of standards at fixed concentrations to ensure injection and chromatographic consistency. <strong>Method 1/Pos 1</strong>: One aliquot is analyzed using acidic positive ion conditions, chromatographically optimized for more hydrophilic compounds. In this method, the extract is gradient-eluted from an <strong>ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters)</strong> column, using water and methanol, containing 0.05% perfluoropentanoic acid (PFPA) and 0.1% formic acid (FA). <strong>Method 2/Pos 2</strong>: A second aliquot is also analyzed using acidic positive ion conditions, but is chromatographically optimized for more hydrophobic compounds. In this method, the extract is <strong>gradient eluted from the aforementioned C18 column</strong> using methanol, acetonitrile, water, 0.05% PFPA and 0.01% FA, and is operated at an overall higher organic content. <strong>Method 3/Neg 1</strong>: A third aliquot is analyzed using basic negative ion optimized conditions using a <strong>separate dedicated C18 column</strong>. The basic extracts are gradient-eluted from the column using methanol and water, however with 6.5 mM Ammonium Bicarbonate at pH 8. <strong>Method 4/Neg 2</strong>: The fourth aliquot is analyzed via negative ionization following elution from an <strong>ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 150 mm; Waters)</strong> HILIC column, using a gradient consisting of water and acetonitrile with 10 mM Ammonium Formate, pH 10.8.</p>	<p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive</strong><em> </em>high resolution/accurate mass spectrometer interfaced with a heated electrospray ionization (<strong>HESI-II</strong>) source and <strong>Orbitrap mass analyzer</strong> operated at 35,000 mass resolution was used. The MS analysis alternates between MS and data-dependent MSn scans using dynamic exclusion.</p>	Raw data were extracted, peak-identified, and QC processed using Metabolon's hardware and software. These systems are built on a web-service platform utilizing Microsoft's .NET technologies, which run on high-performance application servers and fiber-channel storage arrays in clusters to provide active failover and load-balancing.	<p>Compounds were identified by comparison to library entries of purified standards or recurrent unknown entities. <strong>Metabolon </strong>maintains a library based on authenticated standards that contains the retention time/index (RI), mass to charge ratio (m/z), and chromatographic data (including MS/MS spectral data) on all molecules present in the library. More than 4500 commercially available purified standard compounds have been acquired and registered into LIMS for analysis on all platforms for determination of their analytical characteristics. Additional mass spectral entries have been created for structurally unnamed biochemicals, which have been identified by virtue of their recurrent nature (both chromatographic and mass spectral). These compounds have the potential to be identified by future acquisition of a matching purified standard or by classical structural analysis.</p>

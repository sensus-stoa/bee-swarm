Study Title	Alterations in fecal metagenomics and serum metabolomics indicate management strategies for patients with Budd-Chiari syndrome
Study Description	<p><strong>OBJECTIVES:</strong> We investigated the effects of gut microbiota and serum metabolic levels on the patients with Budd-Chiari syndrome (B-CS), and their guidance for clinical management strategies.</p><p><strong>DESIGN:</strong> 214 B-CS subjects including untreated case (n=93) and treated case (n=121), were enrolled in this study. Gut microbiota and serum metabolome were analyzed using shotgun metagenomics and liquid chromatography mass spectrometry (LC-MS). 41 controls were included.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> The gut microbiota in B-CS patients showed greater abundance of Campylobacter and lower Saccharomyces, Deinococcus and Thiomonas (P &lt; 0.05). 13 differential metabolites such as taurocholate and (R)−3−Hydroxybutyric acid were identified in B-CS patients. The random forest (RF) models showed that serum metabolome could effectively identify B-CS from healthy controls and RF-metabolomics exhibited perfect discrimination (AUC = 100%, 95% CI: 100% – 100%), which was significantly higher than that achieved by RF-metagenomics (AUC =58.48%, 95% CI: 38.46% – 78.5%). The results of association analysis showed that Campylobacter concisus and taurocholate were significantly positively correlated in B-CS patients with clinical manifestations (P &lt; 0.05). Moreover, Actinobacteria was significantly increased in untreated B-CS patients vs. treated B-CS patients (P &lt; 0.05). Campylobacter and Veillonella were significantly increased in treated B-CS patients vs. health controls (P &lt; 0.05). In addition, Fewer discrepant microbes were found between treated and untreated groups for the patients without clinical manifestation.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> We identified major alterations in the gut microbiota and serum metabolome of B-CS patients. Alterations in fecal metagenomics and serum metabolomics guided management strategies for the patients with Budd-Chiari syndrome.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All the participants were from Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University and the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University. Patients (from inpatient and outpatient departments) in the cohort were diagnosed with Budd-Chiari syndrome by multidetector CT 3-D vascular reconstruction technique combined with color Doppler ultrasound. The patients and healthy controls did not use antibiotics in the past 3 months, and did not take probiotic preparations or dietary yoghurt in 1 month. In particular, none of the subjects could suffer from any disease affecting the intestinal microorganisms, including: obesity, hypertension, diabetes, intestinal diseases, and long-term diarrhoea and constipation. All of our healthy controls were family members who shared their diet and living environment with B-CS patients. The blood of all individuals was collected in a 5 ml EP tube, then centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to extract the serum to a new EP tube, and stored at -80 °C for further detection.</p>	<p>100 μl of each thawed sample was taken in the 2 ml EP tubes, and extracted as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, 75 μl supernatant was transferred into 2 ml LC/MS glass, and 10 μl of each sample supernatant was taken and pooled as QC samples.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Dunn WB, Broadhurst D, Begley P, Zelena E, Francis-McIntyre S, Anderson N, Brown M, Knowles JD, Halsall A, Haselden JN, Nicholls AW, Wilson ID, Kell DB, Goodacre R; Human Serum Metabolome (HUSERMET) Consortium. Procedures for large-scale metabolic profiling of serum and plasma using gas chromatography and liquid chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry. Nat Protoc. 2011 Jun 30;6(7):1060-83. doi:10.1038/nprot.2011.335. PMID:21720319.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analysis was performed with an Agilent 1290 Infinity UHPLC system coupled to a Triple TOF 6550 (Agilent Technologies) to acquire the full scan MS1 data, a Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer (AB Sciex) to acquire MS/MS spectra, and a UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters)<strong>[1]</strong>. The mobile phase was composed of 25 mmol/l ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/l ammonia hydroxide in water (pH = 9.75) (A) and acetonitrile (B). The analysis elution gradient was carried out as follows: 0~0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5~7.0 min, 95%~65% B; 7.0~8.0 min, 65%~40% B; 8.0~9.0 min, 40% B; 9.0~9.1 min, 40%~95% B; 9.1~12.0 min, 95% B. The column temperature and auto-sampler temperature were set to 25 °C and 4 °C respectively. The injection volumes were 2 μl for both positive and negative modes.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Wang J, Zhang T, Shen X, et al. Serum metabolomics for early diagnosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma by UHPLC-QTOF/MS[J]. Metabolomics, 2016, 12(7):116. doi:10.1007/s11306-016-1050-5.</p>	<p>The parameters for full scan MS1 data acquisition in 6550 QTOF mass spectrometry (Agilent Technologies), with an ESI source, were set as follows: scan range 60-1200 Da; gas temperature&nbsp;250 °C; gas flow 16 l/min; sheath gas temperature 350 °C; sheath gas flow 12 l/min; nebulizer 20 psi; fragmentor 175 V; capillary voltage 3000 V. MS/MS spectra was obtained in an information-dependent basis (IDA) mode in Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer (AB Sciex) with the acquisition software (Analyst TF 1.7, AB Sciex) with the following criteria: 12 precursor ions with intensity above 100 were selected and then fragmented in each cycle (0.56 s) under the collision energy (CE) of 30 V. Gas 1, Gas 2 and Curtain Gas set to 60 Psi, 60 Psi and 30 Psi respectively, source temperature at 600 °C, 5000 V and -4000 V of Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF) for the positive and negative mode respectively, with ESI source conditions.</p>	<p>The raw instrument data were converted to .mzXML files by MSConvert, from the the ProteoWizard suite, and subsequently analyzed with XCMS software (https://metlin.scripps.edu/xcms/) to calculate the normalized peak intensity, retention time, and exact mass.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified using an in-house MS2 database.</p>

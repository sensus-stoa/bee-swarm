Study Description	<p>The Antibiotic Resistant Sepsis Pathogens Framework Initiative aims to develop a framework dataset of 5 sepsis pathogens (5 strains each) using an integrated application of genomic, transcriptomic, metabolomic and proteomic technologies. The pathogens included in this initiative are: <em>Escherichia coli</em>, <em>Klebsiella pneumoniae</em> complex, <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em>, <em>Streptococcus pyogenes</em>, and <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em>. This submission is specifically for <em>Klebsiella pneumoniae</em> complex.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS and LC-MS <em>Klebsiella pneumoniae</em> assays</strong> are reported in the current study <strong>MTBLS2322</strong></p><p><strong>GC-MS and LC-MS <em>Escherichia coli </em>assays</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS2015' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2015</strong></a></p><p><strong>GC-MS and LC-MS <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> assays</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS1898' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS1898</strong></a></p><p><strong>GC-MS and LC-MS <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em> assays</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS2323' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2323</strong></a></p><p><strong>GC-MS and LC-MS <em>Streptococcus pyogenes</em> assays</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS2324' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2324</strong></a></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Culture:</strong></p><p><strong>Day 1</strong> - Streak isolates from glycerol stock to Luria agar plates. Incubate at 37 °C overnight.</p><p><strong>Day 2</strong> - Pick single colony from Luria agar plate and inoculate into a 500 ml flask containing 60 ml Luria broth. Grow shaking (200 rpm) at 37 °C overnight.</p><p><strong>Day 3</strong> - Thaw pooled human sera. Pipette out 25 ml of overnight culture into falcon tube. Pellet bacterial cells by spinning at 14,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min and remove supernatant with a serological pipette (do not pour off supernatant because the cell pellet is soft and will be lost).</p><p><br></p><p>Sera - Resuspend pellet in 25 ml and incubate at 37 °C shaking (200 rpm) for 2 h.</p><p><br></p><p>RPMI - Resuspend pellet in 25 ml and incubate at 37 °C shaking (200 rpm) for 1 h. Transfer 10 ml culture into 50 ml tubes.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Harvest materials:</strong></p><p>- 3:1 Methanol:Water (containing 2 μM 13C-sorbitol + 4 μM 13C, 15N-Valine)</p><p>- Dry ice/ethanol bath</p><p>- Liquid nitrogen</p><p>- 1 x PBS</p><p>- Ice/Water slurry (in esky)</p><p>- 50 ml Falcon tubes</p><p>- Eppendorf brand 1.5 ml centrifuge tubes</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample Harvest/Metabolic Arrest:</strong></p><p>01. Set up 50 ml falcon tubes containing 3 vol of 1 x PBS (relevant to culture vol required).</p><p>02. Place in an ice/water slurry to chill.</p><p>03. Infuse culture into tube containing PBS – allow to sit for at least 5 min.</p><p>04. Centrifuge at 3750 rpm in refrigerated centrifuge, 1 °C, 10 min.</p><p>05. Remove supernatant, and resuspend pellet into 1 ml PBS.</p><p>06. Transfer to 1.5 ml Eppendorf tube.</p><p>07. Centrifuge at 14000 rpm, 30 s, 1 °C.</p><p>08. Remove supernatant.</p><p>09. Wash once more with 1 ml PBS.</p><p>10. Repeat steps 7 and 8, being very careful to remove all PBS.</p><p>11. Store pellets at -80 °C until the pellets are sent for extraction.</p>	<p>All samples were randomized prior to metabolite extraction. Cells were resuspended in 1 ml extraction solution containing internal standards (ITSD, containing methanol/water, 3:1 v/v with 3 nmol 13C515N-Valine, 5 nmol 13C6-Sorbitol, 2 nmol 1,2-13C2-Myristicacid, Cambridge Isotopes), and subjected to freeze-thaw cycles to facilitate cell lysis (15 secs in liquid nitrogen, followed by 15 s in dry ice/ethanol bath for 10 cycles). Samples were centrifuged (14000 rpm, 5 min, 1 °C) to remove cell debris and cell lysate were transferred into fresh tubes. A further aliquot of cell lysate was pooled into a tube to create a pooled biological quality control (PBQC) for monitoring the stability and reproducibility of metabolites during data acquisition.</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p><span class='ql-cursor'>﻿</span>400 μL of cell lysates and PBQC were transferred into glass inserts, and dried completely in a rotational vacuum concentrator (RVC 2-33, John Morris Scientific) at 20°C. To remove all residual water, all samples were washed with 3 x 50 μL methanol. Glass inserts were transferred into 2 mL autosampler vials, followed by online derivatization with autosampler robot (PAL RTC). All samples were methoximated with 20 μL of methoxylamine hydrochloride solution (30 mg/mL in pyridine, Sigma) for 2 hr at 37°C, followed by trimethylsilylation in 20 μL of N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamidecontaining trimethylchlorosilane (BSTFA + 1% TMCS) for 1 hr at 37°C with continuous mixing, andincubated in room temperature for 1 hr prior to GC-QqQ-MS analysis. 1 μL of derivatized sample was injected into GC-QqQ-MS with the injection split ratio of 1:10. The GC oven temperature was ramped from 100°C, at which it was initially held for 4 min, to 320°C, at 10°C/min, and then held for 11 min at 320°C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>50 μl of cell extracts and PBQC were transferred into 2 ml autosampler vials with glass inserts. A mixture of 240 authentic standards (in 5 separate mixes), covering a range of metabolic pathways were run with each sepsis batch to aid metabolite identification (MSI level 1). All samples were kept in an autosampler at 4 °C, prior to be analysed using Agilent 1200 series HPLC system coupled with a 6545 Q-TOF MS. Metabolite separation was performed by injecting 7 μl sample into a SeQuant ZIC-pHILIC PEEK coated column (150 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm polymer, Merck Millipore) maintained at 25 °C, with a binary gradient made up of solvent A (20 mM ammonium carbonate, pH 9.0, Sigma-Aldrich) and solvent B (100% acetonitrile, Hypergrade for LCMS LiChrosolv, Merck) at a flow rate of 300 μl/min. A 33.0 min gradient was setup with time (t) = 0 min, 80% B; t= 0.5 min, 80% B; t= 15.5 min, 50% B; t= 17.5 min, 30% B; t= 18.5min, 5% B; t= 21.0 min, 5% B; t= 23.0 min, 80% B.</p>	<p><strong>GC-MS:</strong></p><p>All samples were analysed using GCMS-TQ8040 (Shimadzu) equipped with an J&amp;W DB-5 capillary column (30 m ×0.25 mm, 1.00 μm film thickness, Agilent Technologies). The inlet temperature was kept at 280°C, and helium was used as a carrier gas (purge flow = 5.0 mL/min, column flow = 1.1 mL/min). The transfer line and ion source temperature were 280°C and 200°C, respectively. Argon was used as the collision-induced dissociation gas. The metabolite detection was performed using the Smart Metabolites Database (Shimadzu), which contains up to 467 metabolites with multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions including precursor ion, product ion, collision energy, retention index and time, with a minimal dwell time of 2 msec setup for the acquisition method. The Automatic Adjustment of Retention Time (AART) in GCMSsolution software (version 4.42, Shimadzu) and a standard alkane series mixture (C7-C33, Restek) were used to correct retention time shifts in the acquisition method when the column is cut or replaced.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS:</strong></p><p>Metabolites were ionized in an electrospray ionization source (ESI) with a capillary voltage of 2.5 kV, drying nitrogen gas flow at 10 l/min, with the temperature and nebulizer pressure of 225 °C and 20 psi, respectively. The voltages for fragmentor and skimmer were set at -125 V and -45 V, respectively. Acquisition was conducted with a scan range of 85 to 1200 m/z at a rate of 1.5 spectra/s in negative all-ion fragmentation (AIF) mode, which included 3 collision energies (0, 10, 20 V). A QTOF reference mass solution with 2.5 μM Hexakis(1H, 1H, 3H-tetrafluoropropoxy)phosphazine and 5 μM Purine in 95:5 Acetonitrile: water (API-TOF Reference Mass Solution Kit, Agilent technologies) was continuously&nbsp;infused into the ESI source for internal mass calibration during data acquisition. In accordance with the metabolomics standards initiative (MSI), metabolite identification (MSI level 1) was based on the retention time and molecular masses matching to an authentic standard. Peak area integration was performed using MassHunter TOF Quantitative Analysis software (version B.07.00, Agilent Technologies) or Metabolomics Australia In-house Data processing pipeline.</p>	<p>Both GC-MS and LC-MS targeted data matrices were collated in sample groupings and integrated peak areas, and subjected to missing value imputation, log transformed, and median normalized prior to multivariate and univariate analyses using R (with in-house statistical analysis package). Unpaired Student t-test was performed to compare minimal RPMI and sera-grown bacteria for each strain, with Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-value used for significant changes (&lt; 0.05).</p>	<p>In accordance with the metabolomics standards initiative (MSI), metabolite identification (MSI level 1) was based on the retention time and molecular masses matching to an authentic standard.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2347
Study Title	Salmonella Typhimurium reprograms macrophage metabolism via T3SS effector SopE2 to promote intracellular replication and virulence
Study Description	<p><em>Salmonella Typhimurium</em> establishes systemic infection by replicating in host macrophages. Here we show that macrophages infected with <em>S. Typhimurium</em> exhibit upregulated glycolysis and decreased serine synthesis, leading to accumulation of glycolytic intermediates. The effects on serine synthesis are mediated by bacterial protein SopE2, a type III secretion system (T3SS) effector encoded in pathogenicity island SPI-1. The changes in host metabolism promote intracellular replication of <em>S. Typhimurium</em> via two mechanisms: decreased glucose levels lead to upregulated bacterial uptake of 2- and 3-phosphoglycerate and phosphoenolpyruvate (carbon sources), while increased pyruvate and lactate levels induce upregulation of another pathogenicity island, SPI-2, known to encode virulence factors. Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of host glycolysis, activation of host serine synthesis, or deletion of either the bacterial transport or signal sensor systems for those host glycolytic intermediates impairs <em>S. Typhimurium</em> replication or virulence.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Mice:</strong></p><p>Male BALB/c mice (6-8 wk old) obtained from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology (Beijing, China) were used for all animal experiments in this work. Mice were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with a 12 h light/dark cycle, at a temperature of 22 ± 2 °C, and a relative humidity of 50 ± 5%. Mice were fed a standard mouse chow diet. All animal studies were conducted according to protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care Committee of Nankai University (Tianjin, China) and performed under protocol no. IACUC 2016030502.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PM isolation and cell culture:</strong></p><p>Mouse peritoneal macrophages (PMs) were elicited using sodium periodate (Sigma S1878) and isolated as follows. BALB/c mice were injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) with 3 ml of sterile 3% sodium periodate in PBS. On day 4, mice were killed, and PMs were collected by flushing the peritoneal cavity with 5 ml of ice-cold PBS. Peritoneal lavage fluid was centrifuged at 300 x g for 5 min and re-suspended in serum-free RPMI medium (Gibco 11879020). Cells were plated in 10-cm tissue culture plates at a density of 5 x 10^6 cells/plate or into 24-well tissue culture plates at a density of 2 x 10^5 cells/well. After 1 h, cells were washed twice with PBS to remove non-adherent cells and were cultured in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco 10100147). The medium was changed every 2 d, and PMs were used at 7 d post collection.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bacterial strains and growth conditions:</strong></p><p>STM strain ATCC 14028 s was used as the wild-type (wt) strain throughout this study.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics analysis:</strong></p><p>PMs (5 x 10^6) were seeded in 10-cm tissue culture plates and were mock infected or infected for 8 h with wt STM (MOI = 10). To avoid a time delay and associated changes in metabolite concentrations during the separation of bacterial contents from macrophages, we quenched the cell culture immediately after STM infection of 8 h and determined the combined metabolite concentrations from infected cells and STM. The cells were harvested and washed 3x with ice-cold PBS to remove residual medium components.</p>	<p>Metabolites were extracted using a mixture of methanol/acetonitrile/dH2O (40/40/20, v/v/v) in 0.1% formic acid. After incubation at -20 °C for 20 min, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected for analysis of metabolite concentrations.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Samples were analyzed with an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatograph (Acquity, Waters) coupled to a Q Exactive Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher). Metabolites were separated on a Luna NH2 3 μm column (2 x 100 mm) (Phenomenex). The mobile phases comprised 20 mM ammonium acetate adjusted to pH 9.0 with ammonium hydroxide (A) and acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid (B).</p>	<p>The Q Exactive Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher) with a heated electrospray ionization source was operated in positive/negative modes (ionization voltage, +3.8 kV/–3.0 kV; sheath gas pressure, 35.0 arbitrary units; capillary temperature, 320 °C), and was operated in full scan (70-1000 m/z, 70,000 resolution).</p>	<p>Data were processed with <strong>Xcalibur 4.0 (Thermo Fisher)</strong>, and ions were annotated according to accurate mass spectra by a search with <strong>Compound Discoverer 2.0 (Thermo Fisher)</strong>. Metabolite levels were normalized to the cell number, and three biological replicates of each sample were analyzed.</p>	<p>To assess the contribution of STM metabolites to the combined metabolite concentrations, we cultured STM in RPMI medium and measured the concentrations of STM metabolites that were determined in the coculture assay. Each STM cell contained only 0.01-0.02% of the concentration of each corresponding combined metabolite measured in a single infected PM (see <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Approximately 94% of the infected PMs contained no more than 10 bacteria at 8 hpi (see <strong>Fig. S1e</strong> in the paper associated with this study), confirming that the combined metabolites were dominated by the host metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical analysis:</strong></p><p>Statistical significance was analyzed with <strong>GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, CA)</strong> using the 2-tailed unpaired Student’s t test, 1-way analysis of variance, log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test, or Mann–Whitney U test according to the test requirements (as stated in the figure legends). A P value of &lt; 0.05 was considered statistically significant.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2436
Study Title	Brown and beige adipose tissue regulate systemic metabolism through a metabolite interorgan signaling axis
Study Description	Brown and beige adipose tissue are emerging as distinct endocrine organs. These tissues are functionally associated with skeletal muscle, adipose tissue metabolism and systemic energy expenditure, suggesting an interorgan signaling network. Using metabolomics, we identify 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, 5-oxoproline, and β-hydroxyisobutyric acid as small molecule metabokines synthesized in browning adipocytes and secreted via monocarboxylate transporters. 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, 5-oxoproline and β-hydroxyisobutyric acid induce a brown adipocyte-specific phenotype in white adipocytes and mitochondrial oxidative energy metabolism in skeletal myocytes both in vitro and in vivo. 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid and 5-oxoproline signal through cAMP-PKA-p38 MAPK and β-hydroxyisobutyric acid via mTOR. In humans, plasma and adipose tissue 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, 5-oxoproline and β-hydroxyisobutyric acid concentrations correlate with markers of adipose browning and inversely associate with body mass index. These metabolites reduce adiposity, increase energy expenditure and improve glucose and insulin homeostasis in mouse models of obesity and diabetes. Our findings identify beige adipose-brown adipose-muscle physiological metabokine crosstalk.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Primary <strong>white adipose stromal vascular cells </strong>were fractionated from 6- to 10-w-old <strong>C57BL/6J male mice</strong> as previously described<strong>[1],[2]</strong>. Stromal vascular cells were then cultured and differentiated into <strong>adipocytes </strong>according to published methods<strong>[1],[3]</strong>. During the 6-d differentiation, cells were cultured with 100 nM GW0742 (Sigma Aldrich), 250 nM HIC (Sigma Aldrich), 10 μM AKV (Sigma Aldrich), 5 μM AHI (Sigma Aldrich), 20 μM MOVA (Sigma Aldrich), 20 μM 5OP (Sigma Aldrich), 20 μM BHIBA (Sigma Aldrich), and 10 μM BHIVA (Sigma Aldrich) or with 1 μM forskolin (Sigma Aldrich) on days 5–6 of differentiation. For media transfer experiments, treatment media was removed, cells were washed 3 times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and 1.1-ml serum-free media was conditioned on fully differentiated primary <strong>white adipocytes</strong> that had remained untreated (<strong>control</strong>) or had received either 100 nM GW0742 throughout differentiation or 1 μM forskolin on days 5–6 of differentiation within each well of a 12-well plate. <strong>Media </strong>was removed after 24 hrs. <strong>Media </strong>was boiled at 100 °C for 5 min for denaturation experiments. <strong>Conditioned media</strong> (1 ml) was added to naïve fully differentiated primary <strong>adipocytes </strong>in each well of a 12-well plate. For assessment of aqueous fraction bioactivity and metabolomic analysis, after 24 h conditioning on cells, <strong>serum-free media</strong> was removed from the cells and spun at 600 g for 2 min to remove debris. Aqueous soluble metabolites were extracted from 1 ml of conditioned <strong>media </strong>as previously described<strong>[4]</strong>. For aqueous fraction bioactivity assessment, dried aqueous fractions were then reconstituted in 1 ml of fresh <strong>serum-free media</strong> by vortex-mixing (5 min) followed by sonication (15 min) and then added to naïve <strong>primary adipocytes</strong> in a 12-well plate for 24 h. <strong>Mouse primary brown preadipocytes</strong> were isolated from <strong>C57BL6/J mice</strong>, cultured and differentiated according to established protocols<strong>[5]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Roberts LD, Boström P, O'Sullivan JF, Schinzel RT, Lewis GD, Dejam A, Lee YK, Palma MJ, Calhoun S, Georgiadi A, Chen MH, Ramachandran VS, Larson MG, Bouchard C, Rankinen T, Souza AL, Clish CB, Wang TJ, Estall JL, Soukas AA, Cowan CA, Spiegelman BM, Gerszten RE. β-Aminoisobutyric acid induces browning of white fat and hepatic β-oxidation and is inversely correlated with cardiometabolic risk factors. Derivatized aqueous samples were injected splitless onto a 30 m × 0.25 mm 5% phenylpolysilphenylene-siloxane column with a 0.25 μm ZB-5 ms stationary phase (Phenomenex). The injector temperature was 230 °C, and the helium carrier gas was used at a flow rate of 1.2 ml/min. The initial column temperature of 70 °C was increased by 10 °C/min to 130 °C and then increased at a rate of 5 °C/min to 230 °C followed by an increase of 20 °C/min to 310 °C and held for 5 min (transfer line temperature = 250 °C).</p>	<p>Trace DSQ II single-quadrupole mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, Cheshire, UK) was operated with an ion source temperature of 250 °C and electron ionization (EI) energy of 70 eV with positive ionisation. Each individual peak was integrated and then normalized to total metabolite concentration. Overlapping peaks were separated using traces of single ions.</p><p>Peak assignment was based on mass fragmentation patterns matched to the <strong>National Institute of Standards and Technology</strong> (<strong>NIST</strong>) library, previously reported literature and comparison to commercially available standards.</p>

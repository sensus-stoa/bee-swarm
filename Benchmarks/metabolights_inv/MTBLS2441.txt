Study Identifier	MTBLS2441
Study Title	Altered Fecal Microbiome and Correlations of the Metabolome with Plasma Metabolites in Dairy Cows with Left Displaced Abomasum
Study Description	<p>Left displaced abomasum (LDA) in postpartum dairy cows contributes to significant economic losses. LDA is characterized by excessive lipolysis and insulin resistance. Although gut dysbiosis is implicated, little is known about the role of the gut microbiota in the abnormal metabolic processes of LDA. To investigate the functional links among microbiota, metabolites, and disease phenotypes in LDA, we performed liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry of fecal samples from cows with LDA (n = 10) and healthy cows (n = 10) matched for parity and days in milk. Plasma marker profiling was synchronously analyzed. Compared with the healthy group, the LDA group showed increased alpha diversity and a shifted microbial community, accompanied by differences in the fecal metabolome and plasma amino acid pools. Based on integrated analysis with the plasma metabolome, eight genera and eight metabolites were associated with LDA. Akkermansia and Oscillospira abundances negatively correlated with 4-pyridoxic acid and tryptophan levels, and positively correlated with non-esterified fatty acid (NEFA) and β-hydroxybutyric acid (BHBA) in amino acid deficit, indicating pyridoxal metabolism-associated gut dysbiosis and lipolysis. The level of salicylic acid derived from microbiota catabolism was decreased in LDA, which negatively correlated with Prevotella, NEFA, and BHBA levels. Conversely, levels of lithocholic acid and taurolithocholic acid were increased in cows with LDA. Changes in branched-chain amino acids implicated novel host–microbial metabolic pathways involving lipolysis and insulin resistance in cows with LDA. Overall, these results suggest interplay between host and gut microbes contributing to LDA pathogenesis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Animals were treated and samples were collected in strict accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of China. All procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Sichuan Agricultural University. We selected 10 healthy postpartum Holstein dairy cows and 10 dairy cows with LDA from a dairy farm in Ziyang, Sichuan Province, China. Clinical criteria used for a determination of a healthy status and an LDA diagnosis have been described in our previous study<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p class='ql-align-justify'>The cows were housed in free-stall barns and had free access to freshwater. The cows were milked 3 times daily and provided a total mixed ration diet 3 times daily. The LDA diagnosis and sample collection were performed prior to feeding. Blood and stool samples were collected on the day of LDA diagnosis from both the LDA cows and healthy cows. The blood was separated into plasma and serum samples at each blood sampling timepoint. All samples were stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Yong K, Luo ZZ, Luo Q, Yang QW, Huang YX, Zhao XX, Zhang Y, Cao SZ. Plasma metabolome alteration in dairy cows with left displaced abomasum before and after surgical correction. J Dairy Sci. 2021 Jul;104(7):8177-8187. doi:10.3168/jds.2020-19761. Epub 2021 Apr 15. PMID:33865591.</p>	<p>Stool samples (60 mg) were added to 1000 µL of methanol:acetonitrile:water (4:4:1, v/v/v) containing an isotopically labeled internal standard mixture. After vortexing for 60 s, the samples were sonicated twice in an ice-water bath for 30 min. The samples were then incubated for 1 h at -20 °C and centrifuged at 14,000 x g for 20 min at 4 °C.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The supernatants were subject to ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC; 1290 Infinity II, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The column temperature was set to 25 °C and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. Samples were processed with the following mobile phase gradient: 0.0-0.5 min, 95% B (acetonitrile); 0.5-7.0 min, 95-65% B; 7.0-8.0 min, 65-40% B; 8.0-9.0 min, 40% B; 9.0-9.1 min, 40-95% B; 9.1-12.0 min, 95% B. Mobile phase A consisted of water with 25 mM ammonium acetate + 25 mM ammonium hydroxide.</p>	<p>Triple time-of-flight was performed on a mass spectrometer (TOF 6600, AB SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization source operated in positive- and negative-ion modes. The source temperature was 600 °C, and the ion source gas and curtain gas were 0.4137 and 0.20685 MPa, respectively. The mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) ranges of the time-of-flight MS scan and tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) ion scan were set to 60-1000 and 25-1000 Da, respectively.&nbsp;The MS/MS spectra were collected in information-dependent acquisition mode.</p>	<p>The ion peak, retention time and peak area were analyzed using <strong>XCMS</strong> software.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The detailed approach used for compound identification of the metabolites is described in our previous report<strong>[1][2]</strong>. Briefly, identification of differential metabolites was carried out by searching an in-house standard MS/MS library and the online database METLIN using MS/MS spectra or exact mass data. The in-house library contains MS/MS spectra of approximately 800 compounds, which were obtained from standards. The MS/MS spectra matching score was calculated using the dot-product algorithm and the score cutoff was set as 0.8. The MS/MS spectra that could not be matched to any of those in the in-house library were searched in online databases. Mass error was set within 25 ppm.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Yong K, Luo ZZ, Luo Q, Yang QW, Huang YX, Zhao XX, Zhang Y, Cao SZ. Plasma metabolome alteration in dairy cows with left displaced abomasum before and after surgical correction. J Dairy Sci. 2021 Jul;104(7):8177-8187. doi:10.3168/jds.2020-19761. Epub 2021 Apr 15. PMID:33865591.</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Luo ZZ, Shen LH, Jiang J, Huang YX, Bai LP, Yu SM, Yao XP, Ren ZH, Yang YX, Cao SZ. Plasma metabolite changes in dairy cows during parturition identified using untargeted metabolomics. J Dairy Sci. 2019 May;102(5):4639-4650. doi:10.3168/jds.2018-15601. Epub 2019 Mar 1. PMID:30827559.</p>

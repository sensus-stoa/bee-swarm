Study Identifier	MTBLS2497
Study Title	High-performance nano-flow liquid chromatography column combined with high- and low-collision energy data-independent acquisition enables targeted and discovery identification of modified ribonucleotides by mass spectrometry (Mixed-mode column chromatography)
Study Description	<p>Over 170 post-transcriptional RNA modifications have been described and are common in all kingdoms of life. These modifications range from methylation to complex chemical structures, with methylation being the most abundant. RNA modifications play a key role in RNA folding and function and their dysregulation in humans has been linked to several diseases such as cancer, metabolic diseases or neurological disorder. Nowadays, liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry is considered the gold standard method for the identification and quantification of these modifications due to its sensitivity and accuracy. However, the analysis of modified ribonucleosides by mass spectrometry is complex due to the presence of positional isomers. In this scenario, optimal separation of these compounds by highly sensitive liquid chromatography combined with the generation of high-information spectra is critical to unequivocally identify them, especially in high-complex mixtures. Here we present an analytical method that comprises a new type of mixed-mode nano-flow liquid chromatography column combined with high- and low-collision energy data-independent mass spectrometric acquisition for the identification and quantitation of modified ribonucleosides. The method produces content-rich spectra and combines targeted and screening capabilities thus enabling the identification of a variety of modified nucleosides in biological matrices by single-shot liquid chromatographic analysis coupled to mass spectrometry.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Mixed-mode column chromatography</strong> is reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS2497' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2497</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>Data-independent acquisition</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS2959' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2959</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Preparation of ribonucleoside standards:</strong></p><p>Ribonucleoside standards (Carbosynth, Ltd) were weighted and individually dissolved in water containing 0-50% methanol to obtain a stock solution of 2-4 mM depending on the compound. A total of 6 different mixes (final concentration 4 µM per ribonucleoside) were prepared with water from the stock solutions combining some (Mix 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5) or all (Monomix) the standard compounds to characterize the different positional isomers. A further dilution with water was made prior LC-MS analysis of the mixes to a final concentration of 800 fmol/µL per compound. A total of 1 µL of each diluted mix (800 fmol/µL) was injected in all experiments except for the calibration curves and the experiment with different loading volumes. In the latter, 1, 2 and 4 µL from diluted mixes of 500, 250 and 125 fmol/µL, respectively, were injected.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Calibration curves:</strong></p><p>Serial dilutions of the Monomix stock solution were prepared at 2000, 1000, 500, 250, 125, 62.5, 31.25, 15.63, 7.81, 3.91, 1.95, 0.98, 0.49, 0.24, 0.12 and 0.06 fmol/µL. A total of 1 µL of each dilution was injected in 3 technical replicates. Before each curve replicate, 2 blanks were injected to avoid carryover and 3 additional blanks were then injected to calculate the limit of detection.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Packing of reference C18 and mixed-mode columns:</strong></p><p>Reference C18 reverse-phase and mixed-mode columns were packed to a length of 40-45 cm using PicoFrit capillaries of 75 µm inner diameter (PF360-75-15-N-5, New Objective) following the protocol as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>. For the preparation of the Reference C18 column, a suspension of C18 reverse-phase material Synergi 4 µm Hydro RP 80 Å (04A-4375, Phenomenex) was used, whereas for the preparation of the mixed-mode column, a suspension containing 95% Synergi 4 µm Hydro RP 80 Å (04A 4375, Phenomenex) and 5% Hypercarb Carbon 3 µm 250 Å (35003-054646, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Both materials were re-suspended in a isopropanol:ethanol solution (1:1) and sonicated for 10 min before column packing.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Preparation of tRNA from <em>Saccharomyces</em>:</strong></p><p>A total of 1 μg of tRNA from <em>E. coli</em> (Merck, 10109541) was digested per triplicate with 1 μL of the Nucleoside Digestion Mix (New England BioLabs, M0649S) and the mixture was incubated at 37 ºC for 1 h. The ribonucleosides were purified with HyperSep Hypercarb SPE Spin Tips (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 10 ng were injected in triplicate.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Sarin LP, Kienast SD, Leufken J, Ross RL, Dziergowska A, Debiec K, Sochacka E, Limbach PA, Fufezan C, Drexler HCA, Leidel SA. Nano LC-MS using capillary columns enables accurate quantification of modified ribonucleosides at low femtomol levels. RNA. 2018 Oct;24(10):1403-1417. doi:10.1261/rna.065482.117. Epub 2018 Jul 16. PMID:30012570.</p>	<p>No extraction was carried out in this experiment.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using an Orbitrap Eclipse Tribrid mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to a EASY-nLC 1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for online LC-MS analysis. Liquid chromatography of the ribonucleosides was performed using either the in-house packed 40-45 cm reference C18 column or the mixed-mode column.</p><p><br></p><p>Ribonucleoside mixes were loaded directly onto the analytical column and were separated by C18 reversed phase or mixed-mode chromatography. Chromatographic gradients with the reference C18 column started at 95% buffer A and 5% buffer B with a flow rate of 300 nL/min for 5 min and gradually increased to 80% buffer A and 20% buffer B in 40 min. After each analysis, the column was washed for 10 min with 20% buffer A and 80% buffer B. The mass spectrometric acquisition was started with the sample loading. In the case of the mixed-mode columns, the chromatographic gradients started at 85% buffer A and 15% buffer B with a flow rate of 300 nL/min and gradually increased to 55% buffer A and 45% buffer B in 40 min. After each analysis, the column was washed for 10 min with 100% buffer B. The acquisition was started with the gradient: Buffer A: 20 mM ammonium acetate pH 4.5; Buffer B: 5% 20 mM ammonium acetate pH 4.5 and 95% acetonitrile.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer was operated in positive ionization mode with nanospray voltage set at 2.4 kV and source temperature at 275 °C. For Parallel Reaction Monitoring (PRM) methods the quadrupole isolation window was set to 1.4 m/z, and MS/MS scans were collected over a mass range of 75-375 m/z, with detection in the Orbitrap at resolution of 60,000. MS/MS fragmentation was performed using HCD at Normalized Collision Energy (NCE) 20 (except stated differently), the auto gain control (AGC) was set to 'Standard' and a maximum injection time of 118 ms was used. In each PRM cycle, one full MS scan at resolution of 120,000 was acquired over a mass range of 100-700 m/z with detection in the Orbitrap mass analyzer. AGC was set to 10^5 and the maximum injection time was set to 50 ms. For the Data Independent Acquisition (DIA) methods cycles of 20 consecutive windows of 8 Da for each collision energy were used to isolate and fragment all precursor ions from 220 to 380 m/z with detection in the Orbitrap at resolution of 30,000 and a scan range of 50-250 m/z. The auto gain control (AGC) was set to 4e^5 and a maximum injection time of 54 ms was used. Precursor ions were fragmented using alternatively HCD at NCE 20 and 80. After a complete cycle of both collision energies, one full MS scan at resolution of 120,000 was acquired over a mass range of 100-700 m/z with detection in the Orbitrap mass analyzer. AGC was set to ‘Standard’ and the maximum injection time was set to ‘Auto’.</p>	<p>DIA20-80 raw files were converter to <strong>MGF</strong> files with <strong>msconvert</strong> (v3.0.11626[GE1], <strong>ProteoWizard)</strong> and an <strong>OpenMS</strong> (v2.5) workflow through <strong>KNIME</strong> Analytics Platform (v4.1.2) to separate MS2 spectra of NCE 20 and 80.</p>	<p>Acquired data were analyzed with the <strong>Skyline</strong> software (v20.2.0.343)<strong>[1][2]</strong>, and extracted fragment areas, retention time and chromatographic properties (e.g., FWMH) of the ribonucleosides were used for the study. Prior analysis, <strong>msconvert</strong> (v3.0.11626, <strong>ProteoWizard</strong>)<strong>[3]</strong> and <strong>OpenMS</strong> (v2.5)<strong>[4]</strong> were used to split the MS2 spectra obtained in the DIA raw files with a collision energy of 20 and 80 NCE. The sum of all fragment ion areas per compound was used to build a multi-point linear calibration curve for each mono-methylated ribonucleoside. The calibration curves were created in <strong>Skyline</strong> using the logarithmic scale. The limit of quantification (LOQ) was calculated using a maximum of 40% coefficient of variation, whereas the limit of detection (LOD) was calculated from the fragment area of the blank samples, plus 3x standard deviation. Reference normalized retention time (nRT) were generated as previously described<strong>[5][6]</strong>. Briefly, cytidine and adenosine were arbitrarily assigned to 10 and 100, respectively, and the nRT values for each ribonucleoside (x) was determined using the following equation: nRTx = 100 − [(RTa − RTx)/(RTa −RTc)] x 90.</p><p><br></p><p>For building the reference spectral library 1 µL (800 fmol/µL) of each diluted mix (Mix 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5) was analyzed by LC-PRM with a collision energy of 80, a quadrupole isolation window of 1.4 m/z, and MS/MS scans collected over a mass range of 50-250 m/z in order to obtain the fragments of each ribonucleoside. <strong>Thermo Xcalibur</strong> (v4.4.16.14) was used to obtain molecular formula for the fragment with relative intensity greater than 10% of each ribonucleoside. Extracted fragment ions were then used to create the target list in <strong>Skyline</strong> to query the DIA acquired data (Mix 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and yeast samples).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Adams KJ, Pratt B, Bose N, Dubois LG, St John-Williams L, Perrott KM, Ky K, Kapahi P, Sharma V, MacCoss MJ, Moseley MA, Colton CA, MacLean BX, Schilling B, Thompson JW; Alzheimer’s Disease Metabolomics Consortium.

Study Identifier	MTBLS2501
Study Title	Pantothenate biosynthesis is critical for chronic infection by the neurotropic parasite Toxoplasma gondii
Study Description	Coenzyme A (CoA) is an essential molecule acting in metabolism, post-translational modification, and regulation of gene expression. While all organisms synthesize CoA, many, including humans, are unable to produce its precursor, pantothenate. Intriguingly, like most plants, fungi and bacteria, parasites of the coccidian subgroup of Apicomplexa, including the human pathogen Toxoplasma gondii, possess all the enzymes required for de novo synthesis of pantothenate. Here, the importance of CoA and pantothenate biosynthesis for the acute and chronic stages of T. gondii infection is dissected through genetic, biochemical and metabolomic approaches, revealing that CoA synthesis is essential for T. gondii tachyzoites, due to the parasite's inability to salvage CoA or intermediates of the pathway. In contrast, pantothenate synthesis is only partially active in T. gondii tachyzoites, making the parasite reliant on its uptake. However, pantothenate synthesis is crucial for the establishment of chronic infection, offering a promising target for intervention against the persistent stage of T. gondii.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Parasite strains, culture and selection</strong></p><p>RH hxgprt-ko ku80-ko parasites (RH)57, RH ku80-ko hxgprt-ko::Tir114, and ME49 hxgprt-ko ku80-ko (ME49) were grown in confluent human foreskin fibroblasts (HFF, ATCC SCRC-1041) in DMEM (Gibco 41965039) supplemented with 5% fetal calf serum and 40 μg/ml gentamycin (Gibco 15710-049). Parasites were selected for 1 w with 25 μg/ml mycophenolic acid and 50 μg/ml xanthine for HXGPRT positive selection58, 1 μM pyrimethamine for DHFR-TS positive selection59, 5 μM FUDR for UPRT negative selection60. Stable parasite populations were cloned by serial dilution.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Pantothenate depletion and media supplementations</strong></p><p>Custom-made pantothenate depleted medium was purchased from Gibco based on regular DMEM recipe (Gibco 41965039). 5% dialyzed FBS (Pan Biotech P30-2102; 10,000 Da exclusion size membrane) and 40 μg/ml gentamycin (Gibco 15710-049) were supplemented. HFFs were grown for at least one day in Pan-depleted medium prior parasite infection. Before medium change, HFFs were washed once with Pan depleted media to remove residual Pan. Regular DMEM + 5% FCS was supplemented with indicated concentrations of additional pantothenate (Enzo ALX-460- 003-G011), PanSH (Sigma 16702) or CoA (Sigma C3144) for experiments described in <strong>Fig. 3l, m </strong>in the paper associated with this study.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Culture Cell Harvest</strong></p><p><strong>T. gondii parasites</strong> were cultured in <strong>complete medium (DMEM, +5 % FCS) </strong>or in custom <strong>culture medium -Pan (DMEM -Pan, +5 % dialyzed FCS) </strong>supplemented with <strong>3 mM 13C5/15N-Val,</strong> <strong>2 mM 13C3/15N-β-Ala </strong>or 40 μM <strong>13C3/15N-Pan</strong> (all Cambridge Stable Isotope Laboratories, CNLM-442, CNLM-3946, CNLM-7964) as indicated for each experiment.</p><p>Freshly egressing parasites were harvested by scraping the infected host-cell monolayers and passing host cells through 28G needles (3x) before removing host-debris through filtration (3 μm pore size, Millipore). Parasites were counted using a Neubauer counting chamber and 108 parasites were pelleted by centrifugation (6,000 g, 10 min, 4 °C) and pellets washed with ice-cold PBS (3x) to remove residual medium. The pellets were immediately processed as described below or stored at -80 °C until metabolite extraction.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Enzyme expression and purification</strong></p><p>Insect cell protein expression: PBAL coding sequence was PCR amplified from cDNA with primers 10090/10091 and cloned KpnI/NotI into a modified pFastBac 1 insect cell expression vector containing an N-terminal Twin-STREP-His10 tag, a TEV recognition sequence followed by a KpnI restriction site. Baculovirus encoding STREP-His10-PBAL were generated following Bac-to-bac standard procedures. Sf9 insect cells were infected for 72 h with second-generation baculovirus.</p><p>Bacterial protein expression and purification: PBAL coding sequence was PCR amplified from cDNA with primers 9975/9976 and cloned by Gibson assembly into a pET-modified vector containing an N-terminal TwinSTREP-His10-maltose binding protein (MBP) tag followed by a TEV recognition sequence). Catalytic domain of PBAL (PBALcd) was selected based on sequence homology and comparison with published crystal structures (PDB: 2X3F), PCR amplified with primers 10166/10167 and cloned NsiI/KpnI in pOV1380. PANC was PCR amplified from E. coli with primers 10076/10077 and cloned NsiI/KpnI into the pET-modified vector. PBAL was expressed in Rosetta DE3 pLysS cells. From a preculture, 1.5 l of cells were grown until reaching OD600 of 0.6, maintained on ice for 1 h and induced by addition of 0.5 mM IPTG. Protein expression was done for 15 h at 20 °C in a shaking incubator. Cells were harvested by centrifugation at 3000 g for 12 min at 4 °C. The pellet was resuspended in 60 ml of lysis buffer (50 mM Tris pH 8, 150 mM NaCl, 3 mM β-mercaptoethanol), lysed by shear forces by passing the sample twice through the Microfluidizer at 15’000 psi. Soluble material was separated from membranes and cell debris by centrifugation at 35’000 g for 35 min at 4 °C. Supernatant was syringe-filtered at 5 μm and applied to a 5 ml STREP-tactin XT Superflow (iba) column. Column was washed first with 40 ml of lysis buffer supplemented with 250 mM NaCl, then with 40 ml of lysis buffer. Bound protein was eluted by applying 15 ml of lysis buffer containing 50 mM Biotin. Collected protein was concentrated and applied to a Superdex 200, 10/300 size-exclusion chromatography column equilibrated in PBS. Fractions containing MBP-PBALcd were pooled and concentrated to 500 µl using AMICON 30 MWCO. Protein concentration was estimated by measuring absorbance at 250 nm using a NanoDrop (ThermoFisher Scientific).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Donald RG, Carter D, Ullman B, Roos DS. PMID: 7777580</p>	<p><strong>GCMS - metabolite extraction, derivatization and analysis</strong></p><p>Metabolites were extracted from cell pellets through addition of 50 μl chloroform and vigorous vortexing, followed by 200 μl methanol/ultrapure water (3:1, containing 5 μM scyllo-inositol (Sigma I8132) as internal standard. Insoluble material was pelleted by centrifugation (21,000 g, 6 min, 4 °C) and the supernatant transferred to a new microtube containing 100 μl ultrapure water. The polar and apolar phases were separated through centrifugation (21,000 g, 6 min, 4 °C). The polar phase was taken off and sequentially dried (50 μl) in a glass vial insert suitable for mass spectrometry vials using a centrifugal evaporator. For GC-MS analysis, samples were methoximated by resuspending the dried extract in 20 μl pyridine (Sigma 270970) containing methoxyamine-hydrochloride (20 mg/ml; room temperature, over-night, Sigma 226904) and trimethylsilylated shortly before analysis through addition of 20 μl N,O-bis(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide with 1 % trimethylchlorosilane (BSTFA-TMCS, Cerilliant, Sigma B-023).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LCMS metabolite extraction (for reverse phase)</strong></p><p>Parasites (108 cells) were harvested as described above. Metabolites were extracted from cell pellets by adding 200 μl acetonitrile/ultrapure water (4:1) and vortexing vigorously. Insoluble material was pelleted through centrifugation (21,000 g, 6 min, 4 °C). For reversed-phase UHPLC-MS experiments the metabolite extract was sequentially dried as described above and resuspended in 50 μl ultrapure water. For HILIC HPLC-MS analyses, the metabolite extract was injected directly. For metabolite quantification experiments, 10 μM 13C3/15N-Pan was included in the extraction solvent as internal standard. In the labelling analysis, no internal standard was used.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PBAL Activity Assay</strong></p><p>PBAL enzymatic activity was assessed by incubating the purified enzymes in a PBS buffer containing final concentrations of 3 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 0.5% BSA, 10 mM MgCl2, 15 mM KCl, 100 µM ATP, with or without 5 mM β-Ala and 5 mM pantoate (pantoic acid sodium salt, Sigma 16682). Buffer&nbsp;composition was based on Miyatake et al.64. Reactions were performed in 15 µl with 500 nM MBP, 500 nM MBP-PS, or 5 µM MBP-PBALcd for 1 hour at 27 °C, or 5-fold serial dilutions (5, 1, 0.2, and 0.04 µM) of MBP and MBP-PBALcd for 30 minutes at 27 °C. The reaction was stopped by addition of 65 μl acetonitrile (final 80%) and centrifuged at 20,000 g at 4 °C to remove salts and proteins. The supernatant was transferred to an insert and vial suitable for mass spectrometry analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p><strong>GCMS Chromatography</strong></p><p>Chromatography as described earlier <strong>[1]</strong>. Samples were analyzed by GC-MS after &gt; 30&nbsp;min incubation at room temperature using a Trace GC Ultra (Thermo Fisher Scientific) GC system equipped with a Phenomenex ZB-5MS capillary column (30&nbsp;m x 0.25&nbsp;mm, 0.25&nbsp;μm, with inert guard).</p><p>2&nbsp;μL of sample were injected with a Triplus RSH autosampler (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The GC was operated in splitless mode for one minute followed by a 1/13 split. The carrier gas (helium) flow rate was held constant at 1&nbsp;mL/min. The temperature of the injector and transfer line were set to 270 °C and 320 °C, respectively. The oven temperature gradient was 70 °C (hold 1&nbsp;min), increased to 295 °C (12.5 °C/min) and raised to 320 °C (25 °C/min, hold for 2&nbsp;min).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Reverse phase Chromatography</strong></p><p>Agilent 1290 Infinity LC Series (Agilent Technologies) equipped with a Cortecs T3 column (150 x 2.1 mm x 1.6 μm) and its 5mm VanGuard cartridge (Waters).</p><p>For the QTRAP 3200 analytical platform, 10 μl of sample (8 °C) were injected and eluted with the following gradient: 0%B (1 min), 5-25% B in 7 min, 25-40% B in 1 min and hold 3 min, wash at 95% B (total runtime of 20 min). Eluent A was 50 mM ammonium acetate buffer (pH 6.9) and eluent B was acetonitrile. Flow rate and column oven temperature were respectively of 250 µl/min and 30&nbsp;°C.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PBAL Activity Assay</strong></p><p>Samples were kept at 6 °C in the autosampler, 5 μl were injected and eluted with a gradient starting at 90% B for 30s, 90-50% B in 15 min, 50-30% B in 2 min, 30-5% B in 1 min, washed for 3 min and reconditioned (total runtime of 28.5 min). Eluent&nbsp;A was 20 mM ammonium carbonate (pH 9.2) and eluent&nbsp;B was acetonitrile. Flow rate and column oven temperature were respectively of 200 µl/min and 40&nbsp;°C. Blanks made of 80% acetonitrile extraction solution were injected after every sample to prevent carry-over.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Krishnan A, Kloehn J, Lunghi M, Chiappino-Pepe A, Waldman BS, Nicolas D, Varesio E, Hehl A, Lourido S, Hatzimanikatis V, Soldati-Favre D. Epub 2020 Jan 27. PMID: 31991093</p>	<p><strong>GCMS - Mass spectrometry</strong></p><p>The GC was coupled to a PolarisQ ion trap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), operated in electron ionization (EI) mode (70 eV). The ion source was operated at 200 °C and full scan data (m/z 50-650) was acquired after a solvent delay of 7&nbsp;min. FA methyl esters (FAMEs) were identified using authentic standards and the abundance was determined based on the peak intensity relative to the total ion chromatogram signal intensity. Abundance of the palmitate-FAME mass isotopologues (m/z 270-286) was determined using XCalibur (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and OpenChrom software.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LCMS - Mass Spectrometry for Reverse phase</strong></p><p>SCIEX QTRAP 3200 (labelling experiments – <strong>Fig. 3d</strong> in the paper associated with this study) or a SCIEX QTRAP 6500 coupled to an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC Series (Agilent Technologies).&nbsp;</p><p>The mass spectrometer was operated in positive polarity with a TurboV ion source fitted with a TurboIonSpray probe (SCIEX). The electrospray voltage was set to 5500 V, the nebulizer gas and auxiliary gas (nitrogen) were set to 40 and 50 psi, respectively. The auxiliary gas heater was set to 500&nbsp;°C and the curtain plate (CUR), entrance lens (EP) and collision cell exit (CXP) potentials were of 10&nbsp;V. The declustering potential (DP) was set to 70&nbsp;V. The MRM transitions of the precursor ion (Q1) and product ion (Q3), as well as the collision energy (CE) are summarized in the <strong>table </strong>(<strong>Q1 and Q3 </strong>were operated at unit resolution - see paper associated with this study)</p><p><br></p><p>For the analyses performed with the QTRAP 6500, the following UHPLC-MS conditions were used: 8&nbsp;μl of sample (6 °C) were injected and eluted with the same gradient and column as described above. The mass spectrometer was operated with the same settings as previously mentioned except the curtain plate (CUR) and collision cell exit (CXP) potentials set to 30&nbsp;V and 8&nbsp;V respectively. The declustering potential (DP) was of 90&nbsp;V. The set of MRM transitions was the same with the addition of 2 transitions to monitor dephospho-CoA.</p><p>Blanks (ultrapure water) were injected after every sample to avoid carry-over.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PBAL Activity Assay</strong></p><p>The mass spectrometer was operated in negative polarity with a HESI II ion source. Electrospray voltage was set to -2500 V; the sheath gas, auxiliary gas and sweep gas were set respectively to 35, 10 and 1 (arbitrary units, nitrogen). The auxiliary gas heater and transfer capillary temperatures were of 200&nbsp;°C and 250°C. The looped MS experiments were a full MS experiment (m/z 70-1050) followed by 5 PRM experiments targeting: ß-Ala, Pan and pantoate. All experiments were acquired in profile mode at 35k resolution with an AGC of 1e6 and a fill time of 100ms (1&nbsp;µscan). The PRM experiments were performed at a normalized collision energy of 45 with a precursor isolation window of 0.7u. </p>	<p><strong>GC-MS</strong> files were analysed as <strong>RAW </strong>files using <strong>Xcalibur </strong>or converted to <strong>mzXML </strong>format using the <strong>RawConverter (v. 1.2.0.0)</strong> and analysed using <strong>OpenChrom</strong>.</p><p><strong>LC-MS</strong> files acquired on the <strong>QExactive </strong>instrument (PBAL activity assay) were analyzed as <strong>RAW </strong>files using <strong>Xcalibur </strong>and converted to <strong>mzML </strong>using <strong>Proteowizard (v. 3.0.12050)</strong> for upload.</p><p><strong>LC-MRM</strong> files acquired on Sciex MS instruments were processed as <strong>WIFF </strong>files in <strong>MultiQuant software</strong> (Sciex) and then converted to <strong>mzML </strong>using <strong>Proteowizard (v. 3.0.12050)</strong> for upload.</p><p><br></p><p>Relative metabolite levels and labelling were quantified using <strong>Excel </strong>(Microsoft). Metabolite levels were determined by quantifying the Q1 area under the curve relative to the internal standard. For labelling analyses, the relative levels of M0 and M4 isotopologues were determined for each metabolite.</p>	<p><strong>GCMS</strong></p><p>All metabolites were identified based on the retention time and mass spectra of authentic standards. 3-trimethylsylil-(TMS)-Pan qualifier ions were: m/z 201, 291 and 420, for 2-TMS-Val: m/z 144, 218, 246 and for 3-TMS-β-Ala: m/z 174, 248 and 290. Previously, a detailed GC-MS study of the 3-TMS-Pan derivative investigated its fragmentation during electron ionization demonstrating that the ion m/z 420 corresponds to [M-CH3]+, while ions m/z 291 and m/z 201 derive from complex rearrangements but contain the full backbone of the β-Ala fraction of Pan 65. Labelling efficiency in Val and β-Ala derivatives was determined by quantifying the ions m/z 218 and 290 and their isotopologues. The Val ion m/z 218 is composed of two distinct fragments, which contain 3 and 5-labelled atoms of 13C5/15N-Val. The M+3 ion is likely derived from loss of the side chain [M-C3H7]+, the atomic composition of the M+5 fragment is unclear. The β-Ala ion m/z 290 corresponds to [M-CH3]+. Labelling in Pan was determined by quantifying the isotopologue distribution for the ion m/z 420 (following 13C5/15N-Val labelling) and m/z 201 (following 13C3/15N-β-Ala labelling). Parental ions and their isotopologues were quantified using <strong>Xcalibur </strong>(ThermoFisher Scientific), <strong>OpenChrom </strong>and <strong>Excel </strong>(Microsoft) software following correction for natural abundance 66.Relative concentration of Pan, Val and β-Ala was determined by quantifying abundance of the parental ions m/z 201, 218 and 290, respectively, in unlabelled samples relative to the ion m/z 318 of the internal standard (scyllo-inositol).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LCMS</strong></p><p>The retention time and mass spectrum of each metabolite was determined using authentic standards.&nbsp;</p>

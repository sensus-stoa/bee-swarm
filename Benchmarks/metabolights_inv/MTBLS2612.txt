Study Identifier	MTBLS2612
Study Title	NPC1 regulates the distribution of phosphatidylinositol 4 kinases at Golgi and Lysosome membranes
Study Description	Cholesterol and phosphoinositides (PI) are two critically important lipids that are found in cellular membranes and dysregulated in many disorders. Therefore, uncovering molecular pathways connecting these essential lipids may offer new therapeutic insights. We report that loss of function of lysosomal Niemann-Pick Type C1 (NPC1) cholesterol transporter, which leads to neurodegenerative NPC disease, initiates a signaling cascade that alters the cholesterol/phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PtdIns4P) countertransport cycle between Golgi-endoplasmic reticulum (ER), as well as lysosome-ER membrane contact sites (MCS). Central to these disruptions is increased recruitment of phosphatidylinositol 4-kinases-PI4KIIα and PI4KIIIβ-which boosts PtdIns4P metabolism at Golgi and lysosomal membranes. Aberrantly increased PtdIns4P levels elevate constitutive anterograde secretion from the Golgi complex, and mTORC1 recruitment to lysosomes. NPC1 disease mutations phenocopy the transporter loss of function and can be rescued by inhibition or knockdown of either key phosphoinositide enzymes or their recruiting partners. In summary, we show that the lysosomal NPC1 cholesterol transporter tunes the molecular content of Golgi and lysosome MCS to regulate intracellular trafficking and growth signaling in health and disease.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Methods regarding sample preparation and collection have been previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, NPC1I1061T knock-in<strong>[2]</strong>, and wild-type (WT) mice from a C57BL/6 background were kindly provided by Daniel Ory (Washington University Saint Louis). Brain tissue was harvested in RIPA buffer (Thermo Scientific, Cat 89900) with Complete, Mini, EDTA-free protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche, Cat 11836170001) for 15 min at 4 °C. Samples were sonicated using a bath sonicator at 4 °C. Postnuclear supernatant was isolated by centrifuging for 20 min at 13,600 x g at 4 °C. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Vivas O, Tiscione SA, Dixon RE, Ory DS, Dickson EJ. Niemann-Pick Type C Disease Reveals a Link between Lysosomal Cholesterol and PtdIns(4,5)P2 That Regulates Neuronal Excitability. Cell Rep. 2019 May 28;27(9):2636-2648.e4. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2019.04.099. PMID:31141688.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Praggastis M, Tortelli B, Zhang J, Fujiwara H, Sidhu R, Chacko A, Chen Z, Chung C, Lieberman AP, Sikora J, Davidson C, Walkley SU, Pipalia NH, Maxfield FR, Schaffer JE, Ory DS. A murine Niemann-Pick C1 I1061T knock-in model recapitulates the pathological features of the most prevalent human disease allele. J Neurosci. 2015 May 27;35(21):8091-106. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4173-14.2015. PMID:26019327.</p>	<p>The method details are the same as previously described<strong>[1]</strong> with minor modifications. Briefly, endogenous lipids from brain samples and internal standards were extracted with n-butanol and chloroform. Extraction efficiency was accessed using 37:4 PIP standards added prior to extraction. 100 μl of n-butanol was added to each cell pellet mixture, vortexed vigorously, and set on ice (10 min). Then 15 μl of 6 N HCl was added and the mixture vortexed again and set on ice (10 min), followed by centrifugation for 2.5 min at 26,300 x g at 4 °C in a Thermofisher Sorvall microcentrifuge; 85 μl of the butanol phase (upper phase) was removed and saved. The remaining aqueous phase was extracted 4x the same way, 1x with 100 μl n-butanol and 3x with 100 μl of chloroform. The 2 butanol extracts (upper phases) and 3 chloroform extracts (lower phases) were combined, centrifuged for 2.5 min, and taken to dryness under N2 prior using a Biotage TurboVap Evaporator prior to derivatization.</p><p><br></p><p>For methyl derivatization of phosphate hydroxyls, 90 μl of methanol/CH2Cl2 4/5 v/v was added to lipid extracts prepared as above or to standards alone. After vigorous vortexing, 20 μl of 2 M TMS-DM was added and the mixture incubated at room temperature for 1 h in sealed microfuge tubes. The samples were then dried under N2.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Traynor-Kaplan A, Kruse M, Dickson EJ, Dai G, Vivas O, Yu H, Whittington D, Hille B. Fatty-acyl chain profiles of cellular phosphoinositides. Biochim Biophys Acta Mol Cell Biol Lipids. 2017 May;1862(5):513-522. doi:10.1016/j.bbalip.2017.02.002. PMID:28189644.</p>	<p>The method details are the same as previously described<strong>[1]</strong> with minor modifications. Briefly, dried samples were re-suspended in 20-100 μl 100% methanol (LC-MS Optima grade, Fisher) at room temperature prior to chromatographic separation. A Waters Acquity FTN autosampler set at 4 °C injected typically 2 μl of sample extract into the UPLC/MS. For chromatography over a C4 column, the mobile phase consisted of a 17 min runtime at a flow rate of 0.1 ml/min by a Waters Acquity UPLC (Waters Acquity UPLC Protein BEH C4, 1.7 μm 1 x 100 mm; 300 Å). The gradient was initiated with 10 mM formic acid in water/10 mM formic acid in acetonitrile (33:67 v/v), held for 1 min, then increased to 15:85, v/v in 9 min following injection, held at 85% for 2 min and then re-equilibrated to starting conditions for 3 min. For initial trials with a C8 column, the mobile phase consisted of a 15 min gradient initiated with 10 mM heptafluorobutyric anhydride (HFBA) in water/10 mM formic acid in acetonitrile (50:50 v/v) delivered at a flow rate of 0.3 ml/min by a Waters Acquity I-Class UPLC. Initial conditions were held for 1 min, then gradually increased to 100% 10 mM formic acid in acetonitrile over the next 9 min. It was held at 100% for 4 min and requilibrated for 1 min back to starting conditions. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Traynor-Kaplan A, Kruse M, Dickson EJ, Dai G, Vivas O, Yu H, Whittington D, Hille B. Fatty-acyl chain profiles of cellular phosphoinositides. Biochim Biophys Acta Mol Cell Biol Lipids. 2017 May;1862(5):513-522. doi:10.1016/j.bbalip.2017.02.002. PMID:28189644.</p>	<p>For quantitative analyses, the effluent was monitored by a Waters XEVO TQ-S MS/MS in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) using electrospray and positive ion mode.</p>	<p>Total integrated areas under peaks from sample and standard elution profiles were quantified using Waters MassLynx software. Peak areas of individual phosphoinositide species from the biological sample were normalized to synthetic standards and further corrected for tissue amount using total protein.</p>	<p>Quantitative mass spectrometry of cell phosphoinositides of many fatty-acyl chain combinations.</p>

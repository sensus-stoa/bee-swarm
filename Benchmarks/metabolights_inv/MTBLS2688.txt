Study Identifier	MTBLS2688
Study Title	Integrated Analyses of the Gut Microbiota, Intestinal Permeability and Serum Metabolome Phenotype in Rats with Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome
Study Description	The etiology of alcohol dependence is not completely understood. Increasing evidence reveals that gut microbiota dysbiosis is associated with certain psychiatric disorders, including alcoholism, through the 'microbiota-gut-brain' axis. The aims were to evaluate the effect of alcohol abuse on the gut microbiota, intestinal permeability and serum metabolic profile and to determine whether alcohol-induced alterations in gut microbiota are correlated with gut permeability and serum metabolic phenotype changes. 16S rRNA gene high-throughput sequencing and nontarget metabolomics techniques were applied in an alcohol-dependent rat model in the present study. The results showed that alcohol intake altered the composition and structure of the colonic microbiota, especially the relative abundance of the commensal microbes <i>Lachnospiraceae</i> and <i>Prevotellaceae</i>, which was significantly decreased. Alcohol-dependent rats developed gut leakiness and a serum metabolic phenotype disorder. The valine, leucine and isoleucine biosynthesis pathways and arginine and proline metabolism pathways were obviously influenced by alcohol intake. Moreover, alcohol consumption disturbed the brain's neurotransmitter homeostasis. The regression analysis showed that alcohol-induced colonic microbiota dysbiosis was strongly associated with increased intestinal permeability and serum metabolic phenotype and neurotransmitter disorders. These results revealed that gut microbiota dysbiosis and serum metabolites alteration might be a cofactor for developing of alcohol dependence. <b>IMPORTANCE</b> The gut microbiota dysbiosis is associated with certain psychiatric disorders through the 'microbiota-gut-brain' axis. Here we revealed that alcohol consumption induced the colonic microbiota dysbiosis, increased intestinal permeability, altered the serum metabolic phenotype in rats, and there were a strong correlation between gut microbiota dysbiosis and serum metabolites disorders. Thus, gut microbiota dysbiosis and serum metabolites alteration may be a cofactor for development of alcohol dependence.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Animals and animal models</strong></p><p>20 Wistar <strong>rats </strong>(6 to 8 wks old, 190 to 210 g) were purchased from Charles River Laboratories (Beijing, China; no. 11400700258815). All <strong>rats </strong>were housed in a quiet room with controlled temperature (21&nbsp;± 2 °C) and humidity (50 ± 5%) in which a 12-12 h light-dark cycle was maintained. All animal experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Xinxiang Medical University, China.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Alcohol withdrawal syndrome assessments</strong></p><p>20 rats were randomly separated into 2 groups,<strong> control group</strong> (<strong>CG</strong>, n = 10) and <strong>alcohol-dependent group </strong>(<strong>AD</strong>, n = 10). All rats were allowed to adapt to their new environment for 1 wk. Then, 6% (vol/vol) alcohol was added to the drinking water for 5 wks in the <strong>AD </strong>group; and normal water was used in the <strong>CG</strong>. The food was provided ad libitum for the duration of the experiment. On the last day of alcohol consumption, the ethanol solution was withdrawn from the <strong>AD </strong>group rats for 24 h. According to previous methods for assessing alcohol with-drawal syndrome<strong>[1]</strong>, the behavioral parameters used to assess withdrawal mainly included locomotor activity, body posture, gait, agitation, tail stiffness, tremor, and audiogenic seizures. The signs of alcohol withdrawal syndrome were observed for 5 min in every rat, and audiogenic seizures were observed for 1 min. The forced swim test and tail suspension test were also performed to evaluate anxiety levels.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample collection</strong></p><p>After 5 wks of alcohol dependence model establishment, <strong>blood </strong>was collected from the heart under isoflurane anesthesia. The <strong>serum </strong>was obtained by centrifugation at 5,000 rpm for 20 min at room temperature and immediately subjected to the next procedure for LC-MS analysis and gut permeability biomarker testing. Then, all rats were euthanized at the end of the experiment.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Erden BF, Ozdemirci S, Yildiran G, Utkan T, Gacar N, Ulak G. Dextromethorphan attenuates ethanol withdrawal syndrome in rats. Pharmacol Biochem Behav. 1999 Mar;62(3):537-41. doi:10.1016/s0091-3057(98)00175-0. PMID:10080248.</p>	<p>Serum samples were prepared as follows: a 200 μL serum sample was transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, 800 mL of pre-cooled MeOH was added, and the tube was vortexed for 60 s and centrifuged for 10 min at 12,000 x g. The supernatants were then transferred to another 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and concentrated by vacuum centrifugation. Then, the samples were dissolved with 300 μL of an aqueous MeOH solution and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane, and the prepared sample extracts were obtained for LC-MS.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Thermo Ultimate 3000 system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC high-strength silica (HSS) T3 column (150 x 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). The temperatures of the autosampler and column compartment were maintained at 4 and 40°C, respectively. The mobile phase consisted of solvents in positive ionization mode, including 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B), while in negative ionization mode, the mobile phase consisted of 5 mM ammonium formate in water (C) and acetonitrile (D). The elution gradient program for the serum samples was as follows: 2% B/D for 1 min, 2 to 50% B/D from 1 to 9.5 min, 50 to 98% B/D from 9.5 to 14min, 98% B/D from 14 to 15 min, 98 to 2% B/D from 15 to 15.5 min, and 2% B/D from 15.5 to 17 min. The injection volumes under partial loop conditions and flow rates were 2 μL and 0.25 ml/min, respectively.</p>	<p>The ESI-MSn experiments were executed on the Thermo Q Exactive Focus mass spectrometer with the spray voltage of 3.8 kV and -2.5 kV in positive and negative modes, respectively. Sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set at 45 and 15 arbitrary units, respectively. The capillary temperature was set to 325 °C. The Orbitrap analyzer scanned over a mass range of m/z 81-1,000 for full scan at a mass resolution of 70,000. Data dependent acquisition (DDA) MS/MS experiments were performed with HCD scan. The normalized collision energy was 30 eV. Dynamic exclusion was implemented to remove some unnecessary information in MS/MS spectra.</p>	<p>The<strong> .raw </strong>data were converted into .<strong>mzXML </strong>format (xcms input file format) using the <strong>Proteowizard</strong> software (<strong>v3.0.8789</strong>).</p><p><br></p>	<p>The <strong>XCMS </strong>package in <strong>R </strong>(<strong>v3.3.2</strong>) was used to perform peaks identification, peaks filtration, and peaks alignment. The main parameters are bw=5, ppm=15, peakwidth=c( 10, 20), mzwid=0.015, mzdiff=0.01, method=centWave. Data matrix was generated and included mass to charge ratio (m/z), retention time (rt) and peak area (intensity).10736 precursor molecules (precursor molecule) were obtained in the positive ion mode and 5056 precursor molecules were obtained in negative ion mode. In order to compare data of different magnitudes, batch normalization of the peak area was performed on the data.</p><p><br></p><p>The accurate mass of the metabolite experiments was &lt; 20 ppm. Fragment information obtained from the MS/MS experiments were used for metabolite identification by searching in the following databases: human metabolome database (<strong>HMDB</strong>)（<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.hmdb.ca</a>), <strong>Metlin </strong>(<a href='http://metlin.scripps.edu' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://metlin.scripps.edu</a>), massbank (<a href='http://www.massbank .jp/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.massbank .jp/</a>), <strong>LipidMaps </strong>(<a href='http://www.lipidmaps.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.lipidmaps.org</a>), <strong>mzclound </strong>(<a href='https://www.mzcloud.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org</a>). And the accurate information of metabolites was obtained by further matching annotation in the standard database built by <strong>panomic</strong>.</p>

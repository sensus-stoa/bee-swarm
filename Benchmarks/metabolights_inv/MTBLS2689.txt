Study Identifier	MTBLS2689
Study Title	Multi-Omics Approach to Elucidate Cerebrospinal Fluid Changes in Dogs with Intervertebral Disc Herniation
Study Description	Herniation of the intervertebral disc (IVDH) is the most common cause of neurological and intervertebral disc degeneration-related diseases. Since the disc starts to degenerate before it can be observed by currently available diagnostic methods, there is an urgent need for novel diagnostic approaches. To identify molecular networks and pathways which may play important roles in intervertebral disc herniation, as well as to reveal the potential features which could be useful for monitoring disease progression and prognosis, multi-omics profiling including high-resolution LC-MS-based metabolomics and proteomics was performed. Furthermore, multi-omics data were integrated to decipher a complex interaction between individual omic layers leading to improved prediction model. Together with metabolic pathways related to amino acids and lipid metabolism, and coagulation cascades, our integromics prediction model identified the key features in IVDH, namely the proteins FSTL1, SCG5, NUCB1 and CRSP2 and the metabolites N-acetyl-D-glucosamine and adenine, involved in neuropathic pain, myelination, neurotransmission and inflammatory response, respectively. Their clinical application is to be further investigated. The utilization of novel integrative interdisciplinary strategy may provide opportunities to apply the innovative diagnostic and monitoring methods for degenerative spinal disorders.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Cerebrospinal fluid was sampled under general anaesthesia from cisterna magna. Induction to general anaesthesia was obtained with propofol (Propofol, Abbott, UK), administered intravenously and maintained with sevoflurane (Sevorane, Abbott, UK) applied through an endotracheal tube. Anaesthesia was adjusted to minimally interfere cerebral blood flow and intracranial pressure. The CSF was collected into sterile Eppendorf tubes (size 1.5 mL), immediately centrifuged at 3000 x g at 4 °C for 5 min, aliquoted into Eppendorf tubes (size 1.5 mL) to avoid multiple freeze-thaw cycles and stored at -80 °C till analyzed. All samples were collected within 6 months.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Metabolomics analysis of CSF samples (<strong>IVDH </strong>N=7 and <strong>control </strong>N=6) was performed by using an untargeted approach as described. In short, pooled sample was prepared by mixing 10 µL of each sample (<strong>control </strong>and <strong>IVDH</strong>). Matrix blank sample contained extraction solvent. For metabolite extraction, a volume of 25 µL of each sample were subjected to chloroform/methanol/water (1:3:1, v/v/v) extraction on a vortex mixer for 2 h at 4 °C<strong>[1]</strong>. All samples (CSF samples, pooled sample, matrix blank) were subsequently centrifuged (13000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C). The supernatant (200 µL) was stored at -80 ˚C until LC-MS/MS analysis.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Mudaliar M, Tassi R, Thomas FC, McNeilly TN, Weidt SK, McLaughlin M, Wilson D, Burchmore R, Herzyk P, Eckersall PD, Zadoks RN. Mastitomics, the integrated omics of bovine milk in an experimental model of Streptococcus uberis mastitis: 2. Label-free relative quantitative proteomics. Mol Biosyst. 2016 Aug 16;12(9):2748-61. doi:10.1039/c6mb00290k. PMID:27412694</p>	<p>Metabolite extracts were separated on a 4.6 mm x 150 mm zwitterionic-polymeric hydrophilic interaction chromatography (ZIC-pHILIC) column (Merck SeQuant) using Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Hemel Hempstead, UK) coupled to a Thermo Orbitrap Q Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using a gradient of 80 to 95% of mobile phase A during 15 min at 25 °C and a flow rate 0.3 mL/min. Mobile phase A contained 20 mM ammonium carbonate in water and B contained 100% acetonitrile. The injection volume was 10 μL. Samples were maintained in the autosampler at 5 °C prior to injection.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer (Q Exactive Plus, Thermo Scientific) was operated with altering positive and negative modes with electrospray ionization at a resolution of 70000 and the full scan <em>m/z</em> range 70-1050. The MS settings for positive electrospray ionization were used as follows: source voltage of 3.8 kV, sheath gas 40 (arbitrary units), auxiliary gas of 5 (arbitrary units) and capillary temperature of 320 °C.</p>	<p>Raw data was converted from the ‘<strong>RAW</strong>’ file format (Thermo Scientific) to an open-source ‘<strong>mzXML</strong>’ file format, centroided and split into positive and negative polarities using <strong>MSConvert </strong>tools. The files were uploaded to <strong>PiMP </strong>tools for further analysis following standard protocol.</p>	<p>&nbsp;A standard mix containing mix of 148 authentic compounds - metabolites was used for metabolite identification. A standard mix and quality control samples were kindly donated by Dr. Richard Burchmore, Glasgow Polyomics, College of Medical, Veterinary and Life Sciences, University of Glasgow, UK. Metabolomics data were analyzed using the Polyomics integrated Metabolomics Pipeline (<strong>PiMP</strong>) available at <a href='http://polyomics.mvls.gla.ac.uk' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://polyomics.mvls.gla.ac.uk</a> using standard workflow and default parameters. Metabolite identification was performed following Metabolite Standards Initiative guidelines by matching retention times and accurate masses of detected peaks with either the authentic standards or were annotated (assigned putatively based on accurate masses) using metabolite libraries search (e.g., The Human Metabolome Database, <strong>HMDB </strong>available at<a href=' https://hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'> https://hmdb.ca/</a>, and/or Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, <strong>KEGG </strong>available at <a href='https://www.genome.jp/kegg/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.genome.jp/kegg/</a>) integrated within <strong>PiMP </strong>standard workflow. The metabolic maps available within <strong>PiMP </strong>software were derived from the <strong>KEGG </strong>database.</p>

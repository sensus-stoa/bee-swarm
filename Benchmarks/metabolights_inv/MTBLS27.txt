Study Identifier	MTBLS27
Study Title	Fatty acid synthesis in colorectal cancer: characterization of lipid metabolism in serum, tumour, and normal host tissues
Study Description	The objective of the experiment was to compare serum fatty acid metabolite abundance between individuals with varying clinical stages of colorectal cancer (stage I-IV) and age-and-gender matched disease-free controls. Fatty acid metabolite abundance was compared between locoregional (stage I-III) and liver metastatic (stage IV) cancer, as well as between all stages of colorectal cancer (stage I-IV) and disease-free controls. Reprogrammed energy metabolism is now listed as one of the central hallmarks of cancer cells. Aberrant fatty acid metabolism contributes to tumourigenesis through provision of substrates for membrane synthesis, signalling molecules, and synthesis of complex lipids. In this thesis, the role of fatty acid metabolism is explored in the context of colorectal cancer. Metabolomics techniques were employed to characterize fatty acid metabolites in serum, and lipogenic gene expression was quantified in tumour and normal tissues to investigate host response to cancer. Fatty acid metabolite abundance was increased in the serum of individuals with colorectal cancer, and a growth factor signalling axis and lipogenic transcription factor upstream of the endogenous fatty acid synthesis pathway were increased in colorectal liver metastases. It was concluded that liver metastases have an effect on growth factor production in the hepatic microenvironment, leading to increased signalling through a pathway that activates the lipogenic transcription factor that regulates fatty acid synthesis.

Study Protocol Description	Serum samples were collected after 12 hr of fasting without further restrictions, other than disease diagnosis. Some patients had been receiving various types of chemotherapy while others had not been receiving any treatment at the time of collection. Serum was collected from stage II, III, and IV colorectal cancer patients at various labs within the hospital. Disease-free samples were collected at a different site. For the serum collection, a vial of blood was collected in the morning after 12 hr of fasting. The blood was allowed to clot for approximately 30 min and then centrifuged at 2000 rpm at room temperature for 15 min. 500 µl of serum was then immediately aliquoted into freezer tubes for storage at -80 ºC until use. Typically, 1500 µl of serum was available, so three tubes per patient were stored. Treatment information for each person is not available. For the serum collection, a vial of blood was collected in the morning after 12 hr of fasting. The blood was allowed to clot for approximately 30 min and then centrifuged at 2000 rpm at room temperature for 15 min. 500 µl of serum was then immediately aliquoted into freezer tubes for storage at -80 °C until use. Typically, 1500 µl of serum was available, so three tubes per patient were stored.	Samples were prepared by extracting the aqueous and organic phase of the serum sample using a standard chloroform:methanol:water extraction procedure. </p> GC-MS derivatization:</br> Metabolite extracts were evaporated to dryness in a fume hood. For chemical derivatization, the dried organic phase was dissolved in 750 µl of chloroform:methanol (1:1) under sonication for 15 min. 100 µl of tridecanoic acid-d25 (200 µg/ml, Cambridge Isotopes Laboratories) was added as an internal standard to each sample. 125 µl of 14% boron trifluoride in methanol was added followed by incubation at 80 ºC for 90 min. Vials were left to cool for 10 min before adding 300 µl of H2O and 600 µl of hexane followed by vortexing. Aqueous layer was discarded, and organic layer was evaporated to dryness overnight. Samples were dissolved in 200 µl hexane prior to analysis by GC-MS. </p> Analytical block construction:</br> Serum samples were randomly divided into three batches with an equal number of each clinical stage (stage I/ II/ III/ IV) and males/females on each day. Age-and-gender matched disease-free controls were run in the same batch as their matching sample. Disease-free controls were matched as closely as possible with the age and gender of the patients but some exact age matches were not possible so the next closest age was used. One batch was run per 24 hr on the GC-MS.	Chromatography was carried out on a G3440A Agilent GC 7890A gas chromatograph. A DB-23 gas chromatography column (Agilent Technologies; 60 m x 0.25 mm x 0.15 µm film thickness) was used. A hexane blank and FAME 37 standard were run interspersed with the samples to monitor retention time variation.	Samples were analyzed using an Agilent GC coupled with a GCT Premier time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Waters). One microliter of each sample was injected in splitless mode and with the port temperature held at 240 ºC throughout analysis. The GC oven temperature program used was: initial hold at 50 ºC for 2 min, increase at 25 ºC/min for 7 min to 175 ºC, followed by an increase of 4 ºC/min until 250 ºC where the temperature was held for an additional two min, giving a total run time of 28 min. The time-of-flight mass spectrometer was operated in electron impact (EI) mode with a source temperature of 225 ºC and electron energy of 70 eV. Data was collected at mass range of m/z 50-800 with scan duration of 0.9 sec.	Raw data files were converted to .CDF format using Databridge. After converting raw data to netCDF format, the data were processed using MET-IDEA software (v 2.08) for peak deconvolution and chromatographic alignment. MET-IDEA parameters for peak detection and deconvolution were: average peak width, 0.15; minimum peak width, 0.2 x average peak width; maximum peak width, 3 x average peak width; peak start/stop slope, 1.5; adjusted rentention time accuracy, 0.9 x average peak width; peak overload factor, 0.9. TOF parameters were: mass accuracy, 0.01; mass range, +/- 0.4; lower mass limit, 120; ions per component, 1.	Metabolites were identified based on their match to the corresponding fatty acid methyl ester in the 37-FAME mix from FAMESTANDARD01. A library of ion/retention times was generated in AMDIS (v2.69) by first identifying the retention time for a fatty acid in the 37-FAME mix followed by the selection of a highly abundant ion specific to the fatty acid. The identities of the metabolites in the 37-FAME mix along with the ion/retention time list generated from the 37-FAME mix was used to identify the metabolites in the samples using MET-IDEA (v2.08).

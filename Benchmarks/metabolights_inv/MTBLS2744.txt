Study Identifier	MTBLS2744
Study Title	Amphotericin B resistance in Leishmania mexicana: Alterations to sterol metabolism, lipid transport and oxidative stress response
Study Description	<p>Amphotericin B is increasingly used in treatment of leishmaniasis. Here, fourteen independent lines of <em>Leishmania mexicana</em> and one <em>L. infantum</em> line were selected for resistance to either amphotericin B or the related polyene antimicrobial, nystatin. Sterol profiling revealed that, in each line, the predominant ergostane-type sterol of wild-type cells was replaced by other sterol species. Broadly, two different profiles emerged among the resistant lines. Whole genome sequencing then showed that these distinct profiles were due either to mutations in the sterol methyl transferase (C24SMT) gene locus or the sterol C5 desaturase (C5DS) gene. In three lines an additional deletion of the miltefosine transporter was found. Differences in sensitivity to amphotericin B were apparent, depending on whether cells were grown in HOMEM, supplemented with foetal bovine serum, or a serum free defined medium (DM). These differences appeared to relate to the presence of lipids in the former. Metabolomic analysis after exposure to AmB showed that a large increase in glucose flux via the pentose phosphate pathway preceded cell death in cells sustained in HOMEM but not DM, indicating the oxidative stress was more significantly induced under HOMEM conditions. Several of the lines were tested for ability to infect macrophages and replicate as amastigote forms, alongside their ability to establish infections in mice. While several lines showed reduced virulence, at least one AmB resistant line displayed heightened virulence in mice whilst retaining its resistance phenotype, emphasising the risks of resistance emerging to this critical drug.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><em>L. mexicana</em> (MNYC/BZ/62/M379) and <em>L. infantum</em> JPCM5 (MCAN/ES/98/LLM-877) promastigotes were cultured at 25 °C and 27 °C, respectively, in <strong>HOMEM</strong> (GE Healthcare and Gibco) supplemented with 10% v/v heat-inactivated foetal bovine serum (HI-FBS) (Labtech International) or in <strong>serum-free defined medium (DM)[1][2]</strong>. <strong>HOMEM</strong> and <strong>DM</strong> culture media (see <strong>Table S2</strong> in the paper associated with this study) were supplemented with 100 μg/ml streptomycin, and 100 IU/ml penicillin. Parasites were sub-cultured weekly starting at a density of 1 x 10^5 cells/ml and growth rate, cell density and morphology were determined by light microscopy with a haemocytometer<strong>[3][4]</strong>. All the 15-individual polyene-resistant lines selected <em>in vitro</em> (see <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study) alongside their respective parental wild type cultured in parallel in the absence of drug. <em>L. mexicana</em> cells were selected for resistance by stepwise increase concentration of AmB (10 lines) in <strong>HOMEM</strong> (32 wk) starting from 50 nM and rising up to 220-300 nM (see <strong>Fig S1A</strong> and <strong>C</strong> in the paper associated with this study) and in <strong>DM</strong> (50 wk) from 100 nM up to 300 nM (see <strong>Fig S1D</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Likewise, AmB (1 line) for <em>L. infantum</em> in <strong>HOMEM</strong> (15 wk) started at 50 nM up to 100 nM (see <strong>Fig S1E</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Nystatin was added to <em>L. mexicana</em> (4 lines) in <strong>HOMEM</strong> from 1.5-up to 12.5 µM (see <strong>Fig S1C</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Individual clones from each independent resistant line were obtained by limiting dilution assay (LDA). The most resistant clones (n=15) (see <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study) were selected for further analyses. Retention of resistance (as promastigotes) was confirmed after at least 10 passages without drug pressure and post-infection <em>in vivo</em> in all resistant lines (n=5) and wild type included in these experiments. Cell lines were preserved in culture medium with 15% DMSO (v/v). Axenic amastigotes were cultured at 32.5 °C with 5% CO2 in Schneider’s medium (SDM) pH 5.5, supplemented with HI-FBS (10% v/v), 1.5 ml of hemin (2.5 mg/ml in 50 mM NaOH, 0.003% v/v), 100 μg/ml streptomycin, and 100 IU/ml penicillin as previously described<strong>[5]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Mid log <em>L. mexicana</em> promastigotes (1 x 10^8) of all AmB-resistant lines alongside their parental wild-type were treated with AmB (Sigma) at 5 x their EC50 for 15 mins as follows: the amount of AmB for lines selected in <strong>HOMEM</strong> was 0.3 μM for both parental lines (<strong>LmWT1</strong> and <strong>LmWT2</strong>), 3 μM for <strong>AmBRcl.14</strong> and <strong>AmBRcl.8</strong>, and 1.74 μM for <strong>AmBRB2</strong>. Both wild type (<strong>LmWT-DM</strong>) and <strong>AmBRA3-DM</strong> cultured in <strong>DM</strong> media were treated with AmB at 1.16 μM and 3.5 μM, respectively.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Merlen T, Sereno D, Brajon N, Rostand F, Lemesre JL.

Study Identifier	MTBLS2782
Study Title	Red-Fleshed Apple Anthocyanin Extracts Attenuate Male Reproductive System Dysfunction Caused by Busulfan in Mice (Mouse blood plasma; UPLC-MS/MS assays)
Study Description	<p>In this research, we analyzed the effect of oral administration of red-fleshed apple anthocyanin extract (RAAE) on busulfan-treated mice. At first, we showed that the most abundant component in RAAE was cyanidin 3-O-galactoside. To determine the effect of RAAE, mice were divided into control and four different concentrations of RAAE feeding treatment groups (BA0, no RAAE; BA0.1, 0.1 mg/kg; BA1, 1 mg/kg; BA5, 5 mg/kg) following busulfan injection. We observed that RAAE treatments displayed ameliorative effects on male reproductive system dysfunction caused by busulfan, including recovering the irregular arrangements of seminiferous tubules, increasing the number of spermatogonia and spermatocytes, improving sperm concentration by three-fold in BA0.1 and improving sperm motility by two-fold in BA1. LC-MS/MS analysis showed significant up- or down-regulation of certain metabolites such as lysophosphatidylcholine (LysoPC), L-arginine, glycine, anandamide, and L-carnitine, which could contribute to the positive effects of RAAE, especially in PBA1 (plasma of BA1) and PBA5 (plasma of BA5). Taken together, our results indicate that 1 mg/kg of RAAE is a suitable concentration for rescuing spermatogenesis in mice. Our research suggests that RAAE could be a potential nutraceutical for protecting spermatogenesis after busulfan therapy in cancer.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>UPLC-MS/MS assays</strong> of <strong>Mouse blood plasma</strong> are reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/editor/study/MTBLS2782' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2782</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>UPLC-MS/MS assay</strong> of <strong>Apple extract</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/editor/study/MTBLS2863' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS2863</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Red-fleshed Apple Anthocyanins:</strong></p><p>The ripe red-fleshed apple (<em>Malus sieversii f. Neidzwetzkyana (Dieck) Langenf</em>) variety '<strong>XJ4</strong>' were collected from the experimental orchard of Qingdao Agricultural University (Qingdao, China). The peel and flesh of '<strong>XJ4</strong>' were ground into a powder in liquid nitrogen using mortar and pestle.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Animals and Diets:</strong></p><p>The Institute of Cancer Research (<strong>ICR</strong>) male mice (3-wk old, weighing 17-18 g) were purchased from Qingdao Institute of Drug Control (Qingdao, China). The mice were raised with 12 h day/night cycle under controlled laboratory conditions (temperature 23 °C and humidity 50-70%).</p><p><br></p><p>50 mice were randomly divided into <strong>5 groups</strong> (1 control and 4 treatment groups, 10 mice in each group), <strong>(1) Control</strong>; <strong>(2) BA0</strong> (busulfan + deionized water); <strong>(3) BA0.1</strong> (busulfan + 0.1 mg/kg Body Weight (BW)); <strong>(4) BA1</strong> (busulfan + 1 mg/kg BW); and <strong>(5) BA5</strong> (busulfan + 5 mg/kg BW). The mice were provided with chow diet and water for 3 d before administration of anthocyanin extracts. 1 d before the administration of anthocyanins extract, they were intraperitoneally injected with busulfan (40 mg/kg BW). The mice of BA0.1, BA1 and BA5 groups mice were fed with anthocyanin extracts at the indicated concentrations, while the BA0 group mice were fed with an equal amount of deionized water (100 μl). The mice were fed daily for 7 wk, and their body weights were measured at 0, 10, 20, 30, 33 and 35 d after anthocyanin extract administration. On the 35th day, the mice were humanely euthanized.</p><p><br></p><p>Mouse Blood Plasma:</p><p>The mice were under diethyl ether anesthesia, and 500 ul blood samples from each mouse was withdrawn by cardiac puncture using syringes. The blood samples were immediately centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min, and then 100 μl supernatant plasma samples were collected and immediately stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p>	<p><strong>Red-fleshed Apple Anthocyanins:</strong></p><p>The total anthocyanins were extracted with HCl-ethanol (1:999, v/v) under dark conditions at 4 °C for 15 h, and the ratio of peel and flesh tissue to extraction buffer was 1:10 (w/v). The extract was evaporated to remove HCl and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) and then concentrated to 3000 mg/l. The stock was stored at -80 °C until use.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Mouse Blood Plasma:</strong></p><p>For <strong>LC-MS/MS</strong> analysis, a total of 20 μl internal standard (10 μl of 2-chloro-l-phenylalanine in methanol, 0.3 mg/ml, and 10 μl of Lyso PC17:0, 0.01 mg/ml) was added into 100 μl plasma sample. Then, 300 μl of methanol and acetonitrile (2:1, V/V) were subsequently added into the mixture. The sample was ultrasonicated for 10 min after vortexing for 1 min. Then, the mixture was stored at -20 °C for 30 min and subsequently centrifuged at 13000 rpm (4 °C) for 10 min. Then 300 μl supernatant was collected in a brown glass vial, dried in a centrifugal freeze-drying equipment (LNG-T98, Huamei Instrument, Taicang, China), and then dissolved in 400 μl methanol and water (1:4, v/v). Samples were vortexed for 30 s, ultrasonicated for 2 min, and then centrifuged at 13000 rpm (4 °C) for 10 min. The supernatant was filtered through 0.22 μm membrane before <strong>LC-MS/MS</strong> analysis.</p>	<p>The samples were analyzed by an ACQUITY UPLC system (Nexera UPLC, Shimadzu; Tokyo, Japan) coupled with an AB Sciex Triple TOF 5600 (LC/MS) as previously reported<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, a total of 10 μl sample was injected into the ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1 mm x 100 mm, 1.7 μm particle size, Waters Corporation, Milford, USA), with a column temperature of 45 °C. The A mobile phase and B mobile phase were water/formic acid (99:1, v/v) and water/acetonitrile (99:1, v/v) respectively. The gradient used was as follows: 0-1.5 min, 5% B; 1.5-3 min, 5-30% B; 3-7 min, 30-60% B; 7-9 min, 60-90% B; 9-11 min, 90-100% B; 11-13min, 100% B; 13-13.2 min, 100-5% B; 13.2-16 min, 5% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Org E, Blum Y, Kasela S, Mehrabian M, Kuusisto J, Kangas AJ, Soininen P, Wang Z, Ala-Korpela M, Hazen SL, Laakso M, Lusis AJ. Relationships between gut microbiota, plasma metabolites, and metabolic syndrome traits in the METSIM cohort. Genome Biol. 2017 Apr 13;18(1):70. doi:10.1186/s13059-017-1194-2. PMID:28407784.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were collected on an AB Sciex Triple TOF 5600 (LC/MS). The mass range was from m/z 100 to 1,000. The resolution was set at 70,000 for the full MS scans and 35,000 for HCD MS/MS scans. The Collision energy was set at 10, 20 and 40 eV. The mass spectrometer operated as follows: spray voltage, 3,000 V (pos) and 2,500 V (neg); sheath gas flow rate, 45 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow rate, 15 arbitrary units; capillary temperature, 350 °C. The QCs were injected at regular intervals (every 10 samples) throughout the analytical run to provide a set of data from which repeatability can be assessed.</p>	<p>The resulting <strong>LC-MS/MS</strong> raw data were analyzed using <strong>Progenesis QI v2.3 </strong>software (Waters Corporation, Milford, MA, United States) following the parameters as previously described[1].</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Liu Z, Liu F, Li G, Chi X, Wang Y, Wang H, Ma L, Han K, Zhao G, Guo X, Xu B. Metabolite Support of Long-Term Storage of Sperm in the Spermatheca of Honeybee (Apis mellifera) Queens. Front Physiol. 2020 Nov 10;11:574856. doi:10.3389/fphys.2020.574856. PMID:33240099.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The metabolites were identified by <strong>Progenesis QI v2.3</strong> using both public databases (<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>http://www.hmdb.ca/</strong></a>; <a href='http://www.lipidmaps.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>http://www.lipidmaps.org/</strong></a>) and and self-built databases by <strong>Lu-Ming Biotech</strong> (Shanghai, China). The differentially expressed metabolites in comparisons of plasma of <strong>BA0</strong> (<strong>PBA0</strong>) vs <strong>Control</strong>, plasma of <strong>BA0.1</strong> (<strong>PBA0.1</strong>) vs <strong>Control</strong>, plasma of <strong>BA1</strong> (<strong>PBA1</strong>) vs <strong>Control</strong>, plasma of <strong>BA5</strong> (<strong>PBA5</strong>) vs <strong>Control</strong> were conducted. The thresholds of variable importance in projection (<strong>VIP</strong>) &gt;1 and <strong>p-value</strong> &lt; 0.05 (<strong>2-tailed Student’s t-test</strong>) were considered as significantly different metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical Analysis:</strong></p><p>The experimental data was presented as means ± SE. 2-way analysis of variance (<strong>ANOVA</strong>) and multiple comparisons were used to determine significant differences among different groups. <strong>GraphPad Prism 5.0</strong> software (San Diego, CA) and <strong>Origin 9.0</strong> software (Northampton, MA, USA) were used to process data and plot charts.</p>

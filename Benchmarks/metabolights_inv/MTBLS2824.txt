Study Identifier	MTBLS2824
Study Title	The Clinical Experiences of Urine Metabolomics of Genitourinary Urothelial Cancer in a Tertiary Hospital in Taiwan
Study Description	There are few studies which have addressed the impact of diagnostic urine metabolites and clinical outcomes associated with genitourinary urothelial (GU) cancer till this date. Furthermore, the longitudinal analysis of the dynamics of urine metabolites contributing to the detection of GU cancer has not yet been fully investigated; therefore, the discovery of novel diagnostic urine biomarkers is of our enormous interest. We explored the correlation of the urine metabolomics profiles to the GU cancers. The aqueous metabolites of the GU cancer and the control were also identified and analyzed through high-resolution1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy. Compared with the control, the urine metabolites of the tumor were studied in relation to changes over time in a linear mixed model for repeated measures. The urine metabololites of eighty-two (58 males and 24 female) patients with GU cancers were systemically analyzed. The urine metabolite profile in GU cancer was significantly higher than those in the control group (p<0.05). Seven urine metabolites including histidine, propylene glycol, valine, leucine, acetylsalicylate, glycine and isoleucine as well as other pathways were identified statistically significantly associated with GU cancer detection with longitudinal analysis. We discovered that histidine, propylene glycol, valine, leucine, acetylsalicylate, glycine, isoleucine, succinic acid, lysine2-aminobutyric acid and acetic acid are involved significantly in the all types of the male patients in whom the urine metabolites of the upper tract urothelial carcinoma(UTUC) were found statistically significance compared with the control. We did not find any statistically significance in urine biomarkers between female and male patients. However, a statistically insignificant correlation was found among the grade and stage with the metabolites.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Study Population</strong></p><p>We screened a consecutive cohort of patients with<strong> GU cancer</strong>. From March 2011 to August 2019, we collected spot urine samples of enrolled subjects with <strong>GU cancer</strong>. The pathologists reviewed hematoxylin and eosin stain slides with suspected cancer. The inclusion criteria were (1) histologically confirmed transitional cell carcinoma of <strong>GU cancer</strong>, (2) surgical resection of primary <strong>GU cancer</strong>, and (3) complete pathological, surgical, treatment, and follow-up data. The <strong>control </strong>group included normal patients, benign diseases of angiolipoma, and simple renal cysts with hematoma. The number of <strong>control </strong>patients was thirteen (eight female and 5 male) patients with an average age of sixty-seven. The sample collections were started at the time of the diagnosis. Informed consent forms were signed by the patients in a tertiary referral center, with a dedicated <strong>GU cancer </strong>interdisciplinary team screening patient enrollment. The exclusion criteria were (1) patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiation therapy, (2) tumor size &lt; 1 cm on computed tomography (CT), and (3) prior to genitourinary surgery. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital.</p><p><br></p><p>All urine samples were collected in the morning and immediately stored at -80 °C in aliquots until required. Urine samples were split into several aliquots and stored at -80 °C immediately until analyzed.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><br></p>	<p>Briefly, 900 mL of thawed urine was mixed with 100 mL of 1.5M phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) in deuterium water which contained 0.04% 3-(trimethylsilyl)-propionic-2,2,3,3-d4 acid sodium salt (TSP) as an internal chemical shift reference standard in deuterium water. The mixtures were vortexed for 20 s and centrifuged at 12,000 g at 4 °C for 30 min.</p>	<p>No data transformation information was supplied for this study. </p>	<p><strong>NMRProcFlow</strong> (<a href='https://www.nmrprocflow.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.nmrprocflow.org</a>) is an open source software that provides comprehensive tools for processing and visualising 1D NMR data. The <strong>raw </strong>1H-NMR spectra were imported into <strong>NMRProcFlow 1.2</strong> for PPM calibration, baseline correction, alignment, spectra bucketing, and data normalization.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>Metabolite identification was performed by using <strong>Chenomx NMR Suite 8.0</strong> professional software (Chenomx Inc., Edmonton AB, Canada).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical analysis</strong></p><p>The exported bucketing data of 1H-NMR spectra were uploaded to <strong>MetaboAnalyst 4.0</strong> (<a href='http://www.metaboanalyst.ca' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.metaboanalyst.ca</a>) with mean-centered, generalized log transformation and scaled by Pareto scaling. To find out the importance of metabolites between different groups, partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), independent t test, and fold change were applied. The pathway analyzed potential biomarkers, for which the p value was smaller than 0.05, to identify the association among these biomarkers. The coordinate values of the observations on the plane are called scores, and hence the</p><p>plotting of such a projected configuration is called a score plot. The variable importance in the projection (VIP) score is a measure of a variable’s importance in the PLS-DA model. The variable importance in the projection (VIP) value of each variable in the model was calculated to indicate its contribution to the classification. A higher VIP value represented a stronger contribution to discrimination among the groups. VIP values &gt; 1.2 were considered significant.</p><p><br></p><p>Data were compared by 2-sample or paired Student’s t test, analysis of variance, or chi-square test, when appropriate. The results are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and as the number (percent) for categorical variables. A P value of &lt; 0.05 was considered significant.</p>	<p>A 600 mL of the supernatant was loaded to a standard 5 mm NMR tube for further analysis.&nbsp;</p>	<p>NMR experiments were performed at Chang Gung Healthy Aging Research Center, Taiwan. 1H-NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker Avance 600 MHz spectrometer (Bruker- Biospin GmbH, Karlsruhe, Germany) equipped with a 5 mm CPTCI 1H cryoprobe. </p>	<p>For each spectrum, 64 transients were collected into 64K data points using a spectral window of 20 ppm during the relaxation time of 4 s. Prior to Fourier transformation, all 1H-NMR spectra were processed with 0-filling and exponential line-broadenings of 0.3 Hz. The acquired spectra were manually phased, baseline corrected and calibrated the&nbsp;internal TSP signal to d 0.0 ppm using <strong>T</strong><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>opSpin 3.2 software </strong>(BrukerBioSpin, Rheinstetten, Germany).&nbsp;</p>

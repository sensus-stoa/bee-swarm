Study Identifier	MTBLS2851
Study Title	CTH exacerbates Helicobacter pylori immunopathogenesis by promoting macrophage metabolic remodeling and activation
Study Description	Macrophages play a crucial role in the inflammatory response to the human stomach pathogen Helicobacter pylori, which infects half of the world's population and causes gastric cancer. Recent studies have highlighted the importance of macrophage immunometabolism in their activation state and function. We have demonstrated that the cysteine-producing enzyme, cystathionine g-lyase (CTH), is upregulated in humans and mice with H. pylori infection. Here we show that induction of CTH in macrophages by H. pylori promotes persistent inflammation. Cth-/- mice have reduced macrophage and T-cell activation in H. pylori-infected tissues, an altered metabolome, and decreased enrichment of immune-associated gene networks, culminating in decreased H. pylori-induced-gastritis. CTH is downstream of the proposed anti-inflammatory molecule, S-adenosylmethionine (SAM). While Cth-/- mice exhibit gastric SAM accumulation, WT mice treated with SAM did not display protection against H. pylori-induced inflammation. Instead, we demonstrate that Cth-deficient macrophages exhibit alterations in the proteome, decreased NF-kB activation, diminished expression of macrophage activation markers, and impaired oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis. Thus, through altering cellular respiration, CTH is a key enhancer of macrophage activation contributing to a pathogenic inflammatory response that is the universal precursor for the development of H. pylori-induced gastric disease.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cth–/– mice </strong>were generated and provided by Solomon Snyder, Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine (Yang et al., 2008). <strong>C57BL/6 wild-type (WT) and Cth–/– mice</strong> were house-bred in our animal facility. Adult age-matched <strong>WT </strong>and <strong>Cth–/– mice</strong> (8-12 wk) of both sexes were used for isolation of BMmacs and for infection with <strong>H. pylori</strong>.&nbsp;<strong>Gastric tissues </strong>were processed and untargeted metabolomics was performed as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Gobert AP, Finley JL, Latour YL, Asim M, Smith TM, Verriere TG, Barry DP, Allaman MM, Delagado AG, Rose KL, Calcutt MW, Schey KL, Sierra JC, Piazuelo MB, Mirmira RG, Wilson KT. Hypusination Orchestrates the Antimicrobial Response of Macrophages. Cell Rep. 2020 Dec 15;33(11):108510. doi: 10.1016/j.celrep.2020.108510. PMID: 33326776</p>	<p>At 8 wk p.i., gastric tissues were homogenized by sonication in water:methanol (9:1) containing 50 mM ammonium acetate (pH~6) to yield a tissue density of 50 mg/ml. An aliquot of the homogenate was combined with HPLC-grade methanol, vortexed vigorously, and centrifuged. A portion of the supernatant was diluted with an equal volume of HPLC-grade acetonitrile.&nbsp;</p>	<p>A Vanquish ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system interfaced to a Q Exactive HF quadrupole/orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to acquire discovery metabolomics data. Each sample was first injected in positive ESI mode followed by a second injection in negative mode. All chromatographic separations were performed using a Zic-cHILIC analytical column (3 um, 2.1 x 150 mm; Merck SeQuant). Mobile phases were made up of 0.2% acetic acid and 15 mM ammonium acetate in (A) H2O:CH3CN (9:1) and in (B) CH3CN:CH3OH:H2O (90:5:5). The total chromatographic run time was 20 min, the sample injection volume was 10 ml, and the flow rate was maintained at 300 ml/min.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Discovery metabolomics data were acquired using a Q Exactive HF quadrupole/orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Mass spectra were acquired over a precursor ion scan range of m/z 100 to 1,200 at a resolving power of 30,000 using the following ESI source parameters: spray voltage 5 kV (3 kV in negative mode); capillary temperature 300 °C; S-lens RF level 60 V; N2 sheath gas 40; N2 auxiliary gas 10; auxiliary gas temperature 100 °C. MS/MS spectra were acquired for the five most abundant precursor ions with an MS/MS AGC target of E5, a normalized collision energy of 30, and a maximum MS/MS injection time of 100 ms.&nbsp;</p>	<p>No data transformation was performed in this study. </p><p><br></p>	<p>Chromatographic alignment, peak picking, and statistical comparisons were performed using <strong>XCMS </strong>(<a href='https://xcmsonline.scripps.edu' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://xcmsonline.scripps.edu</a>)<strong>[1]</strong>. Samples were grouped according to 4 separate biological conditions (i.e. wild-type uninfected, wild-type H. pylori infected, CTH deleted uninfected, and CTH deleted H. pylori infected). Both ANOVA p-values and maximum fold changes were calculated. All differential features (samples vs. controls) having a P value of &lt;0.05 and a fold change of &gt;1.5 were processed for molecular matches in the <strong>Metlin </strong>database based on precursor ion exact masses (+/- 5 ppm) and MS/MS fragmentation patterns. Metabolite matches were then filtered to exclude biologically irrelevant drugs and environmental contaminants, and the finalized list of putative identifications were mapped to relevant biological pathways using <strong>Mummichog</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Tautenhahn R, Patti GJ, Rinehart D, Siuzdak G. XCMS Online: a web-based platform to process untargeted metabolomic data. Anal Chem. 2012 Jun 5;84(11):5035-9. doi: 10.1021/ac300698c. Epub 2012 May 10. PMID: 22533540</p>

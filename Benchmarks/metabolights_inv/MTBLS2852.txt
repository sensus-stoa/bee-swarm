STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS2852
Study Title	Redirected nuclear glutamate dehydrogenase supplies Tet3 with α-ketoglutarate in neurons
Study Description	<p>Tet3 is the main α-ketoglutarate (αKG)-dependent dioxygenase in neurons that converts 5-methyl-dC into 5-hydroxymethyl-dC and further on to 5-formyl- and 5-carboxy-dC. Neurons possess high levels of 5-hydroxymethyl-dC that further increase during neural activity to establish transcriptional plasticity required for learning and memory functions. How αKG, which is mainly generated in mitochondria as an intermediate of the tricarboxylic acid cycle, is made available in the nucleus has remained an unresolved question in the connection between metabolism and epigenetics. We show that in neurons the mitochondrial enzyme glutamate dehydrogenase, which converts glutamate into αKG in an NAD+-dependent manner, is redirected to the nucleus by the αKG-consumer protein Tet3, suggesting on-site production of αKG. Further, glutamate dehydrogenase has a stimulatory effect on Tet3 demethylation activity in neurons, and neuronal activation increases the levels of αKG. Overall, the glutamate dehydrogenase-Tet3 interaction might have a role in epigenetic changes during neural plasticity.</p><p><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>4-5 wk old C57BL/6J (Charles River, Sulzfeld, Germany) wild-type mice of both genders were used. Following brain removal, the hippocampi of each hemisphere were dissected and distributed to an oxygenated (95% O2, 5% CO2) physiological solution (118 mM NaCl, 3 mM KCl, 1 mM NaH2PO4, 25 mM NaHCO3, 10 mM glucose, 1.5 mM CaCl2, 1 mM MgCl2, 0.1% (v/v) DMSO) at 37 °C) until the hippocampi of all replicates were collected. Subsequently, the samples were distributed to 3 different conditions: oxygenated physiological solution (factor name: buffer), oxygenated 25 mM KCl solution (118 mM NaCl, 25 mM KCl, 1 mM NaH2PO4, 25 mM NaHCO3, 10 mM Glucose, 1.5 mM CaCl2, 1 mM MgCl2, 0.1% (v/v) DMSO) (factor name: KCl) and oxygenated 25 mM KCl solution supplemented with 20 µM inhibitor R162 or 40 µM UK5099 (Sigma-Aldrich PZ0160-5MG) (factor name: inhibitor). After 6 h incubation time, hippocampi were transferred into reaction tubes, snap frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until use.</p>	<p>Pre-weighed frozen hippocampal tissues from 6 mice (n = 6) were homogenized using a bullet blender and 5 x 1.4 mm stainless steel beads in a mixture of 1.05 ml ice-cold methanol/chloroform 1:2 (v/v). Following sonication of supernatants for 1 min, an additional 0.7 ml mixture of chloroform/LC grade water, 1:1 (v/v) was added, the samples were vortexed for 20 s and incubated on wet ice for 30 min. Extracts were then centrifuged for 5 min at 16000 x g and 4 °C and 0.6 ml from the upper aqueous phase were collected and dried under nitrogen gas.</p>	<p>The dried extracts were dissolved in 0.25 ml phosphate buffer (0.15 M, pH 7.4) in deuterated water containing 0.04 mM sodium trimethylsilylpropionate-d4 (TSP) as internal standard. 0.18 ml of each solution was transferred to 3 mm NMR tubes.</p>	<p>NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker Avance II NMR spectrometer using a 5 mm TCI ATMA cryoprobe at a proton frequency of 600.1 MHz and ambient temperature of 300 Kelvin.</p>	<p>A 1D NOESY pre-saturation pulse sequence was used to analyze the hippocampus extracts. For each sample 2048 transients were collected into 64k data points using a spectral width of 12335.526 kHz (20 ppm) and an acquisition time of 2.65 s per FID.</p>	<p>Assignments were made usng the ChenomX database and were confirmed by 2D spectroscopy including homonuclear 1H-1H Total Correlation Spectroscopy (TOCSY) and 1H-13C Heteronuclear Signal Quantum Coherence (HSQC) spectroscopy.</p>	<p>Spectra were manually phased and baseline corrected in TopSpin 3.0 (Bruker Biospin). The actual concentration of TSP was calculated using the PULCON method and a pre-calibrated ERETIC sample with the same experimental parameters. Then all spectra were imported in ChenomX NMR Suite 8.4 (ChenomX Inc. Canada) for metabolite quantification based on the know concentration of TSP.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2871
Study Title	Microbial Diversity and Non-volatile Metabolites Profile of Low-Temperature Sausage Stored at Room Temperature
Study Description	Sausage is a highly perishable food with unique spoilage characteristics primarily because of its specific means of production. The quality of sausage during storage is determined by its microbial and metabolite changes. This study developed a preservative-free low-temperature sausage model and coated it with natural casing. We characterized the microbiota and non-volatile metabolites in the sausage after storage at 20°C for up to 12 days. <i>Bacillus velezensis</i> was the most prevalent species observed after 4 days. Lipids and lipid-like molecules, organoheterocyclic compounds, and organic acids and their derivatives were the primary non-volatile metabolites. The key non-volatile compounds were mainly involved in protein catabolism and β-lipid oxidation. These findings provide useful information for the optimization of sausage storage conditions.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The low-temperature sausages were supplied by a local Chinese meat factory of Fuxin, Liaoning province, China, in accordance with Chinese food safety standards, as described recently with some modifications<strong>[1]</strong>. A total of 5 independent batches were collected. The formulation included pork belly (95%, the ratio of fat to skin is about 3:1), salt (2.5%), starch (2%), sugar (0.4% [wt/wt]) and ice water (0.5-1.5%). Meats were minced using a grinder with a 8 mm plate, and mixed with other supplementary materials. Then, the mixture were stuffed into 30-35 mm diameter porcine natural casings, 10-12 cm long. Thereafter, the raw sausages were steamed at a temperature of 70-80 °C for approximately 30 min until the internal temperature reached 74 °C<strong>[2]</strong>. When the end-products decreased to the room temperature, then were quickly transferred to the laboratory within 2 h by using portable constant temperature incubator (BX-10B, Kai Hang Instrument Co., Ltd, Changzhou, China). The sausages were then packed under aseptic laboratory conditions into permeable polyethylene bags (200 x 300 mm) and stored in a constant temperature humidity chamber (LHS-50CL, Shanghai Yiheng Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) at 20 °C and a humidity of 50% (± 5). The sausages used for physicochemical, metabolites, and microbiological analysis were harvested at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 days during storage.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Jin SK, Yang HS, Choi JS. Effect of Gleditsia sinensis Lam. Extract on Physico-Chemical Properties of Emulsion-Type Pork Sausages. Korean J Food Sci Anim Resour. 2017;37(2):274-287. doi: 10.5851/kosfa.2017.37.2.274. Epub 2017 Apr 30. PMID: 28515651.</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Walton J, McCarthy M. New method for determining internal temperature of cooking meat via NMR spectroscopy. Journal of Food Process Engineering. 2007;22:319-330. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-4530.1999.tb00488.x</p>	<p>The metabolites extraction were determined as described recently with some modifications<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, an aliquot of 100 mg of sample were individually ground with liquid nitrogen and homogenized in 80% methanol and 0.1% formic acid, following which the mixtures were stored in an ice bath for 5 min. The samples were then centrifuged at 15,000 rpm at 4 °C for 5 min. A part of the supernatant was diluted with LC-MS grade water to a final concentration containing 60% methanol. The samples were subsequently transferred into an Eppendorf tube with a 0.22 μm filter and were centrifuged at 15,000 x g at 4 °C for 10 min. Finally, the filtrate was collected and stored for analysis. Equal metabolite extracts from each sausage sample were used for quality control (QC).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Wen D, Liu Y, Yu Q. Metabolomic approach to measuring quality of chilled chicken meat during storage. Poult Sci. 2020 May;99(5):2543-2554. doi: 10.1016/j.psj.2019.11.070. Epub 2020 Mar 11. PMID: 32359590.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher, Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Metabolite extracts were separated on Hyperil Gold column (C18, 100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm particle, 4 °C, 0.2 mL/min). The eluents of positive polarity mode consisted of eluent A (0.1% formic acid) and eluent B (Methanol). The eluents of negative polarity mode consisted of eluent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9.0) and eluent B (Methanol). The solvent gradient was initial 2% B (1.5 min) then 2% to 100% B (12 min), 100% B (14 min), 100% to 2% B (14.1 min) and 2% B (16 min).</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted on an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer operating in positive and negative polarity modes by data-dependent MS/MS (dd-MS2) scans. The MS acquisition parameters of instrument were set as follows: spray voltage of 3.2 kV; capillary temperature of 320 °C; sheath gas flow rate of 35 arb; aux gas flow rate of 10 arb; 100-1500 m/z mass range; full MS resolution of 60,000 (FWHM); dd-MS2 resolution of 15,000 (FWHM); AGC target for full MS was 3e^6 with the 100 ms maximum injection time; AGC target for dd-MS2 was 2e^3 with the 25 ms maximum injection time.</p>	<p>Raw data were processed using <strong>Compound Discoverer</strong> (Version 3.0, Thermo Fisher, Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).</p>	<p>Data preprocessing included peak alignment, peak picking and quantitation. The parameters were applied as follows: 0.2 min retention time tolerance; 5 ppm actual mass tolerance; 30% signal intensity tolerance; 3 signal/noise ratio; and 100,000 minimum intensity. After that, peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity. Then, the molecular formula was predicted according to the additive ions, molecular ion peaks and fragment ions. After that, the peaks were matched with the <strong>mzCloud </strong>(<a href='https://www.mzcloud.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org/</a>) and <strong>ChemSpider</strong> (<a href='http://www.chemspider.com/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.chemspider.com/</a>) to obtained the accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS2914
Study Title	High-Throughput Detection of an Alkaloidal Plant Metabolome in Plant Extracts Using LC-ESI-QTOF-MS.
Study Description	Plant alkaloids represent a diverse group of nitrogen-containing natural products. These compounds are considered valuable in drug discovery and development. High-throughput identification of such plant secondary metabolites in complex plant extracts is essential for drug discovery, lead optimization, and understanding the biological pathway. The present study aims to rapidly identify different classes of alkaloids in plant extracts through the liquid chromatography with electrospray ionization-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS) approach using 161 isolated and purified alkaloids. These are biologically important unique alkaloids belonging to different sub-classes such as isoquinoline, quinoline, indole, tropane, pyridine, piperidine, quinolizidine, aporphine, steroidal, and terpenoid. The majority of these are not available commercially and are known to manifest valuable biological activities. Four pools of a maximum of 50 phytostandards each were prepared, based on their log <i>P</i> value to minimize co-elution for rapid and cost-effective analyses. MS/MS spectra were acquired in the positive ionization mode by using their [M + H]<sup>+</sup> and/or [M + Na]<sup>+</sup> with both the average collisional energy (25.5-62 eV) and individual collisional energies (10, 20, 30, and 40 eV). Accurate mass, high-resolution mass spectrometry (HR-MS) data, MS/MS data, and retention times were curated for each compound. The developed LC-MS/MS method was successfully used to interrogate and fast dereplicate alkaloids in 13 medicinal plant extracts and a herbal formulation. A total of 56 alkaloids were identified based on the reference standard retention times (RTs), HR-MS spectra, and/or MS/MS spectra. The MS data have been submitted to the MetaboLights online database (MTBLS2914). The mass spectrometric and chromatographic data will be useful for the discovery of new congeners and the study of biological pathways of alkaloids in the plant kingdom.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Authentic alkaloid standards (purity, ≥ 98) of the plant source and 13 plant extracts were obtained from the Molecular Bank of the Dr. Panjwani Center for Molecular Medicine and Drug Research, International Center for Chemical and Biological Sciences, University of Karachi, and a herbal formulation, that is, <strong>Johar Joshanda [Qarshi Industries (Pvt) Ltd.]</strong>, was purchased from the local market.</p>	<p>Stock solutions of all the reference standard alkaloids and internal standards (ISs) were prepared mainly in methanol or, in some cases, in chloroform with a final concentration of 1 mg/ml and were stored below 0 °C. The standards were grouped into 4 pools based on their log P value and exact masses. Each pool contained a mixture of 22−50 chemical standards with different lipophilicities along with 5 internal standards (ISs) chosen based on their polarity/retention time. These pools were prepared by mixing equal volumes (10 μl) of standard stock solutions and ISs and further diluting them 10x for each pool. The resultant standard solution pools were filtered through a Millipore syringe filter (0.22 μm) before being injected into the LC−MS instrument. Log P values of all the compounds were calculated through <strong>ACD Lab software (version 2081.1)</strong>.</p>	<p>The analysis was performed using liquid chromatography in combination with an electrospray ionization maXis II quadrupole time-of-flight (QTOF) mass spectrometer (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen Germany). Chromatographic separation was carried out using an UltiMate 3000 rapid separation liquid chromatography system RSLC (Dionex) equipped with a reversed-phase column (RP-C18) (Nucleodur C18 Gravity, 100 x 2.0 mm, 1.8 μm, Macherey-Nagel) and a guard column (2.0 x 4 mm, 1.8 μm). The injection volume was 5 μl. The mobile phases were as follows: eluent A, H2O (0.1% formic acid) and eluent B, MeOH (0.1% formic acid). The flow rate was 0.5 ml/min and a gradient elution program was maintained. The chromatographic procedure was initialized at 5% B, raised to 95% B in 9 min, followed by holding for 1 min, then returned to initial conditions for equilibration until 13 min.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry data was collected&nbsp;using a Bruker Daltonics maXis II quadrupole time-of-flight (QTOF) mass spectrometer. Electrospray ionization (ESI) and collision-induced dissociation mass spectral data of alkaloid standards and samples were recorded in the positive ionization mode at room temperature to ensure that the maximum number of compounds can be ionized and detected. The high-purity nitrogen gas was used as a collision and a nebulizer gas. The ESI mass spectral parameters are mentioned as follows: the capillary voltage at 4500 V, endplate offset at 500 V, nebulizer gas 2.8 bar, drying gas flow rate 10.0 L/min, drying gas temperature at 300 °C, and scan speed set as 5 Hz for MS and 12 Hz spectra rate for MS/MS analysis. The time-of-flight (TOF) scan range of m/z was set at 50-1200 amu. MS/MS analysis of alkaloid standards was carried out in two ways as follows: <strong>(i)</strong> autoMS/MS mode and <strong>(ii)</strong> targeted MS/MS mode using a scheduled precursor list (SPL). In the auto-MS/MS method, collision energy was ramped between 25.5 and 62 eV, an active exclusion number of 3 was set as the optimum number and an intensity threshold in the 1000 count range was set to trigger the acquisition of precursor ions for fragmentation; however, in the targeted method, predetermined collision energies (i.e., 10, 20, 30, and 40 eV) were used.</p>	<p>The calibration solution of sodium formate (10 mM) was injected at 3 μL/min for mass-scale calibration, before each run using a syringe pump.</p>	<p>The targeted screening of the acquired data was performed making use of the <strong>TargetAnalysis software version 1.3 (Bruker Daltonik)</strong> that reads a search list [comma separated value list (<strong>CSV file</strong>)], framed for each pool of alkaloids separately, containing retention time, m/z, name of compounds, and chemical formula along with the information of their possible sodium adducts to extract high-resolution traces and retention times. For MS/MS data, this targeted processed data file is further processed using <strong>Compass DataAnalysis 4.4 software (Bruker Daltonik)</strong> to extract MS/MS data of each compound. The obtained MS and MS/MS spectra of all the standard alkaloids were compiled via <strong>LibraryEditor 4.4 (Bruker)</strong> into a mass spectral library.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS3075
Study Title	Dynamic metabolic and transcriptomic profiling reveals the biosynthetic characteristics of hydroxycinnamic acid amides (HCAAs) in sunflower pollen
Study Description	<p>Sunflower pollen is a natural nutritious food with a long history and multiple functions, however, the main chemical components apart from flavonoids and their biosynthesis processes have not been thoroughly investigated. In this study, seven hydroxycinnamic acid amides (HCAAs) (1-7) abundant in sunflower pollen were isolated and identified as one type of the pollen's main chemicals. For a comprehensive understanding of HCAA biosynthesis in <em>Helianthus annuus</em> flowers, RNA-seq, metabolomics, and key genes related to biosynthesis in the sunflower were studied. A large number of compounds at different sunflower growth stages (the 7th, 14th, 21st and 28th days) and high expression levels of related genes in the transcriptome were detected. A molecular network was constructed to clarify the synthetic pathway of HCAAs, which revealed high transcriptional levels of spermidine hydroxycinnamoyl transferase genes (<em>HaSHT2795</em> and <em>HaSHT2436</em>) in 14-21 day-old flowers. <em>HaSHT2795</em> enzymes catalyze tri-coumaroylspermidine formation, and virus-induced gene silencing to inhibit <em>HaSHT2795</em> and <em>HaSHT2436</em> could significantly reduce the synthesis of hydroxycinnamic acid amides in sunflower pollen. HCAAs were inferred to be related to the formation of pollen walls and the health effects of pollen. Analyzing HCAA biosynthesis and accumulation in <em>H. annuus</em> pollen will be helpful to understand the functions of HCAAs in the development of pollen and its nutritional value.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The bee pollen of <strong><em>Helianthus annuus L.</em></strong> (confectionery sunflower SH363) was purchased from Kashgar, Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, China, and was authenticated by Professor Mian Zhang of the Research Department of Pharmacognosy, China Pharmaceutical University. A voucher specimen (No. 2014-XRK) was deposited in the Department of Natural Medicinal Chemistry, China Pharmaceutical University. Fresh sunflower pollen (varieties: KT929) was collected from unopened tubular flowers cultivated in a medicinal botanical garden of China Pharmaceutical University (Nanjing, Jiangsu Province, China) in a room with no sunlight. Organs (collected at 4 timepoints during flower growth; days 0-7 [Line 1], 7-14 [Line 2], 14-21 [Line 3] and 21-28 [Line 4], with 3 biological replicates for transcription analyses and 6 biological replicates for metabolomics analyses) were frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for RNA isolation.</p>	<p>For metabolic testing, 50 mg samples (collected at 4 timepoints during flower growth; days 0-7 [Line 1], 7-14 [Line 2], 14-21 [Line 3] and 21-28 [Line 4], with 6 biological replicates for each sample) were extracted with the addition of 1000 μL of extract solvent (acetonitrile:methanol:water, 2:2:1), containing the internal standard 2-chloro-L-phenylanlanine. After 30 s of vortex mixing, ultrasonic extraction was performed in ice water for 5 min, and the sample was left to rest for 1 h at -20 °C. After centrifugation at 12,000 x g and 4 °C for 15 min, the resulting supernatants were transferred to LC-MS vials and stored at -80 °C until the UHPLC-QE Orbitrap MS analysis. A quality control sample (QC) was prepared by mixing the same amount of experimental sample extract and was used to analyze the repeatability of samples under the same processing method. In the process of instrumental analysis, a quality control sample was tested into every 6-10 test analysis samples to monitor the repeatability of the analysis process.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (1290, Agilent Technologies) with a UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 mm x 100 mm, 1.8 μm) coupled to Q Exactive (Orbitrap MS, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water as a positive control and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate in water as a negative control, and the mobile phase B was acetonitrile. The elution gradient: 0.0 min, 1% B; 1.0 min, 1% B; 8.0 min, 99% B; 10.0 min, 99% B; 10.1 min, 1% B; 12.0 min, 1% B. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min. The injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>The QE mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on an <strong>information-dependent basis</strong> (<strong>IDA</strong>) during an LC-MS experiment. In this mode, the acquisition software (<strong>Xcalibur</strong> 4.0.27, Thermo) continuously evaluates the full scan survey MS data as it collects and triggers the acquisition of MS/MS spectra depending on preselected criteria.</p><p><br></p><p>ESI source conditions were set as follow: sheath gas flow rate, 45 arb; auxiliary gas flow rate, 15 arb; capillary temperature, 320 °C; and spray voltage, 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.1 kV (negative). Two acquisition modes were adopted. The QC, Ha and blank control samples were analyzed in the full MS mode with an m/z range of 70-1000, a resolution of 70 000 full width at half maximum (FWHM). The parameters for MS/MS were as follows: resolution, 17,500 (FWHM); collision energy, 20/40/60 eV in an NCE model; acquisition rate, 7 spectra/s; MS scan power MSn, for n = 1-10 synchronous precursor selections and 10 precursors per MS2 scan. Different m/z range (70-200, 190-400, 390-600 and 590-1000 m/z) were set for the QCs to maximize MS2 spectra.</p>	<p>MS raw data (<strong>.raw</strong>) files were converted to the <strong>.mzXML</strong> format using <strong>ProteoWizard</strong>, and processed by <strong>R</strong> package <strong>XCMS</strong> (version 3.2), including retention time alignment, peak detection and peak matching<strong>[1]</strong>. Then the data were filtered by the followed criterion: sample numbers contain a metabolite was less than 50% all sample numbers in a group (QC were also taken as a group). Normalization to an internal standard<strong>[2]</strong> for each sample was done subsequently. Next, missing values were replaced by the half of the minimum value found in the dataset by default<strong>[3]</strong>. The preprocessing results generated a data matrix that consisted of the retention time (RT), mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) values and peak intensity.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Smith CA, Want EJ, O'Maille G, Abagyan R, Siuzdak G. XCMS: processing mass spectrometry data for metabolite profiling using nonlinear peak alignment, matching, and identification. Anal Chem. 2006 Feb 1;78(3):779-87. doi:10.1021/ac051437y. PMID:16448051.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Roberts LD, Souza AL, Gerszten RE, Clish CB. Targeted metabolomics. Curr Protoc Mol Biol. 2012 Apr;Chapter 30:Unit 30.2.1-24. doi:10.1002/0471142727.mb3002s98. PMID:22470063.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Xia J, Psychogios N, Young N, Wishart DS. MetaboAnalyst: a web server for metabolomic data analysis and interpretation. Nucleic Acids Res. 2009 Jul;37(Web Server issue):W652-60. doi:10.1093/nar/gkp356. Epub 2009 May 8. PMID:19429898.</p>	<p><strong>Data annotation</strong></p><p>Identification of small molecules in metabolomics studies, <strong>OSISMMS</strong> (version 1.0. Dalian chem data solution information technology Co. Ltd) was used for peak annotation after data processing with in-house MS/MS database. Metabolite annotation was performed by applying the search modules (5 ppm mass tolerance) of <strong>mzCloud</strong> (<a href='https://www.mzcloud.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org</a>) and <strong>ChemSpider </strong>(<a href='http://www.chemspider.com' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.chemspider.com</a>), in <strong>Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes</strong> (<strong>KEGG; </strong><a href='https://www.genome.jp/kegg' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.genome.jp/kegg</a>).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical analysis</strong></p><p>The metabolite profile data were normalized and exported to <strong>SIMCA-P</strong> V12.0.0 Demo (Umetric, Umea, Sweden) for principal components analysis (PCA), partial least-squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) and orthogonal projection to latent structures-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), which were applied for all samples using <strong>R</strong> package models (<a href='https://www.r-project.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.r-project.org</a>). A variable importance in projection (VIP) score of the (O)PLS model was applied to rank the metabolites that were best distinguished between two groups. The VIP threshold was set to 1. In addition, a t-test was used for univariate analysis to screen differential metabolites. Those with a t-test p-value＜0.05 and VIP ≥ 1 were considered differential metabolites between two groups.</p>

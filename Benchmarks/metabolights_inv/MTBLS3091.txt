Study Identifier	MTBLS3091
Study Title	Metabolome of pig endometrium and placenta.
Study Description	<p>Neonatal mortality is mostly due to a reduced piglet maturity. An essential question for adaptation to extra-uterine environment lies in the optimization of the crosstalk between maternal and fetal genomes for a distribution of resources that allows fetuses to express healthier phenotypes at birth. The CO-LOcATION project proposes an integrative biology approach combining transcriptomics, metabolomics, lipidomics and maturity phenotypes to answer this crucial question for both research and pig production sustainability. The project will use samples from the former ANR PORCINET project, which produced pure genotypes and reciprocal crossbreed genotypes with contrasted robustness at birth. The complexity of the maturity phenotypes will be addressed by studying the trade-off between growth and survival with a focus on endometrial-placental interactions using whole-genome approaches.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>COLOcATION</strong> project has taken advantage of the unique collection of placenta and endometrium samples already collected in the <strong>PORCINET</strong> project (ANR-09-GENM-005-01, 2010-2014). This genetic protocol producing pure and reciprocal crossed fetuses by insemination with mixed semen, using 2 extreme breeds for piglet mortality at birth: Large White and Meishan at 90 and 110 days of gestation (dg) (period of fetal maturity acquisition for pigs). The detailed experimental design was previously described<strong>[1][2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Pregnant sows were anesthetized and the fetuses were extracted by cesarean for physiological phenotyping. The placenta of each fetus was identified by the fetal number. The pregnant sows and fetuses were finally euthanized for tissue sampling and the uterus was extracted from the carcass. For each fetus a pre-determined area of the 2 adjacent tissues (placenta/endometrium) was dissected.</p><p><br></p><p>The 2 tissues were dissociated with dissection forceps from each determined area and immediately snapfrozen into liquid nitrogen. Then the tissues were placed in a zip lock bag labelled with animal number and tissue name and stored temporarily on dry ice during the experiment. Between each tissue and between each animal, dissection forceps and scalpels were washed which 70% ethanol. The tissues are then stored at -80 °C in the laboratory. Before extraction, the tissues were disrupted, homogenized and ground to a fine powder with the mixer-mill MM400 by rapid agitation for 1 min at 30 Hz in a liquid-nitrogen cooled container with stainless steel beads. Fine powder samples were conserved at -80 °C until extraction.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Voillet V, SanCristobal M, Lippi Y, Martin PG, Iannuccelli N, Lascor C, Vignoles F, Billon Y, Canario L, Liaubet L. Muscle transcriptomic investigation of late fetal development identifies candidate genes for piglet maturity. BMC Genomics. 2014 Sep 17;15(1):797. doi:10.1186/1471-2164-15-797. PMID:25226791</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Lefort G., Servien R., Quesnel H., Billon Y., Canario L., Iannuccelli N., Canlet C., Paris A., Vialaneix N., Liaubet L. The maturity in fetal pigs using a multi-fluid metabolomic approach. Scientific Reports. 2020 Nov 16;10(1):19912. doi:10.1038/s41598-020-76709-8 . PMID:33199811</p>	<p>Briefly, 100 mg of tissue were collected in a 2 ml Fast-Prep tube containing lysing matrix S (MP Biomedicals, Irvine,CA, USA) over liquid nitrogen to maintain frozen samples. 335 µl of cold methanol was then added to each tube (to quench metabolism and prevent lipids oxidation during storage) and the tubes were stored at -80 °C before performing the homogenization step. On the day of the extraction step, tissue samples stored in methanol at 80 °C were placed on ice. Next, 170 µl of cold MilliQ water were added to each tube. Then the tubes were placed in the adapters set (2 x 24, stored at -20 °C before use) of a Mixer-Mill MM400 (Retsch, Haan, Germany) and 2 homogeneization cycles of 5 min at 30 Hz were performed. The adapters were stored for 10 min at -20 °C and the samples were placed on ice between the 2 homogeneization cycles. After homogeneization, 950 µl of cold methanol and 800 µl of cold MilliQ water were added to each tube containing the homogenate, and the tubes were vortexed for 10 s. 1.25 ml of cold dichloromethane was added and the tubes were vortexed for 10 s once again. The samples were then centrifuged for 15 min at 2870 x g at 4 °C. Finally, 2 ml of the aqueous phase (superior phase) were transferred to a 5 ml polypropylene tube and stored at -20 °C until the evaporation step.</p>	<p>The aqueous extract obtained for each sample was evaporated to dryness using a vacuum concentrator (Concentrator plus, Eppendorf, Germany) After the evaporation step, the dried aqueous extract was dissolved in 450 µl of phosphate buffer prepared in deuterated water (0.2 M, pH 7.0), containing TMSP as internal standard (8 mg TMSP for 100 ml). 5 ml PP tube was vortexed 5 min and its content was transferred to a 1.5 ml microtube and centrifuged at 2870 x g for 15 min at 4 °C. Finally, 200 μl were transferred to a 3 mm NMR tube using TECAN liquid handler robot.</p><p><br></p>	<p>All 1H NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker Avance DRX-600 spectrometer (Bruker SA, Wissembourg, France) operating at 600.13 MHz for 1H resonance frequency at 300 K.</p>	<p>The 1H signal was acquired using the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) spin-echo pulse sequence, by accumulating 256 transients over a spectral width of 20 ppm (note: chemical shift units kept ppm), collecting 64K data points.</p>	<p>The PEPS NMR package embedded in the <strong>R package ASICS[1] (version 2.6.1)</strong> was used to perform spectra pre-processing with default parameters. The region corresponding to water resonance (5.1-4.5 ppm) was excluded to eliminate artifacts of residual water.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lefort G, Liaubet L, Canlet C, Tardivel P, Père MC, Quesnel H, Paris A, Iannuccelli N, Vialaneix N, Servien R. ASICS: an R package for a whole analysis workflow of 1D 1H NMR spectra. Bioinformatics. 2019 Nov 1;35(21):4356-4363. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btz248. PMID:30977816.</p>	<p>Spectra were normalized by the area under the curve and aligned with the preprocessing functions available in the <strong>Bioconductor R package ASICS (version 2.16.0)[1]</strong>. Then, the metabolites in each sample were identified and quantified using the <strong>ASICS</strong> function available in the same package. The quantification was performed using the default reference library provided in the package and was processed jointly (joint alignment, independant <strong>FWER</strong> quantification, as is the default in the package<strong>[2]</strong>), independently for each tissue. As to other parameters of the function, default values were used, except for <strong>noise.thres</strong>, which was set to 0.01. Finally, metabolites were kept only if they had at least 80% of non-null values in at least 1 condition (stages of gestation x genotypes x sex x maturity). The other ones were removed from the list of identified metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lefort G, Liaubet L, Canlet C, Tardivel P, Père MC, Quesnel H, Paris A, Iannuccelli N, Vialaneix N, Servien R. ASICS: an R package for a whole analysis workflow of 1D 1H NMR spectra. Bioinformatics. 2019 Nov 1;35(21):4356-4363. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btz248. PMID:30977816.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Lefort G, Liaubet L, Marty-Gasset N, Canlet C, Vialaneix N, Servien R. Joint Automatic Metabolite Identification and Quantification of a Set of 1H NMR Spectra. Anal Chem. 2021 Feb 9;93(5):2861-2870. doi:10.1021/acs.analchem.0c04232. Epub 2021 Jan 26. PMID:33497193.</p>

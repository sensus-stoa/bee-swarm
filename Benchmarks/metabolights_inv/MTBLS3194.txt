Study Title	Study on the Hepatoprotection of Schisandra chinensis Caulis Ploysaccharides in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease in rats based on metabolomics
Study Description	<p>The aim of this study was to investigate the hepatoprotection of <em>Schisandra chinensis</em> Caulis polysaccharides (SCP) in the nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) induced by high-fat diet (HFD) in rats. A total of 30 Wistar rats were randomly divided into control group (CON), model group (MOD) and <em>Schisandra chinensis</em> caulis polysaccharide (SCP) group. Except for those in CON group, the other rats were fed with high-fat diet for 4 weeks to establish a NAFLD model. From the 5th week, rats in SCP group were given SCP solution (100 mg/kg) by gavage for 6 weeks, and those in CON and MOD groups were given an equal volume of distilled water in the same way. The serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels were detected. The small molecular metabolites in the blood of rats were determined by the metabolomics method of ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole/electrostatic field orbitrap high-resolution mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q-Orbitrap-MS/MS) combined with multivariate analysis. The enrichment analysis and pathway analysis of the different metabolites were carried out. The therapeutic mechanism of SCP in NAFLD rats was verified by Western blot. The results showed that the levels of AST, ALT, TG, TC and LDL-C in the serum of rats in SCP group were significantly lower, and the levels of HDL-C were significantly higher than those in MOD group. The screening and analysis of the metabolic pathways showed that SCP could alleviate the development of NAFLD by regulating the expression of UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (UGP2), UDP-glucose 6-dehydrogenase (UGDH), acetyl CoA carboxylase (ACC) and fatty acid synthase (FAS) in the blood of NAFLD rats. This study may provide a theoretical basis for the development and utilization of SCP.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Male Wistar rats, weighing 250–300 g, were provided by Changchun Yisi Experimental Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Changchun, China), and the certificate number was SCXK (Ji) 2019-0007. The rats were reared in separate cages in a sterile feeding room at a temperature of 18–23 °C and in a humidity of 40–60%. The standard feed and the high-fat feed for experimental rats were provided by Changchun Yisi Experimental Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Changchun, China). The high-fat diet contained lard (15%), sucrose (20%), cholesterol (1.2%), sodium cholate (0.2%), casein (10%), calcium hydrogen phosphate (0.6%) and basic diet (53%). Animal experiments were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Beihua University (Jilin, China), and all the experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.</p><p><br></p><p>A total of 30 rats were randomly divided into control group (<strong>CON</strong>), model group (<strong>MOD</strong>) and <em>Schisandra chinensis</em> caulis polysaccharide (<strong>SCP</strong>) group. Except for those in CON group, all rats were fed with the high-fat diet for 4 weeks. Then, rats in SCP group were given 100 mg/kg SCP solution for 6 weeks by gavage from the 5th week, and those in CON and MOD groups were given an equal volume of distilled water in the same way. Rats in all groups were given standard diet from 5th week to 10th week. Blood samples of all the rats were collected from the abdominal aorta of anesthetized rats by the intraperitoneal injection of 25% urethane (100 mg/kg) on the 11th week. The blood samples were left standing at room temperature for 1 h, then centrifuged at 3000 rpm to separate the serum, and the serum samples were stored at -20 °C for standby.</p>	<p>A total of 100&nbsp;µL of the serum&nbsp;were added with&nbsp;400 µL methanol. The serum-methanol solution was&nbsp;mixed by a vortex&nbsp;oscillator for 20 s,&nbsp;and then&nbsp;centrifuged&nbsp;at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 5 min&nbsp;to obtain&nbsp;the supernatant.&nbsp;The supernatant was evaporated&nbsp;to dryness with nitrogen, and its volume fixed&nbsp;to 1 mL&nbsp;with methanol&nbsp;for&nbsp;use.</p>	<p>A Ultimate 3000 ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (UHPLC, Thermo, San Jose, CA, USA) was used for the separation, in which the chromatographic column was Supelco C18 column (3.0 × 50 mm, 2.7 μm; Sigma-Aldrich, USA), the column temperature was 35 °C, acetonitrile and water with 0.1% formic acid were used as mobile phases A and B, respectively. The gradient elution procedures were as follows: 65% B (0–5 min), 65–45% B (5–10 min), 45–15% B (10–20 min), 15–10% B (20–25 min) and 10–65% B (25–30 min), the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min and the injection volume was 10 μL.</p>	<p>A Q-Orbitrap-MS/MS (Thermo, San Jose, CA, USA) was used for the determination by mass spectrometry, in which the positive ion mode and negative ion mode were set. The electrospray ionization (ESI) source conditions were as follows: the sheath gas flow was 35 arb, the auxiliary gas flow rate was 10 arb, the sweep gas flow was 1 arb, the S-Lens RF was 50%, the capillary voltage was +4.0 kV, the capillary temperature was 340 °C, and the mass scanning range was m/z&nbsp;100–1500 Da.</p>	<p>LC-MS data were extracted, filtered and normalized using Thermo software <strong>Xcalibur</strong> (version 4.3) to obtain the molecular weight, retention time and absorption peak area of the compounds in each sample.</p>	<p><strong>SIMCA</strong> 14.1 software (Umetrics, kinelon, NJ) was used for the multivariate data analysis, and potential marker compounds with importance of variables (VIP) &gt; 1 were screened by using principal component analysis (PCA), orthogonal partial least square discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) and s-plot score analysis. The molecular weight, retention time and MS/MS fragmentation ion characteristics of the different compounds were compared by using <strong>Xcalibur</strong> (version 4.3), and the structures were identified and elucidated in <strong>HMDB</strong> and <strong>KEGG</strong> databases. Finally, the selected endogenous compounds were input into the <strong>MetaboAnalyst</strong> software to identify potential metabolic pathways related to the different compounds.</p>

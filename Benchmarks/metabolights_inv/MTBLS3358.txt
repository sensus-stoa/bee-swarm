Study Identifier	MTBLS3358
Study Title	Metabolomics Analysis of the Effect of GAT-2 Deficiency on Th1 Cells in Mice
Study Description	<p>The classic neurotransmitter gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) has been shown to shape the activation and function of immune cells. There are four high affinity GABA transporters (GATs, including GAT-1, GAT-2, GAT-3 and GAT-4) responsible for the transmembrane transport of GABA in mice. To explore the effect of GAT-2 on type 1 helper T (Th1) cells, naïve CD4+ T cells were isolated from splenocytes of GAT-2 knockout (KO) and wild-type (WT) mice and cultured for Th1 cell differentiation, and then metabolomics analysis of Th1 cells was performed via gas chromatography coupled to time-of-flight mass spectrometry (GC-TOF-MS) added with multivariate analyses. The study will provide insights into T cell response to GAT-2 deficiency in mice.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Animals and Ethics Statement</strong></p><p><strong>WT C57BL/6J mice</strong> were obtained from the Institute of Comparative Medicine at the Yangzhou University (Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province, China), and <strong>GAT-2 KO mice </strong>(on a C57BL/6J genetic background) were obtained from Prof. Wenkai Ren (Guangdong Provincial Key Laboratory of Animal Nutrition Control, Institute of Subtropical Animal Nutrition and Feed, College of Animal Science, South China Agricultural University, Guangzhou, China).6 Mice were between 8 and 12 weeks of age with the body weight of 20 ± 2 g. All the animals were housed in a controlled environment with temperature of 22 ± 1 °C, relative humidity of 50 ± 1% and a light/dark cycle of 12/12 h. All experiments complied with institutional animal care guidelines and were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Yangzhou University [approval ID: SYXK (Su) 2005-0005].7</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample Preparation and Collection</strong></p><p><strong>WT </strong>and <strong>KO </strong>mice were euthanized by an intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital [50 mg/kg body weight]. Next, spleens were collected, gently ground, and resulting <strong>cells </strong>were prepared as a single <strong>cell </strong>suspension. The red blood cell lysis buﬀer was added following the isolation of cells. Next, the cells were washed twice with sterile phosphate buﬀered saline (PBS) and spun at 300 g for 10 min. Then naive CD4+ T cells were isolated from mouse splenocytes using a CD4+ CD62L+ T cell isolation kit II (Miltenyi Biotec, purity &gt; 95%). For Th1 differentiation, cells were stimulated with plate-bound anti-CD3ε (5 μg/ml, 100313; Biolegend) and soluble anti-CD28 (2 μg/ml, 102111; Biolegend) antibodies supplemented with 20 ng/ml IL-2 (212-12-20; Peprotech), 20 ng/ml IL-12 (210-12-10; Peprotech) and 10 μg/ml anti-IL-4 (504122; Biolegend) for 5 days.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The cells were collected into the 2 mL EP tubes, extracted with 1 mL extraction liquid (Methanol:Chloroform, 3:1, (v:v)), and added 5 μl of L-2-Chlorophenylalanine (1 mg/mL stock in distilled H2O) as internal standard; Homogenized in ball mill for 4 min at 45 Hz, then ultrasound treated for 5 min (incubated in ice water); Centrifuged for 15 min at 12,000 rpm, 4 °C; Transferred the supernatant 850 μL into a fresh 1.5 ml EP tubes. QC samples were prepared by taking 60 μL from each sample. Samples were dried completely in a vacuum concentrator without heating. For derivatisation, 20 μL of methoxyamination hydrochloride (20 mg/ml in pyridine) were added and incubated for 30 min at 80 °C. Then 30 μL of the BSTFA regent (1% TMCS, v/v) were added to the sample aliquots and incubated for 1.5 h at 70 °C.</p>	<p>GC-TOF-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph system coupled with a Pegasus HT time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The system utilized a DB-5MS capillary column coated with 5% diphenyl cross-linked with 95% dimethylpolysiloxane (30m×250μm inner diameter, 0.25μm film thickness; J&amp;W Scientific, Folsom, CA, USA). 1 μL aliquot of the analyte was injected in splitless mode. Helium was used as the carrier gas, the front inlet purge flow was 3mL/min, and the gas flow rate through the column was 1mL/min. The initial temperature was kept at 50 °C for 1 min, then raised to 310 °C at a rate of 10°C/min, then kept for 8 min at 310 °C. </p>	<p>GC-TOF-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph system coupled with a Pegasus HT time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The injection, transfer line, and ion source temperatures were 280, 280, and 250 °C, respectively. The energy was -70 eV in electron impact mode. The mass spectrometry data were acquired in full-scan mode with the m/z range of 50-500 at a rate of 12.5 spectra per second after a solvent delay of 6.33 min.</p>	<p><strong>Chroma TOF 4.3X</strong> software of<strong> LECO Corporation</strong> and <strong>LECO-Fiehn Rtx5 database</strong> were used for raw peaks exacting, the data baselines filtering and calibration of the baseline, peak alignment, deconvolution analysis, peak identification and integration of the peak area. Both of mass spectrum match and retention index match were considered in metabolites identification.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Compound identification of metabolites by GC/TOF-MS spectra with an <strong>in-house database</strong> established with available authentic standards. Both of mass spectrum match and retention index match were considered in metabolites identification.</p><p><br></p><p>The output data exported from <strong>Chroma TOF 4.3X </strong>software were imported into <strong>SIMCA-P </strong>software (version 11.0, Umetrics, Umeå, Sweden) for multivariate statistical analyses including a principal component analysis (PCA) and pairwise orthogonal projections to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA).</p>

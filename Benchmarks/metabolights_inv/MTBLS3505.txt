Study Identifier	MTBLS3505
Study Title	UPLC-Q-TOF/MS-Based Plasma and Urine Metabolomics Contribute to the Diagnosis of Sepsis
Study Description	In this study, we aimed to identify potential metabolic biomarkers that can improve the diagnostic accuracy of sepsis. Sixty-six patients including 30 septic and 36 nonsepsis patients from an intensive care unit were recruited. The global plasma and urine metabolomic profiles were determined by ultraperformance liquid chromatography coupled with a quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry-based methodology. The risk factors, including both traditional physiological indicators and metabolic biomarkers, were investigated by binary logistic regression analysis and used to build a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (Lasso) regression model to evaluate the ability of diagnosis. Fifty-five metabolites in plasma and 11 metabolites in urine were identified through orthogonal projections to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). Among them, ten (PE (20:4(5Z, 8Z, 11Z, 14Z)/P-18:0), harderoporphyrinogen, chloropanaxydiol, (<i>Z</i>)-2-octenal, <i>N</i><sup>1</sup>,<i>N</i><sup>8</sup>-diacetylspermidine, 1-nitroheptane, venoterpine, α-CEHC, LysoPE (20:0/0:0), corticrocin) metabolites were identified as risk factors. The Lasso regression model incorporating these ten metabolic biomarkers and five traditional physiological indicators displayed better differentiation than the traditional model, represented by the elevated area under receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) from 96.80 to 100.0%. Furthermore, patients with septic shock presented a significantly lower level of PE-Cer (d16:1(4E)/19:0). This study suggests that metabolomic profiling could be an effective tool for sepsis diagnosis.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Patient Enrollment:</strong></p><p>In general, patients with age more than 18 yr and hospitalized between June 2017 and January 2018 in the intensive care units (ICUs) at the first affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-sen University were recruited, including 30 septic patients and 36 nonseptic patients. These sepsis patients were diagnosed by the attending medical team according to the sepsis-3<strong>[1]</strong> definitions. The exclusion criteria included the following: age younger than 18 years, mental disease, acquired immune deficiency syndrome, anuria, no complete electronic records, and refusal to enter the study. According to the occurrence of shock, the patients with sepsis were divided into 2 subgroups: <strong>septic shock group (SS)</strong> and <strong>nonseptic shock group (NSS)</strong>. The <strong>SS group</strong> contained <strong>21 patients</strong>, and <strong>NSS</strong> contained <strong>9 patients</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sampling and Processing:</strong></p><p>In the morning within 24 h of ICU admission, the blood and urine samples were acquired. And plasma was obtained after 10 min centrifugation at 4000 rpm. The urine was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min in order to collect the supernatant. The obtained plasma and urine samples were kept at -80 °C until processing.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Singer M, Deutschman CS, Seymour CW, Shankar-Hari M, Annane D, Bauer M, Bellomo R, Bernard GR, Chiche JD, Coopersmith CM, Hotchkiss RS, Levy MM, Marshall JC, Martin GS, Opal SM, Rubenfeld GD, van der Poll T, Vincent JL, Angus DC. The Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3). JAMA. 2016 Feb 23;315(8):801-10. doi:10.1001/jama.2016.0287. PMID:26903338.</p>	<p>The sample processing was similar to our previous method<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, for lipidomics analysis of plasma, the fat-soluble metabolites in 50 μl plasma were extracted by methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE)/methanol/H2O (20:6:5 v/v/v) twice. Then, the collected upper organic solvent was dried by a nitrogen blow-dryer and stored in a -80 °C refrigerator. The residue was dissolved in 80 μl of isopropanol/methanol (1:1 v/v) before it subjected to UPLC−Q-TOF/MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>For plasma and urine metabolomics analyses, the water-soluble metabolites in 50 μl of plasma or urine were obtained by protein precipitation with 150 μl of methanol. Then, the collected upper organic solvent was dried by the nitrogen blow-dryer and stored in the −80 °C refrigerator. The residue was dissolved in 50 μl of water prior to UPLC−Q-TOF/MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>To monitor the repeatability during the analysis, quality control samples (QCs) were made by mixing 10 μl of every sample and assayed prior to sample analysis and between every 8 samples.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Liu K, Jia B, Zhou L, Xing L, Wu L, Li Y, Lu J, Zhang L, Guan S. Ultraperformance Liquid Chromatography Coupled with Quadrupole Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry-Based Metabolomics and Lipidomics Identify Biomarkers for Efficacy Evaluation of Mesalazine in a Dextran Sulfate Sodium-Induced Ulcerative Colitis Mouse Model. J Proteome Res. 2021 Feb 5;20(2):1371-1381. doi:10.1021/acs.jproteome.0c00757. PMID:33356298.</p>	<p>Samples were separated by an HSS T3 column (2.1 mm x 100 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters ACQUITY) in an Agilent Technologies 1290 II Infinity LC system. The system was coupled with an Agilent 6545 Q-TOF mass spectrometer.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>UPLC gradient for lipidomic analysis:</strong></p><p>Flow 0.4 (ml/min)</p><p>0-0.5, 70% A, 30% B; 0.5-2, 50% A, 50% B; 2-15, 35% A, 65% B; 15-19, 0% A, 100% B; 19-22, 0% A, 100% B; 22-22.5, 70% A, 30% B; 22.5-25, 70% A, 30% B.</p><p>Mobile phase A: ACN/H2O (6:4 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate.</p><p>Mobile phase B: 2-propanol/ acetonitrile (9:1 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid and 10 mM ammonium acetate.</p><p>The column temperature was 55 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>UPLC gradient for metabolomic analysis:</strong></p><p>Flow 0.45 (ml/min)</p><p>0-0.5, 70% A, 30% B; 0.5-2, 70% A, 30% B; 2-8, 15% A, 85% B; 8-11, 1% A, 99% B; 11-13, 99.9% A, 0.1% B; 13-15, 99.9% A, 0.1% B.</p><p>Mobile phase A: 100% H2O with 0.1% formic acid.</p><p>Mobile phase B: ACN/2-propanol (7.5:2.5 v/v) with 0.1% formic acid.</p><p>The column temperature was 45 °C.</p>	<p>Data was collected using a with an Agilent 6545 Q-TOF mass spectrometer. The ion source of mass was AJS ESI, and samples were operated in both ESI(−) and ESI(+) modes. The capillary voltage, the fragmentor voltage, the skimmer voltage, and the nozzle voltage were 3500, 140, 65, and 1500 V, respectively. The source temperature was set at 350 °C. The sheath gas flow and drying gas were 12 and 6 L/min, respectively. The nebulizer pressure was 45 psi, and the mass extended dynamic range was 50−1700 m/z.</p>	<p>The molecular feature was extracted by Agilent Masshunter ProFinder (B.08.00) from the original data obtained from UPLC−Q-TOF/MS. In the process of molecular feature extraction, the software completed noise filtering, retention time alignment, peak type recognition, peak area calculation, and other normalized processing according to the default parameters.</p>	<p>The potential biomarkers were identified in the <strong>KEGG database</strong> (<a href='https://www.kegg.jp' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.kegg.jp</a>) and <strong>HMDB database</strong> (<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.hmdb.ca</a>) with the information about the retention time, accurate molecular mass, and mass spectrum. The pathway and enrichment analysis were operated in <strong>MetaboAnalyst 4.0</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical Analysis:</strong></p><p>The standardized data were further processed by <strong>SIMCA-P 14.1</strong> (Umetrics AB, Sweden) using <strong>orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA)</strong>. The <strong>OPLS-DA</strong> model was generated and cross-validated. The X variable of the <strong>OPLS-DA</strong> model was the MS data and the Y variable was the class information identifier. The model was evaluated by the <strong>interpretation rate to the matrix (R2)</strong> and the <strong>predictive ability (Q2)</strong>. To screen potential biomarkers, <strong>variable importance for projection (VIP)</strong> plots were calculated based on the <strong>OPLS-DA</strong> model. For each peak repeatedly detected in all samples, the peak area between the 2 groups was compared by <strong>Student’s t-test</strong>, and <strong>p &lt; 0.05</strong> was considered statistically significant. Between different groups, metabolites with <strong>VIP &gt; 1, p &lt; 0.05</strong>, and <strong>fold change (FC) ≥1.5 (or ≤0.67)</strong> were considered as potential biomarkers. <strong>FC</strong> represents the content ratio between the <strong>septic group</strong> and the <strong>control group</strong>, which can quickly investigate the content changes of each metabolite in different groups. <strong>FC larger than 1.50 or less than 0.67</strong> is accepted in many studies<strong>[1][2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>SPSS 19.0</strong> (IBM, U.K.) was used for the following analysis. Either the unpaired <strong>Student's t-test</strong> or <strong>Fischer’s exact test</strong> was employed to compare the demographic data whenever appropriate, and <strong>p &lt; 0.05</strong> was considered statistically significant. The risk factors of sepsis were investigated by binary logistic regression analysis. The correlation between metabolites and physiological variables was analyzed by Pearson's or Spearman's method according to the data’s normality status. The least absolute shrinkage and <strong>selection operator (Lasso) regression</strong> was conducted for each type of risk factor and for the combined risk factors by the <strong>R Programming Language (New Zealand)</strong>. These <strong>Lasso regression</strong> models were used to generate the <strong>receiver operating characteristic (ROC)</strong> curves. To validate the <strong>Lasso regression</strong> model of combined risk factors, the <strong>Lasso regression</strong> model, which comprised two-thirds of all samples, was used as a training set and the rest of the samples formed a test set. Every sample was randomly selected to train or test set, and the model was verified 3 times.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Aye SM, Galani I, Han ML, Karaiskos I, Creek DJ, Zhu Y, Lin YW, Velkov T, Giamarellou H, Li J. Lipid A profiling and metabolomics analysis of paired polymyxin-susceptible and -resistant MDR Klebsiella pneumoniae clinical isolates from the same patients before and after colistin treatment. J Antimicrob Chemother. 2020 Oct 1;75(10):2852-2863. doi:10.1093/jac/dkaa245. PMID:32696049.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Lana A, Alexander K, Castagna A, D'Alessandro A, Morandini F, Pizzolo F, Zorzi F, Mulatero P, Zolla L, Olivieri O. Urinary Metabolic Signature of Primary Aldosteronism: Gender and Subtype-Specific Alterations. Proteomics Clin Appl. 2019 Jul;13(4):e1800049. doi:10.1002/prca.201800049. PMID:30580498.</p>

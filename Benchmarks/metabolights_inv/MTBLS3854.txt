Study Identifier	MTBLS3854
Study Title	Long-term low-dose ionizing radiation induced chromosome-aberration-specific metabolic phenotype changes in radiation workers
Study Description	<p>Long-term low-dose ionizing radiation (LLIR) widely exists in human life and has been confirmed to have potential pathogenic effects on cancer and cardiovascular diseases. However, it is technically and ethically unfeasible to explore LLIR-induced phenotypic changes in the human cohort, leading to slow progress in revealing the pathogenesis of LLIR. In this work, we recruited 32 radiation workers and 18 healthy non-radiation workers from the same city with the same eating habits for radiation damage evaluation and metabolomics profiling. It was found that clear metabolic phenotypic differences existed between LLIR and non-LLIR exposed participants. Moreover, LLIR exposed workers can be further divided into 2 types of metabolic phenotypes, corresponding to high and low damage types, respectively. 3-hydroxypropanoate and glycolaldehyde were identified as sensitive indicators to radiation damage, which specific response to the chromosomal aberration of workers and may be potential monitoring markers for LLIR protection. Taurine metabolism-related pathways were identified as the main differential metabolic pathway under LLIR inducing, which had been confirmed to have a response to acute or chronic radiation exposure. We expect our study can be helpful to LLIR damage monitoring and symptomatic intervention in the future.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study included plasma samples collected from 32 <strong>radiation workers</strong> (<strong>LLIR</strong>) and 18 healthy <strong>non-radiation workers</strong> (<strong>Control</strong>) who were prospectively recruited from General Hospital of Tisco (Shanxi, China) from May 2019 to May 2020. The plasma sample was obtained by centrifuging whole blood samples (approximately 3&nbsp;mL) collected from a heparin sodium anticoagulant tube within 30&nbsp;min. Blood cell samples from radiation workers were further used to detect radiation damage related indicators, including chromosome aberration, chromatid aberration and lymphocyte micronucleus, in the central laboratory. Plasma samples from all participants were repackaged in 120&nbsp;μL volumes, then immediately quenched with liquid nitrogen and transferred to -80 °C refrigerator for storage until metabolomics experiments usage. A day before sample collecting, all participants were put on a diet. The usage of these samples was approved by the Ethics Committee of General Hospital of Tisco.</p><p><br></p><p>The metabolomics experiment was implemented on Agilent 1290-TripleTOF 6600+ platform (Shanghai Omicsolution Co., Ltd) based on the standard operating procedure made by Omicsolution.</p>	<p>Plasma samples were thawed under an ice bath, and 100&nbsp;μL of each sample was transferred into a new tube. Then, 200&nbsp;μL of methyl alcohol and 200&nbsp;μL of acetonitrile were added to it for protein precipitation. After vortexing 30&nbsp;s and sonicating 10&nbsp;min, proteins were prone to precipitate by being incubated 1&nbsp;h at 20&nbsp;°C. Subsequently, the samples were centrifuged at 13,000&nbsp;rpm for 15&nbsp;min at 4&nbsp;°C, and the supernatant was collected and evaporated to dryness in a vacuum concentrator. The dry extracts were then resuspended in 100&nbsp;μL of acetonitrile:water solution (1:1, v/v) and sonicated for 10&nbsp;min. Finally, the mixture was centrifuged at 13,000&nbsp;rpm for 15&nbsp;min at 4&nbsp;°C, and the supernatant was collected for LC-MS based metabolomic detection.</p>	<p>The LC-MS based metabolomic analysis was performed on TripleTOF 6600+ mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, USA) coupled with an Agilent 1290 liquid chromatography system (Agilent, USA). For metabolites separation, the ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (100&nbsp;mm x&nbsp;2.1&nbsp;mm, i.d. 1.7&nbsp;µm) was used. A total of 5&nbsp;μL prepared sample was injected and separated using 0.5&nbsp;mL/min flow rate. Chromatographic mobile phase A was water solution with 25&nbsp;mM ammonium acetate and 25&nbsp;mM ammonium hydroxide, and mobile phase B was pure acetonitrile. The chromatographic gradient is shown below: 0.0&nbsp;min, 95% B; 0.5&nbsp;min, 95% B; 7.0&nbsp;min, 65% B; 8.0&nbsp;min, 40% B; 9.0&nbsp;min, 40% B; 9.1&nbsp;min, 95% B; 12.0&nbsp;min, 95% B.</p>	<p>The electrospray ionization mass spectra were acquired in positive ion mode and negative ion mode, respectively. <strong>Information dependent acquisition</strong> (<strong>IDA</strong>) was used to collect full scan MS and MS/MS information, simultaneously. Mass data were collected between m/z of 60-1200&nbsp;Da. The ion spray voltage was set to 5000&nbsp;V for positive mode and -4000&nbsp;V for negative mode, and the heated-capillary temperature was maintained at 600&nbsp;°C. The curtain gas flow, nebulizer and heater gas were set to 35, 60 and 60 arbitrary units, respectively. The collision energy was set to 10&nbsp;eV at full scan mode, and 30&nbsp;eV at information dependent acquisition mode. The declustering potential was set to 60&nbsp;V. Collision gas was selected as high-purity argon (≥ 99.999%). MS/MS resolution ratio ≥&nbsp;25,000.</p>	<p><strong>ProteoWizard</strong> 3.0.6150 was used to covert raw MS data files to <strong>mzXML</strong> format and MS2 data files to <strong>mgf</strong> format. All <strong>mzXML</strong> format MS files were processed using <strong>R</strong> package <strong>XCMS</strong> 1.46.0 for peak detection and alignment.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was achieved by searching the database of <strong>MetDNA</strong> (<a href='http://metdna.zhulab.cn' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://metdna.zhulab.cn</a>) with the MS1 peak table and MS2 data files.</p><p><br></p><p>In order to select differential metabolites, the MS1 peak table was uploaded to <strong>MetaboAnalyst</strong> (<a href='http://www.metaboanalyst.ca' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.metaboanalyst.ca</a>) to perform differential metabolite discovery. Multivariable analysis, including principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discrimination analysis (PLS-DA), as well as metabolic pathway analysis, were all performed by <strong>MetaboAnalyst</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>MS1 peak intensity value was normalized by sum and scaled by mean-centering. Student’s t-test and chi-square test were used to respectively evaluate the significance of differences in continuous and discrete variables. p&nbsp;&lt;&nbsp;0.05 were considered to have statistically significant. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the effectiveness of constructed classifiers, using the area under the ROC curve (AUC) as the evaluation criterion. PLS-DA was performed for differential metabolites screening, with the threshold of VIP score of 0.9. Spearman correlation analysis was used to confirm the correlation among variables, with 0.35 as the threshold of the correlation coefficient.</p>

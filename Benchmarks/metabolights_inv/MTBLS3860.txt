Study Identifier	MTBLS3860
Study Title	Metabolomic characterization of pitaya fruit from three red-skinned cultivars with different pulp colors
Study Description	Pitaya is a new fruit crop, whose exotically colored fruits have excellent nutritional and antioxidant properties. In this study, the primary metabolite profiles of three pitaya cultivars i.e. 'Guanhuahong' (red peel with red pulp), 'Guanhuabai' (red peel with white pulp) and 'Guanhuahongfen' (red peel with pink pulp) were investigated using GC-MS and Ultraviolet–visible spectroscopy. In the fruit pulp, levels of starch, organic acids, and inositol decreased as the fruit matured. Glucose, fructose, sucrose and sorbitol contents increased gradually during fruit maturation and reached their highest levels in the pulp at the mature stage. Citramalic acid was identified for the first time in the pulp of Hylocereus species. Higher levels of total phenols, flavonoids and antioxidant activities were detected in the peel than in the pulp during fruit maturation of all three cultivars. The finding of higher levels of total phenols and flavonoids in the pitaya peel than in the pulp at the mature stage suggests that pitaya peels are a good source of natural phenols and flavonoids.

Study Protocol Description	<p>4 new red-skinned fruit cultivars, i.e., '<strong>Guanhuahong</strong>' (red peel with red pulp), '<strong>Guanhuabai</strong>' (red peel with white pulp), '<strong>Guanhuahongfen</strong>' (red peel with pink pulp) and '<strong>Shuangse</strong>' were selected from '<strong>Conghuazhixing</strong>' - the hybrid offspring of <em>H. monacanthus</em> (Lem.) Britton and Rose and <em>H. undatus</em> (Haw.) Britton and Rose - through seedling selection. The cultivars were planted in the same orchard of Dalingshan Forest Park, Dongguan City, Guangdong Province, China. Fruits were harvested from mid-July to mid-August 2014 at 5 critical stages of maturity development, i.e., black seed stage (on the 1<strong>9th day after flowering; DAF</strong>), 3 pulp coloration stages (on the <strong>23rd</strong>, <strong>25th</strong> and <strong>27th DAF</strong>) and at the mature stage (on the <strong>29th DAF</strong>) (see <strong>Fig. 1</strong> in the paper associated with this study). During this period, temperatures ranged from 27 to 36 °C (see <strong>Fig. S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study). The time from full flowering to fruit harvest was 28-30 d. 5 uniformly sized fruits were randomly selected from each cultivar. A section (including peel and pulp) ap- proximately 1 cm thick was sliced from each whole fruit at its equator and then divided into four equal parts. Peel and pulp fractions from the section were separated, frozen quickly in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until used for metabolite analyses.</p>	<p>Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) was used to determine sugars and organic acids according to a previously published method<strong>[1]</strong>, with some modifications. Briefly, 100 mg of pulp were ground to powder in liquid nitrogen and then transferred into a 2 ml tube. Each sample was extracted with 1.4 ml of 70% (v/v) pre-cooled methanol (−20 °C) by vortexing for 10 s. Then, as an internal quantitative standard, 60 μl of ribitol (0.2 mg/ml dissolved in doubly distilled H2O) was added to each mixture and vortexed for 10 s. The mixtures were shaken at 70 °C in a Thermomixer Compact (Eppendorf, Germany) at 950 rpm for 10 min, followed by centrifugation at 11,000 x g for 10 min. The supernatants were transferred to Schott GL14 glass vials. To each supernatant, a mixture of 750 μl pre-cooled chloroform (at -20 °C) and 1.5 ml of pre-cooled doubly distilled H2O (4 °C) was added, and the mixtures were vortexed for 10 s. The mixtures were then centrifuged at 2200 x g for 15 min. For each mixture, 100 μl of the upper phase (polar phase) was transferred into a new 1.5 ml tube, and the tubes were dried in a vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf Concentrator plus, Germany) at room temperature. The dried samples were oximated with 40 μl of methoxyamination reagent. 1 derivatization reaction without samples was used as a control. The reaction mixtures were incubated at 37 °C in a Thermomixer Compact (Eppendorf, Germany) at 700 rpm for 2 h. After oximation, silylation was performed by adding 60 μl of MSTFA to each mixture, and the mixtures were incubated at 37 °C in a Thermomixer Compact (Eppendorf, Germany) at 700 rpm for 30 min. 80 μl of each derivatization products were transferred into glass vials for GC-MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lisec J, Schauer N, Kopka J, Willmitzer L, Fernie AR. Gas chromatography mass spectrometry-based metabolite profiling in plants. Nat Protoc. 2006;1(1):387-96. doi:10.1038/nprot.2006.59. Erratum in: Nat Protoc. 2015 Sep;10(9):1457. PMID:17406261.</p>	<p>GC-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890A GC system equipped with an Agilent 7693 autosampler and Agilent 5975C-inert MSD with Triple Axis Detector (Agilent, Atlanta, GA). A HP-5MS capillary column (5% phenyl methyl siloxane, 30 m * 0.25 mm i.d., 0.25 μm film thickness) was used with helium gas at a constant flow of 1 ml/min. 1 μl of derivatized sample was injected at 230 °C in split mode with the split ratio adjusted to 1:20. The total flow rate of the injection port was 24 ml/min. The following temperature regime was used: 70 °C for 2 min, followed by 210 °C from 2 to 16 min with a 10 °C per min hold, followed by 250 °C from 16 to 24 min with a 5 °C per min hold, followed by a 10 °C per min ramp to 330 °C from 24 to 36 min and a final holding for 3 min. The electron ionization was set at 70 eV, and the mass spectra were recorded over the range of 50-600 m/z.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were collected on a Agilent 5975C-inert MSD with Triple Axis Detector (Agilent, Atlanta, GA) with electron ionization set at 70 eV, and data recorded over the range of 50-600 m/z.</p>	<p>No Data transformation information was provided for this study.</p>	<p>In the extracted ion features, only the variables having more than 50% of non-zero measurement values in at least 1 group were kept. For peak picking, the following parameters were used: mass spectrogram and peak time.</p><p><br></p><p>All experiments were performed in triplicate. Mean values, standard deviations and analyses of variance (ANOVA) were calculated with SPSS Statistics17 software. Multiple mean comparisons were performed using Duncan's test (p ≤ 0.05), with 95% confidence intervals for each mean.</p>

Study Title	Intestinal Microbe-Metabolite Network Associated with The Efficacy of Fecal Microbiota Transplantation Therapy in Inflammatory Bowel Disease
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:</strong> Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), is a type of recurrent and incurable disease, which can lead to the loss of workability, increase cancer risk and bring serious economic burden to patients. Abnormal intestinal microbe interaction contributes to the pathogenesis of IBD, and abnormal host-microbe cometabolites are considered to be one of the causes of dysbiosis of microbe interaction. Fecal microbial transplantation (FMT) is an effective strategy for IBD treatment, but it is unclear how FMT changes intestinal microbial interaction, which to some extent leads to the erratic efficacy of FMT application. It is of great significance to further explore the host-microbiota interaction mode during FMT treatment, which can provide a basis for improving the efficacy of FMT.</p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> 37 IBD patients (including 15 UC and 22 CD) and 16 healthy donors were recruited from October 2019 to May 2021. 15 UC patients received FMT treatment and 12 of them with therapeutic reactions. 15 CD patients received pure FMT treatment, 9 of them present therapeutic reaction, and 2 of them present clinical unresponsive. 7 CD patients received scheduled ileocolic resection. 2 FMT unresponsive CD patients and 7 postoperative CD patients received infliximab (IFX)-FMT combination treatment, and 5 of them present therapeutic reactions. Clinical evaluation was performed after 14 weeks of FMT treatment. Fecal samples were collected at baseline timepoint (prior treatment) and post-FMT timepoint. 16S sequencing was used to obtain the microbiome information of subjects. LC-MS-based quantitative targeting strategy was used to collect host-microbe cometabolite information. Spearman correlation network analysis was used to construct IBD prognosis marker-microbe-metabolite ternary interaction network.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> FMT therapy, but not IFX therapy, shifted α- and β-diversity of intestinal microbiota in patients with UC and CD toward that of healthy donors. Between subjects with and without clinical response both in UC and CD cohort, β-diversity did not vary significantly but α-diversity showed a significantly difference. On the other hand, FMT therapy can shift host-microbe co-metabolic profiles of baseline subjects toward that of healthy donors, so can IFX-FMT combination therapy in operative or FMT unresponsive CD subjects. After excluding microbes or metabolites unrelated to the FMT treatment response, we found that the remaining microbes or metabolites had nonnegligible associations with the baseline characteristics of the subjects (including age, gender, onset time, inflammatory degree, etc.). On the contrary, we did not find enough association between IBD prognostic indicators (including CRP, calprotectin, and MAYO or CDAI score) and FMT response-related microbes or metabolites. Nevertheless, we constructed the IBD prognosis marker-FMT response related microbe-FMT response related metabolite ternary interaction network and found that the number of associations increased significantly after FMT treatment relative to baseline subjects.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSION:</strong> It may take the intestinal microbe-metabolite interaction network as the standard or indicator for donor-receptor matching during FMT therapy, rather than simply considering the differences of bacteria or metabolites between donor and receptor.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 37 IBD patients were prospectively recruited from Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital from October 2019 to May 2021. Among them, 15 were diagnosed as UC and 22 were diagnosed as CD. For CD patients, 15 were received FMT treatment, 7 were first received scheduled ileocolic resection and then received infliximab (IFX)-FMT combination treatment. Healthy donors were recruited from Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital (n=11) and Shanghai Zhangjiang Institute of Medical Innovation (n=5).</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC system using an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column (2.1×100 mm, 1.7 µm) maintained at 40°C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) water with 0.1% formic acid and (B) acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The gradient program was: 0-1 min 2% B, 1-8 min 2-98% B, 8-10 min 98% B, followed by re-equilibration at 2% B for 3 min. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min and injection volume was 2 µL.</p>	<p>Raw data were converted from instrument format to mzML using ProteoWizard msconvert (version 3.0). Peak detection, alignment, and gap-filling were performed using XCMS (R package) with parameters: method = 'centWave', ppm = 10, peakwidth = c(5,20), snthresh = 6, mzdiff = 0.01. After feature extraction, data were normalized by total ion intensity and log-transformed before statistical analysis.</p>	<p>Fecal samples (100 mg) were homogenized in 300 µL ice-cold methanol containing internal standards (e.g., 13C-sorbitol). After vortexing for 30 s, samples were incubated at -20°C for 30 min, then centrifuged at 14,000 g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant was transferred to a new tube and dried under nitrogen. The residue was reconstituted in 100 µL of water/acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) prior to LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC-Xevo TQ-S triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in alternating positive and negative ion mode. The source parameters were: capillary voltage 3.0 kV, cone voltage 30 V, source temperature 150°C, desolvation temperature 500°C, cone gas flow 150 L/h, and desolvation gas flow 1000 L/h. Data were acquired in full scan mode over m/z 60-1000.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using targeted analysis based on authentic standards. Compounds were identified by matching retention times and multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions with reference standards. For each metabolite, the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) and retention time were recorded. Identification confidence followed the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) guidelines, with all reported metabolites at MSI level 1.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS3943
Study Title	Health outcomes of fruit juice and fruity beverage: a comparative analysis of gut microbiota and metabolomics in rats (Metabolomics assay)
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:</strong> Evidence on the relationship between food processing and health outcomes has been increasing steadily. Under this context, ultra-processing food, such as sugar-sweetened fruity beverages (SS-FB), is associated with a higher risk of metabolic syndrome. However, whether drinking fruit juice not from concentrate (NFC-FJ), which belongs to minimally processed food, causes poor health or not is controversial. The major goal was to determine the impact of fruit drinks with different processing degrees on health via the interaction of gut microbiota and metabolomics.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> Significant different trends in metabolic syndrome were found between NFC-FJ and SS-FB in a 4-week experiment. NFC-FJ increased alpha diversity and decreased F/B ratio compared to SS-FB. The modulated bacterial taxa by NFC-FJ increased lipid metabolism as well as decreased insulin resistance and inhibited the dimethyl-L-arginine (ADMA) formation from S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM), contributing to a lower risk of metabolic syndrome in rats. However, SS-FB led to a higher risk of metabolic syndrome in rats with increased food intake and weight change. Additionally, SS-FB with the fructose-to-glucose ratio of 1.5 corresponded to an increase in kidney mass and serum C17-sphinganine level, boosting risks for kidney disease. More than 10 times higher flavonoid content in NFC-FJ than SS-FB was caused by processing degrees, which might be partly responsible for the alteration of gut microbiota.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> Our results revealed that rich flavonoids in minimally processed fruit juice decreased the risk of metabolic syndrome in rats through the pathway of insulin resistance via gut microbiota alteration, while ultra-processed fruit beverages enhanced the risk of metabolic syndrome. Based on our data, NFC fruit juice should be considered a healthy choice.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics assay</strong>&nbsp;is reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS3943' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS3943</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>Lipidomics assay</strong>&nbsp;is reported in&nbsp;<a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS4108' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS4108</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Ethics approval and consent to participate</strong></p><p>All animal experiments were performed in the lab of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All experimental procedures described here were conducted following the National Institutes of Health Guidelines on the Care and Use of Laboratory Animal of Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. The approve code is SCXK-2016–0006. The permission code is P2021032.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Drinks preparation</strong></p><p>We chose 2 kinds of popular drinks from the local market as the objects of our study. For <strong>FB</strong> (<strong>SS-FB</strong>), its label claims no less than 10% orange juice content. For <strong>FJ</strong> (<strong>NFC-FJ</strong>), its label claims 100% orange juice content. All drinks samples were stored at room temperature and shaken before use.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Animal experiment</strong></p><p>First, 6-8 week-old SD (Rattus norvegicus) rats (male; SPF; the mean weight of 209 g) were purchased from Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co. Ltd (Beijing, China). The animal feeds were purchased from Keao Xieli Feed Co., Ltd (Beijing, China), and the primary nutrients of animal feed are shown in <strong>Table S4</strong> in the paper associated with this study. The rats were kept under controlled light conditions (12 h light-dark cycle) with free access to feed and water throughout the entire intervention.</p><p><br></p><p>The design of drink intervention study is shown in <strong>Figure S5</strong>. The rats were acclimatized for 7 days, and the weight, fasting blood glucose (FBG), total cholesterols (TC), total triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were recorded on the last day of acclimatization. When the rats were adapted to the experiment condition during the first week, body weight, FBG, TC, TG, HDL-C and LDL-C were measured to divide these rats into 4 groups, denoted as <strong>C0</strong>, <strong>control</strong> (C-4W), <strong>FJ-intake</strong> (fruit juice not from concentrate; HJ (NFSQ)) and <strong>FB-intake </strong>(sugar-sweetened fruity beverages; LJ (MZY)), respectively. After grouping, there are no significant differences in these indexes among groups.</p><p><br></p><p>For C0, cecum and serum samples were directly collected after 7 days of acclimatization and stored at -80 °C until analysis. Then, rats were dissected to obtain kidney, brain, liver, fat, colon, intestine and cecum. The organ mass was recorded and the organs were stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>The control and 2 intervention groups (control, FJ-intake and FB-intake) proceeded with formal intervention for 28 days. Under the regular diet, the rats were gavaged with normal saline for control, FJ and FB, FJ-intake and FB-intake, respectively (twice per day). Each rat was given the equivalent of one adult (60 kg) drinking 300 mL of drinks a day. The specific volume of gavage was calculated as 31.5 times the volume of each rat's last measured body weight (for example, if the last measured weight of a rat is 0.2 kg, its gavage volume is 6.3 mL per day). During the 28-day diet, the weight, FBG, TC, TG, HDL-C and LDL-C were recorded once per week. On the last day, samples from the 3 groups were collected and stored under the same condition as C0. The organ index was calculated based on the ratio of organ mass to body weight. In total 120 samples of the cecum and serum samples (60 samples each) from all the rats were obtained.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics &amp; Lipidomics</strong></p><p>The ultrahigh-pressure liquid chromatography coupled with a quadrupole time-of-flight mass (UHPLCQTOF MS) was performed. The samples were subjected to a reversed-phase chromatography using an LC system (SCIEX, USA). Mass spectrometric analysis was performed with a QTOF MS with a DuoSpray ion source (TripleTOF 6600, SCIEX, USA). Quality control (QC) samples were made by pooling equal aliquots of each sample together in an analytical run. The QC sample was injected and analyzed at regular intervals of every 6 samples in UHPLC-QTOF MS acquisition. These QC sample data were used to monitor the reproducibility and stability of the analytes in samples during the analysis. We thank the Shanghai Luming biological technology co., LTD (Shanghai, China) for providing metabolomics services.</p>	<p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>For juice drinks: </strong></p><p class='ql-align-justify'>The metabolomics was conducted on 2 drinks according to the previous method with modifications<strong>[1]</strong>. The 2 drinks were filtered using a 0.22-μm polytetrafluoroethylene membrane and 2 drinks with 6 replicates each were obtained.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><br></p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>For cecum samples:</strong></p><p>The cecum content samples were performed according to previous methods with modifications<strong>[2][3]</strong>. First, 300 mg of cecum contents was weighed with 1.5 mL methanol:water (1:1, v/v, 4 °C) solution added. The mixture was vibrated for 30 s, frozen in liquid nitrogen for 30 s, then thawed in water at room temperature, and repeated 3 times. After the last thawing, an ultrasound was performed for 30 s followed by 10 s vibration and 30 s standing; then, the process was repeated 5 times. Centrifugation was performed at 15,000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C. Finally, the supernatant was filtered using a 0.22-μm nylon membrane for analysis.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><br></p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>For serum samples:</strong></p><p>The serum content samples were performed according to a previous method with modifications<strong>[4]</strong>. The serum was thawed in a refrigerator at 4 °C. After thawing, the serum was rotated for 5 s. The 400 μL methanol was added to 200 μL serum, and the mixture was rotated for 10 s and stood at room temperature for 15 min. Then, centrifugation was performed at 10,000 x g for 20 min at 4°C. Finally, the supernatant was filtered using a 0.22-μm nylon membrane for analysis.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><br></p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>[1]</strong> Wang K, Xu Z. Comparison of freshly squeezed, Non-thermally and thermally processed orange juice based on traditional quality characters, untargeted metabolomics, and volatile overview. Food Chem. 2022 Mar 30;373(Pt B):131430. doi:10.1016/j.foodchem.2021.131430. Epub 2021 Oct 20. PMID:34731802.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>[2]</strong> Tian Y, Zhang L, Wang Y, Tang H. Age-related topographical metabolic signatures for the rat gastrointestinal contents. J Proteome Res. 2012 Feb 3;11(2):1397-411. doi:10.1021/pr2011507. Epub 2011 Dec 27. PMID:22129435.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>[3]</strong> Xie G, Zhong W, Zheng X, Li Q, Qiu Y, Li H, Chen H, Zhou Z, Jia W. Chronic ethanol consumption alters mammalian gastrointestinal content metabolites. J Proteome Res. 2013 Jul 5;12(7):3297-306. doi:10.1021/pr400362z. Epub 2013 Jun 14. PMID:23763674.</p><p class='ql-align-justify'><strong>[4]</strong> Lin L, Huang Z, Gao Y, Yan X, Xing J, Hang W. LC-MS based serum metabonomic analysis for renal cell carcinoma diagnosis, staging, and biomarker discovery. J Proteome Res. 2011 Mar 4;10(3):1396-405. doi:10.1021/pr101161u. Epub 2011 Feb 4. PMID:21186845.</p>	<p>The ultrahigh-pressure liquid chromatography coupled with a quadrupole time-of-flight mass (UHPLCQTOF MS) was performed. The samples were subjected to a reversed-phase chromatography using an LC system (SCIEX, USA). Mass spectrometric analysis was performed with a QTOF MS with a DuoSpray ion source (TripleTOF 6600, SCIEX, USA).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For juice drinks: </strong></p><p>The separation column is ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (1.8 μm, 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters, USA), maintained at 40 °C, and the autosampler was maintained at 4 °C. The injection volume and flow rate were 2 μL and 0.300 mL/min. Mobile phases A and B were 0.2% formic acid in water and acetonitrile, respectively. Solvent gradient was as follow: 0.0-11.50 min, 5-30% B; 11.50-11.51 min, 30-100% B; 11.51-15.00 min, 100% B; 15.00-15.01 min, 100-5% B; 15.01-18.00 min, 5% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For cecum samples: </strong></p><p>The separation column is C18 (1.7 μm, 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters, USA), maintained at 40 °C, and the autosampler was maintained at 4 °C. The injection volume and flow rate were 2 μL and 0.300 mL/min. Mobile phases A and B were 0.1% formic acid in water, and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile, respectively. Solvent gradient was as follow: 0.0-0.10 min, 5% B; 0.10-2.00 min, 5-13% B; 2.00-4.00 min, 13-28% B; 4.00-8.50 min, 28-40% B; 11.50-13.00 min, 40-70% A; 13.00-13.50 min, 73-100% B; 13.50-16.00 min, 100% B; 16.00-20 min, 5% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For serum samples: </strong></p><p>The separation column is C18 (1.7 μm, 2.1 x 100 mm, Waters, USA), maintained at 40 °C, and the autosampler was maintained at 4 °C. The injection volume and flow rate were 2 μL and 0.300 mL/min. Mobile phases A and B were 0.1% formic acid in water, and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile, respectively. Solvent gradient was as follow: 0.0-0.10 min, 5% B; 0.10-1.50 min, 5-13% B; 1.50-2.50 min, 13-28% B; 2.50-5.50 min, 28-40% B; 5.50-7.50 min, 40-70% A; 7.50-15.00 min, 70-90% B; 15.00-16.00 min, 90-100% B; 16.00-19.00 min, 100% B; 19.01-22.00 min, 5% B.</p>	<p>The ultrahigh-pressure liquid chromatography coupled with a quadrupole time-of-flight mass (UHPLCQTOF MS) was performed. The samples were subjected to a reversed-phase chromatography using an LC system (SCIEX, USA). Mass spectrometric analysis was performed with a QTOF MS with a DuoSpray ion source (TripleTOF 6600, SCIEX, USA).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For juice drinks: </strong></p><p>The SWATH with a cycle time of 545 ms was composed of a TOF MS scan (accumulation time, 50 ms; CE, 10 eV) and a series of product ion scans (accumulation time, 30 ms each; CE, 35 eV, CES, 15 eV) of 15 Q1 windows of 60 Da from 100-1000 m/z.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For cecum samples:</strong></p><p>The SWATH with a cycle time of 550 ms was composed of a TOF MS scan (accumulation time, 50 ms; CE, 10 eV) and a series of product ion scans (accumulation time, 30 ms each; CE, 35 eV, CES, 15 eV) of 15 Q1 windows from 50-1200 m/z.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>For serum samples:</strong></p><p>The SWATH with a cycle time of 550 ms was composed of a TOF MS scan (accumulation time, 50 ms; CE, 10 eV) and a series of product ion scans (accumulation time, 30 ms each; CE, 35 eV, CES, 15 eV) of 15 Q1 windows from 50-1200 m/z.</p>	<p>UHPLC-QTOF MS data were processed by MS-DIAL 4.60 to get the MS1 raw peak list and intensity. The parameters were set as follows: MS1 and MS2 tolerance were 0.01 and 0.025 Da in peak extraction, and the maximum charge number was 2. MS1 and MS2 tolerance were 0.01 and 0.05 Da in qualitative extraction, and the retention time deviation in peak alignment was 0.2 min. The MS1 deviation was 0.015 Da, the detection rate reached 80% in at least one group, and the S/N ratio (maximum in the sample/mean in the blank sample) was 5. The exported MS1 peak list was analyzed by Excel 2020 (Microsoft Corporation, USA) to remove the MS1 features with a detection rate was &lt; 80% or RSD &gt; 30% in the QC group.</p>	<p>For drinks, the MS1 features with p-value &lt; 0.05, FC &gt; 5, and variable importance for projection (VIP) &gt; 1.5 based on the orthogonal partial-least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) model were selected as characteristic markers for further identification. For cecum and serum samples, all the MS1 features were further identified. Specifically, the MS1 features that met the above conditions were identified by exporting their MS2 spectra from <strong>MS-DIAL</strong> 4.60 into <strong>MS-FINDER</strong> 3.50 for putative annotation by matching the databases, such as <strong>HMDB</strong>, <strong>LIPID MAPS</strong>, <strong>PubChem</strong> and <strong>FooDB</strong>. The parameters, MS1 tolerance and MS2 tolerance were set as 10 and 15 ppm, respectively</p>

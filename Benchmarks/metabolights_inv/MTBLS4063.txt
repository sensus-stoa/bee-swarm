Study Identifier	MTBLS4063
Study Title	Fate and PPARγ and STATs-driven effects of the mitochondrial complex I inhibitor tebufenpyrad in liver cells revealed with multi-omics.
Study Description	The biological effects of the pesticide and mitochondrial complex I inhibitor tebufenpyrad (TEBU) on liver cells were investigated by combining proteomics and metabolomics. Both cell culture media and cellular lysates were analyzed in dose-response and kinetic experiments on the HepaRG cell line. Responses were compared with those obtained on primary human and rat hepatocytes. A multitude of phase I and II metabolites (>80) mainly common to HepaRG cells and primary hepatocytes and an increase in metabolization enzymes were observed. Synthesis of mitochondrion and oxidative phosphorylation complex constituents, fatty acid oxidation, and cellular uptake of lipids were induced to compensate for complex I inhibition and the decrease in ATP intracellular contents caused by TEBU. Secretion of the 20 S circulating proteasome and overall inhibition of acute inflammation followed by IL-6 secretion in later stages were observed in HepaRG cells. These effects were associated with a decrease in STAT1 and STAT3 transcription factor abundances, but with different kinetics. Based on identified TEBU targets, docking experiments, and nuclear receptor reporter assays, we concluded that liver cell response to TEBU is mediated by its interaction with the PPARγ transcription factor.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cell culture:</strong></p><p>The human hepatic cell line HepaRG (Biopredic International, Rennes, France) was used at passages 14–20. Cells were cultured in Williams E medium (Eurobio, Les Ulis, France) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) (Perbio, Brebières, France), 100 units/ml penicillin (Invitrogen Corporation, Illkirch, France), 100 μg/ml streptomycin (Invitrogen Corporation), 5 μg/ml insulin (Sigma-Aldrich, Lyon, France), 2 mM L-glutamine (Thermofisher, Waltham, MA, USA), and 25 μg/ml hydrocortisone succinate (Pharmacia &amp; Upjohn, Guyancourt, France) at 37 °C in an atmosphere containing 5% CO2. 1 x 10^6 HepaRG cells were seeded in a 75 cm^2 flask and were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 and medium was changed every 2 d. For experimentation, after 14 d of culture in 75 cm^2 flask, cells were seeded at 2.5 x 10^4 cells/cm^2 in 12-well plates. For differentiation, cells were incubated for 2 wk before the addition of 1.7% DMSO to the culture medium for 2 more wk. In all cases, the medium was changed 3 times a wk.</p><p><br></p><p>Cryopreserved PHHs derived from a 43-year old Caucasian female (Batch number: HEP187526-PA05) suffering of hepatocellular adenoma were obtained from Biopredic International (Saint-Grégoire, France). Male Wistar rat cryopreserved hepatocytes (Batch number: HEP137007) were obtained from Biopredic International (Saint-Grégoire, France). PHHs and PRHs were cultured according to the supplier’s protocol. Briefly, the vial of hepatocytes was rapidly thawed in a water bath at 37 °C, and the content of the vial was transferred into pre-warmed medium and centrifuged at 100 x g for 6 min at room temperature. After removing the supernatant, the hepatocytes were gently resuspended in pre-cooled medium. Cell count and cell viability were measured using trypan blue exclusion method and a viability thresholf of 75% was applied. The hepatocytes were seeded at a density of 2 x 10^6 cells/well for PHHs and 1.6 x 10^6 cells/well or PRHs in 6-well collagen I-coated plates, prior to incubation overnight at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Cell treatments for metabolomics experiment:</strong></p><p>2 types of experiments were conducted with HepaRG cells in 12-well plates <strong>(i)</strong> a dose-response study after 24 h of exposure with 1, 5 and 10 μM TEBU and <strong>(ii)</strong> a kinetic study with 1, 4, 8 and 24 h time points at 10 μM TEBU. For each time point, solvent controls (0.1% DMSO) were included. Triplicate wells were performed for each treatment condition. For PHHs and PRHs, TEBU was incubated for 24 h at 2 μM. For all experiments, at the end of the treatment period, media were collected and frozen to -80 °C until further use for metabolomics analysis. Cells were washed 2 times in 0.9% NaCl and lysed in 100 μl of lysis buffer (Pierce IP lysis buffer, Thermo Scientific) composed of 25 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1% dNP-40 and 5% glycerol and supplemented in 50 mM NaF, 0.1 M sodium orthovanadate (both from Sigma Aldrich) and protease inhibitors (complete mini EDTA-free, Roche Life Sciences, 1 tablet/10 ml). After centrifugation (10 min at 15,000 x g), the supernatants (protein lysates) were kept at -80 °C for metabolomics analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites present in cell culture media (500 μl) and cell lysates (100 μg) were extracted following protein precipitation with acetone at -20°C. Internal labeled standards ronidazole-d3, albendazole-13C6, erythromycin-d6 and flunixin-d3 were added to samples at a final concentration of 25 ng/ml, before Solid Phase Extraction (SPE) and injection. Metabolites were concentrated and desalted using Strata-X 96-well plate (30 mg, Phenomenex) prior to LC-MS/MS analyses.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>For each experimental triplicate, metabolites mixtures were analyzed by a Q-Exactive mass spectrometer coupled to a Vanquish Flex Binary UHPLC system (both from Thermo Scientific). In addition, 3 Quality Control (QC) samples were included to check acquisition reproducibility. Chromatographic separations were performed with the following parameters: column Acquity UPLC HSS T3 (1.8 μm 1 mm x 150 mm, 100 Å), 80 μl/min low, gradient rising from 95% solvent A (water, 0.1% formic acid) to 45% solvent B (100% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid) in 9 min, then to 65% solvent B in 10 min, to 100% solvent B in 3 min, isocratic flow at 100% B during 3 min, final step with a column regeneration during 2 min giving a total run time of 28 min.</p>	<p>Metabolites mixtures were analyzed in triplicate by a Q-Exactive mass spectrometer. Small molecules were analyzed in the Orbitrap in full ion scan mode in negative and positive modes, at a resolution of 70,000 (at m/z 200) with a mass range of m/z 70–1050. Molecules with unassigned charge state were not set for MS2. Fragments were obtained by Higher-energy Collisional Dissociation (HCD) activation with a collisional energy of 27%, an isolation width of 1.4 Da. MS/MS data were acquired in the Orbitrap cell in a data-dependent mode in which the 20 most intense precursor ions were fragmented, with a dynamic exclusion of 30 s and a resolution of 17 500. Automatic Gain Control (AGC) targets were set to 3e^6 and 2e^5 for respectively MS and MS/MS steps. Maximum ion accumulation times were set to 50 and 45 ms for respectively MS and MS/MS acquisitions.</p>	<p><strong>Compound Discoverer 3.2 software</strong> (Thermo Scientific) was used. Retention times were aligned with a maximum shift of 2 min and a 5 ppm mass tolerance according to an adaptive curve alignment model. Detection parameters were: intensity tolerance 30, mass tolerance 5 ppm, S/N threshold of 3, max element C90H190Br3Cl4K2N10Na2O15P3S5, min peak intensity 1000000 in positive mode and 500000 in negative mode. A Quality Control (QC) correction was applied for each dataset. Measured area were normalized with a constant median normalization type.</p>	<p>The search mass lists node of <strong>Compound Discoverer</strong> was used for metabolite identifications with an 4400 endogenous metabolites database (Thermo Scientific) and an in-house metabolite database specific to HepaRG cells. <strong>Chemspider</strong> and the predicted compositions algorithm of <strong>Compound Discoverer</strong> were also used to identify metabolites. <strong>Mzcloud</strong> webtool was used to confirm identifications with MS2 spectra. XLogP3 and LogP concordance with experimental retention times (RT) were used to verify metabolite identifications. Linear correlations of RT with LogP was at least characterized by a minimal R2 of 0.7 for each dataset. Neutral losses (61 species) were scanned in MS2 spectra using the Neutral Losses node from Compound Disccoverer in order to identify modified metabolites and more specifically phase I and II metabolites in the presence/absence of TEBU. For each comparison of two experimental conditions, an <strong>analysis of variance (ANOVA)</strong> test followed by a <strong>Tukey HSD test (posthoc)</strong> were applied and p-values were adjusted with a <strong>Benjamini-Hochberg</strong> correction. Abundance variations of metabolites were considered if adjusted p-values were below 0.05.</p>

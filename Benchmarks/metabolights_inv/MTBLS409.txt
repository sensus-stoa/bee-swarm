Study Identifier	MTBLS409
Study Title	RNA-Seq and UHPLC-Q-TOF/MS Based Lipidomics Study in Lysiphlebia japonica
Study Description	Lipids play an important role in energy storage, membrane structure stabilization and signaling. Parasitoids are excellent models to study lipidomics because a majority of them do not accumulate during their free-living life-stage. Studies on parasitoids have mostly focused on the changes in the lipids and gene transcripts in hosts and little attention has been devoted to lipidomics and transcriptomics changes in parasitoids. In this study, a relative quantitative analysis of lipids and their gene transcripts in 3-days-old Lysiphlebia japonica larva (3 days after spawning) and pupae were performed using liquid chromatography, mass spectrometry and RNA-seq. Thirty-three glycerolipids and 250 glycerophospholipids were identified in this study; all triglycerides and the vast majority of phospholipids accumulated in the pupal stage. This was accompanied by differentially regulated lipid uptake and remolding. Furthermore, our data showed that gene transcription was up-regulated in key nutrient metabolic pathways involved in lipid synthesis in 3-days-old larvae. Finally, our data suggests that larva and pupa of L. japonica may lack the ability for fatty acids synthesis. A comprehensive, quantitative, and expandable resource was provided for further studies of metabolic regulation and molecular mechanisms underlying parasitic response to hosts defense.

Study Protocol Description	L. japonica were collected at the Institute of Cotton Research of Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, and reared in the laboratory on cotton-melon aphids. Rearing conditions: 24 ± 1 °C, a 14:10 hr light/dark (L:D) photoperiod, and 75 ± 5 % relative humidity (RH). </p> Third instar cotton-melon aphids from the same mother were exposed to wasps, and the parasitized aphids were collected to separate the wasp larva after 3 days. Other parasitized aphids were reared on cotton leaves and collected 5 days later, when they became mummified alates. After dissection, both the 3-day-old (named group Y) and mummified (named group J) L. japonica larva were cleaned with ultrapure water and stored at -80 °C. Each group had 6 biological repetitions, for a total of 12 samples.	20 mg of each L. japonica sample was used for the extraction of lipid metabolites. Tissue was homogenized using a ball mill (5 balls and 100 μl water at 4 °C, 30 Hz for 3 min) and the homogenates were transferred to glass tubes, containing 400 μl of CH2Cl2:MeOH (2:1, v/v). The solution was vortexed for 30 s and subsequently centrifuged for 15 min at 2500 rpm. The organic layer (~200 μl) was then transferred to a clean glass vial and evaporated. The dried extract was reconstituted in 100 μl CH2Cl2:MeOH (1:1, v/v), in preparation for UHPLC-QTOFMS analysis. ACN (Honeywell, Japan), HCOONH4 (Thermo, USA), and isopropanol (IPA) (Thermo, USA) were used in the experiments. A pooled quality control (QC) aliquot was created by mixing 10 µl of all samples. QC samples were measured at the begining and the end of the run, and inbetween every 3 samples.	Lipids were tested with UHPLC-q-TOF/MS (Agilent 1290 UHPLC + AB Triple TOF 6600+), and the chromatographic column used was Kinetex 1.7 μm C18 100 A 100 x 2.1 mm (Phenomenex, USA).	The TripleTOF mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on an information-dependent basis (IDA) during an LC/MS experiment. In this mode, the acquisition software (Analyst TF 1.7, AB Sciex) continuously evaluates the full scan survey MS data as it collects and triggers the acquisition of MS/MS spectra depending on preselected criteria. In each cycle, 6 precursor ions whose intensity greater than 100 were chosen for fragmentation at collision energy (CE) of 35 V (15 MS/MS events with product ion accumulation time of 50 msec each).	Raw data were converted into .mzXML format using ProteoWizard, and further analyzed by XCMS. Metabolite identification was achieved using accurate mass search (< 30 ppm) and MS/MS spectral match using an in-house standard MS/MS library.	Molecular lipids were analyzed in both positive and negative ion modes using multiple precursor ion scanning-based methods. Molecular lipid species were identified and relatively quantified (TAG, PC, PE, PS, LPC, LPE, PG, SM, PI, PA, PC-P, and PE-P) . Lipids are referred to according to CHEBI and internal lipid nomenclature recommendations.

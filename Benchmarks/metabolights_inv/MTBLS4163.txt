Study Title	Macrosteatosis Cause Graft Failure Via Proliferative Arrest in Rats after Liver Transplantation
Study Description	We created a rat model with macrosteatosis (MaS) liver via methionine-cholinedeficient food (MCD) diet and transplanted the MaS liver into normal rats, then the rats without any MaS will be set as control. In the whole process of surgeries, we procured the liver tissue samples in different time points (before LT, 1 week after LT, and 4 months after LT). Then samples were prepared for further transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Adult male<strong> Lewis rats </strong>(at 3-4 weeks age with body weight ranged between 180 and 220 g, purchased from Beijing Weitong Lihua Laboratory Animal Limited Company) were categorized into donor and recipient groups for further LT experiments. According to previous literature, donor liver MaS was induced by feeding by <strong>methionine-choline deficient diet </strong>(<strong>MCD</strong>, 519580, Dyets, USA) for around 4 wks. Correspondingly, the donor rats with <strong>non-MaS liver</strong> were induced by feeding <strong>methionine and choline sufficient diet </strong>(<strong>MCS</strong>, 519581, Dyets, USA) for 4 weeks as control. All recipient rats were fed by standardized rodent diet in similar duration with donor rats.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Orthotopic liver transplantation</strong> (OLT) was performed respectively by an experienced technician (Y.H) according to procedures referred to our previous publications. The chimeric rats were fed by standardized rodent diet and sterilized water ad libitum after OLT. Graft tissues were categorized by MaS status and sampled from donor rats at the beginning of cold ischemia (grouped as <strong>pre-MaS/pre-non-MaS</strong>) and 7 d after LT (<strong>grouped as post-MaS/post-non-MaS</strong>). Meanwhile, <strong>pre-transplant </strong>or <strong>post-transplant</strong> samples were also categorized respectively by GF occurrence in follow-up duration after LT (grouped as <strong>pre-GF/pre-non-GF or post-GF/post-non-GF</strong>). Long-term survivors were sampled for the 3rd time and executed after around 4 months follow-up duration, which were categorized by MaS status before LT (<strong>grouped as pre-MaS in long-term/pre-non-MaS in longterm</strong>). Tissues were stored by snap freezing in liquid nitrogen for further metabolomic assays.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Liver tissues (100 mg) were individually grounded with liquid nitrogen and the homogenate was resuspended with prechilled 80% methanol and 0.1% formic acid by well vortexing. The samples were incubated on ice for 5 min and then were centrifuged at 15,000 rpm, 4 °C for 5 min. A some of supernatant was diluted to final concentration containing 53% methanol by LC-MS grade water. The samples were subsequently transferred to a fresh Eppendorf tube and then were centrifuged at 15,000 x g, 4 °C for 10 min. Finally, the supernatant was injected into the LC-MS/MS system analysis.</p>	<p>LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive series mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher). Samples were injected onto an Hyperil Gold column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm) using a 16 min linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The eluents for the positive polarity mode were eluent A (0.1% FA in Water) and eluent B (Methanol). The eluents for the negative polarity mode were eluent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9.0) and eluent B (Methanol). The solvent gradient was set as follows: 2% B, 1.5 min; 2-100% B, 12.0 min; 100% B, 14.0 min; 100-2% B, 14.1 min; 2% B, 17.0 min.</p>	<p>Q Exactive series mass spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with spray voltage of 3.2 kV, capillary temperature of 320 °C, sheath gas flow rate of 35 arb and aux gas flow rate of 10 arb.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The <strong>.raw </strong>data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed using the <strong>Compound Discoverer 3.1 </strong>(CD3.1, Thermo Fisher) to perform peak alignment, peak picking and quantitation for each metabolite. The main parameters were set as follows: retention&nbsp;time tolerance, 0.2 min; actual mass tolerance, 5 ppm; signal intensity tolerance, 30%; signal/noise ratio, 3; and minimum intensity, 100000. After that, peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity.</p>	<p>The normalized data was used to predict the molecular formula based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks and fragment ions. And then peaks were matched with the <strong>mzCloud</strong> (<a href='https://www.mzcloud.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.mzcloud.org/</a>), <strong>mzVault</strong> and <strong>MassList</strong> database to obtained the accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results. Statistical analyses were performed using the statistical software <strong>R</strong> (version R-3.4.3), <strong>Python</strong> (version Python 2.7.6) and <strong>CentOS</strong> (release 6.6), When data were not normally distributed, normal transformations were attempted using of area normalization method.</p><p><br></p><p>These metabolites were annotated using the <strong>KEGG </strong>database (<a href='http://www.genome.jp/kegg/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.genome.jp/kegg/</a>), <strong>HMDB </strong>database (<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.hmdb.ca/</a>) and <strong>Lipidmaps </strong>database (<a href='http://www.lipidmaps.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.lipidmaps.org/</a>). Principal components analysis (PCA) and Partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) were performed at <strong>metaX </strong>(a flexible and comprehensive software for processing metabolomics data). We applied univariate analysis (t-test) to calculate the statistical significance (P-value). The metabolites with VIP &gt; 1 and P-value &lt; 0.05 and fold change ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5 were considered to be differential metabolites. Volcano plots were used to filter metabolites of interest which based on Log2(FC) and -log10(P-value) of metabolites.</p><p><br></p><p>For clustering heat maps, the data were normalized using z-scores of the intensity areas of differential metabolites and were plotted by <strong>Pheatmap </strong>package in <strong>R </strong>language. The correlation between differential metabolites were analyzed by cor () in <strong>R</strong> language (method = pearson). Statistically significant of correlation between differential metabolites were calculated by cor.mtest () in <strong>R </strong>language. P-value &lt; 0.05 was considered as statistically significant and correlation plots were plotted by corrplot package in <strong>R </strong>language. The functions of these metabolites and metabolic pathways were studied using the <strong>KEGG </strong>database. The metabolic pathway enrichment of differential metabolites were performed, when ratio were satisfied by x/n &gt; y/N,&nbsp;metabolic pathway were considered as enrichment, when P-value of metabolic pathway &lt; 0.05, metabolic pathway were considered as statistically significant enrichment.&nbsp;</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS4206
Study Title	Altered fecal metabolomics and potential biomarkers of psoriatic arthritis differing from rheumatoid arthritis
Study Description	Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory joint disease, and the diagnosis is quite difficult due to the unavailability of reliable clinical markers. This study aimed to investigate the fecal metabolites in PsA by comparison with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and to identify potential diagnostic biomarkers for PsA. The metabolic profiles of the fecal samples from 27 PsA and 29 RA patients as well as 36 healthy controls (HCs) were performed on ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with hybrid triple quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS). And differentially altered metabolites were screened and assessed using multivariate analysis for exploring the potential biomarkers of PsA. The results showed that 154 fecal metabolites were significantly altered in PsA patients when compared with HCs, and 45 metabolites were different when compared with RA patients. A total of 14 common differential metabolites could be defined as candidate biomarkers. Furthermore, a support vector machines (SVM) model was performed to distinguish PsA from RA patients and HCs, and 5 fecal metabolites, including α/β-turmerone, glycerol 1-hexadecanoate, dihydrosphingosine, pantothenic acid and glutamine, were determined as biomarkers for PsA. Through the metabolic pathways analysis, we found that the abnormality of amino acid metabolism, bile acid metabolism and lipid metabolism might contribute to the occurrence and development of PsA. In summary, our research provided ideas for the early diagnosis and treatment of PsA by identifying fecal biomarkers and analyzing metabolic pathways.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Between January 2019 and January 2021, 27 <strong>PsA patients</strong>, 29 <strong>RA patients</strong> (disease control) and 36 <strong>healthy controls</strong> (HCs) with matched age and gender were enrolled from the Second Hospital of Shanxi Medical University. Fresh <strong>stool </strong>samples were collected from each subject within the first to 2 days of the patient’s hospitalization, frozen immediately, and stored at -80&nbsp;°C&nbsp;until use.&nbsp;</p>	<p>Briefly, 20 mg of lyophilized feces were weighed, and dissolved in 1mL of ice-cold water, then vortexed and extracted by ultrasonicating in an ice bath for 20 min. After centrifuged, the supernatant was immediately transferred. The feces were further extracted with 1mL of ice-cold methanol for 20 min. After centrifuged,&nbsp;each supernatant was combined, and ice-cold methanol was added, vortexed to precipitate protein and centrifuged. The supernatant was evaporated to dryness using the speed vacuum concentrator. The residue was redissolved in methanol-water (80:20, v/v) for metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p>Briefly, the metabolic profiles of the fecal samples were performed on a UHPLC (ExionLC AD) coupled with Triple TOF 5600+ mass spectrometer (American, AB Sciex). Chromatographic separation was acquired on a Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 (1.8&nbsp;μm, 2.1 × 100 mm). The column temperature was set at 40&nbsp;°C and the injected volume was 5 µL. Polar extracts analysis: The mobile phase was consisted of 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution (A) and ACN (B) with the optimized gradient elution program as follows: 0-2 min, 2% B; 2-3.5 min, 15% B; 3.5-5 min, 15% B; 5-18 min, 60% B; 18-27 min, 60% B; 27-29 min, 95% B; 29-36 min, 95% B; 36-36.5 min, 2% B; 36.5-39 min, 2% B. The flow rate was set at 0.3 mL/min.</p>	<p>Date acquisition was performed in full scan mode both in the positive and negative ion modes, and also coupled with information-dependent acquisition (IDA) trigger product ion scan modes. The parameters of the MS acquisition of electron spray ionization (ESI) source were listed as follows: ion spray voltage, 4500 V in the negative ion mode and 5500 V in the positive mode; nebulizer gas of 55 psi; heater gas of 55 psi; curtain gas of 30 psi; decluttering potential&nbsp;of 60 V (positive) and -60V (negative);&nbsp;collision energy of 35 eV (positive) and -35 eV (negative); turbo spray temperature of 550 °C; the full scan range of 100-1500 m/z and the ion scan range of 50-1250 m/z with high sensitivity.</p>	<p>The <strong>raw </strong>data was imported to <strong>XCMS </strong>(<strong>version 3.6.3)</strong>&nbsp;for&nbsp;automatic data prepossessing including peak picking and retention time correction.&nbsp;Then&nbsp;the resulting data matrix were imported into <strong>SIMCA 14.0 </strong>software (Umetrics, Sweden) for multivariate data analysis.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified by <strong>OSI/SMMS software</strong>&nbsp;(Dalian ChemData Solution Information Technology Co., Ltd, PR China) and other online databases, including Human Metabolome Database (<a href='http://www.hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>http://www.hmdb.ca/</a>), <strong>Lipidmaps </strong>(<a href='https://lipidmaps.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://lipidmaps.org/</a>) and <strong>LipidBlast </strong>(<a href='https://fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/projects/lipidblast' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/projects/lipidblast</a>).&nbsp;</p>

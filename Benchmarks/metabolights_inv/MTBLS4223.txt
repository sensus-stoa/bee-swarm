STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS4223
Study Title	The functional role of OGDH for maintaining mitochondrial respiration and identity of primed human embryonic stem cells
Study Description	<p>Human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) can self-renew infinitely or differentiate into the 3 germ layer lineages<em> in vitro</em> with certain cues, holding great promise to model early human embryo development <em>ex vivo</em>. It is wildly accepted that hESCs take advantage of both glycolysis and mitochondrial respiration to favor the naïve pluripotency, while prefer glycolysis to support the primed pluripotency. Thus, the function of mitochondrial respiration in primed hESCs has been underestimated for a long time, and has not been fully understood yet. Herein, we report that the adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production rate is comparable between mitochondrial respiration and glycolysis, suggesting that mitoATP may serve as one of the major sources of the ATP pool in primed hESCs. To further reveal the function of mitochondrial respiration, we deployed inducible CRISPRi method to inhibit OGDH expression with high efficiency, resulting in the TCA cycle blockade as well as the diminishment of mitochondrial respiration activity and total ATP level. Of note, OGDH deficiency led to the exit of primed pluripotency accompanied by cell death. Furthermore, the treatment with small molecule inhibitors to block electron transport chain (ETC) can phenocopy the loss-of-function of OGDH in hESCs. Therefore, genetic and pharmacological perturbations on mitochondrial respiration can impair the stemness of primed hESCs. Collectively, the current study unveils that OGDH acts as a key regulator to fine-tune the abundance of TCA cycle metabolites to ensure the mitochondrial respiration activity in primed hESCs, and highlights the pivotal roles of mitochondrial respiration in terms of ATP production and the maintenance of primed pluripotency. Our findings may provide insights into the linkage between pluripotent states and energy metabolism in hESCs.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>hESCs were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 x 10^5 cells/well.&nbsp;After 24 h, hESCs were cultured in the <strong>presence</strong> or <strong>absence</strong> of <strong>Dox</strong> for an additional 4 days to knockdown OGDH. Before measurement, the culture medium from the hESCs was removed using pipette and the cells were digested by ACCUTASE after washed. There are 6 independent samples for <strong>control</strong> group (-Dox) and <strong>treat</strong> group (+Dox 4 days), respectively. LC-MS/MS analysis was conducted by Applied Protein Technology (Shanghai, China).</p>	<p>Take out the samples which at -80 °C, add 1 mL methanol:acetonitrile aqueous solution (2:2:1, v/v), vortex for 60 s, sonicate at low temperature for 30 min (twice), place at -20 °C for 1 h to precipitate proteins and centrifuge at 14,000 rcf for 20 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was freeze-dried.</p>	<p>The samples were separated using an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system with a ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) column. Mobile phases were as follows: A solution is 10 mM ammonium acetate aqueous solution and B solution is acetonitrile. The samples were placed in an autosampler at 4 °C, the column temperature was 45 °C, the flow rate was 300 μL/min and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p><p><br></p><p>In the sample cohort, a QC sample was set at every interval of a certain number of experimental samples to detect and evaluate the stability and repeatability of the system; the standard mixture of energy metabolites was set in the sample cohort for the correction of chromatographic retention time.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry was performed on a AB&nbsp;SCIEX&nbsp;Triple&nbsp;Quad&nbsp;5500 in negative ion mode. The ESI source conditions were as follows: source temperature, 450 °C; ion Source Gas1 (Gas1), 45; Ion Source Gas2 (Gas2), 45; Curtain gas (CUR), 30; ionSapary Voltage Floating (ISVF), -4500 V. MRM is used - mode detects the transition to be analyzed.</p>	<p>Chromatographic peak areas and retention times were extracted using <strong>Multiquant</strong> software. The retention time was corrected by using the standard substance of energy substitute for metabolite identification. The following parameters were used: for peak picking, centWave, m/z = 25 ppm, peakwidth = c(10,60), prefilter = c(10,100); for peak grouping, bw = 5, mzwid = 0.025, minfrac = 0.5. Only the variables having more than 50% of the nonzero measurement values in the least group were kept.</p>	<p>Compound identification of metabolites by MS/MS spectra with an in house database established with available authentic standards. With targeted metabolomics, selective reaction/multiple reaction monitoring (SRM/MRM), with reference to standard substances, can detect and analyze specific metabolite groups in a targeted and specific manner, so as to obtain absolute quantitative results of target metabolites.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS4231
Study Title	Leptin increases hepatic triglyceride export via a vagal mechanism in humans
Study Description	Recombinant human leptin (metreleptin) reduces hepatic lipid content in patients with lipodystrophy and overweight patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and relative hypoleptinemia independent of its anorexic action. In rodents, leptin signaling in the brain increases very-low-density lipoprotein triglyceride (VLDL-TG) secretion and reduces hepatic lipid content via the vagus nerve. In this randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial (EudraCT Nr. 2017-003014-22), we tested whether a comparable mechanism regulates hepatic lipid metabolism in humans. A single metreleptin injection stimulated hepatic VLDL-TG secretion (primary outcome) and reduced hepatic lipid content in fasted, lean men (n = 13, age range 20-38 years) but failed to do so in metabolically healthy liver transplant recipients (n = 9, age range 26-62 years) who represent a model for hepatic denervation. In an independent cohort of lean men (n = 10, age range 23-31 years), vagal stimulation by modified sham feeding replicated the effects of metreleptin on VLDL-TG secretion. Therefore, we propose that leptin has anti-steatotic properties that are independent of food intake by stimulating hepatic VLDL-TG export via a brain-vagus-liver axis.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Study design</strong></p><p>The study was designed as a randomized, placebo-controlled, crossover balanced within-subject trial involving 3 separate cohorts: Cohort 1 was intended to examine the effects of a single s.c. metreleptin injection on hepatic VLDL1-TG secretion, hepatic lipid content and energy metabolism in healthy, male volunteers. In cohort 2 we investigated the effects of a vagus stimulation on VLDL-TG secretion in healthy, male volunteers using modified sham feeding (MSF). In cohort 3, we examined whether the effects of a single s.c. metreleptin injection on hepatic VLDL1-TG secretion observed in cohort 1 are abrogated in liver transplant recipients, a model of permanent surgical liver denervation. All experiments were conducted at the Department of Internal Medicine III, Division of Endocrinology and Metabolism at the Medical University of Vienna, Austria and were approved by both the local ethics committee (EK Nr.:1884/2017) and the Austrian Agency for Health and Food Safety. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study was pre-registered on the European Clinical Trial database (EudraCT Nr. 2017- 003014-22).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Inclusion and exclusion criteria</strong></p><p>Participants recruited for cohorts 1 and 2 were male, aged between 18 and 70 years with plasma triglycerides below 150 mg/dl at screening. In cohort 3, female liver transplant recipients were also eligible. Further inclusion criteria for cohort 3 were: liver transplantation more than 6 months ago, unchanged immunosuppressive medication for at least 4 weeks (unchanged drugs; minor dose adjustments were allowed) and plasma triglyceride levels below 700 mg/dl. Exclusion criteria for all participants included overt diabetes mellitus (HbA1c ≥ 6.5%), a history of recent acute pancreatitis (&lt; 6 months), serum triglycerides &gt; 700mg/dl, laboratory signs of liver damage (defined as aspartate aminotransferase or alanine aminotransferase 3x above upper normal limit), pregnancy, T-cell lymphoma, autoimmune disease, consummation of alcoholic beverages during the last 48 h, history of polyneuropathy or gastropareses (including regular use of gabapentin and/or metoclopramide or other common drugs to treat gastropareses or neuropathy), body-mass-index &gt; 35 kg/m², allergies against soy products, eggs, peanuts and a glomerular filtration rate &lt; 65 mL/min/1.73 m2 (calculated with the MDRD formula). For participation in cohort 1, hepatic lipid content ≥ 10 % water signal in the MRS, tendency to claustrophobia and/or metallic objects in or on the body were also exclusion criteria. All participants were instructed to eat a balanced diet (55% carbohydrate, 20% fat, 15% protein), not to exercise, and not to drink alcohol 3 days before each study day.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p><p>Each subject was overnight fasted and participated in both conditions (placebo 0.9% saline and metreleptin 0.1 mg/kg body weight) on two separate days at least 48 h apart. The sequence of s.c. placebo/metreleptin was randomized using randomizer.org and the subject was blinded to the treatment. Two MR sessions (approx. 1 h each) were performed, one at baseline, the second 3 hrs after injection of placebo/metreleptin followed by the intralipid infusion test.</p>	<p>20 μl of the sample was transferred into a glass vial, and 10 μl internal standard solution (SPLASH Lipidomix, Avanti Polar Lipids) and 140 μl methanol were added. After vortexing, 500 μl methyl tert-butyl ether was added and the mixture was incubated in a shaker for 10 min at room temperature. A phase separation was induced by adding 130 μl MS-grade water. After 10 min of incubation at room temperature, the samples were centrifuged at 1,000 x g for 10 min. A 450 μl aliquot of the upper phase was collected and dried under nitrogen. The samples were reconstituted in 200 μl methanol and used for liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) analysis.</p>	<p>The LC analysis was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Lipid separation was performed by reversed-phase chromatography using an Accucore C18, 2.6-μm, 150 × 2 mm (Thermo Fisher Scientific) analytical column at a column temperature of 35 °C. As mobile phase A, an acetonitrile/water (50/50, vol/vol) solution containing 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid was used. Mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile/isopropanol/water (10/88/2, vol/vol/vol) containing 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was set to 400 μl/min. A gradient of mobile phase B was applied to ensure optimal separation of the analysed lipid species.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometer was operated in ESI-positive and ESI-negative modes, using the following settings: capillary voltage of 3,500 V (positive) and 3,000 V (negative), vaporize temperature of 320 °C, ion transfer tube temperature of 285 °C, sheath gas of 60 AUs, aux gas of 20 AUs and sweep gas of 1 AU. The Orbitrap MS scan mode at 120,000 mass resolution was used for lipid detection. The scan range was set to 250–1,200 m/z for both positive and negative ionization mode. The AGC target was set to 2.0 x e^5 and the intensity threshold to 5.0 x e^3.</p><p><br></p><p>The data-dependent tandem MS scan using higher-energy collisional dissociation and collision-induced dissociation with fixed collision energy mode set to 35% with an inclusion list was used to obtain tandem MS spectra. The data analysis was performed using the <strong>TraceFinder 4.1 </strong>software (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Lipid-class-specific isotopically labelled internal standards signal was used to obtain semi-quantitative values for lipids.</p>	<p>No data transformation was performed in this study.</p>	<p>Mass spectra raw files were processed in <strong>Trace Finder 4.0 </strong>software (Theromo Scientific) with default settings. Peak picking and area under the peak were calculated by default automated algorithms of <strong>Trace Finder</strong> software. Peak areas were exported as <strong>Excel </strong>files. Relative quantification was performed by comparing peak areas of analytes vs internal standards. </p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS4294
Study Title	Untargeted LC-HRMS Based-Plasma Metabolomics Reveals 3-O-Methyldopa as a New Biomarker of Poor Prognosis in High-Risk Neuroblastoma
Study Description	Neuroblastoma (NB) is the most common extracranial malignant tumor in children. Although the survival rate of NB has improved over the years, the outcome of NB still remains poor for over 30% of cases. A more accurate risk stratification remains a key point in the study of NB and the availability of novel prognostic biomarkers of 'high-risk' at diagnosis could help improving patient stratification and predicting outcome. In this paper we show a biomarker discovery approach applied to the plasma of 172 NB patients. Plasma samples from a first cohort of NB patients and age-matched healthy controls were used for untargeted metabolomics analysis based on high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS). Differential expression analysis highlighted a number of metabolites annotated with a high degree of identification. Among them, 3-O-methyldopa (3-O-MD) was validated in a second cohort of NB patients using a targeted metabolite profiling approach and its prognostic potential was also analyzed by survival analysis on patients with 3 years follow-up. High expression of 3-O-MD was associated with worse prognosis in the subset of patients with stage M tumor (log-rank p < 0.05) and, among them, it was confirmed as a prognostic factor able to stratify high-risk patients older than 18 months. 3-O-MD might be thus considered as a novel prognostic biomarker of NB eligible to be included at diagnosis among catecholamine metabolite panels in prospective clinical studies. Further studies are warranted to exploit other potential biomarkers highlighted using our approach.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The study included a total of 172 patients with histologically confirmed neuroblatoma (<strong>NB</strong>). A written consent allowing the collection of samples and the use of clinical and nongenetic data for clinical research was signed by the patient's guardians. The study was approved by the Regional Ethical Committee (ANTECER_Neuroblastoma 16/09/2019). All NB patients were diagnosed between 2011 and 2018 in 29 institutions belonging to the Associazione Italiana di Emato-Oncologia Pediatrica. <strong>NB</strong> patients were assigned to the different risk groups according to the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) pretreatment risk schema and addressed to the different treatment protocols<strong>[1]</strong>. Plasma samples were obtained from peripheral venous blood collected in 3 mL EDTA K3-containing tubes, centrifuged at 4000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C and stored at -80 °C until analyzed. Leftover plasma samples of 27 outpatients after routine clinical analyses were used as healthy <strong>controls</strong>. Samples were divided in 2 sets: the<strong> first set (n=77)</strong> was used for biomarker discovery purposes while the second set (n=122) was used for biomarker validation. The <strong>first set of samples (n=77)</strong> was obtained from <strong>27 control</strong> subjects (CTR) and <strong>50 NB patients</strong> among which <strong>28 patients with localized NB (INRG L1, L2) or MS</strong> and <strong>22 with metastatic NB (INRG M)</strong> which have been sampled at 2 timepoints: at diagnosis and after induction chemotherapy.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong>&nbsp;Cohn, S.L., Pearson, A.D.J, London, W.B., Monclair, T., Ambros, P.F., Brodeur, G.M., Faldum, A.,&nbsp;Hero,&nbsp;B., Iehara, T.,&nbsp;Machin, D.,&nbsp;Mosseri,&nbsp;V., Simon, T., Garaventa,&nbsp;A., Castel,&nbsp;V., Matthay, K.K.,&nbsp;INRG Task Force. The International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) Classification System: An INRG Task Force Report. J Clin Oncol (2009) 27:289–97. doi: 10.1200/JCO.2008.16.6785</p>	<p>A <strong>50 µL</strong> aliquot of <strong>plasma</strong> was extracted adding 150 µL cold (-20&nbsp;°C) methanol containing an internal standard mixture of creatine (methyl-D3, 98%), vitamin B3 (D4, 98%), uracil (1,3-15N2, 98%), chenodeoxycholic acid (2,2,4,4-D4, 98%). After over-night protein precipitation, samples were centrifugated for 10 min at 14000 x g and 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and stored at -80 °C until analysis, when 50 µL were added to an equal volume of the internal standard (<strong>IS)</strong> used in this study (<strong>MSK-QC-KIT</strong>, Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc, USA), vortex mixed and 5 μL were injected in the UHPLC system.</p><p><br></p><p>Quality control <strong>(QC)</strong> samples were prepared by pooling together supernatants obtained from all the samples; external quality control <strong>(EQC)</strong> samples were prepared by pooling together plasma obtained from healthy adult volunteers extracted as described above.</p>	<p><strong>LC-HRMS</strong> analysis was carried out using a <strong>Vanquish Horizon UHPLC system</strong> (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Milan, Italy) as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.&nbsp;2 different chromatographic conditions were used: reversed phase <strong>(C18)</strong> and hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography<strong> (HILIC)</strong>. The reverse phase column <strong>ACQUITY BEH C18 (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm inner diameter x 100 mm, Waters) </strong>was eluted at a flow rate of 250&nbsp;µL/min with 99% of mobile phase A (0.1% formic acid in water) for 0.1&nbsp;min followed by a linear gradient to 100% of mobile phase B (0.1% formic acid in methanol) over 15&nbsp;min, then kept constant for 5&nbsp;min, brought back to the initial conditions in 0.5&nbsp;min, and maintained for 5&nbsp;min. The<strong> HILIC </strong>column<strong> ACQUITY BEH Amide (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm inner diameter x 150 mm, Waters)</strong> was eluted at a flow rate of 200&nbsp;µL/min with 90% of mobile phase B (acetonitrile) for 0.1&nbsp;min followed by a linear gradient to 70% of mobile phase A (H2O, 5 mM ammonium formate, pH 3) over 15&nbsp;min, then kept constant for 5&nbsp;min, brought back to the initial conditions in 0.5&nbsp;min, and maintained for 9&nbsp;min. The <strong>C18 </strong>and<strong> HILIC</strong> column compartment temperatures was set at 40 °C and 25 °C, respectively. The injection volume was 5&nbsp;µL.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lavarello, C., Barco, S., Bartolucci, M., Panfoli, I., Magi, E., Tripodi, Petretto,&nbsp;G.,&nbsp;Cangemi,&nbsp;G. 2021. Development of an Accurate Mass Retention Time Database for Untargeted Metabolomic Analysis and Its Application to Plasma and Urine Pediatric Samples. Molecules (2021) 26(14):4256. doi: 10.3390/molecules26144256</p>	<p><strong>﻿</strong>A <strong>Q-Exactive Plus Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer</strong> equipped with <strong>heated-ESI-II ion source</strong> was employed (Thermo Fisher Scientific). 2 analytical sequences (one in <strong>ESI+</strong> and one in<strong> ESI-</strong> ionization mode) were executed. The capillary temperature was set at 300&nbsp;°C. Nitrogen sheath gas was set at a flow rate of 35 arbitrary units (AU). Nitrogen auxiliary gas was set at a flow rate of 5 AU. The positive and negative spray voltage were 3.9 kV and 3.7 kV, respectively. Extracted samples were splitted into 4 injection batches, and processed combining the 4 different conditions mentioned above (2 different chromatographic conditions and 2 polarities). For each batch, analyses were performed using 2 different acquisition modes with an<strong> m/z range 70–1200</strong>. <strong>A full MS scan </strong>at 70000 resolution, an auto gain control target under 1 × 10^5&nbsp;and a maximum injection time of 100&nbsp;ms.&nbsp;The other acquisition mode was <strong>a full MS followed by a data-dependent MS2&nbsp;(DDA) scan</strong>, with a resolution of 17500, an auto gain control target of 1 × 10^5, a maximum injection time of 65&nbsp;ms, a loop count of top 5 peaks and an isolation window of m/z 1.2.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>All <strong>MS2</strong>&nbsp;spectra of the compounds were acquired at 3 collision energies, 20, 40 and 80&nbsp;eV. Moreover, for each risk group, we had plasma pools on which we maximized the metabolite identification with multiple acquisitions in full scan and MS/MS scan modes. The <strong>MS1</strong> mass range was divided into 10 mass ranges with a width of 50 m/z each on which the data dependent analysis was carried out. Plasma pools were also analyzed in<strong> DDA</strong> mode with priority fragmentation for the masses for which we previously built an in house database, Accurate Mass Retention Time (<strong>AMRT</strong>) library<strong>[1]</strong>, to obtain a level 1 metabolite identification.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lavarello, C., Barco, S., Bartolucci, M., Panfoli, I., Magi, E., Tripodi, Petretto,&nbsp;G.,&nbsp;Cangemi,&nbsp;G. 2021. Development of an Accurate Mass Retention Time Database for Untargeted Metabolomic Analysis and Its Application to Plasma and Urine Pediatric Samples. Molecules (2021) 26(14):4256. doi: 10.3390/molecules26144256</p>	<p><strong>MS1</strong> full-scan .RAW files have been converted into .ABF files using <a href='https://www.reifycs.com/AbfConverter/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>ABF converter</strong></a> and processed with <strong>MS-DIAL ver.4.24</strong> software<strong>[1]</strong> for deconvolution, peak picking, alignment and compound identification. Compounds, initially, were identified by matching retention times, accurate precursor masses, and MS/MS spectra with a previously published <strong>AMRT database[2]</strong> to provide a level 1 identification of metabolites<strong>[3]</strong>. For <strong>MS2</strong> spectra and precursor mass matching, the freely available library <strong>MassBank of North America (</strong><a href='https://mona.fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MoNA</strong></a><strong>) </strong>and <a href=' https://www.mzcloud.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>mzCloud</strong></a> were also used. The 4 master area tables obtained by <strong>MS-DIAL</strong>, generated combining the 2 different chromatographic methods and the 2 different MS polarities, were exported as a .txt file. The dataset, composed of 4 different sample sets and QCs was imported and statistically analyzed in <a href='https://maxquant.net/perseus' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Perseus</strong></a>. The QC variation coefficient was calculated and then used to exclude features variations &gt; 0.4. Subsequently, the expression data was log2 transformed and the QC group removed. Each feature was, therefore, filtered by row with a valid value of at least 70% for each group. The missing data were replaced by the <strong>QRILC method</strong>, which performs the imputation of left-censored missing data, using random draws from a truncated distribution with parameters estimated by quantile regression. <strong>Quantile normalization</strong> was instead used to normalize the intensity values which were further filtered with the MS/MS assigned category, considered necessary to continue the analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Tsugawa H, Cajka T, Kind T, Ma Y, Higgins B, Ikeda K, &nbsp;Kanazawa, M.,&nbsp; VanderGheynst,&nbsp;J., Fiehn, O.,&nbsp;Arita, M. MS-DIAL: Data-Independent MS/MS Deconvolution for Comprehensive Metabolome Analysis. Nat Methods (2015) 12:523–6. doi: 10.1038/nmeth.3393.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Lavarello, C., Barco, S., Bartolucci, M., Panfoli, I., Magi, E., Tripodi, Petretto,&nbsp;G.,&nbsp;Cangemi,&nbsp;G. 2021. Development of an Accurate Mass Retention Time Database for Untargeted Metabolomic Analysis and Its Application to Plasma and Urine Pediatric Samples. Molecules (2021) 26(14):4256. doi: 10.3390/molecules26144256</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Fiehn, O., Robertson, D., Griffin, J., vab der Werf, M., Nikolau, B., Morrison, N.,&nbsp;Sumner, L.W.,&nbsp;Goodacre,&nbsp;R., Hardy, N.W.,&nbsp;Taylor,&nbsp;C., Fostel,&nbsp;J., Kristal, B.,&nbsp;Kaddurah-Daouk, R., Mendes,&nbsp;P., van Ommen,&nbsp;B., Lindon, J.C.,&nbsp;Sansone, S-A. The Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI). Metabolomics (2007) 3:175–8. doi: 10.1007/s11306-007-0070-6.</p>	<p>For the annotations of <em>in silico</em> compounds, starting from ionic features with associated MS2 events, <strong>MS-FINDER ver.3.26</strong> was used. The <strong>MS-FINDER </strong>annotation matrix was merged with the quantitative matrix by employing the matching row by name<strong> </strong><a href='https://maxquant.net/perseus/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Perseus</strong></a> option and the Alignment ID was used as the unique classifier. A <strong>MS-FINDER</strong> Structure Rank score &gt; 5 and a <a href='https://www.mzcloud.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>mzCloud</strong></a> identification score &gt; 80 were used to select features that were worthy of statistical investigation.</p>

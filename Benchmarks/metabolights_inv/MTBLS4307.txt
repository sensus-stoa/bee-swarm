Study Identifier	MTBLS4307
Study Title	TFAM loss induces nuclear actin assembly upon mDia2 malonylation to promote liver cancer metastasis
Study Description	The mechanisms underlying cancer metastasis remain poorly understood. Here, we report that TFAM deficiency rapidly and stably induced spontaneous lung metastasis in mice with liver cancer. Interestingly, unexpected polymerization of nuclear actin was observed in TFAM-knockdown HCC cells when cytoskeleton was examined. Polymerization of nuclear actin is causally linked to the high-metastatic ability of HCC cells by modulating chromatin accessibility and coordinating the expression of genes associated with extracellular matrix remodeling, angiogenesis, and cell migration. Mechanistically, TFAM deficiency blocked the TCA cycle and increased the intracellular malonyl-CoA levels. Malonylation of mDia2, which drives actin assembly, promotes its nuclear translocation. Importantly, inhibition of malonyl-CoA production or nuclear actin polymerization significantly impeded the spread of HCC cells in mice. Moreover, TFAM was significantly downregulated in metastatic HCC tissues and was associated with overall survival and time to tumor recurrence of HCC patients. Taken together, our study connects mitochondria to the metastasis of human cancer via uncovered mitochondria-to-nucleus retrograde signaling, indicating that TFAM may serve as an effective target to block HCC metastasis.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cell culture and tissue collection</strong></p><p>Human HCC cells SNU-739 and <strong>SNU-368</strong> were purchased from the Korean Cell Line Bank (Seoul, Republic of Korea), JHH-2, HLF, and HLE were purchased from the Japanese Collection of Research Bioresources Cell Bank (Osaka, Japan), and LM3, MHCC-97H, Hep3B, Huh7, and Huh6 were maintained in our laboratory. Immortalized liver cells LO2 were purchased from the Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). The cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Waltham, MA) or Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM, Gibco). All HCC cell lines were authenticated using short tandem repeat DNA testing by the FMMU Centre for DNA Typing and were tested mycoplasma negative.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics analysis</strong></p><p>Targeted metabolomics was conducted by Shanghai Bioprofile Technology Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China) to quantitatively measure the metabolites associated with glycolysis and TCA in the mitochondrial and cytosolic fractions. The mitochondrial isolation kit (Cat. No. C3601, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), which enriched the mitochondrial fraction from the cytosolic fraction of mammalian cells, was used in the present study. Briefly, HCC cells were homogenized with a mitochondrial separation reagent and centrifuged at 1000 x g to remove the nucleus and plasma membrane fragments. The supernatant was transferred and centrifuged at 11,000 x g, and the supernatant was collected as the cytosolic fraction, while the Pellet is intact mitochondria. The mitochondrial protein COX4 and cytoplasmic tubulin were used as markers to identify the purity of each fraction. The collected mitochondria and cytoplasm were frozen in nitrogen for 15 min, stored at -80 °C and transported with sufficient dry ice. <strong>NC</strong> stands for SNU-368 <strong>control</strong> group, <strong>SH</strong> stands for SNU-368 <strong>TFAM knockdown</strong> group.</p>	<p>For cytoplasmic fluid samples, weigh 500 μL of each sample and freeze-dried. After freeze-drying, add 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v), let it stand for a period of time with ultrasound in an ice bath (1 h), centrifuge at 16,000 x g at 4 °C for 30 min, take 800 μL of supernatant and add 800 μL of supernatant to each sample. The same amount of internal standard Succinate_D6 and L-Glutamate_D5 were then vacuum dried. For mass spectrometry detection, 100 μL of acetonitrile:water solution (1:1, v/v) was added to reconstitute, centrifuged at 16,000 x g at 4 °C for 30 min, and the supernatant was injected for analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>For mitochondrial samples, add 1 mL of pre-cooled methanol:acetonitrile:water (2:2:1, v/v/v), let it stand for a period of time by ultrasonic in an ice bath (1 h), centrifuge at 16,000 x g at 4 °C for 30 min and remove the supernatant. The pellet was reconstituted with SDT for protein quantification. Take the same amount of cells according to the protein concentration. That is, 900 μL of supernatant from the sample with the smallest protein concentration as the standard, take the corresponding amount of supernatant from each sample, add the same amount of internal standard Succinate_D6 and L-Glutamate_D5 to each sample, and then vacuum dry. For mass spectrometry detection, 100 μL of acetonitrile:water solution (1:1, v/v) was added to reconstitute, centrifuged at 16,000 x g at 4°C for 30 min, and the supernatant was injected for analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>To ensure data quality for metabolic profiling, quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling aliquots of all samples that were representative of all samples under analysis and were used for data normalization. QC samples were prepared and analyzed using the same procedure as that used for the experimental samples in each batch.</p>	<p>LC-MS analysis was performed using a Shimadzu Nexera LC-30AD UHPLC system (Shimadzu, Japan) coupled to an AB SCIEX QTRAP 5500 mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, Framingham, MA). Mobile phase: Solution A was 5% acetonitrile in water, 10 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9, and solution B was 95% acetonitrile in water, 10 mM ammonium acetate, pH 9. The sample was reconstituted in 100 µL of 50% acetonitrile in water and placed in an autosampler at 4 °C, column temperature 40 °C, flow rate 300 µL/min and injection volume of 5 µL. The relevant liquid phase gradients were as follows: 0.0-2.0 min, B is maintained at 95%; 2.0-9.0 min, B changes linearly from 95 to 70%; 9.0-10.0 min, B changes linearly from 70 to 30%; 10.0-11.0 min, B is maintained at 30%; 11.0-11.5 min, B linearly changes from 30 to 95%; 11.5-15.0 min, B is maintained at 95%.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a QTRAP 5500 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX) in positive/negative ion mode. The ESI source parameters were as follows: For positive ion mode: Source Temperature, 550 °C; Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1), 40 psi; Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2), 50 psi; Curtain Gas (CUR), 35 psi; Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF), 5500 V; For negative ion mode: Source Temperature, 550 °C; Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1), 40 psi; Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2), 50 psi; Curtain Gas (CUR), 35 psi; Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF), -4500 V. Use MRM mode to detect the analyte transition. QC samples are inserted into the sample queue to monitor and evaluate the stability of the system and the reliability of experimental data.</p>	<p>Raw MRM data files were processed by peak finding, alignment, extraction and filtering using the <strong>MultiQuant</strong> software.</p>	<p>Metabolite identification was performed using energy metabolite standards to correct for retention times. Metabolite extracted ion peak areas were normalized by internal standards Succinate_D6 and L-Glutamate_D5.</p><p><br></p><p>Serial dilutions of each standard metabolite were prepared for LC-MS analysis. The peak area of each standard metabolite from the serially diluted reference standard solution was used to construct a calibration curve. A calibration curve with a correlation coefficient (R2) higher than 0.99 was acceptable. The quantitative concentrations of metabolites were calculated based on a calibration curve.</p>

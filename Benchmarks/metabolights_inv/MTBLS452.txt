STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS452
Study Title	Host-parasite co-metabolic activation of antitrypanosomal aminomethyl-benzoxaboroles
Study Description	Recent development of benzoxaborole-based chemistry gave rise to a collection of compounds with great potential in targeting diverse infectious diseases, including human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT), a devastating neglected tropical disease. However, further medicinal development is largely restricted by a lack of insight into mechanism of action (MoA) in pathogenic kinetoplastids. We adopted a multidisciplinary approach, combining a high-throughput forward genetic screen with functional group focused chemical biological, structural biology and biochemical analyses, to tackle the complex MoAs of benzoxaboroles in Trypanosoma brucei. We describe an oxidative enzymatic pathway composed of host semicarbazide-sensitive amine oxidase and a trypanosomal aldehyde dehydrogenase TbALDH3. Two sequential reactions through this pathway serve as the key underlying mechanism for activating a series of 4-aminomethylphenoxy-benzoxaboroles as potent trypanocides; the methylamine parental compounds as pro-drugs are transformed first into intermediate aldehyde metabolites, and further into the carboxylate metabolites as effective forms. Moreover, comparative biochemical and crystallographic analyses elucidated the catalytic specificity of TbALDH3 towards the benzaldehyde benzoxaborole metabolites as xenogeneic substrates. Overall, this work proposes a novel drug activation mechanism dependent on both host and parasite metabolism of primary amine containing molecules, which contributes a new perspective to our understanding of the benzoxaborole MoA, and could be further exploited to improve the therapeutic index of antimicrobial compounds.

Study Protocol Description	Bloodstream form cells were treated with 5 x IC50 of drug or DMSO (NDC) in HMI-9 for the time required for growth to be inhibited (8 hr for AN3057, 6 hr for AN5568). 1 x 10^8 cells were centrifuged at 16 x g for extraction.	Cells were extracted for 1 hr, shaking in 200 µL UPLC grade chloroform:methanol:water (1:3:1) on ice. Samples were centrifuged and stored at -80 °C.	Samples were analysed by hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (UltiMate 3000 RSLC; Thermo Fisher) with a 150 × 4.6 mm ZIC-pHILIC column running at 300 µl/min. Buffers consisted of (A) 20 mM ammonium carbonate in H2O, and (B) acetonitrile. The gradient ran from 20% A, 80% B to 80% A, 20% B in 15 min, followed by a wash at 95% A, 5% B for 4 min, and equilibration at 20% A, 80% B for 6 min.	Eluting compounds were detected from m/z 70-1050 using a Q Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) in positive and negative ionisation modes. Lock masses of 74 and 88 were used to calibrate the mass. The following voltages were used: S lens: 25V, Skimmer: 15V, Inject flatopole offset: 8V, Bent flatopole DC: 6V, MP2 and MP3 RF: 384V, Gate lens voltage: 5.88V, C-trap RF: 700V. Fragmentation was performed on batch 2 (AN5568) with an MS2 isolation width of 980 at 65 HCD.	Raw data were converted to mzXML using msconvert from Proteowizard. Data were filtered, aligned and annotated using the Orbiwarp algorithm in PiMP (http://polyomics.mvls.gla.ac.uk/).	PiMP (http://polyomics.mvls.gla.ac.uk/) was used with a retention time window of 30 s and a ppm error of 3 to match masses and retention times to authentic standards and to provide metabolite annotations. Fragmentation data were not uploaded to PiMP, but were used to provide MS1 level identifications using mzCloud along with isotope distributions observed in the raw data files and viewed using Thermo Xcalibur.

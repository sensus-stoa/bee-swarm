Study Identifier	MTBLS519
Study Title	The Kalanchoe genome provides insights into convergent evolution and building blocks of crassulacean acid metabolism
Study Description	Crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) is a water-use efficient adaptation of photosynthesis that has evolved independently many times in diverse lineages of flowering plants. We hypothesize that convergent evolution of protein sequence and temporal gene expression underpins the independent emergences of CAM from C3 photosynthesis. To test this hypothesis, we generated a de novo genome assembly and genome-wide transcript expression data for Kalanchoe fedtschenkoi, an obligate CAM species within the core eudicots with a relatively small genome (~260 Mb). Our comparative analyses identified signatures of convergence in protein sequence and re-scheduling of diel transcript expression of genes involved in nocturnal CO2 fixation, stomatal movement, heat tolerance, circadian clock and carbohydrate metabolism in K. fedtschenkoi and other CAM species in comparison with non-CAM species. These findings provide new insights into molecular convergence and building blocks of CAM and will facilitate CAM-into-C3 photosynthesis engineering to enhance water-use efficiency in crops.

Study Protocol Description	Kalanchoë fedtschenkoi ‘M2’ plants were purchased from Mass Spectrum Botanicals (Tampa, FL, USA). </p> Samples of mature leaves from 1 year old plants were collected in triplicate from leaves every 2 hr over a 24 hr time course under 12 hr light/12 hr dark (L/D; Diel time). Additional tissues were sampled in triplicate including roots, flowers, shoot tips plus young leaves, and stems and frozen in liquid nitrogen and ground using a mortar and pestle, and frozen at -80 °C until analyzed.	Approximately 100 mg FW of each sample was extracted twice overnight at room temperature with 2.5 ml 80% ethanol containing sorbitol (75 µl of a 1 mg/ml solution) as an internal standard. Following extraction, the samples were centrifuged at 4 °C for 20 min at 4500 rpm. 1 ml of the combined extract was dried under a stream of nitrogen. Dried extracts were silylated for 1 hr at 70 ºC to generate trimethylsilyl (TMS) derivatives for GC-MS analysis after 2 days.	1 µl aliquots were injected into an Agilent Technologies Inc. (Santa Clara, CA) 7890A GC fitted with an Rtx-5MS with Integra-guard (5% diphenyl/95% dimethyl polysiloxane) 30 m x 250 µm x 0.25 µm film thickness capillary column coupled to a Agilent 5975C inert XL gas chromatograph-mass spectrometer.	The Agilent 5975C inert XL MS was operated in EI mode (70 eV), 2.46 scans/s , with a scanning range of m/z 50-650 Da. The injector inlet was configured in splitless mode. The injection port, transfer line, MS Source and MS Quad temperatures were 250 °C, 300 °C, 230 °C and 150 °C respectively. The initial oven temperature was 50 °C, held for 2 min and programmed to increase 20 °C per min to 325 °C and held for 11 min.	The peaks were quantified by area integration using Chemstation and normalized to the quantity of internal standard used (tridecane), amount of sample extracted, and amount of sample injected. The Wiley Registry 10th Edition/NIST 14 combined with the runs of commercially available standards were used to identify metabolites of interest to be quantified.	Metabolite peaks were extracted use a key selected m/z to minimize co-eluting metabolites and then scaled back up to the total ion chromatogram using predetermined scaling factors. The peaks were quantified by area integration and normalized to the quantity of internal standard used (sorbitol), amount of sample extracted, and amount of sample injected. The Wiley Registry 10th Edition/NIST 14 combined with the runs of commercially available standards, and a user database were used to identify metabolites of interest to be quantified.

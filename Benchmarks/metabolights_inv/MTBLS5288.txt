Study Identifier	MTBLS5288
Study Title	Immunomodulatory fecal metabolites are associated with mortality in COVID-19 patients with respiratory failure
Study Description	Respiratory failure and mortality from COVID-19 result from virus- and inflammation-induced lung tissue damage. The intestinal microbiome and associated metabolites are implicated in immune responses to respiratory viral infections, however their impact on progression of severe COVID-19 remains unclear. We prospectively enrolled 71 patients with COVID-19 associated critical illness, collected fecal specimens within 3 days of medical intensive care unit admission, defined microbiome compositions by shotgun metagenomic sequencing, and quantified microbiota-derived metabolites (NCT 04552834). Of the 71 patients, 39 survived and 32 died. Mortality was associated with increased representation of Proteobacteria in the fecal microbiota and decreased concentrations of fecal secondary bile acids and desaminotyrosine (DAT). A microbiome metabolic profile (MMP) that accounts for fecal secondary bile acids and desaminotyrosine concentrations was independently associated with progression of respiratory failure leading to mechanical ventilation. Our findings demonstrate that fecal microbiota composition and microbiota-derived metabolite concentrations can predict the trajectory of respiratory function and death in patients with severe SARS-Cov-2 infection and suggest that the gut-lung axis plays an important role in the recovery from COVID-19.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Assays in this Study:</strong></p><p><strong>Assay 1 (CI-GC-Q-MS)</strong>: <strong>Pentafluorobenzyl bromide (PFBBr)</strong> derivatization for targeted metabolomics with <strong>(-)CI-GCMS</strong> (quantitative and normalized relative abundance)</p><p><strong>Assay 2 (UPLC-QTOF-MS): </strong>Bile acid targeted metabolomics <strong>(-)ESI-LC-QTOF</strong> (quantitative and normalized relative abundance)</p><p><strong>Assay 3 (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong> <strong>Desaminotyrosine (DAT)</strong> and <strong>indole-3-carboxaldehyde (I3C)</strong> targeted quantitation by <strong>(-)ESI-LC-QQQ</strong> (quantitative)</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Stool Collection:</strong></p><p>Freshly obtained stool samples were collected from patients in a sterile specimen vessel (University of Chicago, IRB 20-1102). Samples were immediately placed on ice or at 4 °C and then immediately aliquoted for downstream analyses.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Stool Splitting and Storage:</strong></p><p>Stool samples were then split into aliquots for downstream analyses (metabolomics, genomics). For each analyses, stool was split into a separate 1.5 ml Eppendorf tube. Mass requirements for analyses were: metabolomics, ~100 mg; genomics, ~250 mg (16S and shotgun sequencing). Minimum requirements for metabolomic analyses in this study was 20 mg/assay. The bulk stool sample and split Eppendorf tubes were kept on ice and the aliquots were immediately stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p>Extraction solvent (80% methanol spiked with internal standards and stored at -80 °C) was added to pre-weighed fecal samples at a ratio of 100 mg of material/ml of extraction solvent in beadruptor tubes (Fisherbrand; 15-340-154). Samples were homogenized at 4 °C on a Bead Mill 24 Homogenizer (Fisher; 15-340-163), set at 1.6 m/s with 6x 30-s cycles, 5 s off per cycle. Samples were then centrifuged at -10 °C, 20,000 x g for 15 min and the supernatant was used for subsequent metabolomic analysis. Supernatant was stored at -80 °C before being used in the analyses.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PFBBr targeted analyses (CI-GC-Q-MS) - Preparation for Pentafluorobenzyl Bromide (PFBBr) Derivatization:</strong></p><p>Samples were derivatized as previously described<strong>[1]</strong> with the following modifications. Briefly, the metabolite extract (100 µl) was added to 100 µl of 100 mM borate buffer (pH 10) (Thermo Fisher, 28341), 400 µl of 100 mM pentafluorobenzyl bromide (Millipore Sigma; 90257) in acetonitrile (Fisher; A955-4), and 400 µl of n-hexane (Acros Organics; 160780010) in a capped mass spectrometer autosampler vial (Microliter; 09-1200). Samples were heated in a thermomixer C (Eppendorf) to 65 °C for 1 h while shaking at 1300 rpm. After cooling to RT, samples were centrifuged at 4 °C, 2000 x g for 5 min, allowing phase separation. The hexanes phase (100 µl) (top layer) was transferred to an autosampler vial containing a glass insert and the vial was sealed. Another 100 µl of the hexanes phase was diluted with 900 µl of n-hexane in an autosampler vial.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bile acid targeted analyses (UPLC-QTOF-MS):</strong></p><p>For bile acid analysis, 75 µl of supernatant was added to pre-labeled microcentrifuge tubes and dried down completely under a nitrogen stream at 30 l/min (top) 1 l/min (bottom) at 30 °C (Biotage SPE Dry 96 Dual; 3579M). Samples were reconstituted in 50:50 water:methanol (750 µl) and added to a thermomixer C (Eppendorf) to resuspend analytes at 4 °C, 1000 rpm for 15 min with an infinite hold at 4 °C. Samples were then centrifuged at 4 °C, 20,000 x g for 15 min to remove insoluble debris, and the supernatant (700 µl) was transferred to fresh, pre-labeled mass spectrometry autosampler vials (Microliter; 09-1200) for LCMS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>DAT and I3C targeted analyses (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong></p><p>For analysis of desaminotyrosine and indole-3-carboxyaldehyde, 200 µl of supernatant was added to pre-labeled microcentrifuge tubes and dried down completely under a nitrogen stream at 30 l/min (top) 1 l/min (bottom) at 30 °C (Biotage SPE Dry 96 Dual; 3579M). Samples were reconstituted in 50:50 water:methanol (50 µl) and added to a thermomixer C (Eppendorf) to resuspend analytes at 4 °C, 1000 rpm for 15 min with an infinite hold at 4 °C. Samples were then centrifuged at 4 °C, 20,000 x g for 15 min to remove insoluble debris, and supernatant (40 µl) from each sample was transferred to a 96 well plate (Agilent; 5065-4402) for LCMS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Haak BW, Littmann ER, Chaubard JL, Pickard AJ, Fontana E, Adhi F, Gyaltshen Y, Ling L, Morjaria SM, Peled JU, van den Brink MR, Geyer AI, Cross JR, Pamer EG, Taur Y. Impact of gut colonization with butyrate-producing microbiota on respiratory viral infection following allo-HCT. Blood. 2018 Jun 28;131(26):2978-2986. doi:10.1182/blood-2018-01-828996. PMID:29674425.</p>	<p><strong>PFBBr targeted analyses (CI-GC-Q-MS):</strong></p><p>Concentrated and dilute samples were analyzed using a GC-MS (Agilent 8890/7890B GC system, Agilent 5977B MSD) with a 1 µl split injection (1:10 split ratio). Oven ramp parameters: 1 min hold at 60 °C, 25 °C/min up to 300 °C with a 2.5 min hold at 300 °C. Inlet temperature was 280 °C and transfer line was 310 °C. Using a HP-5ms ultra inert column (30 m x 0.25 mm, 0.25 µm; Agilent Technologies 19091S-433UI), methane as the reagent gas (99.999% pure) and 1 µl split injection (1:10 split ratio).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bile acid targeted analyses (UPLC-QTOF-MS):</strong></p><p>Samples were analyzed using a liquid chromatography system (Agilent 1290 Infinity II). Each sample (5 µl) was injected onto an XBridge BEH C18 Column (3.5 µm, 2.1 x 100 mm; Waters Corporation, PN) fitted with an XBridge BEH C18 guard (Waters Corporation, PN) at 45 °C. Elution started with 72% A (Water, 0.1% formic acid) and 28% B (Acetone, 0.1% formic acid) with a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min for 1 min and linearly increased to 33% B over 5 min, and then linearly increased to 65% B over 14 min. The flow rate was then increased to 0.6 ml/min and B was increased to 98% over 0.5 min, and these conditions were held constant for 3.5 min. Finally, re-equilibration at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min of 28% B was performed for 3 min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>DAT and I3C targeted analyses (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong></p><p>Samples were analyzed on an Agilent 1290 infinity II liquid chromatography system. Each sample (2 µl) was injected into an Acquity UPLC HSS PFP column, 1.8 µm, 2.1 x 100 mm (Waters; 186005967) equipped with an Acquity UPLC HSS PFP VanGuard Pre-column, 100 Å, 1.8 μm, 2.1 mm x 5 mm (Waters; 186005974) at 45 °C. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid in water and mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid in 95:5 acetonitrile:water. The flow rate was set to 0.5 ml/min starting at 0% B held constant for 3 min, then linearly increased to 50% over 5 min, then linearly increased to 95% B over 1 min, and held at 100% B for the next 3 min. Mobile phase B was then brought back down to 0% over 0.5 min and held at 0% for re-equilibration for 2.5 min.</p>	<p><strong>PFBBr targeted analyses (CI-GC-Q-MS):</strong></p><p>Concentrated and dilute samples were analyzed using a GC-MS (Agilent 8890/7890B GC system, Agilent 5977B MSD) operating in negative chemical ionization mode with methane used as the reagent gas (99.999% pure). The instrument was in Scan acquisition mode (m/z 50-600) with a solvent delay of 4.2 with Normal Scanning.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bile acid targeted analyses (UPLC-QTOF-MS):</strong></p><p>Samples were analyzed using a quadrupole time-of-flight (QTOF) mass spectrometer (Agilent 6546) (coupled with Agilent 1290 Infinity II) in negative mode, equipped with an Agilent Jet Stream Electrospray Ionization source. The electrospray ionization conditions were set with the capillary voltage at 3.5 kV, nozzle voltage at 2 kV, gas temperature 325 °C, gas flow 10 l/min, nebulizer 20 psig, sheath gas temperature 275 °C, and sheath gas flow 11 l/min. Data was acquired at a scan rate of 2 spectra/s and detection window set to 100-1700 m/z with continuous infusion of a reference mass (Agilent ESI TOF Biopolymer Analysis Reference Mix) for mass calibration.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>DAT and I3C targeted analyses (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong></p><p>Samples were analyzed using an Agilent 6470 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (coupled with Agilent 1290 Infinity II) in negative mode, equipped with an Agilent Jet Stream Electrospray Ionization source. The electrospray conditions were set with capillary voltage at 4 kV and nozzle voltage at 500 V, and Dynamic MRM was used with a cycle time of 500 ms. Sheath gas flow and temperature were 11 l/min and 275 °C, respectively. Transitions were monitored in negative mode, and the transitions for desaminotyrosine and indole-3-carboxaldehyde were 165.17 m/z to m/z 93.1 and 144.16 m/z to 116 m/z, respectively. DAT acquisition: Fragmentor (70), Collision Energy (32), Cell Accelerator Voltage (5). I3C acquisition: Fragmentor (90), Collision Energy (16), Cell Accelerator Voltage (5).</p>	<p>All internal standards were purchased from <strong>Cambridge Isotope Laboratories</strong>. Data was converted from Agilent proprietary files (<strong>.d</strong>) to open-source files (<strong>.mzML</strong>) with <strong>ProteoWizard MSconvert GUI</strong>. Settings for conversion: 32-bit Binary encoding precision, Write index, TPP compatibility, peak picking filter, and TitleMaker filter.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PFBBr targeted analyses (CI-GC-Q-MS):</strong></p><p>A 10-point calibration curve was prepared with acetate (100 mM), propionate (25 mM), butyrate (12.5 mM), and succinate (50 mM), with 9 subsequent 2x serial dilutions. Data analysis was performed using <strong>MassHunter Quantitative Analysis software (version B.10, Agilent Technologies)</strong> and confirmed by comparison to authentic standards. Normalized peak areas were calculated by dividing raw peak areas of targeted analytes by raw peak areas of spiked internal standards. Internal standards used for quantitation were (D3-acetate DLM-3126-25; D5-propionate DLM-1601-1; D7-butyrate DLM-7616-PK; D9-valerate DLM-572-5 DLM-572-5; D8-valine DLM-488-0.25; D6-succinate DLM-831-PK; D6-phenol DLM-370-PK). Relative abundance data were normalized to the average of D8-valine/D6-valerate or D7-proline/D6-phenol.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bile acid targeted analyses (UPLC-QTOF-MS):</strong></p><p>A 10-point calibration curve was used for quantitation of 8 bile acids (cholic acid, glycocholic acid, taurocholic acid, deoxycholic acid, lithocholic acid, isodeoxycholic acid, alloisolithocholic acid and 3-oxolithocholic acid). Bile acid data analysis was performed using <strong>MassHunter Profinder Analysis software (version B.10, Agilent Technologies)</strong>. Identification of endogenous compounds was confirmed by comparison to authentic standards. Normalized peak areas were calculated by dividing raw peak areas of targeted analytes by averaged raw peak areas of spiked internal standards.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>DAT and I3C targeted analyses (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong></p><p>An 11-point calibration curve (ranging from 0.98 nM to 1 mM) was prepared using desaminotyrosine and indole-3-carboxaldehyde standards. Data analysis was performed using <strong>MassHunter Quant software (version B.10, Agilent Technologies)</strong> and confirmed by comparison with authentic standards. Normalized peak areas were calculated by dividing raw peak areas of targeted analytes by averaged raw peak areas of spiked internal standards.</p>	<p>Each standard solution was individually derivatized, in the case of the PFBBr targeted analyses and analyzed to determine retention time, transition m/z, and extraction efficiency. Endogenous compounds of interest are then spiked into a bulk human plasma quality control sample. The plasma quality control sample is freshly extracted with every project and analyzed across all assays for the targeted compounds. Additional heavy isotope control compounds are spiked into the extraction solvent and evaluated in all samples to determine retention time drift, extraction efficiency, and instrument performance (i.e. column, source, leaks).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>PFBBr targeted analyses (CI-GC-Q-MS):</strong></p><p>Authentic standards were purchased for all targeted compounds and prepared at 1 mg/ml in methanol.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bile acid targeted analyses (UPLC-QTOF-MS):</strong></p><p>Authentic bile acid standards were prepared at 1 mg/ml in methanol (<strong>Avanti Polar Lipids and Sigma Aldrich</strong>). </p><p><br></p><p>Additionally, ions with epimer/isomers were purchased and analyzed individually and as mixtures to ensure accurate identification in biological samples. Compounds that were not resolvable with clear overlapping epimer/isomers were removed from analyses (if applicable). All identified compounds are reported at &lt; 10 ppm difference between the known m/z and the detected m/z.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>DAT and I3C targeted analyses (UPLC-TQ-MS):</strong></p><p>Stock solutions of desaminotyrosine and indole-3-carboxaldehyde (1 mg/ml in 50:50 water:methanol) were prepared using standards purchased from <strong>Sigma Aldrich</strong>.</p>

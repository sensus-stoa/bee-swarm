Study Identifier	MTBLS5383
Study Title	Apoplastic metabolomics reveals sugars as mesophyll messengers regulating guard cell ion transport under red light
Study Description	Here we sought to identify mesophyll messenger(s) that promote stomatal opening under red light. We first established a method to isolate leaf apoplastic fluids from Arabidopsis thaliana and Vicia faba and ascertained that the apoplastic fluid enhances red light-induced stomatal opening. We then applied multiple targeted and non-targeted metabolomics approaches, detecting 464 metabolites as components of the plant apoplastic metabolome – a rich resource for future studies. We identified several small and organic molecules as red light-stimulated apoplastic metabolites in both species and tested the impacts of these candidate mesophyll messengers on stomatal responses. We found that low concentrations of sugars, including sucrose, strongly enhanced stomatal opening under red light. The sucrose enhancement effect was diminished in the H+ ATPase null mutant, aha1-6, and phosphopeptide immunohistochemical assays demonstrated sugar upregulation of H+ ATPase phosphorylation in wild-type, which is correlated with increased ATPase activity. Our whole cell patch clamp assays revealed inhibition of anion efflux through slow anion channels by low (1-3 mM) sucrose concentrations. These regulatory impacts on ion transporters are explanatory of the observed enhancement of stomatal opening. This comprehensive study thus answers the decades-long question concerning the presence, identity, and mechanistic impact of the mesophyll messenger.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Apoplastic fluids were prepared as previously described24, with modifications. Plants were placed under red light at a intensity of 125 μmol m-2 s–1 for 3 h. Meanwhile, another set of plants was place in the dark for 3 hours, from which the control apoplastic fluids were harvested'. Leaves were detached immediately after either dark or red light treatment and briefly rinsed with deionized water. After immersing leaves in infiltration solution containing 5 mM KCl and 0.1 mM CaCl2, vacuum was applied for 75 sec and then slowly released. Excess fluid on the infiltrated leaves was then removed using Kimwipes (Kimberly-Clark Professional, USA). Apoplastic fluids were collected from leaves by centrifugation for 10 min at room temperature using a speed setting of 3 (approximately 1400 RPM; corresponding to approximately 200 g) on an IEC clinical tabletop centrifuge (International Equipment Co. Inc., USA) with 215 4-place central rotor. Previous research utilizing V. faba and other species has confirmed that at g forces &lt;1000, there is negligible cytoplasmic contamination of the apoplastic fluid25. For A. thaliana, 700 g is used to collect symplast-free extracellular vesicles26, and 1000 g was recently reported to be appropriate for collection of apoplastic fluid from this species27, confirming the suitability of our protocol. Our yield of apoplastic fluid was ~0.4 mL per g of A. thaliana leaves (fresh weight) and ~1.0 mL per g of V. faba leaves (fresh weight).&nbsp;</p>	<p>An UltiMateTM 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) was used to collect fine fractions of V. faba or A. thaliana apoplastic fluid. Lyophilized apoplastic fluid was first reconstituted with solvent A (0.1% trifluoride acid (TFA) in 10% methanol). Then 80 µL was injected and separated on a Gemini C18 column (particle size 5 µm, pore size 110Å, 250 mm length, 4.6 mm diameter, Phenomenex Inc., USA) using a gradient with solvent B (0.1% TFA in 90% methanol) at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min. The HPLC gradient started at 100% A for 5 min, followed by ramping from 0% B to 30% B over 40 min, then ramping to 100% B over 5 min, held at 100% B for 5 min, and then return to 0% B and held at 0% B for 5 min. HPLC fractions were collected , lyophilized, and reconsititued using solution containing 5 mM KCl and 0.1 mM CaCl2. Reconstitued fractions were then tested for enhancement effects on red light induced stomatal opening in V. faba epidermal peels as described above. Eluates between 6.5-9 min for A. thaliana apoplastic fluid and 6.5-10 min for V. faba apoplastic fluid were effective is in promoting&nbsp;stomatal opening under red light (Fig. 3) and thus were collected&nbsp;for metabolomics analysis.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p>	<p>Five biological replicates of each sample (i.e., dark treated A. thaliana (AtD), red light treated A. thaliana (AtRL), dark treated V. faba (VfD), and red light treated V. faba (VfRL)) fractions were analyzed on three different metabolomics platforms, GC-MS (targeted, GCMS-TQ8040, Shimadzu Corp., Japan) at RIKEN Center for Sustainable Resource Science (Japan), GC-MS (untargeted, Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, LECO Corp., USA) at University of California, Davis, and LC-MS (targeted, QTRAP 4000, Sciex, USA) at University of Florida.&nbsp;</p><p>For the targeted analysis on the GCMS-TQ8040, adonitol was first added to each sample as an internal standard, then all samples were lyophilized. Then 100 µL of methoxamine (MOX) reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) was added to each lyophilized sample, followed by overnight incubation at 30 °C on a shaker (1200 rpm). Then 50 µL of MSTFA (N-methyl-N-trimethylsilyltrifluoroacetamide) plus 1% TMCS (trimethylchlorosilane) (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) was added to each sample, followed by shaking at 1200 rpm at 37 °C for 30 min. After spin-down, the supernatant of each sample was transferred to a glass vial. One microliter of each sample was then injected into a Shimadzu GCMS-TQ8040 with a temperature gradient: 60 °C for 2 min then ramping to 330 °C at 15°C/min followed by 330 °C for 3 min.&nbsp;</p><p>For the targeted analysis on the QTRAP 4000, effective fractions were lyophilized and then redissolved in 10% methanol (with lidocaine as internal standard) and vortexed for 15 min at room temperature. After centrifugation at 4°C, 13,000 g for 15 min, the supernatants were transferred to glass vials for HPLC-MRM-MS analysis. Samples were separated on an Eclipse XDB-C18 column (diameter 4.6 mm, length 250 mm, particle size 5 μm) with 0.1% formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B. The HPLC gradient started at 1% solvent B for 5 min, followed by ramping from 1% B to 99.5% B over 41.5 min, then holding at 99.5% B for 4.5 min, and then returning to 1% B over 0.3 min and holding at 1% B for 8.7 min. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min. The mass spectrometer conditions were: 30 psi curtain gas, 50 psi GS1, 55 psi GS2, ion source voltage 4,500 V, with a TurboIon electrospray ionization (ESI) interface temperature of 350°C. MRM-transitions were distributed into different periods based on the compounds’ retention time to increase the number of compounds detected in a single run 69 (Geng et al., 2016).</p><p>For the untargeted analysis on the Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, samples were derivatized as previously described&nbsp;(Fiehn et al., 2008) and then injected (0.5 µL of each sample) into an Agilent 6890 gas chromatograph (Agilent Technologies, USA). Separation was performed on an Rtx®-5Sil MS column (length 30 m, internal diameter 0.25 mm, 0.25 µm&nbsp;film made of 95% dimethyl/5% diphenylpolysiloxane; Restek Corp., USA) with a 20 min temperature gradient: 50 °C for 1 min then ramping to 330 °C at 20 °C/min followed by 330 °C for 5 min. The transfer line temperature between gas chromatograph and mass spectrometer (Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, LECO Corp., USA) was set to 230 °C. Mass spectra were acquired from 80-500 Da at 17 spectra per second at -70 eV ionization energy and 1800 V detector voltage with ion source at 250°C. Data processing and metabolite identification were performed as previously described70.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Five biological replicates of each sample (i.e., dark treated A. thaliana (AtD), red light treated A. thaliana (AtRL), dark treated V. faba (VfD), and red light treated V. faba (VfRL)) fractions were analyzed on three different metabolomics platforms, GC-MS (targeted, GCMS-TQ8040, Shimadzu Corp., Japan) at RIKEN Center for Sustainable Resource Science (Japan), GC-MS (untargeted, Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, LECO Corp., USA) at University of California, Davis, and LC-MS (targeted, QTRAP 4000, Sciex, USA) at University of Florida.&nbsp;</p><p>For the targeted analysis on the GCMS-TQ8040, adonitol was first added to each sample as an internal standard, then all samples were lyophilized. Then 100 µL of methoxamine (MOX) reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) was added to each lyophilized sample, followed by overnight incubation at 30 °C on a shaker (1200 rpm). Then 50 µL of MSTFA (N-methyl-N-trimethylsilyltrifluoroacetamide) plus 1% TMCS (trimethylchlorosilane) (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) was added to each sample, followed by shaking at 1200 rpm at 37 °C for 30 min. After spin-down, the supernatant of each sample was transferred to a glass vial. One microliter of each sample was then injected into a Shimadzu GCMS-TQ8040 with a temperature gradient: 60 °C for 2 min then ramping to 330 °C at 15°C/min followed by 330 °C for 3 min.&nbsp;</p><p>For the targeted analysis on the QTRAP 4000, effective fractions were lyophilized and then redissolved in 10% methanol (with lidocaine as internal standard) and vortexed for 15 min at room temperature. After centrifugation at 4°C, 13,000 g for 15 min, the supernatants were transferred to glass vials for HPLC-MRM-MS analysis. Samples were separated on an Eclipse XDB-C18 column (diameter 4.6 mm, length 250 mm, particle size 5 μm) with 0.1% formic acid in water as solvent A and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile as solvent B. The HPLC gradient started at 1% solvent B for 5 min, followed by ramping from 1% B to 99.5% B over 41.5 min, then holding at 99.5% B for 4.5 min, and then returning to 1% B over 0.3 min and holding at 1% B for 8.7 min. The flow rate was 0.5 mL/min. The mass spectrometer conditions were: 30 psi curtain gas, 50 psi GS1, 55 psi GS2, ion source voltage 4,500 V, with a TurboIon electrospray ionization (ESI) interface temperature of 350°C. MRM-transitions were distributed into different periods based on the compounds’ retention time to increase the number of compounds detected in a single run 69 (Geng et al., 2016).</p><p>For the untargeted analysis on the Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, samples were derivatized as previously described&nbsp;(Fiehn et al., 2008) and then injected (0.5 µL of each sample) into an Agilent 6890 gas chromatograph (Agilent Technologies, USA). Separation was performed on an Rtx®-5Sil MS column (length 30 m, internal diameter 0.25 mm, 0.25 µm&nbsp;film made of 95% dimethyl/5% diphenylpolysiloxane; Restek Corp., USA) with a 20 min temperature gradient: 50 °C for 1 min then ramping to 330 °C at 20 °C/min followed by 330 °C for 5 min. The transfer line temperature between gas chromatograph and mass spectrometer (Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS, LECO Corp., USA) was set to 230 °C. Mass spectra were acquired from 80-500 Da at 17 spectra per second at -70 eV ionization energy and 1800 V detector voltage with ion source at 250°C. Data processing and metabolite identification were performed as previously described70.</p><p><br></p>	<p>For analyses on targeted metabolomics platforms (QTRAP4000 and GCMS-TQ8040), metabolites were identified by paired mass/charge (m/z) ratios of the precursor ion and a selected product ion along with the chromatographic retention time, as acquired from analysis of authentic compounds (standards). For quantification of QTRAP4000 data, peak area of each metabolite from each sample was first normalized to the internal standard. For quantification of GCMS-TQ8040 data, the peak area of each metabolite from each sample was first normalized by the LOWESS/Spline normalization algorithm with a pooled QC sample using MRMPROBS71. For analysis on the untargeted metabolomics platform (Pegasus® IV GC/TOF-MS), peak height was reported because peak heights are more precise for low abundance metabolites than peak areas, due to the larger influence of baseline determinations on areas compared to peak heights72. Peak heights were log2 transformed for statistical analysis. Fold change was calculated using average value of each group (dark and red light) and Student’s t test was performed to assess the statistical significance of the difference between the two groups. Metabolites with a fold change &gt;1.2 or &lt;0.8 are defined as significantly changed metabolites between dark and red light treatments. </p><p><br></p>	<p>According to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative60, 61, our targeted LC-MS analyses are confident “level 1” identification based on fragmentation spectra from authentic standards. Our GCMS-TQ8040 and GC/TOF-MS identifications are based on fragmentation pattern-matching against in-house mass spectral libraries at RIKEN, and at the West Coast Metabolomics Center, respectively, and thus can be considered as “level 2” identification.&nbsp;</p>

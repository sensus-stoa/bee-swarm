Study Identifier	MTBLS5401
Study Title	Bacteria regulate aging physiology in microalgae via induced vesicle production and the methionine cycle (Metabolomics profiling of FACS purified EVs)
Study Description	<p>In annually reoccurring patterns, microalgae form blooms that persist and decline thereby contributing massively to global biogeochemical cycles. The decline of blooms is mainly caused by nutrient limitation and goes ahead with the aging of individual algal cells. Nutrient intake can re-initiate proliferation, but the processes involved are poorly understood. By investigating the bloom-forming diatom Coscinodiscus radiatus, we demonstrate how algae recover after nutrient influx. The rejuvenation physiology of the algae is characterized by metabolomic re-organization and the formation of extracellular vesicles. Regulated pathways mediating aging are centered around the methionine cycle in C. radiatus. Vesicles shuttle reactive oxygen species, oxylipins and other harmful metabolites out of the old cells, thereby re-enabling their proliferation. Metabolic processes involved in aging and vesicle production are modulated by bacteria. Using chemical signaling bacteria can trigger vesicle production thereby releasing organic nutrients for their growth and supporting algal growth as well.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics profiling of FACS purified EVs</strong>&nbsp;is reported in the current study&nbsp;<a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS5401' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS5401</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>Metabolomics analysis</strong>&nbsp;is reported in&nbsp;<a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS5368' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS5368</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Strains, media and cultivation</strong></p><p>The diatom Coscinodiscus radiatus was obtained from the Roscoff culture collection RCC 7277 (https://roscoff-culture-collection.org/rcc-strain-details/7277). The bacteria Mameliella sp. CS4 and Marinobacter sp. CS1 were isolated in our lab as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>. C. radiatus was cultivated in sterile Guillard’s (f/2) enrichment medium (G9903, Sigma-Aldrich) prepared with natural seawater (ATI, Helgoland, Germany). To optimize the growth of diatom, silicate was added to reach the final concentration of 400 μM and the medium was noted as f/2+Si. Depending on the volume, cultures were maintained in tissue culture (TC) Flasks (T175 for 150 mL cultures, T25 for 20 mL cultures Sarstedt, Numbrecht, Germany). 300 μL cultures were grown in 48 well plates and 100 μL cultures were grown in 96 well plates (Sarstedt, Numbrecht, Germany). Cultures were maintained at 13 °C with a light: dark cycle of 15:9 h and light intensity of 25 μmol/m^2/s for running experiments and 20 μmol/m^2/s for storage.</p><p><br></p><p>For the preparation of axenic cultures, we applied a previously used method to minimize the bacterial community from C. radiatus<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, the cells were washed with 20 μg/mL Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich) on a filter (40 μm pore size, pluriSelect, Leipzig, Germany) followed by treatment with antibiotics, 50 μg/mL kanamycin and 20 μg/mL ciprofloxacin overnight. After another filter process, the antibiotics were removed by repeated centrifugation and washing with excess fresh f/2+Si medium. Cells were re-suspended in a sterile Falcon tube. Young cells were stored under low light intensity in an incubator and used within a week. Old cells were used for the experiment on the same day of the filtration process. No bacteria were detectable under microscope and after plating out on marine broth (MB, Sigma-Aldrich, Taufkirchen, Germany) agar plates (1.5% agar). Note: Since the diatoms are closely associated with bacteria the cultures are strongly reduced in bacteria after the treatment but not necessarily entirely axenic.</p><p><br></p><p>Bacterial cultures were prepared from single colonies picked from marine broth (MB, Sigma-Aldrich, Taufkirchen, Germany) agar plates (1.5% agar). Picked colonies were transferred into 3 mL liquid MB medium in 10 mL sterile tubes and grown at 28 °C with 150 rpm shaking. When the cultures reached the late exponential phase at an OD600 0.4-0.7, 30 μL were transferred into 3 mL fresh MB. When the cultures reached an OD600=0.4-0.7, they were harvested by centrifugation at room temperature, washed 2 times with f/2+Si medium to remove the MB medium and resuspended in f/2+Si medium to reach an OD600=0.4 (about 5 x 10^6 cells/mL).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics profiling of FACS purified EVs</strong></p><p>The experimental work flow is depicted in <strong>Extended Data Figure 8A</strong> in the paper associated with this study. Old cells were used for EV production 2 days after inoculation into fresh medium. Diatom cells were removed by filtration on 40 μm filters. The filtrates containing EVs were left for settlement of the vesicles for 30 min. Then, the vesicles were concentrated. The enriched EV samples were stained with the ROS probe CDCFDA for 15 min in the dark before fluorescence activated cell sorting (FACS) purified EVs FACS analysis. Vesicles were analyzed and sorted with a BD FACSAria Fusion (BD Biosciences , San Jose, CA, USA). The solution containing 3.5% sea-salt (Instant Ocean, Aquarium Systems, Mentor, OH, US) was used as FACS running buffer. Sorting temperature was kept at room temperature. Vesicles were sorted into 5 mL FACS-tubes (Falcon). Chlorophyll a fluorescence was detected in the BV650 channel. The ROS-dye CDCFDA was detected in the FITC channel. As the single staining for ROS-dye CDCFDA could not be created, compensation was not performed. [GP1]&nbsp;[YD2]&nbsp;To control for media contamination in the sorted vesicle-containing droplets, Accu-drop beads (BD) were diluted into the corresponding supernatant and sorted as above. The total sorted events for each sample are listed in <strong>Table S2</strong>. FACS purified EV and control samples were dried in a Vacufuge plus vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf) for 2 h. The dried samples were dissolved in 300 μL methanol and centrifuged at 16,100 x g for 10 min. The supernatant of 10 μL was loaded on the LC-MS for untargeted metabolomics profiling as described above.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Deng Y, Mauri M, Vallet M, Staudinger M, Allen RJ, Pohnert G. Dynamic Diatom-Bacteria Consortia in Synthetic Plankton Communities. Appl Environ Microbiol. 2022 Nov 22;88(22):e0161922. doi:10.1128/aem.01619-22. Epub 2022 Oct 27. PMID:36300970.</p>	<p>After sorting, samples were dried in a Vacufuge plus vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf) overnight. The dried samples were dissolved in 300 µL methanol and transferred into 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes for centrifugation at 16,100 x g for 10 min. The supernatant was transferred into the insert of a glass vial and submitted to high-resolution LC-MS analysis. And 5 µL of each sample were combined to give the quality control QC pools.</p>	<p>In total 10 µL sample were injected into the UHPLC-HR-MS (UltiMate 3000 UHPLC Dionex, Sunnyval, CA, USA) connected to an Accucore C-18 column (100 x 2.1 mm, 2.6 µm; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) coupled to a Q-Exactive Plus Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany). The LC separation was performed in a 12 min duration time, starting with 100% of A: aqueous phase (2% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid in water) and increasing with the B: acetonitrile phase (100% acetonitrile) from 0.2 to 8.0 min until reaching 100%. This was held for 3 min before switching back to 100% of water phase and equilibration for 1 min. The flow rate is 0.4 mL/min. The column oven was control at 25 °C.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometry (Q-Exactive Plus Orbitrap) was conducted in positive and negative modes with a scan range from 80 to 1200 m/z at a peak resolution of 70,000, heated electrospray ionization was used to generate molecular ions (ionization). AGC target was set to 3 x 10^6&nbsp;and maximum ion time was set to 200 ms. The MS/MS spectra of precursor ions, selected with an inclusion list, was obtained from cell extracts with the above-mentioned parameters, and within an isolation window of&nbsp;m/z&nbsp;0.4 at a peak resolution of 280,000 (NCE 15, 35, 45).</p>	<p>The peak picking, deconvolution and identification of the metabolites from raw data were performed by Compound Discoverer&nbsp;software version 3.3.0.550 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Mass tolerance for MS identification was 5 ppm, retention time tolerance was 0.2 min, minimum MS peak intensity was 1 x 10^5, and intensity tolerance for isotope search was 50%. Relative standard deviation value was set to 50%. The compound list was exported as.csv file.</p>	<p>The peaks of methionine-related metabolites and lipid-related metabolites were selected to extract manually. The identity of selected ions was confirmed by comparison of retention time and MS2&nbsp;spectra between the QC pool and analytical standards.</p>

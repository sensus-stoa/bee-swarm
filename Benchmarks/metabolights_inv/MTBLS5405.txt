Study Identifier	MTBLS5405
Study Title	2E,4E-decadienoic acid, a novel anti-oomycete agent from co-culture of Bacillus subtilis and Trichoderma asperellum
Study Description	<p><em>Phytophthora nicotianae</em> is an oomycete pathogen of global significance threatening many important crops. It is mainly controlled by chemosynthetic fungicides, which endangers ecosystem and human health; thus, there is an urgent need to explore alternatives for these fungicides. In this study, a new anti-oomycete aliphatic compound was obtained through co-culture of <em>Bacillus subtilis</em> and <em>Trichoderma asperellum</em>. The action mode of this natural product on <em>P. nicotianae</em> was preliminarily explored by using transcriptome, metabolome and physiological index detection.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Strains</strong></p><p><em>Phytophthora nicotianae</em> (<em>P. nicotianae</em>) JM01 (PRJNA389504) was isolated from tobacco infected with tobacco black shank, cultivated on oat agar (OA) medium at 28 °C<strong>[1]</strong>, and deposited at the Marine Agriculture Research Center, Tobacco Research Institute of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Qingdao, China. <em>T. asperellum</em> HG1 (CGMCC NO.19276) and <em>B. subtilis</em> Tpb55 (CGMCC NO. 2843) were isolated from Hainan mangrove samples and the surface of tobacco leaves, respectively, being cultured on potato dextrose agar (PDA) medium and Luria-Bertani agar (LA) medium (Qingdao Hope Bio-Technology Co., Ltd, Qingdao, China) at 28 °C for further study<strong>[2]</strong>. <em>T. asperellum</em> HG1 and <em>B. subtilis</em> Tpb55 were deposited at the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC), Beijing, China.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Co-culture conditions</strong></p><p>In this study, liquid nutrient broth (N8300, Solarbio Science &amp; Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) was utilized for co-culturing. The spores of <em>T. asperellum</em> HG1 were obtained by washing the 5-day-old culture with sterile water, followed by filteration through degreasing cotton. The number of spores was counted using an automatic cell counter (Countstar IC1000, Shanghai, China), and diluted to 1 x 10^6 spores/mL as spore suspension. <em>B. subtilis</em> Tpb55 was transferred from 2-day-old culture in LA medium into NB broth and incubated overnight (28 °C, 180 rpm/min), following which the number of cells was measured using a microplate reader (SP-Max 2300A2, Flash, China) and diluted to 1 x 10^6 CFU/mL as a seed liquid. The spore suspension of <em>T. asperellum</em> HG1 (5 mL, 10^6/mL) was inoculated in NB medium (250 mL/500 mL bottle) and cultured for 24 h (28 °C and 180 rpm), followed by inoculation with <em>B. subtilis</em> Tpb55 (0.5 mL, 10^6/mL); the fermentation was continued for 7 days.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Isolation and identification of DDA</strong></p><p>The fermentation filtrate from the co-culture of <em>T. asperellum</em> HG1 and <em>B. subtilis</em> Tpb55 was extracted by EtOAc 3 times, then evaporated under vacuum to obtain the final extract (9.4 g). The extract was separated using a Sephadex LH-20 chromatography column (CC) (CH2Cl2/MeOH, v/v, 1:1) after being eluted on silica gel CC with 10% EtOAc-petroleum ether. It was further purified by HPLC (Waters e2695-2998, Waters, Milford, MA, USA) using MeOH-H2O (75%) with 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) to yield DDA (3.2 mg). The DDA structure was identified using 1H and 13C NMR (DD2 500 MHz NMR spectrometer, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), and ESI (Micromass Q-TOF spectrometer, Waters) spectra and compared to the literature and the standard compound purchased from Toronto Research Chemicals Inc. (North York, Ontario, Canada). 1H NMR (DMSO-d6, 500 MHz) δ 12.16 (s), 7.14 (1H, dd, J = 15.5, 10.0 Hz), 6.22 (1H, dd, J = 15.5, 10.0 Hz), 6.17–6.27 (1H, m), 5.78 (1H, d, J = 15.5 Hz), 2.13 (2H, q, J = 7.0 Hz), 1.39 (2H, dt, J = 14.5, 7.5 Hz), 1.23–1.30 (4H, m), 0.86 (3H, t, J = 7.0 Hz); 13C NMR (DMSO-d6, 125 MHz) δ 167.7 (C), 144.6 (CH), 144.2 (CH), 128.3 (CH), 120.1 (CH), 32.3 (CH2), 30.9 (CH2), 27.9 (CH2), 21.9 (CH2), 13.9 (CH3); ESIMS m/z 169.11 (M+H)+.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Synthesis and purification of DDA</strong></p><p>Jones reagent (8.5 mL, 1.5 eq, J&amp;K Scientific Ltd.) was added dropwise to a stirred solution of 2E,4E-deca-2,4-dienal (2 mL, 1.0 eq, J&amp;K Scientific Ltd., Shanghai, China) with 25 mL acetone at 0 °C. The reaction mixture was agitated at 0 °C for 10 min before moved to room temperature (25 °C) for stirring overnight, followed by filteration to remove the residue and rinsing with 3x 5 mL acetone. The filtrate was dried and redissolved in 20 mL EtOAc. The organic solution was washed with distilled water (3x 10 mL); subsequently, 4 M NaOH was added to the EtOAc phase until a pH of 8-9 was reached and extracted with 10 mL distilled water (x3). Hydrochloric acid (4 M) was added to the combined aqueous phases until the pH reached 1-2. The solution was extracted with 10 mL EtOAc (x3), and the mixed organic extracts were washed with saturated NaCl solution (3x 20 mL) and then condensed in a vacuum to yield residues. The reaction was monitored by TLC using Yantai Jiangyou (Yantai, China) GF254 silica gel plates. The obtained residues were initially purified by silica gel CC with gradient elution of EtOAc-petroleum ether from 10% to 100%, then separated on an ODS column eluted with 30-90% MeOH:H2O and purified on Sephadex LH-20 CC (CH2Cl2/MeOH, v/v, 1/1), followed by purification by HPLC using 70% MeOH–H2O + 0.1% TFA to yield DDA. The instrument data were identical to those of the standard compound.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>The <strong>mycelia</strong> of <strong><em>P. nicotianae</em></strong> was (1.5 g) were treated with 35 μg/mL of <strong>2E,4E-decadienoic acid</strong> (<strong>DDA</strong>) in oat liquid medium (30 mL) at 28 °C for 24 h.&nbsp;Collected mycelia were washed thrice with PBS, and frozen with liquid nitrogen for GC-MS analysis. Mycelia without DDA treatment were used as <strong>controls</strong>. This experiment was performed with 6 biological replicates.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Zhang C, Gao J, Han T, Tian X, Wang F. Integrated control of tobacco black shank by combined use of riboflavin and Bacillus subtilis strain Tpb55. BioControl. 2017 Dec;62(6):835-845.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Han T, You C, Zhang L, Feng C, Zhang C, Wang J, Kong F. Biocontrol potential of antagonist Bacillus subtilis Tpb55 against tobacco black shank. BioControl. 2016 Apr;61(2):195-205.</p>	<p>The extraction method of metabolites was slightly modified<strong>[1]</strong>. A total of 50 mg mycelia was mixed with a 0.5 mL methanol:water solution (MeOH:H2O, v/v, 4:1), containing l-2-chloro-phenylalanine internal standard at 0.02 mg/mL, followed by grinding in a -20 °C grinder with chloroform (50 Hz, 3 min). Samples were then extracted using an ultrasonic water bath and centrifuged (13000 RCF, 4 °C, 15 min). The supernatants were transferred into glass derivatization bottles and dried with nitrogen. A total of 80 μL methoxyamine pyridine hydrochloride solution (15 mg/mL) was added to the bottles, which were vortexed for 2 min, followed by oximation in shaking incubator (37 °C, 90 min). Subsequently, the samples were mixed with 80 μL of bis(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide (BSTFA, containing 1% TMCs) derivatization reagent, and reacted at 70 °C for 60 min after being vortexed for 2 min. Subsequently, the samples were placed at room temperature for 30 min and used for further analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Jin X, Guo L, Jin B, Zhu S, Mei X, Wu J, Liu T, He X. Inhibitory mechanism of 6-Pentyl-2H-pyran-2-one secreted by Trichoderma atroviride T2 against Cylindrocarpon destructans. Pestic Biochem Physiol. 2020 Nov;170:104683. doi:10.1016/j.pestbp.2020.104683. Epub 2020 Aug 20. PMID:32980051.</p>	<p>Detection was performed using a gas chromatographer-mass spectrometer (8890B-5977B, Agilent, USA). After derivatization, the samples were injected into the GC-MS system in split mode for analysis. The injection volume is 1 µL and the split ratio is 5:1. The sample was separated by passing through DB-5MS capillary column (40 M x 0.25 mm, 0.25 µm, Agilent 122-5532G) and detected by mass spectrometry. The temperature of the injection port is 260 °C; the carrier gas is high-purity helium; the carrier gas flow rate is 1 mL/min; the septum purge flow rate is 3 mL/min; and the solvent delay is 5.5 min. Temperature rise procedure: the initial temperature was 60 °C and maintained for 0.5 min, then the temperature was raised to 310 °C at 8 °C/min and maintained for 6 min.</p>	<p>For the MS analysis (8890B-5977B, Agilent, USA), an electron bombardment ion source (EI) was used with a transmission line temperature of 310 °C, an ion source temperature of 230 °C, a quadrupole temperature of 150 °C, an electron energy of 70 eV. Data acquisition was conducted in full scan mode with a range of m/z 50-500 and a scan interval of 3.2 s.</p>	<p>GC-MS data were processed with <strong>MassHunter Workstation Quantitative Analysis</strong> (v10.0.707.0) to extract raw peaks, flter and calibrate data baselines, and align peaks. Then the deconvolution analysis, peak identifcation and integration of peak areas could be conducted. The resulting matrix detected at least 80%&nbsp;in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, minimum metabolite values were imputed for specific samples in which the metabolite levels fell below the lower limit of quantitation and each metabolic features were normalized by sum. The internal standard was used for data QC (reproducibility), metabolic features which the relative standard deviation (RSD) of QC &gt; 30% were discarded. Following normalization procedures and imputation, statistical analysis was performed on log transformed data to identify significant differences in metabolite levels between comparable groups. The data were further analyzed using the <strong>Majorbio Cloud</strong> Platform.</p>	<p>Mass spectra of these metabolic features were identified by using <strong>Fiehn</strong> database.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS608
Study Title	Metabolic characterization of directly reprogrammed renal tubular epithelial cells (iRECs)
Study Description	Fibroblasts can be directly reprogrammed to induced renal tubular epithelial cells (iRECs) using four transcription factors. These engineered cells may be used for disease modeling, cell replacement therapy or drug and toxicity testing. Direct reprogramming induces drastic changes in the transcriptional landscape, protein expression, morphological and functional properties of cells. However, how the metabolome is changed by reprogramming and to what degree it resembles the target cell type remains unknown. Using untargeted gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) and targeted liquid chromatography-MS, we characterized the metabolome of mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs), iRECs, mIMCD-3 cells, and whole kidneys. Metabolic fingerprinting can distinguish each cell type reliably, revealing iRECs are most similar to mIMCD-3 cells and clearly separate from MEFs used for reprogramming. Treatment with the cytotoxic drug cisplatin induced typical changes in the metabolic profile of iRECs commonly occurring in acute renal injury. Interestingly, metabolites in the medium of iRECs, but not of mIMCD-3 cells or fibroblast could distinguish treated and non-treated cells by cluster analysis. In conclusion, direct reprogramming of fibroblasts into renal tubular epithelial cells strongly influences the metabolome of engineered cells, suggesting that metabolic profiling may aid in establishing iRECs as in vitro models for nephrotoxicity testing in the future.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Animals:</strong></p><p>For whole kidney metabolomic analysis kidneys of 3 adult <strong>C57BL/6N</strong> mice were used. After excision with a scalpel blade, kidneys were weighed and directly prepared for further metabolomic analysis. Mice were housed in a SPF facility with free access to chow and water and a 12 h day/night cycle. All animal experiments were conducted according to the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, as well as the German law for the welfare of animals, and were approved by the local authorities (Regierungspräsidium Freiburg). <strong>MEFs</strong> were generated as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Cell culture:</strong></p><p><strong>iRECs</strong> were generated by direct reprogramming of <strong>mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs)</strong> as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>. In short, KspCre reporter <strong>MEFs</strong> were transduced with 4 lentivirus, each containing one of the 4 transcription factors Hnf1b, Hnf4a, Emx2 and Pax8 cloned into the pWPXLd lentiviral vector (Addgene no. 12258). Virus concentrate was diluted 1:1000 to 1:100 in MEFM containing 8 µg/ml Polybrene (sc-134220). Cells were incubated for 12 h on 7 consecutive days. 14 days after the last viral transduction GFP-positive cells were sorted using a BD FACSAria Fusion flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson). Afterwards, the cells were expanded in MEFM (Dulbecco’s modified Eagle's medium (DMEM), 2 mM L-glutamine, penicillin/streptomycin, 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS)). All cells were cryoconserved in freezing medium (containing 50% MEFM, 40% FBS, 10% DMSO) and stored in liquid nitrogen.</p><p><br></p><p>For metabolomic analysis <strong>MEFs</strong>, <strong>iRECs</strong> and <strong>mIMCD-3 (ATTC)</strong> cells were thawed and counted. 2 x 10^6 cells were seeded per 10 cm culture dish and expanded until harvested. All 3 cell lines were grown in MEFM containing DMEM High Glucose (4.5 g/l). Medium was changed on a daily basis; the medium for metabolomic analysis was incubated on cells for 24 h before harvesting. For cisplatin exposure, all 3 cell lines were incubated in MEFM containing 6 µl/ml cisplatin for 24 h (Cisplatin stock solution 0.1 mg/ml, Apotheke des Universitätsklinikums Freiburg).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Kaminski MM, Tosic J, Kresbach C, Engel H, Klockenbusch J, Müller AL, Pichler R, Grahammer F, Kretz O, Huber TB, Walz G, Arnold SJ, Lienkamp SS. Direct reprogramming of fibroblasts into renal tubular epithelial cells by defined transcription factors. Nat Cell Biol. 2016 Dec;18(12):1269-1280. doi:10.1038/ncb3437. PMID:27820600.</p>	<p>Cells were washed twice on ice with 5 ml 0.9% NaCl. Metabolism was quenched by adding 1.5 ml ice-cold extraction medium, i.e. methanol/water (9/1 v/v) containing 1 µg/ml ribitol and phenyl-beta-D-glucopyranoside as internal standards. Cells were harvested by scratching them off with cell scrapers and transferred into 2 ml screw-cup vials prefilled with 300 mg glass beads and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. Cells were lyzed by a Precellys 24 tissue homogenizer by applying 3 cycles of 15 s maximal intensity and 10 s break at -20 °C. After centrifugation (21,000 x g, 4 °C, 10 min), 550 µl of the supernatant were transferred in a new 2 ml Eppendorf tube and dried overnight in an Eppendorf Concentrator plus vacuum rotator. Metabolite pellets were stored under nitrogen atmosphere until derivatization.</p><p><br></p><p>Kidneys were weighed and washed with 0.9% NaCl. The whole kidneys were transferred into screw-cup vials prefilled with 300 mg glass beads and quenched with extraction medium. The volume was adjusted to tissue weight to yield equal concentrations (see <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study). Kidneys were lyzed by a Precellys 24 tissue homogenizer by applying 3 cycles of 30 s maximal intensity and 30 s break at -20 °C twice, with 5 min between the 2 runs. Lysates were centrifuged (21,000 x g, 4 °C, 10 min) and 100 µl were dried in an Eppendorf Concentrator plus vacuum rotator.</p><p><br></p><p>Medium was centrifuged (5 min, 20,000 x g, 4 °C) to remove detached or dead cells. 100 µl medium were added to 900 µl ice-cold acetonitrile/methanol (3/1 v/v) containing 1 µg/ml ribitol and phenyl-beta-D-glucopyranoside as internal standards. After 10 s of vortexing, the medium was centrifuged (1 h, 20,000 x g, 4°C) and 100 µl of the supernatant were transferred to a new 2 ml Eppendorf tube and dried for 3 hr.</p>	<p><strong>Derivatization:</strong></p><p>Metabolite pellets gained from cells, tissue or medium were derivatized by methoxymethylation of keto- and aldehyde-groups as well as trimethylsilylation of amines, hydroxyl- and carboxylic groups. Therefor, 20 µl methoxyamine hydrochloride (20 mg/ml in pyridine) were added to the metabolite pellet and shaken for 90 min at 28 °C with 1200 rpm. Afterwards, 50 µl N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl)-trifluoroacetamide were added and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C with 1200 rpm.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC/MS-Analysis:</strong></p><p>1 μl of the derivatized sample was splitlessly injected by a Gerstel MPS2 XL autosampler into an Agilent 7890A/5975C system in randomized order (sample order is shown in <strong>Table S2</strong> of the paper associated with this study). Chromatographic separation was performed using an HP5-MS column (5%-diphenyl-95%-dimethylpolysiloxane, 60 m x 0.25 mm x 0.25 μm). The gradient was 80 °C hold for 3 min, to 320 °C with 5 °C/min and hold for 14 min. The carrier gas flow rate (He) was set to 1 ml/min and the septum purge flow was 3 ml/min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC/MS-Analysis:</strong></p><p>Cells or medium pellets were resuspended in 100 µl resuspension buffer (10 mM NH4CH3CO2 pH 9, acetonitrile/water 1/1). 5 µl were injected onto a BEH Amide 150 x 2.1 mm column. The column temperature was set to 40 °C. 2 targeted methods were applied: 1 targeting amino acids, the other targeting intermediates of glycolysis and the TCA-cycle. Buffer A consisted of 10 mM NH4CH3CO2 pH 9 acetonitrile/water 20/80 and buffer B of 10 mM NH4CH3CO2 pH 9 acetonitrile/water 95/5. The chromatographic program for amino acid separation was 99.9% B for 0.5 min, to 80% B until 0.6 min, to 25% B until 5 min, hold until 8 min, to 99.9% B until 8.5 min and hold until 12 min. Separating intermediates of glycolysis and the TCA-cycle was achieved by the following program: 99.9% B hold for 1 min, to 30% B until 5 min, to 25% B until 5.5 min hold until 8.5 min, to 99.9% B until 9 min and hold until 14 min. Each flow rate was set to 400 µl/min.</p>	<p><strong>GC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Spectra were acquired in full scan mode with a scan rate of 1.99/s from 50 m/z to 800 m/z. Equilibration time and post run time was 1 min each. The inlet temperature was 230 °C, the MS source temperature 230 °C and the quadrupole analyzer temperature 150 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Multiple-Reaction-Monitoring (MRM) was optimized for each compound with analytical standards using Agilent's MRM-Optimizer. All transitions are displayed in <strong>Table S3</strong> of the paper associated with this study. Instrument parameters (6460 triple quadrupole Agilent Technologies) were set to 350 °C gas temperature, 8 l/min gas flow, 30 psi nebulizer pressure, 250 °C sheath gas temperature, 5 l/min sheath gas flow, +3 kV/−3.5 kV capillary voltage and +500 V/−300 V nozzle voltage. As internal standards, 13C3-pyruvic acid, d4-succinic acid and 13C4-15N-aspartic acid were used for intermediates of energy metabolism and amino acids, respectively. The results of methodology validation are displayed in <strong>Table S4</strong> of the paper associated with this study.</p>	<p><strong>GC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Within the GC/MS sample set, there was one C10-C40 n-alkane standard sample for building system independently Kováts' retention index (RI)<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Automated Mass spectral Deconvolution and Identification System (AMDIS)<strong>[2]</strong> was used for retention index calculation, peak identification, deconvolution and integration. A compound matrix was generated by <strong>spectconnect</strong> online tool<strong>[3]</strong> with compounds found in more than 50% of the replicates and an 80% similarity threshold of mass spectra. We applied these rather loose settings purposely in order not to miss too many cell type specific metabolites essential to establish cellular metabolic identity. Annotated compounds were normalized to phenyl-glucose. Further, all compounds were normalized to the sum of all compounds to take different cell numbers and mass into account<strong>[4]</strong>. For cells, blank plates treated equally as plates containing cells were analyzed the same way and their average intensity of each compound was subtracted from each compound in each condition.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Data were normalized to the isotopically labeled standard according to the corresponding pathway. For cells, averaged blank values were subtracted from each metabolite.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Kováts E. Gas-chromatographische Charakterisierung organischer Verbindungen. Teil 1. Retentionsindices aliphatischer Halogenide, Alkohole, Aldehyde und Ketone. HCA 41, 1915–1932 (1958).</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Stein SE. An Integrated Method for Spectrum Extraction and Compound Identification from Gas Chromatography/Mass Spectrometry. Data 10, 770–781 (1999).</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Styczynski MP, Moxley JF, Tong LV, Walther JL, Jensen KL, Stephanopoulos GN. Systematic identification of conserved metabolites in GC/MS data for metabolomics and biomarker discovery. Anal Chem. 2007 Feb 1;79(3):966-73. doi:10.1021/ac0614846. PMID:17263323.</p><p><strong>[4]</strong> Hutschenreuther A, Kiontke A, Birkenmeier G, Birkemeyer C. Comparison of extraction conditions and normalization approaches for cellular metabolomics of adherent growing cells with GC-MS. Anal. Methods 4, 1953 (2012).</p>	<p><strong>GC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Compounds were annotated by matching the obtained spectra with library spectra of <strong>Nist14[1]</strong>, <strong>FiehnLib[2]</strong> and <strong>golmDB[3]</strong>, where a match factor threshold of 750 and a retention index deviation of less than 5% were applied. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC/MS analysis:</strong></p><p>Metabolites were identified by Quantitative Analysis software (Agilent) using retention time and specific MRM-transitions as well as their qualifier ratios.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical analysis:</strong></p><p><strong>Metaboanalyst 3.0[4]</strong>, an <strong>R</strong> based statistic suit for metabolomics, was used. Half of the minimum positive value of each metabolite replaced missing values in GC/MS analysis. In this study, following tests were performed: <strong>ANalysis Of VAriance (ANOVA)</strong>, <strong>Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate</strong> with a <strong>q-value</strong> cut-off of 0.05, principal component analysis and heat map generation both including range-scaling (mean-centered and divided by the range of each variable), cluster and distance analysis after <strong>Pearson and Ward</strong>, pathway analysis as well as <strong>Very ImPortant feature (VIP)</strong>-plot calculation. A VIP-score above 1 was considered as meaningful.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Babushok VI, Linstrom PJ, Reed JJ, Zenkevich IG, Brown RL, Mallard WG, Stein SE. PMID:19928838.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Kopka J, Schauer N, Krueger S, Birkemeyer C, Usadel B, Bergmüller E, Dörmann P, Weckwerth W, Gibon Y, Stitt M, Willmitzer L, Fernie AR, Steinhauser D. GMD@CSB.DB: the Golm Metabolome Database. Bioinformatics. 2005 Apr 15;21(8):1635-8. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/bti236. PMID:15613389.</p><p><strong>[4]</strong> Xia J, Wishart DS.

Study Identifier	MTBLS6082
Study Title	Critical shifts in lipid metabolism promote megakaryocyte differentiation and proplatelet formation (Shotgun lipidomics assays without inhibition)
Study Description	<p>During megakaryopoiesis, megakaryocytes (MK) undergo cellular morphological changes with strong modification of membrane composition and lipid signaling. Here we adopt a lipid-centric multi-omics approach to create a quantitative map of the MK lipidome during maturation and proplatelet formation. Data reveal that MK differentiation is driven by an increased fatty acyl import and de novo lipid synthesis, resulting in an anionic membrane phenotype. Pharmacological perturbation of fatty acid import and phospholipid synthesis blocked membrane remodeling and directly reduced MK polyploidization and proplatelet formation resulting in thrombocytopenia. The anionic lipid shift during megakaryopoiesis was paralleled by lipid-dependent relocalization of the scaffold protein CKIP-1 and recruitment of the kinase CK2α to the plasma membrane, which seems to be essential for sufficient platelet biogenesis. Overall, this study provides a framework to understand how the MK lipidome is altered during maturation and the impact of MK membrane lipid remodeling on MK kinase signaling involved in thrombopoiesis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Shotgun lipidomics assays without inhibition</strong> are reported in the current study <strong>MTBLS6082</strong></p><p><strong>Shotgun lipidomics assays after inhibition with FSG67 and Triacsin C</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS6083' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS6083</strong></a></p><p><strong>Targeted lipidomics assay without inhibition</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS6084' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS6084</strong></a></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Bone marrow cells were isolated from femora bones of 10-14 wk old C57BL/6J mice via a previously published centrifugation protocol<strong>[1].</strong> BM cells were cultivated at 37 °C, 5% CO2 and MK differentiation was induced by adding (1%) recombinant thrombopoietin to the DMEM. Cells were harvested after 1, 3 and 7 d at 1000 rpm, 5 min, resuspended in PBS and MKs isolated via a BSA gradient (bottom: 1.5 ml 3% BSA in PBS, top: 1.5 ml 1.5% BSA in PBS). After 40 min, the supernatant was removed and the cell pellet washed 3x with 500 µl PBS. 200,000 cells per tube were snap frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane and sacrificed by cervical dislocation following the institutional guidelines and German law for the welfare of animals.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Heib T, Gross C, Müller ML, Stegner D, Pleines I. Isolation of murine bone marrow by centrifugation or flushing for the analysis of hematopoietic cells - a comparative study. Platelets. 2021 Jul 4;32(5):601-607. doi:10.1080/09537104.2020.1797323. PMID:32726163.</p>	<p>Lipids were extracted using a 2-phase, MTBE-based method based on the SIMPLEX procedure<strong>[1]</strong> with modifications. To each sample, 10 µl deuterated internal standard mix mouse SPLASH LIPIDOMICS, 6 µl Ceramide/Sphingoid internal standard mixture II (1:10 in MTBE:Methanol), 6 µl Cholesterol-d7 (12446 µM) and 6 µl Lyso-Sphingomyelin-d7 (212 µM) (all purchased from Avanti Polar Lipids) were added prior to extraction. Dried lipid fractions were reconstituted in 100 µl IPA/MeOH/CHCl3 (4:2:1, v/v/v) (IPA and CHCl3 were purchased from Sigma Aldrich) containing 7.5 mM ammonium acetate (Merck) and stored in brown glass lipid vials containing glass inlets until further analysis. Dried protein pellets were dissolved in lysis buffer (1% SDS, 150 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris/HCl, pH 7.8). Protein concentrations determined by Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were used to normalize lipid amounts.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Coman C, Solari FA, Hentschel A, Sickmann A, Zahedi RP, Ahrends R. Simultaneous Metabolite, Protein, Lipid Extraction (SIMPLEX): A Combinatorial Multimolecular Omics Approach for Systems Biology. Mol Cell Proteomics. 2016 Apr;15(4):1453-66. doi:10.1074/mcp.M115.053702. PMID:26814187.</p>	<p>A shotgun lipidomics direct infusion method was applied using a TriVersa NanoMate ion source (Advion Biosciences). 12 µl of sample was delivered over 14 min (backpressure 0.95 psi), in both positive and negative ion mode (polarity switching after 6 min from +1.25 to -1.25 kV). Each sample was maintained at 10 °C on the plate.</p>	<p>Data was acquired on a Q Exactive HF (Thermo Fisher Scientific) mass spectrometer with following parameters: Ion transfer tube temperature 250 °C, RF Lens 60%, MS full spectra were monitored in a mass range from 350-1200 m/z (positive) and 350-1600 (negative). Resolution was 240000, AGC target 1e^6 with maximum IT of 105 ms. Data independent (DIA) MS2 acquisition was performed of precursor masses with isolation windows of 1.001 Da. Resolution in MS2 spectra was 60000, AGC target 1e^5 and maximum IT 105 ms. HCD collision energy was 21% in positive and 26% in negative ion mode. 1 lock mass per polarity was used for internal mass calibration (m/z 476.3065 positive, m/z 529.4629 negative).</p>	<p>The native <strong>MS raw data</strong> was acquired using <strong>Xcalibur (version 2.9-290033/2.9.0.2926)</strong>. It was converted from profile to centroid data with <strong>MSConvert (ProteoWizard, version 3.0.20186)</strong> using vendor peak picking &amp; centroiding on <strong>MS1</strong> &amp; <strong>MS2</strong>.</p>	<p>Lipids were identified with <strong>LipidXplorer (LIFS, version 1.2.8.1, </strong><a href='https://zenodo.org/record/3570469' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>https://zenodo.org/record/3570469</strong></a><strong>)</strong> under the following settings: mass tolerance 5 ppm with an intensity threshold of 1e^5 for <strong>MS1</strong> and a mass tolerance of 10 ppm with an intensity threshold of 5e^4 for <strong>MS2</strong>. <strong>MFQL</strong> files for identification were written based on previous work<strong>[1][2]</strong>. The detection of glycerolipids was employed in positive ion mode. Glycerophospholipids were identified in negative ion mode. Class specific deuterated internal standards were analyzed accordingly. Lipids are reported when user-defined parameters and criteria from <strong>MFQL</strong> files were met. The <strong>LipidXplorer</strong> output file is provided in the project directory.</p><p><br></p><p>Lipids were quantified when present in &gt;50% of samples in 1 group (i.e. day 1). The precursor error threshold cut off was set to ± 2.5 ppm and lipid species intensities were compared to intensities in blank samples. The concentration of the lipid species was calculated by normalization of lipid signals to the corresponding internal standard and the protein concentration of the sample.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Herzog R, Schuhmann K, Schwudke D, Sampaio JL, Bornstein SR, Schroeder M, Shevchenko A. LipidXplorer: a software for consensual cross-platform lipidomics. PLoS One. 2012;7(1):e29851. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0029851. PMID:22272252.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Herzog, R., et al., A novel informatics concept for high-throughput shotgun lipidomics based on the molecular fragmentation query language. Genome Biol, 2011. 12(1): p. R8.</p><p>Herzog R, Schwudke D, Schuhmann K, Sampaio JL, Bornstein SR, Schroeder M, Shevchenko A. A novel informatics concept for high-throughput shotgun lipidomics based on the molecular fragmentation query language. Genome Biol. 2011;12(1):R8. doi:10.1186/gb-2011-12-1-r8. PMID:21247462.</p><p><br></p>

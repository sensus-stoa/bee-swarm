Study Title	Hypoxia Dependent Inhibition of Glioblastoma Cell Proliferation, Invasion, and Metabolism by the Choline-Kinase Inhibitor JAS239
Study Description	<b>Background:</b> Elevated choline kinase alpha (ChoK) levels are observed in most solid tumors, including glioblastomas (GBM), and ChoK inhibitors have demonstrated limited efficacy in GBM models. Given that hypoxia is associated with resistance to GBM therapy, we hypothesized that tumor hypoxia could be responsible for the limited response. Therefore, we evaluated the effects of hypoxia on the function of JAS239, a potent ChoK inhibitor in four GBM cell lines. <b>Methods:</b> Rodent (F98 and 9L) and human (U-87 MG and U-251 MG) GBM cell lines were subjected to 72 h of hypoxic conditioning and treated with JAS239 for 24 h. NMR metabolomic measurements and analyses were performed to evaluate the signaling pathways involved. In addition, cell proliferation, cell cycle progression, and cell invasion parameters were measured in 2D cell monolayers as well as in 3D cell spheroids, with or without JAS239 treatment, in normoxic or hypoxic cells to assess the effect of hypoxia on JAS239 function. <b>Results:</b> Hypoxia and JAS239 treatment led to significant changes in the cellular metabolic pathways, specifically the phospholipid and glycolytic pathways, associated with a reduction in cell proliferation via induced cell cycle arrest. Interestingly, JAS239 also impaired GBM invasion. However, effects from JAS239 were variable depending on the cell line, reflecting the inherent heterogeneity of GBMs. <b>Conclusions:</b> Our findings indicate that JAS239 and hypoxia can deregulate cellular metabolism, inhibit cell proliferation, and alter cell invasion. These results may be useful for designing new therapeutic strategies based on ChoK inhibition, which can act on multiple pro-tumorigenic features.

Study Protocol Description	<p>All GBM cell lines (F98, 9L, U-87 MG and U-251 MG) were maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 humidified atmosphere (see <strong>Section 1S </strong>in the paper associated with this study). Cells were passaged every 2-3 d at 80-90% confluency up to passage 10. For hypoxic preconditioning, cells were incubated for 72 h at 1% O2 (hypoxia) in a hypoxic workstation (Don Whitley Hypoxic Workstation, England), prior to drug treatment. Once treated, cells were incubated for a further 24 h during drug exposure. Drug treatment was performed within the workstation without reoxygenation of the cells. They were washed with PBS 3x prior to scraping into PBS before the wet cell pellets were frozen at -80 °C prior to extraction.</p>	<p>Aqueous metabolites were extracted using 400 μl of ice-cold solution of 50% HPLC-grade acetonitrile: 50% double distilled water per sample. Samples were sonicated in an ice bath (4 °C) for 3x 30 s at 10 kHz using a microtip probe. Samples were then vortexed and centrifuged for 5 min at 12,000 x g and 4 °C. The cell pellets were discarded and the supernatants were snap frozen in liquid nitrogen and lyophilized overnight. Samples were stored at -80 °C until further use.</p>	<p>Samples were reconstituted in a solution containing sodium phosphate (pH 7.4), deuterated (d4) trimethyl silyl propionate (TSP), 1.2 mM sodium azide, and 99.8% D2O. Samples were vortexed for 30 s followed by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 5 mins at 4 °C. Samples were then transferred using glass Pasteur pipettes into NMR tubes.</p>	<p>1D 1H NMR spectra were acquired using a Bruker Advance III HD 700MHz spectrometer with a 5 mm TCI cryoprobe at pH 7.4 and 25 °C.</p>	<p>1D 1H spectra were acquired for each sample along with water suppression, using the following Bruker pulse sequence <strong>cpmgpr1d</strong> (Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill [CPMG]) with <strong>256 transients</strong> a 15ppm spectral width, 32K points, and a 3.1 s acquisition time.</p>	<p>Initial processing of each spectrum was carried out in <strong>Bruker Topspin v3.2</strong>. The spectra were normalized to the total NMR signal intensity from each sample and integrals bucketed per peak into metabolite specific intensities using in-house server <strong>‘tameNMR’</strong> built within <strong>Galaxy toolkit</strong> (<a href='https://github.com/PGB-LIV/tameNMR' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://github.com/PGB-LIV/tameNMR</a>)<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Al-Mutawa YK, Herrmann A, Corbishley C, Losty PD, Phelan M, Sée V. Effects of hypoxic preconditioning on neuroblastoma tumour oxygenation and metabolic signature in a chick embryo model. Biosci Rep. 2018 Aug 29;38(4):BSR20180185. doi:10.1042/BSR20180185. PMID:30026261.</p>	<p><strong>Chenomx v8.2 software</strong> (<a href='https://www.chenomx.com' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>www.chenomx.com</a><a href='www.chenomx.com,' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>,</a> Canada) was used for metabolite annotation with identification against in-house metabolite library. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Statistical Analysis:</strong></p><p><strong>R Studio</strong> (<a href='https://www.r-project.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>www.r-project.org</a>) and <strong>MetaboAnalyst 5.0</strong> (<a href='https://www.metaboanalyst.ca,' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>www.metaboanalyst.ca,</a> Canada) were used for statistical analysis<strong>[1]</strong>. Multivariate analysis was performed, including <strong>principal component analysis (PCA)</strong> and <strong>partial least square discriminant analysis (PLS-DA)</strong>. For supervised <strong>PLS-DA</strong>, cross validation was performed to determine model robustness on a training set of 70% of the data with area under the <strong>receiver operating curve (ROC)</strong> presented for each group with respect to all other groups in the model<strong>[2]</strong>. A threshold of &gt;1.0 <strong>variable importance in projection (VIP)</strong> score was used to assess the discriminating metabolites. To elucidate the relevance of metabolites and metabolic pathways, <strong>MetaboAnalyst 5.0</strong> was used for pathway enrichment analysis<strong>[1]</strong>. Once a list of metabolites was uploaded, enrichment analysis was performed using the <strong>Small Molecule Pathway Database</strong>, and then subjected to <strong>over representation analysis (ORA)</strong> using the <strong>hypergeometric test</strong> to evaluate whether a particular metabolite set is represented more than expected by chance within the given compound list. Based on the <strong>VIP</strong> loadings of <strong>PLS-DA</strong> analysis, only metabolites with a <strong>VIP</strong> &gt;2.0 were analysed for enrichment, and <strong>one-tailed p values</strong> were used after adjusting for multiple testing.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Pang Z, Chong J, Zhou G, de Lima Morais DA, Chang L, Barrette M, Gauthier C, Jacques PÉ, Li S, Xia J.

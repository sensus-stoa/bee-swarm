Study Title	The phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase-3 inhibitor 3PO is a modulator of neutrophil metabolism, ROS production and NET release
Study Description	Neutrophils are key effector leukocytes of the innate immune system and play a pivotal role in defending the host against microbial infections. Recent studies have identified a crucial link between glycolysis and neutrophil cellular functions. We have investigated in human neutrophils, the intricate relationship between glycolysis, extracellular glucose availability, and the enzyme 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase 3 (PFKFB3), in the regulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) production. We have identified that the PFKFB3 inhibitor 3PO is a key regulator of neutrophil ROS and NETs, which are both implicated in the pathogenesis of immune-mediated inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA). 3PO blocked the production of ROS and NETs in a dose-dependent manner in both RA and HC neutrophils (p<0.01), and RA neutrophils were more sensitive to lower concentrations of 3PO. Bacterial killing was only partially inhibited by 3PO, and the proportion of live neutrophils after 24h incubation was unchanged. Using NMR metabolomics, we identified that 3PO increases the concentration of lactate, phenylalanine and L-glutamine in neutrophils, as well as significantly decreasing intracellular glutathione (adj. p-value<0.05), suggesting 3PO may have molecular targets beyond PFKFB3. We also demonstrated that RA neutrophils produce ROS and NETs in culture conditions which mimic the low glucose environments encountered in the RA synovial joint. By dissecting the intricate interplay between metabolism and neutrophil effector functions, this study advances the understanding of the molecular mechanisms governing pro-inflammatory neutrophil responses and identifies 3PO as a potential therapeutic for conditions characterized by dysregulated neutrophil activation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Ultrapure neutrophils (&gt;99.9% purity) were isolated using the untouched neutrophil isolation kit (Stem Cell). After the blood was incubated 1:5 ratio with Hetasep (Stem Cell) for 30 min at 37 °C, the upper layer of nucleated cells was collected and resuspended 1:1 with ice cold isolation buffer (PBS containing 2% BSA and 0.2 mM EDTA). The suspension was then centrifuged at 400 x g for 5 min and pellet resuspended in 2 ml of isolation buffer. 25 μl of the antibody cocktail was added to the cell suspension and then incubated for 10 min on ice before adding 50 ml of magnetic beads and further incubating for 10 min in an EasySep magnet. Cells were then collected and resuspended in RPMI media (+ L-glutamine, -HEPES, -phenol red) containing either no glucose or glucose (11 mM) at 5 x 10^6/ml. In some experiments, neutrophils were pre-treated with 3PO, BPTES, MB05032 or CP-91149 at a range of concentrations (10-50 mM), 2-DG (50 mM) or AZ-PFKFB3-67 (10 nM - 10 mM) for 15 min prior to activation with PMA (concentrations below).</p>	<p>Neutrophils were prepared for metabolite extraction as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, cells were centrifuged at 1000 x g at 25 °C for 2 min, the pellet washed with ice cold PBS then centrifuged at 1000 x g at 25 °C for 2 min. The supernatant was discarded, while pellets were heated at 100 °C for 1 min, and then snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C. Metabolites were extracted by addition of 50:50 v/v ice cold HPLC grade acetonitrile:water at 500 μl per cell pellet, followed by a 10 min incubation on ice. Samples were sonicated 3x for 30 s at 23 kHz and 10 μm amplitude using an exponential probe, with 30 s rest between sonications in an ice water bath. Sonicated samples were centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 5 min at 4 °C and the supernatant transferred to cryovials, flash frozen in liquid N2 and lyophilised.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Chokesuwattanaskul S, Fresneda Alarcon M, Mangalakumaran S, Grosman R, Cross AL, Chapman EA, Mason D, Moots RJ, Phelan MM, Wright HL. Metabolic Profiling of Rheumatoid Arthritis Neutrophils Reveals Altered Energy Metabolism That Is Not Affected by JAK Inhibition. Metabolites. 2022 Jul 15;12(7):650. doi:10.3390/metabo12070650. PMID:35888774.</p>	<p>Each lyophilised sample was resuspended in 200 μl of 100 μM deuterated sodium phosphate buffer pH 7.4, with 100 μM trimethylsilyl propionate (TSP) and 0.05% NaN3. Each sample was vortexed for 20 s and centrifuged at 21,500 x g for 5 min at 5 °C. Then 180 μl of each cell extract sample was transferred to 3 mm (outer diameter) NMR tubes for acquisition.</p>	<p>The samples were analysed using a 700 MHz NMR Avance III HD Bruker NMR spectrometer equipped with a TCI cryoprobe. For the quality assurance of the spectrometer, temperature was calibrated to 25 ºC (with margin of error of 0.1 ºC) via a methanol thermometer and 3D shimming via Bruker Sucrose standard prior each day of acquisition. </p>	<p>Samples were referenced to trimethylsilylpropanoic acid (TSP) at 0 ppm. 1H Spectra were acquired at 25 °C using the 1D 1H <strong>Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG)</strong> edited pulse sequence (vendor supplied <strong>cpmgpr1d</strong>) with 512 scans and a 4 s interscan delay and 1.8 s acquisition time.</p>	<p>Spectra were processed using vendor supplied auto routine (<strong>apk0.noe</strong>) prior to manual acceptance via quality control criteria. Spectra were assessed to conform to minimum quality criteria as outlined by the <strong>Metabolomics Standards Initiative[1]</strong> to ensure consistent linewidths at half height within 1 standard deviation, with consistent signal-to-noise, baseline correction and water suppression.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Sumner LW, Amberg A, Barrett D, Beale MH, Beger R, Daykin CA, Fan TW, Fiehn O et al. Proposed minimum reporting standards for chemical analysis Chemical Analysis Working Group (CAWG) Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI). Metabolomics. 2007 Sep;3(3):211-221. doi:10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2. PMID:24039616.</p><p><br></p>	<p>All spectra passing quality criteria were then divided into peak boundaries 'bins' that were defined globally by the peak limits using <strong>Chenomx NMR Suite 8.2</strong> <strong>(Chenomx Inc., Edmonton, Alberta, Canada)[1]</strong>. All peaks, both annotated in <strong>Chenomx</strong> (via manual analyses in <strong>TopSpin</strong> and <strong>Chenomx software</strong>) and unknowns, were included in the bin table. A <strong>correlation-based reliability scoring (CRS) method</strong> comparing metabolite annotated peaks via a <strong>Pearson correlation</strong> was applied to the data which aimed to address the problem of selecting appropriate representative bins from feature extraction in <strong>multivariate analysis[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Weljie AM, Newton J, Mercier P, Carlson E, Slupsky CM. Targeted profiling: quantitative analysis of 1H NMR metabolomics data. Anal Chem. 2006 Jul 1;78(13):4430-42. doi:10.1021/ac060209g. PMID:16808451.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Grosman R. NMR metabolic profiling of mosquito species to understand insecticide resistance. 2020 doi:10.17638/03067218.</p>

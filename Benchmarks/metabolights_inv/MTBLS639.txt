Study Identifier	MTBLS639
Study Title	Alterations in the metabolic footprint induced by Mayaro virus infection in cell culture
Study Description	This study focuses metabolic footprinting of Vero cells infected by Mayaro virus. Nuclear magnetic resonance combined with multivariate analytical methods and tools of pattern recognition shows that metabolic changes can be attributed to the effect of Mayaro virus infection. Vero cells infected by Mayaro and incubated for periods of 2h, 6h and 12h show in each period different variations in the levels of several metabolites such as amino acids, organic acids and carbohydrate. These organic compounds are metabolites involved in the pathway of glycolysis, tricarboxylic acid cycle, pentose phosphate pathway, and the oxidation pathway of fatty acids (via the β-oxidation). This study of footprinting analysis demonstrates the effect of the action of virus in the metabolism of the Vero cells, moreover, these analyses indicate intracellular metabolic state, and contributes to the knowledge of the microorganism influence on cellular metabolism.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The MAYV strain BeAR-20.290 MAYV was used in the experiments. This strain was isolated from Haemagogus mosquitoes in 1960 in Pará state, Brazil. The strain was initially propagated in suckling mice brains (<em>Mus musculus</em>), and then in C6/36 cells for production of viral stock. To assess the metabolic alterations of Vero cells when infected with MAYV, the cells were seeded in 6 wells plates with MEM supplemented with 10% of FBS, for 24 h at 37 ºC and 5% CO2, with 95% confluence. Then the monolayer was infected with MAYV at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 5. Supernatant of the cells monolayers was collected at 2, 6 and 12 h post infection (p.i.). The Vero E6 and C6/36 cell lines were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) (Manassas, VA, USA).</p>	<p>The ultra-filtration 3 kDa Vivaspin tube has a filtration column with a membrane containing residual quantities of glycerine and sodium azide, which interfere in 1H NMR analyses. Before adding the samples to the Vivaspin, the membrane was pre-washed, adding 2 ml of purified water (deionized), centrifuging at 4000 rpm, 4 °C, for 24 min. This stage was performed in 20 washing cycles. A minimum of 10 washing cycles is recommended to remove contaminants from the Vivaspin before use on 1H NMR. After washing, the Vivaspin was stored in a refrigerator (4 °C) with purified water, covering the membrane surface for later 1H NMR procedures.</p>	<p>All of the infected and non-infected Vero cell supernatants collected at periods of 2 h, 6 h and 12 h p.i. were thawed and added to the vivaspin. Next, they were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10-15 min at 4 °C to filter extracellular metabolites. Finally, 550 μl of filtrate was added in 50 μl of D2O, and transferred to 5 mm tubes appropriate for 1H NMR analyses.</p>	<p>NMR spectra were acquired in a <strong>Bruker Avance HD III</strong> equipment operating at <strong>600 MHz</strong> and equipped with a triple channel cryoprobe. All measurements were performed at <strong>293 K</strong>.</p>	<p>The standard Bruker 1D pulse sequence <strong>NOESYPR1D</strong> was used for water suppression with mixing time of 100 ms. <strong>16 scans</strong> were collected with 4 dummy scans. Spectral width of 14 ppm was used and 32k data points. Relaxation delay was set to 5 s with 0.2 ms of gradient recovery. Before Fourier transformation, a line broadening of 1 Hz was applied to each FID. Following, each spectra were manually phased and referenced to methyl doublet of lactate at 1.33 ppm. To assist metabolite identification, <strong>Total Correlation Spectroscopy (TOCSY)</strong> was performed using a DPSI sequence for mixing and pre-saturation for water suppression in selected samples. A mixing time of 80 ms was chosen and spectrum was collected using <strong>64 scans</strong> with 4k data points in the direct dimension and 512 in the indirect dimension. Relaxation delay was kept in 5 s. Also, <strong>13C-1H Heteronuclear Single Quantum Coherence (HSQC)</strong> spectroscopy was collected in order to support metabolite identification. Each spectrum was collected using 1k data points in the direct dimension and 256 in the indirect dimension and 150 scans were recorded. Relaxation delay of 1.5 s were used and gradient recovery of 0.2 ms applied.</p>	<p>All spectra were processed in <strong>TopSpin 3.2</strong> software (Bruker, Germany). Binning was manually performed for removing noise and water signal regions. Data was organized following the time point label (2, 6 or 12 h), the sample condition (“c” for control and “i” for infected) and the biological replica (a number from 1-6).</p>	<p>Metabolite identification were performed using <strong>Chenomx profiler</strong> software. In order to confirm <strong>Chenomx</strong> fits in the 1D 1H-NMR spectra, <strong>TOCSY</strong> spectra were used to check for cross-peaks. Also, <strong>13C-HSQC</strong> were also used to assign metabolites for each specific signal.</p>

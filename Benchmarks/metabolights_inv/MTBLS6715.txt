Study Title	Transcriptomics and Metabolomics Reveal Alterations in Xenobiotic Metabolism and Appearance of Steroidal Alkaloid Metabolites in Mouse Livers after Tomato Consumption
Study Description	<p><strong>SCOPE:</strong> Tomato consumption is associated with many health benefits including lowered risk for developing certain cancers. It is hypothesized that tomato phytochemicals are transported to the liver and other tissues where they alter gene expression in ways that lead to favorable health outcomes. However, the effects of tomato consumption on mammalian liver gene expression and chemical profile are not well defined.</p><p><strong>METHODS AND RESULTS:</strong> We hypothesized that tomato consumption would alter mouse liver transcriptomes and metabolomes compared to a control diet. C57BL/6 mice (n=11-12/group) were fed a macronutrient matched diet containing either 10% red tomato, 10% tangerine tomato, or no tomato powder for 6 weeks after weaning. RNA-Seq followed by gene set enrichment analyses indicated that tomato type and consumption, in general, altered expression of phase I and II xenobiotic metabolism genes. Untargeted metabolomics experiments revealed distinct clustering between control and tomato fed animals. Nineteen molecular formulas (representing 75 chemical features) were identified or tentatively identified as steroidal alkaloids and isomers of their phase I and II metabolites; many of which are reported for the first time in mammals.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSION:</strong> These data together suggest tomato consumption may impart benefits partly through enhancing detoxification potential.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The tangerine processing tomato variety FG04-167 (enriched in <em>cis</em>-structured lycopene and its precursors due to the t3183 allele<strong>[1][2]</strong>) and the red processing varieties OH8243, OH8245, and FG99-218 (elevated all-<em>trans</em>-lycopene concentration due to the old gold crimson (ogc) allele<strong>[3]</strong>) were grown, processed into single strength juice, and freeze-dried as previously described<strong>[2][4]</strong>. Animal diets were manufactured by Dyets (Bethlehem, PA, USA) by combining tomato powders into a modified AIN-93G diet at 10% (w/w) prior to pelleting<strong>[5]</strong>. To maintain macronutrient composition across all treatments, control diets were supplemented with corn starch and dextrose to compensate for the carbohydrates naturally present in tomato<strong>[6]</strong>. Animal diets were stored at −20 °C and mice received fresh diet every 2-3 d to minimize phytochemical degradation.</p><p><br></p><p>At 4 wk of age, male C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned treatment groups (n=11-12/group) and fed either a control diet, a diet supplemented with 10% (w/w) red tomato powder, or a diet supplemented with 10% (w/w) tangerine tomato powder (protocol 2010A00000095). Mice were sacrificed after 6 wk of dietary intervention and 1 liver lobe from each mouse was split for chemical profiling or transcriptomic analysis. Samples were flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Isaacson T, Ronen G, Zamir D, Hirschberg J. PMID:14600090.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Polar metabolites were extracted from 50 mg (± 5 mg) of frozen liver using a modified extraction method<strong>[1]</strong>. 5 x 2 mm zirconium oxide beads (Union Process; Akron, OH, USA) were placed in 1.5 ml tubes containing sample tissue and 500 μl of chilled methanol with 0.1% BHT was immediately added. Samples were homogenized using a SPEX Geno/Grinder 2010 for 30 s at 1400 RPM within a chilled Cryo−Block (SPEX Sample Prep; Metuchen, NJ, USA). Samples were centrifuged at 21,130 x g for 5 min at 4 °C, and 475 μl of supernatant fluid from each sample was passed through a solid-phase extraction cartridge (Oasis PRiME HLB 1cc/30mg, Waters; Milford, MA, USA) to remove phospholipids. An additional 100 μl of acetonitrile was passed through the cartridges to elute any remaining polar metabolites. 3 separate 135 μl aliquots were reserved for analysis and a final 75 μl aliquot from each sample was combined to create a pooled quality control (QC) sample. Process blanks used to identify features derived from laboratory consumables were created using the same extraction, with water substituted for liver. Samples, process blanks, and QCs were dried down under a stream of nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Masson P, Alves AC, Ebbels TM, Nicholson JK, Want EJ. PMID:20715759.</p>	<p>Extracts were redissolved in 100 μl of 1:1 methanol:water and centrifuged at 21,130 x g for 3 min to precipitate undissolved solids. The supernatant was run on an ultra−high performance liquid chromatography (Agilent 1290 Infinity II, UHPLC) system coupled with an quadrupole time of flight mass spectrometer (Agilent 6545, QTOF-MS). Analytes were separated on a C18 bridged ethylene hybrid column (Waters, 2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 µm particle size), maintained at 40 °C, using water (A) and acetonitrile (B), both with 0.1% formic acid at 0.5 ml/min. The gradient used was as follows: 0% B held for 1 min, a linear gradient to 100% B over 10 min, 100% B held for 2 min, and a return to 0% B for an additional 2 min. Between injections, the needle and sample loop were washed with acetonitrile for 8 s, isopropanol for 10 s, and water for 5 s to reduce carryover. Samples were maintained at 4 °C and the injection volume was 5 μl.</p>	<p>The Agilent 6545 QTOF-MS was operated in electrospray positive ionization mode and data were collected from 50-1700 m/z. Gas temperature was 350 °C with a flow of 10 L/min. Nebulizer gas was 35 psig. Sheath gas temperature and flow were 375 °C and 11 L/min, respectively. To condition the instrument, a QC was repeatedly injected until base peak chromatograms stabilized. QC samples were run every 6 injections to monitor intra-run variability.</p>	<p>Feature extraction and alignment were conducted in <strong>Agilent MassHunter Profinder 10.0 </strong>(Agilent Technologies).

Study Identifier	MTBLS682
Study Title	Molecular changes in cancer cells exposed to an HDAC inhibitor: A metabolomics approach
Study Description	Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors are part of a new generation of epigenetic drugs for cancer treatment. It is known that histone acetylation plays a key role in controlling essential chromosome functions, including gene regulation, and this process has been linked with cancer development and progression. Better understanding of molecular mechanisms involving HDAC inhibitors is needed for the design of new targeted drugs, and also to evaluate the effectiveness of current treatments. In this study, an untargeted metabolomics approach was used to identify intracellular metabolite deregulation after treating cancer cell lines with the HDAC inhibitor HC-Toxin. Metabolomics analysis was performed using high resolution mass spectrometry, in combination with univariate and multivariate statistics and pathway analysis. HDAC inhibition showed highly specific metabolic changes in cancer cell lines compared to non-cancerous cells. In particular, N-acetyl-L-cysteine, N-acetylmethionine, and N-acetyl-L-carnitine showed a dose dependent change. Moreover, pathways controlling protein biosynthesis, as well as tryptophan, cysteine and methionine metabolism were significantly altered by HDAC inhibition. This study illustrates that HDAC inhibition has multiple effects on different metabolic pathways and our results can be extrapolated to inform on the molecular transitions in human cells.

Study Protocol Description	<p>HCT116, HeLa and RPE1 cell lines were obtained from the European Collection of Cell Cultures (HPACC, UK). Cells were grown as monolayers in DMEM culture medium, supplemented with 10% FCS and antibiotics (1% penicillin/streptomycin), and maintained under a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. A stock solution of HC-toxin was prepared in DMSO at a concentration of 2 mM and the working concentrations were again dissolved in DMSO to achieve concentrations between 10-1000 µM. An equal number of cells (2 x 10^5 cells/ml) were seeded into 6 well plates (3 replicated per concentration) until 80% of confluence was reached. Once media was replaced, 2 µl of the drug at different concentrations were added to the wells, and plates were incubated for 24 h. Control cells were treated with sterile DMSO and in all cases concentration of DMSO was maintained at 0.1%.</p>	<p>Metabolite extraction was performed following reported protocols. Briefly, media was quickly removed from the plates and each well was washed twice with ice-cold PBS. An extracting solution containing acetonitrile, methanol and water (2:2:1, kept at 4 °C), was used to simultaneously quench all metabolic reactions and to extract intra-cellular metabolites. Plates were incubated on dry ice for 10 min and cell extracts were recovered from plastic using a cell scraper. Cell suspensions were transferred to 1.5 ml Eppendorf tubes (Stevenage, UK) followed by centrifugation (4 °C) at 14000 rpm for 10 min to remove the denatured proteins. Supernatants were collected and stored at -80 °C prior to LC-MS analysis.</p>	<p>Separations were performed on a Thermo Ultimate 3000 RSLC liquid chromatography system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Hemel Hempstead, UK). Chromatography was carried out using a ZIC-pHILIC 150 mm x 2.1 mm, 5 μm column (VWR, Lutterworth, UK) kept at 35 °C as previously described. Mobile phase A consisted in 20 mM ammonium carbonate in water, and acetonitrile was used as mobile phase B. The gradient program started with 80% B and then linearly decreased to 20% B within 32 min. Next, the column was washed with 5% B for 5 min and subsequently re-equilibrated with 80% B at a flow rate of 240 µl/min. Samples were maintained at 4 °C and the injection volume was 10 µl.</p>	<p>Mass spectra were collected using an Orbitrap Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Hemel Hempstead, UK) with a HESI-II probe operated in polarity switching mode. MS parameters were adjusted as follows: Resolution 70,000, AGC target 1 x 10^6, Scan Range (m/z) 75-1000, Sheath gas 50, Auxiliary gas 20 and Capillary temperature 275 °C. Source parameters were adjusted using the default settings at the determined flow rate. Mass calibration was performed for each polarity immediately before each analysis batch.</p>	<p>Raw data was converted into <strong>mzML</strong> files by using <strong>MSConvert</strong> software (<strong>ProteoWizard, version 3.0.06245</strong>). Peak picking was performed by <strong>XCMS</strong> using optimised parameters obtained by <strong>IPO</strong>.</p>	<p>The significant features were annotated by <strong>PUTMEDID_LCMS</strong> analysis, <strong>METLIN</strong> database, and the corresponding MS/MS spectra were confirmed with the online <strong>MzCloud</strong> library. The elemental composition was derived from with a mass error of less than 10 ppm.</p>

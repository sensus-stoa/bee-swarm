Study Identifier	MTBLS6847
Study Title	Glycerol 3-phosphate phosphatase/PGPH-2 counters metabolic stress and promotes healthy aging via a glycogen sensing-AMPK-HLH-30-autophagy axis in C. elegans
Study Description	Metabolic stress caused by excess nutrients accelerates aging. We recently demonstrated that the newly discovered enzyme glycerol-3-phosphate phosphatase (G3PP; gene Pgp), which operates an evolutionarily conserved glycerol shunt that hydrolyzes glucose-derived glycerol-3-phosphate to glycerol, counters metabolic stress and promotes healthy aging in C. elegans. However, the mechanism whereby G3PP activation extends healthspan and lifespan, particularly under glucotoxicity, remained unknown. Here, we show that the overexpression of the C. elegans G3PP homolog, PGPH-2, decreases fat levels and mimics, in part, the beneficial effects of calorie restriction, particularly in glucotoxicity conditions, without reducing food intake. PGPH-2 overexpression depletes glycogen stores activating AMP-activate protein kinase, which leads to the HLH-30 nuclear translocation and activation of autophagy, promoting healthy aging. Transcriptomics reveal an HLH-30-dependent longevity and catabolic gene expression signature with PGPH-2 overexpression. Thus, G3PP overexpression activates three key longevity factors, AMPK, the TFEB homolog HLH-30, and autophagy, and may be an attractive target for age-related metabolic disorders linked to excess nutrients.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong><em>C. elegans</em> strains and maintenance:</strong></p><p>Nematodes strains were maintained and synchronized using standard culture methods<strong>[1]</strong>. All experiments were conducted at 20 °C unless noted otherwise. Strains used in this study are available in <strong>Table S10</strong> in the paper associated with this study. For normal growth conditions, <strong>OP50 <em>E. Coli</em> </strong>strain was used.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Glucose treatment experiment:</strong></p><p><strong>Wild-type</strong> and <strong>pgph-2 o/e one gravid</strong> adult animals (2 pellets of 250 μl) have been synchronized using bleaching technique. Worms have been arrested 12 h at the L1 stage then plated either on Medium NGM plates seeded with <strong>OP50 <em>E. coli</em></strong> bacteria (<strong>Control</strong>) or on plates supplemented with 2% glucose and seeded with <strong>OP50 <em>E. coli</em>.</strong> Worms were grown at 20 °C for 48 h. When the animals reached the L4/young adult stage, they were harvested and washed with M9 buffer 3x and then kept in pre-weighed bead-containing tubes. Pellets of approximately 200 μl were flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and kept for metabolites extraction at -80 °C. For every independent experiment and repeat, we aimed for 4 biological samples from every group and the experiment has been repeated independently 2x.</p><p><br></p><p>NGM plates and bleach solution have been prepared according to wormbook and standard worm protocols. Metabolites were analyzed by <strong>LC-MS/MS</strong> as previously described<strong>[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Brenner, S., The genetics of Caenorhabditis elegans. Genetics, 1974. 77(1): p. 71-94.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Mugabo Y, Zhao S, Seifried A, Gezzar S, Al-Mass A, Zhang D, Lamontagne J, Attane C, Poursharifi P, Iglesias J, Joly E, Peyot ML, Gohla A, Madiraju SR, Prentki M. Identification of a mammalian glycerol-3-phosphate phosphatase: Role in metabolism and signaling in pancreatic β-cells and hepatocytes. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2016 Jan 26;113(4):E430-9. doi:10.1073/pnas.1514375113. PMID:26755581.</p><p><br></p>	<p>To frozen pellets, 4.25 volumes of extraction buffer (99% MeOH, 2mM Ammonium Acetate pH 9.0, 10µM IS-AMP) are added. Worms are homogenized first by bead beating in a cooled Precellys 24 (Bertin Technologies) using the following protocol: 6000 rpm, 2 x 25 s, 15 s pause; second by sonication at 150 W during 2 min (10 s on/10 s off) in a Cup Horn (Qsonica 431C2) filled with ice-cold water, attached to a Qsonica Q700 sonicator. Homogenates are then incubated on ice 15 min and centrifuged at 20,000 x g, 15 min at 4 °C. 800 µl of supernatant is transferred into 12 x 75 mm glass tubes for liquid/liquid extraction (LLE). For LLE, 0.28 volumes of cold MilliQ water and 1.6 volumes of cold 3:1 CHCl3:Heptane are added to the glass tubes, which are vortex-mixed twice for 10 s, incubated 10 min on ice and centrifuged at 4,500 x g, 15 min at 4 °C. 700 µl of the upper aqueous phase is transferred into 1.5 ml screw cap PP tubes on ice and dried down in 2 steps: MeOH is first evaporated in a refrigerated CentriVap (Labconco) at 15 °C, then tubes are frozen in liquid nitrogen and freeze-dried.</p>	<p>Pellets are reconstituted in 30 µl of ice-cold MilliQ water. Tubes are centrifuged at 20,000 x g, 5 min at 4 °C and 20 µl of supernatant transferred into glass inserts-equipped HPLC vials. Chromatographic separation is achieved on a Shimadzu Nexera X2 HPLC system at 0.75 ml/min using the following gradient: 0 min, 5% B; 0.69 min, 5% B; 3.05 min, 11% B; 3.64 min, 11% B; 5.41 min, 23% B; 8.65 min, 85.5% B; 8.66 min, 100% B; 10.12 min, 100% B; 10.13 min, 5% B to 12.6 min, where A is 10 mM terbutylamine, 15 mM acetic acid in water, pH 5.20, and B is acetonitrile 95% in water, 0.1% formic acid. Samples of 3 µl are injected and separated at 40 °C using a Poroshell 120 EC-C18 2.1 x 5 mm, 2.7 µm particles guard column (Agilent Technologies) followed by a Poroshell 120 EC-C18, 2.1 x 75 mm, 2.7 µm particles HPLC column.</p>	<p>Targeted <strong>ESI-MS/MS</strong> analyses are performed on a Sciex 4000 Qtrap with a Turbo V source in negative MRM mode using the following source parameters: CUR 30 psi; CAD High; IS -4500 V; TEM 725 °C; GS1 65 psi; GS2 70 psi. Transitions used are: pyruvate, 87.0/43.1; alanine, 87.9/87.9; leucine, 129.8/84.0; glutamine, 144.9/127.0; glutamate, 145.7/101.9; DHAP, 168.8/96.8; glycerol 3-phosphate, 170.9/62.9; lactate, 89.0/43.0; arginine, 173.0/130.9; adenosine, 265.9/133.9; glutathione, reduced, 305.9/271.7; GMP, 362.1/78.8; NAD, 662.2/540.2; aspartate, 131.9/88.0; acetoacetate, 100.8/56.9; succinate, 116.9/73.1; malate, 132.9/114.8; cAMP, 327.9/134.0; cGMP, 344.0/149.8; AMP, 345.9/78.9; IS-AMP 361.0/79.0; glutathione, oxidized, 611.3/306.4; oxaloacetate, 130.6/86.6; alpha-ketoglutarate, 144.9/57.1; GDP, 441.9/78.9; NADP, 742.2/620.0; isocitrate/citrate, 190.8/110.9; ADP, 426.0/78.9; NADH, 664.2/78.9; fumarate, 114.9/71.0; NADPH, 744.2/78.9; acetoacetyl-CoA, 850.2/78.8; ATP, 506.0/78.9; GTP, 521.9/78.9; acetyl-CoA, 808.7/79.0; HMG-CoA, 910.2/78.9; malonyl-CoA, 852.1/78.9; 2-phosphoglycolate, 154.8/78.8.</p>	<p>Raw peak areas were adjusted for IS-AMP internal standard peak area. When analytes were found in blanks, adjusted blank peak areas were subtracted from samples values. These values were then corrected to take into account volumes left over during extraction procedures and normalized to the protein content of the homogenates pellets. Values are expressed as relative abundance after adjustments and normalization.</p>	<p>This was a targeted analysis. A mix of pure standards was analyzed alongside the samples to verify retention times.</p>

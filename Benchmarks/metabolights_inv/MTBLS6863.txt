Study Identifier	MTBLS6863
Study Title	Resting natural killer cell homeostasis relies on tryptophan/NAD+ metabolism and HIF-1α
Study Description	<p>Natural killer (NK) cells are forced to cope with different oxygen environments even under resting conditions. The adaptation to low oxygen is regulated by oxygen-sensitive transcription factors, the hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs). The function of HIFs for NK cell activation and metabolic rewiring remains controversial. Activated NK cells are predominantly glycolytic, but the metabolic programs that ensure the maintenance of resting NK cells are enigmatic. By combining <em>in situ</em> metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses in resting murine NK cells, our study defines HIF-1a as a regulator of tryptophan metabolism and cellular nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) levels. The HIF-1a/NAD+ axis prevents ROS production during oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos) and thereby blocks DNA damage and NK cell apoptosis under steadystate conditions. In contrast, in activated NK cells under hypoxia, HIF-1a is required for glycolysis, and forced HIF-1a expression boosts glycolysis and NK cell performance <em>in vitro</em> and <em>in vivo</em>. Our data highlight two distinct pathways by which HIF-1a interferes with NK cell metabolism. While HIF-1a-driven glycolysis is essential for NK cell activation, resting NK cell homeostasis relies on HIF-1a-dependent tryptophan/NAD+ metabolism.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Linked cross omic data sets:</strong></p><p>RNA-seq data associated with this study are available in ArrayExpress (BioStudies): accession <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/arrayexpress/studies/E-MTAB-12082' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>E-MTAB-12082</a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Mouse models</strong></p><p><em>In vivo</em> targeted deletions of HIF-1a and VHL in NK cells were achieved by as previously described<strong>[1][2]</strong>. To mitigate the influence of strain variation, mice were kept in a &gt; 99% C57Bl/6J background. Male and female mice at the age of 8-12 weeks were used at equal ratios. All animal experiments were approved by the local animal ethics committee (Kantonales Veterinarsamt Zurich, licenses ZH208/18 and ZH189/18) and performed according to local guidelines (TschV, Zurich) and the Swiss animal protection law (TschG).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>NK cell purification</strong></p><p>Splenocytes, bone marrow and liver cell suspensions were obtained from HIF-1a WT and KO mice. NK cells from spleen and bone marrow were purified on ice using 'EasySep Mouse NK Cell Isolation Kit' following the manufacturer’s protocol. NK cells from liver were purified as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Metabolomics</strong></p><p>Freshly isolated NK cells from 20 mice were pooled for the untargeted metabolomics, and NK cells from 2 mice were pooled for the targeted metabolomics. Once isolated, NK cells were washed once in cold PBS and transferred in a cryotube. After centrifugation, the supernatant was removed, and the cell pellet was snap frozen in liquid nitrogen. Samples were kept at -80 °C until they were sent to Creative Proteomics who performed the sample preparation and the metabolomics experimental workflow.</p><p><br></p><p>Splenocytes suspension was obtained from Hif1a (fl/fl) and Ncr1(cre) Hif1a (fl/fl) mice. Natural Killer cells were isolated with magnetic negative selection.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Krzywinska E, Kantari-Mimoun C, Kerdiles Y, Sobecki M, Isagawa T, Gotthardt D, Castells M, Haubold J, Millien C, Viel T, Tavitian B, Takeda N, Fandrey J, Vivier E, Sexl V, Stockmann C. Loss of HIF-1α in natural killer cells inhibits tumour growth by stimulating non-productive angiogenesis. Nat Commun. 2017 Nov 17;8(1):1597. doi:10.1038/s41467-017-01599-w. PMID:29150606.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Krzywinska E, Sobecki M, Nagarajan S, Zacharjasz J, Tambuwala MM, Pelletier A, Cummins E, Gotthardt D, Fandrey J, Kerdiles YM, Peyssonnaux C, Taylor CT, Sexl V, Stockmann C. The transcription factor HIF-1α mediates plasticity of NKp46+ innate lymphoid cells in the gut. J Exp Med. 2022 Feb 7;219(2):e20210909. doi:10.1084/jem.20210909. Epub 2022 Jan 13. PMID:35024767.</p>	<p><strong>Sample solution</strong></p><p>Each cell pellet sample was made into 80% methanol, at 200 μL/1e^6 cells according to the cell information provided on the sample submission form. The samples were lysed with the aid of 2 metal beads on a MM 400 mill mixer at 30 Hz for 1 min 3x times, followed by sonication in an ice-water bath for 30 s and subsequent centrifugation at 21,000 x g and 5 °C for 10 min.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Standard solution</strong></p><p>A standard solution containing all the 25 targeted metabolites was prepared in an internal standard solution of isotope-labeling AMP, ATP, GMP, GTP, tryptophan, nicotinic acid, nicotinamide, 1-methylnicotinamide, NAD, NADH and adenosine. This solution was further diluted in the same solution to have calibration solutions in a concentration range from 0.00001 to 20 μM.</p>	<p>Briefly, 200 μL of the supernatant of each sample was taken out to an LC microvial and dried under a gentle nitrogen gas flow. The residue was reconstituted in 60 μL of the same internal standard solution. After vortexing and centrifugation, 10 μL aliquots of the clear supernatants together with each of calibration solutions were injected into a ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 μm) column to run UPLC-MRM/MS on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC system coupled to a Sciex QTRAP 6500 Plus mass spectrometer. To detect the negatively-charged metabolites, the instrument was operated in the negative-ion MRM scanning mode. The mobile phase was a tributylamine buffer (solvent A) and acetonitrile (solvent B) as the mobile phase for binary-solvent gradient elution of 5% to 40% B over 20 min at 50 °C and 0.25 mL/min. For detection of the positively-charged metabolites, the LC column was a polar reversed-phase C18 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.6 μm), with a 0.02% HFBA buffer (solvent A) and methanol (solvent B) as the mobile phase for binary-solvent gradient elution of 0% to 60% B over 15 min at 50 °C and 0.25 mL/min.</p>	<p>Briefly, 200 μL of the supernatant of each sample was taken out to an LC microvial and dried under a gentle nitrogen gas flow.

Study Identifier	MTBLS6881
Study Title	Single-cell RNA sequencing decipher hepatic injury and APOA4 as a biomarker in males with intrauterine growth restriction
Study Description	<p>Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) is associated with increased relative liver weight at birth, hepatic function decline and a higher risk for chronic liver and cardiovascular diseases in adults. Precise mechanisms of early developmental plasticity to intervene in poor fetal programming and adult disease remain largely elusive and warrant extensive research. Selecting natural piglets’ model of IUGR, using the liver as a readout and combining previous transcriptome findings, a map of cellular landscape was created to reveal a sex-dependent manner in IUGR-induced hepatic injury and its long-term functional repercussions. At the one-week postnatal node, we reveal that in contrast to a converting trend about an immune-adapted phenotype in female IUGRs, the more severe hepatic injury in male IUGRs is attributed to persistent inflammation caused by abnormal lipid metabolism, which disrupts the interactions of non-immune cells, resulting in impaired liver regeneration lasting into adulthood. In addition, we consider APOA4 as a new biomarker for the detection of IUGRs and discover its underlying role in hypoxic conditions. Our study opens the possibility to early therapy and screening of gender-specific hepatic injury brought on by IUGR.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>'NBW1-8' liver samples of male piglets about normal body weight at an age of 7 days without any treatment, collecting after euthanasia by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (25 mg/kg body weight), and then were stored at -80 °C after quick freezing in liquid nitrogen. 'IUGR1-8' liver samples of male piglets about intrauterine growth restriction at an age of 7 days without any treatment, collecting after euthanasia by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (25 mg/kg body weight), and then were stored at -80 °C after quick freezing in liquid nitrogen.</p>	<p>Briefly, 20 mg of each sample (piglets' liver tissue) was homogenized with 1 mL mixture (including MTBE:methanol (3:1, v/v) and internal standard mixture) by a mixer mill with zirconia beads for lipid extraction. After mixed by vortex for 2 min and emulsified by sonication for 5 min, 200 μL of water were added in the samples and mixed by vortex for 1 min, and then 300 μL supernatant were obtained by centrifuging at 4 °C 12,000 x g for 10 min. Finally, 300 μL supernatant were dried with nitrogen and reconstituted in acetonitrile:isopropanol (1:9, v/v) for UPLC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Ultra Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system (ExionLC AD, SCIEX, Framingham, MA) with Tandem Mass Spectrometry (QTRAP 6500+) was used to acquire data. The chromatographic columns (Accucore C30, 2.6 μm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm i.d.) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) was applied for sepration of lipids. The solvent system contained mobile phase A composed of acetonitrile:water (60/40, v/v, 0.1% formic acid, 10 nmol/L ammonium formate) and mobile phase B composed of acetonitrile:isopropanol (10/90, vv/v, 0.1% formic acid, 10 nmol/L ammonium formate). A gradient elution was performed at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min with 45 °C as follows: A/B (80:20, v/v) at 0.0 min; A/B (70:30, v/v) at 2.0 min; A/B (40:60, v/v) at 4.0 min; A/B (15:85, v/v) at 9.0 min; A/B (10:90, v/v) at 14.0 min; A/B (5:95, v/v) at 15.5 min; A/B (5:95, v/v) at 17.3 min; A/B (80/20, v/v) at 17.5 min; A/B (80/20, v/v) at 20.0 min.</p><p><br></p>	<p>MS was employed on an AB Sciex QTRAP 4500 MS (SCIEX, Framingham, MA) equipped with an ESI.&nbsp;The effluent was alternatively connected to an ESI-triple quadrupole-linear ion trap (QTRAP)-MS. The detection and quantification of lipids were performed in the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode using SCIEX Analyst software (v1.6.3). The parameters were as follows: ion source temperature, 500 °C; source gas1, 45 psi; gas2, 55 psi; curtain gas, 35 psi.</p>	<p>For obtaining lipid data matrix, SCIEX MultiQuant software (v3.0.3) was performed to integrate and correct the characteristic fragment peaks of each lipid.</p>	<p>Based on internal standard method, lipid identification was achieved on the acquired lipid which was compare and match to MetWare database (MEtWare Biological Science and Technology Co., Ltd, Wuhan, China), through retention time, accurate precursor/product ion information and MS/MS spectrum patterns.</p>

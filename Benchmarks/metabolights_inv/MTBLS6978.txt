Study Identifier	MTBLS6978
Study Title	N6-methyladenosine modification is not a general trait of positive-sense RNA viral genomes
Study Description	<p>Despite the nuclear localization of the m6A machinery, the genomes of multiple exclusively-cytoplasmic RNA viruses, such as chikungunya (CHIKV) and dengue (DENV), are reported to be extensively m6A-modified. However, these findings are mostly based on m6A-seq, an antibody-dependent technique with a high rate of false positives. Here, we addressed the presence of m6A in CHIKV and DENV RNAs. For this, we combined m6A-seq and the antibody-independent SELECT and nanopore direct RNA sequencing techniques with functional, molecular, and mutagenesis studies. Following this comprehensive analysis, we found no evidence of m6A modification in CHIKV or DENV transcripts. Furthermore, depletion of key components of the host m6A machinery did not affect CHIKV or DENV infection. Moreover, CHIKV or DENV infection had no effect on the m6A machinery’s localization. Our results challenge the prevailing notion that m6A modification is a general feature of cytoplasmic RNA viruses and underscore the importance of validating RNA modifications with orthogonal approaches.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>HEK293T</strong> (ATCC; CRL-11268; female) and <strong>Huh7</strong> (a gift from Prof. Francis Chisari, The Scripps Research Institute) cells were cultured at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium with glutamine (DMEM) (Gibco) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco) and 1% (v/v) non-essential amino acids (Gibco).&nbsp;Stocks of Chikungunya virus (<strong>CHIKV</strong>) strain LR2006-OPY1 (GenBank: DQ443544, kindly provided by Prof. A. Merits, University of Tartu) were generated in <strong>BHK-21</strong> cells and titered by standard plaque assay in <strong>HEK293T</strong> cells. <strong>Poly(A)+ RNA</strong> was isolated from <strong>mock-</strong> or <strong>CHIKV-infected HEK293T</strong> and <strong>Huh7</strong> cells at <strong>12 h </strong>post-infection (p.i.), a time at which ~50% of ribodepleted RNA reads correspond to <strong>CHIKV RNAs[1]</strong>, and at multiplicity of&nbsp;infection&nbsp;(MOI) of 4 (virus titers were determined by plaque assay using <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>HEK293T</strong> cells).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Jungfleisch, J., Böttcher, R., Talló-Parra, M., Pérez-Vilaró,&nbsp;G., Merits,&nbsp;A., Novoa, E. M., Díez, J. CHIKV infection reprograms codon optimality to favor viral RNA translation by altering the tRNA epitranscriptome.&nbsp;<em>Nat Commun</em>&nbsp;13, 4725 (2022). doi:10.1038/s41467-022-31835-x. PMID:35953468.</p>	<p><strong>Total RNA</strong> was extracted using <strong>TRIzol</strong> (Thermo Fisher) according to the supplier’s protocol and <strong>TURBO DNA-free Kit</strong> (Thermo Fisher) was used to remove any contaminating DNA from RNA samples. Isolated <strong>RNA</strong> was further purified by <strong>ethanol</strong> precipitation and 30-40 µg of total RNA were subjected to <strong>poly(A)-selection</strong> with the use of Dynabeads Oligo (dT)25 (Thermo Fisher) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Isolated poly(A)+ RNA was firstly subjected to a clean-up and concentration step using the RNA clean &amp; concentrator kit (Zymo Research). Then, <strong>200 ng of poly(A)+ RNA</strong> were digested&nbsp;with a nucleoside digestion mix (New England Biolabs) at 37 °C for 1 h. Samples were further desalted using HyperSep Hypercarb SPE Spin Tips (Thermo Fisher) and run using LC/MS-MS. Approximately <strong>100 ng</strong> of digested ribonucleosides were analyzed for<strong> HEK293T</strong> samples and around <strong>75 ng</strong> for <strong>Huh7</strong> samples.</p>	<p>Samples were run using a <strong>Thermo Scientific EASY-nLC 300 System (UPLC)</strong>. Ribonucleosides were loaded directly onto the analytical column and were separated by reversed-phase chromatography using a 50-cm homemade column with an inner diameter of 75 μm, packed with 4 μm Hydro-RP 80 Å (Phenomenex cat 04A-4375), as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Chromatographic gradients started at 95% buffer A and 5% buffer B with a flow rate of 250 nL/min for 5 min and gradually increased to 20% buffer B in 40 min. After each analysis, the column was washed for 10 min with 20% buffer A and 80% buffer B. Buffer A consisted of 20 mM ammonium acetate pH 4.5 and buffer B was 95% acetonitrile and 5% 20 mM ammonium acetate pH 4.5.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Sarin, L.P., Kienast, S.D., Leufken, J., Ross, R.L., Dziergowska, A., Debiec, K., Sochacka, E., Limbach, P.A., Fufezan, C., Drexler, H.C.A., Leidel, S.A. 2018. Nano LC-MS using capillary columns enables accurate quantification of modified ribonucleosides at low femtomol levels. RNA 24(10):1403-1417 (2018).&nbsp;doi:10.1261/rna.065482.117. PMID:30012570.</p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using a <strong>Thermo Scientific LTQ Orbitrap XL</strong> operated in positive ionisation mode with nanospray voltage set at 2 kV and source temperature at 200 °C. <strong>Full MS scans</strong> were set at 1 μscan with a resolution of 60,000 and a mass range of <strong>100-700 m/z</strong> in the Orbitrap mass analyzer. A list of masses was defined for further fragmentation (see <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with the study). Fragment ion spectra were produced via collision-induced dissociation (CID) at normalised collision energy of 35% and they were acquired in the ion trap mass analyzer. Isolation window was set to 2.0 m/z and activation time of 10 ms.</p>	<p>All data were acquired with <strong>Xcalibur software v2.1</strong>. Acquired data were analyzed with the <strong>Skyline software (v20.2.0.343)</strong> and extracted precursor areas of the ribonucleosides were used for quantification. Modified ribonucleosides were normalized by the median of the precursor area of AUGC.</p>	<p>Serial dilutions were prepared using commercial pure ribonucleosides (1-2000 pg/µL, Carbosynth, Toronto Research Chemicals) to establish the linear range of quantification and the limit of detection of each compound. See <strong>Table S8</strong> in the paper associated with the study for the commercial ribonucleosides catalogue numbers. A mix of commercial ribonucleosides was injected before and after each batch of samples to assess the instrument stability and to be used as an external standard to calibrate the retention time of each ribonucleoside. </p>

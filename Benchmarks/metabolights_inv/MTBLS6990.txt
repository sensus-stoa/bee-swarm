Study Identifier	MTBLS6990
Study Title	Serum metabolomic profiles for distinguishing lung cancer from pulmonary tuberculosis: identification of rapid and noninvasive biomarker
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:</strong> Pulmonary tuberculosis (TB) and lung cancer (LC) have similar clinical symptoms and atypical imaging findings which are easily misdiagnosed. There is an urgent need for a noninvasive, rapid and accurate biomarker for distinguishing LC from TB.</p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> A total of 694 subjects were enrolled and divided into discovery set (n=122), validation set (n=214) and another validation set (n=358). Metabolites were identified by multivariate and univariate analyses. The random forest (RF) algorithm and receiver operating characteristic curve analysis were used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of the biomarkers.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> Seven metabolites were identified and validated. The mean decrease accuracy of phenylalanylphenylalanine ranked first in RF analysis. The area under the curve of phenylalanylphenylalanine was 0.8885 (95% CI 0.8444-0.9326), the sensitivity was 71.43%, and the specificity was 92.13%. Compared with that in NC subjects, the level of phenylalanylphenylalanine was elevated in LC patients (fold change = 2.92, p value &lt; 0.01) and reduced in TB patients (fold change = 0.80, p value &lt; 0.05).</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> The metabolomic profile of LC and TB was described and a key biomarker, phenylalanylphenylalanine, was identified. We produced a rapid and noninvasive method to supplement existing clinical diagnostic examinations for distinguishing LC from TB.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 694 subjects were recruited in this study between 2020 and 2021.&nbsp;<strong>Lung cancer patients</strong> (<strong>LC</strong>, n=262), <strong>healthy volunteers</strong> (<strong>NC</strong>, n=220)&nbsp;and <strong>pneumonia patients</strong> (<strong>P</strong>, n=30)&nbsp;were recruited from&nbsp;the Second Hospital of Tianjin Medical University; <strong>Pulmonary tuberculosis patients</strong> (<strong>TB</strong>, n=182)&nbsp;were recruited&nbsp;from Tianjin Haihe Hospital.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>The inclusion criteria for the disease groups were as follows: (1) patients with a confirmed pathological diagnosis of LC; (2) patients with confirmed TB according to established international guidelines (pathological diagnosis, molecular biological diagnosis, and sputum examinations (smear and culture))<strong>[1]</strong>, and the TB included in this study were post-primary tuberculosis; and (3) patients with detailed medical record information. Healthy volunteers were confirmed to be healthy after a health checkup and had no history of related diseases. The details of exclusion criteria were shown in the <strong>Supplemental Information</strong> in the paper associated with this study.</p><p><br></p><p>After the samples were included in strict accordance with the study criteria, the samples meeting the criteria were randomly grouped.&nbsp;Then, the clinical information of the samples was statistically matched. Among them, 55 LC, 37 NC and 30 TB serum samples comprised the <strong>discovery set</strong>, and 63 NC, 63 TB and 88 LC serum samples comprised the <strong>validation set</strong>. These samples were collected and assessed by using nontargeted metabolomic analysis based on UPLC-Q-TOF/MS to identify and verify biomarkers. Subsequently, another <strong>independent set</strong> comprising 120 NC, 89 TB, 119 LC and 30 P serum samples was subjected to targeted metabolomic analysis based on UPLC (I-Class)-MS (XEVO TQD).</p><p><br></p><p>This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the ethical rules of good clinical practice and was approved (approval number KY2020K089) by the ethics committee of the Second Hospital of Tianjin Medical University. We obtained all subjects’ informed consent. This study was registered in the China Clinical Trial Registration Center (registration number ChiCTR2000040666, Registered 07 December 2020, http://www.chictr.org.cn/index.aspx), and the registration unit is the Second Hospital of Tianjin Medical University.</p><p><br></p><p>All blood samples used in this study were collected into the serum blood collection tubes after an overnight fast. Then, the blood samples were separated by centrifugation at 3500 r/min for 10 min at 4 °C within 2 h Subsequently, the supernatant was removed and divided into 2 mL cryotubes, ensured at least 1 mL in each tube. At last, the serum samples were immediately stored at -80 °C until metabolomic analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>Equal amount of each serum sample was pipetted into a centrifuge tube to prepare quality control (QC) sample, so as to conduct methodological investigation. The same QC sample made 6 consecutive injections (instrument precision test), 6 QC samples were prepared in parallel and continuously injected for analysis (method precision test), and the same QC sample was injected at 6 timepoints during the whole injection process (sample stability test).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Lewinsohn DM, Leonard MK, LoBue PA, Cohn DL, Daley CL, Desmond E, Keane J, Lewinsohn DA, Loeffler AM, Mazurek GH, O'Brien RJ, Pai M, Richeldi L, Salfinger M, Shinnick TM, Sterling TR, Warshauer DM, Woods GL. Official American Thoracic Society/Infectious Diseases Society of America/Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Clinical Practice Guidelines: Diagnosis of Tuberculosis in Adults and Children. Clin Infect Dis. 2017 Jan 15;64(2):111-115. doi:10.1093/cid/ciw778. PMID:28052967.</p>	<p><strong>Nontargeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>The samples stored at -80 °C were completely thawed in the refrigerator at 4 °C, and 80 μL of serum was mixed with acetonitrile at a 1:3 volume ratio. After swirling for 1 min, the samples were ultrasonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min and centrifuged at 4 °C for 15 min at 13,000 rpm.&nbsp;Then, 200 μL supernatant was obtained for nontargeted metabonomic analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>According to the physicochemical properties of the specific metabolite and to ensure that the measurement could be performed within the linear detection range, the extraction of serum metabolites in targeted analysis are as follows:&nbsp;The samples stored at -80 °C were thawed completely in the refrigerator at 4 °C.&nbsp;Then, 5 μL serum&nbsp;was placed into an eppendorf&nbsp;tube, and 995 μL&nbsp;methanol&nbsp;was added.&nbsp;The solution was vortexed for 1 min, allowed to stand for 5 min and centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 10 min, and the supernatant was then obtained for targeted metabolomic analysis.</p>	<p><strong>Nontargeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>Waters ACQUITY UPLC (Waters Corporation, USA) was used under the following conditions: chromatographic column, UPLC BEH C18 (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters Corporation, Milford, USA); column temperature, 45 °C; flow rate, 0.3 mL/min; injection volume, 5 μL; and mobile phase composition, A 0.1% formic acid (chromatography-grade formic acid (ROE, USA)) in water and B 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The elution gradient was 0.0-0.5 min, 1% B; 0.5-2.0 min, 1-50% B; 2.0-9.0 min, 50-99% B; 9.0-10.0 min, 99% B; 10.0-10.5 min, 99-1% B; and 10.5-12.0 min, 1% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>The chromatographic column was a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 column and the column and sample-chamber temperatures were 35 and 4 °C, respectively. The mobile phase comprised A, namely, 0.1% formic acid in water, and B, namely, 1% formic acid water in acetonitrile; the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min and the injection volume was 1 μL. The specific elution gradient was 0.0-1.0 min, 5% B; 1.0-2.3 min, 5-15% B; 2.3-3.0 min, 15-100% B; 3.0-4.5 min, 100% B; 4.5-5.0 min, 100-5% B; and 5.0–7.0 min, 5% B.</p>	<p><strong>Nontargeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>Using electrospray ionization (ESI source), XEVO-G2QTOF YCA345 mass spectrometric analysis was performed in positive ionization mode&nbsp;with the following conditions: capillary voltage, 2.0 kV; ionization source temperature, 100 °C; dry gas flow rate, 10 mL/min; elevation flow rate, 600 L/D; desolvation temperature, 450 °C; cone air flow rate, 50 L/D; and quadrupole scanning range 50-1000 m/z. In the process of sample injection, to avoid batch effects, each group of samples was injected alternately.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomic analysis</strong></p><p>UPLC (I-Class)-MS (XEVO-TQD QCA446) was used for targeted analysis.&nbsp;The source voltage was ESI+&nbsp;3.0 kV, source temperature was 400 °C, gas-flow rate was 700 L/h and cone rate was 50 L/h. The ion pair information of phenylalanylphenylalanine was 313.1/119.9, the cone voltage was 30 V and the collision voltage was 18 V. A series of 250, 100, 50, 25, 10, 5, 2.5, 1, and 0.5 ng/mL solutions were prepared for use as the standard solution, and the standard curve was detected every day to determine the linearity and the minimum limit of quantitation.</p>	<p>Data collected by the instrument were passed through the <strong>MassLynx</strong> (version 4.1) data processing system. The data obtained included retention time, m/z and normalized peak area. Molecular Weight Tolerance: 0.01 Da. Retention time error: 0.5 min. The data were removed according to the 80% rule, and the missing values were filled by the k-nearest neighbor method.</p><p><br></p><p>The data after preprocessing were imported into <strong>SIMCA-P</strong> (version 14.1, Umetrics, Sweden) for multivariate statistical analysis. Unsupervised principal component analysis (PCA) and supervised partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) models were established to identify potential discriminant variables. PCA was used to remove outlier samples. The discovery set excluded 5 TB samples (t2, t4, t11, t12, t15), 2 LC samples (c2, c18) and 1 NC (k1) sample. The validation set excluded 12 TB samples (T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T7, T17, T18, T19, T30, T39, T46), 2 LC samples (C5, C6) and 4 NC samples (K9, K39, K48, K54). PLS-DA was used to determine the differential metabolites of different groups. The variable importance in projection (VIP &gt; 1) parameter of the metabolic ion in the PLS-DA model and Student’s t test (<strong>SPSS</strong> 25.0, IBM, US) were used to screen out the differential metabolites (adjusted P value &lt; 0.05) between 2 groups. The P values were adjusted to account by Benjamini-Hochberg method (<strong>BioLadder</strong>, China)<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>Pathway enrichment analysis, heatmap and random forest (RF) were performed on <strong>MetaboAnalyst</strong> 5.0<strong>[2]</strong>. RF is an integrated machine learning algorithm, and the function of variable importance analysis was applied<strong>[3]</strong>. The higher the mean decrease accuracy (MDA) is, the greater the contribution to classification. A binary logistic regression model was established for potential biomarkers, and ROC curves were generated based on <strong>SPSS</strong> and <strong>GraphPad Prism</strong> 8.3.0 to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of the biomarkers. To test whether the biomarkers were triggered by interindividual differences, we conducted Spearman analysis (<strong>SPSS</strong> 25.0).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Zhou J, Zhong L. Applications of liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry based metabolomics in predictive and personalized medicine. Front Mol Biosci. 2022 Nov 3;9:1049016. doi:10.3389/fmolb.2022.1049016. PMID:36406271.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Pang Z, Zhou G, Ewald J, Chang L, Hacariz O, Basu N, Xia J. Using MetaboAnalyst 5.0 for LC-HRMS spectra processing, multi-omics integration and covariate adjustment of global metabolomics data. Nat Protoc. 2022 Aug;17(8):1735-1761. doi:10.1038/s41596-022-00710-w. Epub 2022 Jun 17. PMID:35715522.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Yan J, Li D, Guo R, Yan H, Wang Y. [Research on the classification of dentate tongue based on deep learning and random forest[J/OL]]. Chinese Archives of Traditional Chinese Medicine.</p>	<p>The m/z was used to search through <strong>HMDB</strong> (<a href='https://hmdb.ca/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://hmdb.ca/</a>) and substances with an error value of less than 20 ppm were further reviewed. According to the secondary fragment information, the differential metabolites were determined preliminarily.</p>

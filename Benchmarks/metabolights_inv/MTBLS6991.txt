STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS6991
Study Title	Targeted metabolomics data from two freshwater strains of SAR11
Study Description	In an abundant aquatic groups of microorganisms, SAR11, the transition between salt- and freshwater environments has happened only once: all freshwater SAR11 belong to subclade IIIb/LD12, which has also been found to inhabit coastal environments where salinity varies widely. The first reported isolates of the SAR11 freshwater clade LD12 and a member of the sister clade IIIa from the same region are now available. This project quantifed concentrations of select, known intracellular metabolites within two strains of SAR11: LSUCC0261 and LSUCC0530.

Study Protocol Description	<p>2 strains for members of the Alphaproteobacteria order Pelagibacteriales (SAR11) were cultured in the laboratory. LSUCC0261 is an isolate from the SAR11 subclade IIIa and was grown in JW2 media<strong>[1]</strong>. LSUCC0530 is a freshwater strain in the LD12 subclade, and was grown in JW5 media<strong>[2]</strong>. Cells were harvested at late exponential phase by filtration onto 0.1 µm Omnipore (Millipore) filters. The filters were stored frozen at -80 °C until extraction in the laboratory. In the metadata, 'volume' is the volume filtered for each sample. For each sample, 'cells' is the number of cells per ml.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Henson MW, Pitre DM, Weckhorst JL, Lanclos VC, Webber AT, Thrash JC. Artificial Seawater Media Facilitate Cultivating Members of the Microbial Majority from the Gulf of Mexico. mSphere. 2016 Apr 27;1(2):e00028-16. doi:10.1128/mSphere.00028-16. Erratum in: mSphere. 2016 May-Jun;1(3). pii:e00124-16. doi:10.1128/mSphere.00124-16. Erratum in: mSphere. 2018 Aug 15;3(4): PMID:27303734.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Henson MW, Lanclos VC, Faircloth BC, Thrash JC. Cultivation and genomics of the first freshwater SAR11 (LD12) isolate. ISME J. 2018 Jun;12(7):1846-1860. doi:10.1038/s41396-018-0092-2. Erratum in: ISME J. 2020 Mar;14(3):877. PMID:29599519.</p>	<p>A protocol adapted from<strong>[1]</strong> was used for extraction of intracellular metabolites. Filters were removed from the cryovials and cut into small pieces with methanol-rinsed scissors on combusted aluminum foil. The pieces of the filter were placed in an 8 ml combusted amber glass vial and 1 ml of -20 °C 40:40:20 acetonitrile:methanol:water + 0.1 M formic acid was added to each vial. 25 µl of 1 µg/ml deuterated standard mix (d3-glutamic acid, d4-4-hydroxybenzoic acid, and d5-taurocholate) were added to act as extraction recovery standards. The vials were vortexed to shake apart filter pieces and fully expose them to the ice-cold solvent. They were sonicated for 10 min and the extract was transferred to micro-centrifuge tubes using Pasteur pipettes. The filter pieces were rinsed 3x with 500 µl of extraction solvent and the rinse was added to the eppendorf tubes. The extracts were centrifuged at 20,000 x g for 5 min. The supernatant was transferred to new 8 ml amber glass vials leaving behind any scraps of filter or pieces of cellular detritus. The extracts were neutralized with 51.2 µl of 6 M ammonium hydroxide and dried down in a vacufuge. The samples were reconstituted in 100 µl labeled injection mix (95:5 water:acetonitrile containing D2 biotin, D6 succinic acid, D4 cholic acid, and D7 indole 3 acetic acid). The solution (100 µl) was placed in a glass insert in an autosampler vial for targeted metabolomic analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Rabinowitz JD, Kimball E. Acidic acetonitrile for cellular metabolome extraction from Escherichia coli. Anal Chem. 2007 Aug 15;79(16):6167-73. doi:10.1021/ac070470c. PMID:17630720.</p>	<p>Chromatography was performed using an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (Vanquish UHPLC, Thermo Scientific) coupled with an Altis Plus triple quadrupole mass spectrometer with a heated electrospray ionization source (H-ESI). A Waters Acquity HSS T3 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.8 μm), equipped with a Waters Vanguard pre-column, was used for chromatographic separation at 40 °C. The column was eluted at 0.5 ml/min with a combination of solvents: <strong>A)</strong> 0.1% formic acid in water and <strong>B)</strong> 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The chromatographic gradient was as follows: 1% B (1 min), 15% B (1-3 min), 50% B (3-6 min), 95% B (6-9 min), 95% B (10 min). The column was washed and re-equilibrated with 1% B (2 min) between 5 μl autosampler injections. A single 5 μl injection was used for each sample.</p>	<p>The Altis Plus triple quadrupole mass spectrometer was operated under selected reaction monitoring (SRM) mode, with at least 2 SRM transitions monitored for each target compound. The instrument was used with polarity switching to allow both positive and negative ion mode transitions to be collected with a single injection for each sample. ESI spray voltage was set at 3000 V, and source gases were set at 60 (sheath), 15 (auxillary), and 1 (sweep). The heated capillary temperature was 380 °C, and the vaporizer temperature was 350 °C. For compound quantification, a standard mixture of all targeted metabolites was run at 0, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, and 1000 ng/ml concentrations. Samples were run in a random order, with pooled samples injected throughout the sample run.</p>	<p>The resulting data files were converted to <strong>mzML</strong> files using the <strong>msConvert</strong> tool<strong>[1]</strong> and processed with <strong>El-MAVEN[2]</strong>. Data are given as ng per ml in the extract. The extract volume for all samples is 100 μl.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Chambers MC, Maclean B, Burke R, Amodei D, Ruderman DL, Neumann S, Gatto L et al. A cross-platform toolkit for mass spectrometry and proteomics. Nat Biotechnol. 2012 Oct;30(10):918-20. doi:10.1038/nbt.2377. PMID:23051804.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Agrawal S, Kumar S, Sehgal R, George S, Gupta R, Poddar S, Jha A, Pathak S. El-MAVEN: A Fast, Robust, and User-Friendly Mass Spectrometry Data Processing Engine for Metabolomics. Methods Mol Biol. 2019;1978:301-321. doi:10.1007/978-1-4939-9236-2_19. PMID:31119671.</p>	<p>The targeted metabolomics compound identifications were based on measurements of authentic standards on the same mass spectrometer. Identifications were '<strong>MSI Level 1</strong>' identifications, unless otherwise noted, based on the established criteria<strong>[1]</strong>. The method in use cannot separate alanine and sacrosine, the concentrations are reported as '<strong>alanine/sarcosine</strong>'. The method also cannot separate threonine and homoserine, the concentrations are reported as '<strong>threonine/homoserine'</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Sumner L, Amberg A, Barrett D, Beale M, Beger R, Daykin C, Fan T, Fiehn O, Goodacre R, Griffin J, Hankemeier T, Hardy N, Harnly J, Higashi R, Kopka J, Lane A, Lindon J, Marriott P, Nicholls A, Reily M, Thaden J, and Viant M (2007) Proposed minimum reporting standards for chemical analysis. Metabolomics 3, 211-221. doi:10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2. PMID:3772505</p>

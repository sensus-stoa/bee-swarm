Study Identifier	MTBLS7053
Study Title	Lsr2, a novel cyclic di-GMP receptor, regulate the synthesis of keto-mycolic acid and control biofilm formation in mycobacteria
Study Description	<p>Cyclic di-GMP (c-di-GMP) is a well-known second messenger that plays a key role in many physiological processes in bacteria. The synthesis of lipids is essential for bacterial biofilm formation. However, whether c-di-GMP signaling modulates the synthesis of lipid and further regulates biofilm formation in mycobacteria is unclear, and the c-di-GMP receptor involved remains unknown. In this study, we characterized the nucleoid-associated protein (NAP) Lsr2 as a novel c-di-GMP receptor in mycobacteria. c-di-GMP specifically binds to Lsr2 at a ratio of 1:1. We showed that c-di-GMP promotes mycobacterial biofilm formation in a manner dependent on Lsr2. Furthermore, Lsr2 mediates the synthesis of keto-mycolic acid, the lipid component of the mycobacterial cell wall, by positively regulating the expression of HadD, a (3R)-hydroxyacyl-ACP dehydratase, thus, Lsr2 ultimately controls biofilm formation. Finally, c-di-GMP promotes the positive regulation of HadD by Lsr2 and mycobacterial biofilm formation. Thus, we report a novel c-di-GMP receptor that links the second messenger’s function to lipid synthesis and biofilm formation in mycobacteria.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong><em>Mycobacterium smegmatis</em></strong><em> </em><strong><em>MC2155 </em>WT</strong><em> </em>and<strong> knockout<em> </em>strains</strong>&nbsp;were cultured in 7H9 medium to <strong>OD600 1.2 for WT</strong> and <strong>OD600 1.0 for knockout strains</strong> and harvested for bacterial lipidomic analysis. Bacteria were collected by centrifugating at 6000 rpm for 5 min and washing with PBS 3 times. Thereafter, quantification of the bacteria was 1.0 g for 6 duplicates. Lipid extraction and analyses were performed at Novogene Co., Ltd (Beijing, China) as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Xu, H., Su, Z., Li, W., Deng, Y., He, Z.G. 2021. MmbR, a master transcription regulator that controls fatty acid beta oxidation genes in <em>Mycolicibacterium smegmatis</em>. Environ Microbiol 23(2):1096-1114, doi:10.1111/1462-2920.15249. PMID: 32985741.</p>	<p><strong>Methanol </strong>(<strong>0.75 mL</strong>) was added to a <strong>100 mg cells/sample </strong>in a glass tube with a Teflon lined cap, and the tube was vortexed. Then, <strong>2.5 mL of MTBE</strong> was added and the mixture was incubated for 1 h at room temperature in a shaker. Phase separation was induced by adding <strong>0.625 mL of MS-grade water</strong>. Upon 10 min of incubation at room temperature, the sample was centrifuged at 1000 x g for 10 min. The upper (organic) phase was collected, and the lower phase was re-extracted with <strong>1 mL of the solvent mixture</strong> used before (<strong>MTBE/methanol/water (10:3:2.5, v/v/v)</strong>), collecting the upper phase. Both organic phases were then combined, dried, and dissolved in <strong>100 μL of isopropanol</strong> for storage. Then analyzed by LC-MS/MS.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>QC</strong> samples were prepared by mixing equal volume from the samples to be analyzed. The same reagents used for samples are used for <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>blank sample</strong><span style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>, following the same procedure detailed before</span>.</p>	<p><strong>UHPLC-MS/MS</strong> analyses were performed by Novogene Co., Ltd (Beijing, China), using a <strong>Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System </strong>and a <strong>Accucore C30 (2.6 µm, 2.1 mm x 150 mm; Thermo Scientific)</strong> column, and a 20-min linear gradient at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min. The column temperature was set at 40 °C. Mobile phase A was acetonitrile/water (6/4) with 10 mM ammonium acetate and 0.1% formic acid, whereas mobile phase B was acetonitrile/isopropanol (1/9) with 10 mM ammonium acetate and 0.1% formic acid. The solvent gradient was set as follows: 0-2 min, 30% B; 5 min, 43% B; 5.1 min, 55% B; 11 min, 70% B; 16 min, 99% B; 18.1 min, 30% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>QC</strong> samples were injected at the beginning of the sample set, to monitor the instrument performance and balance the system, and mid set to evaluate the system stability during the whole experiment process and conduct sample correlation analysis. A <strong>blank </strong>was injected 3 times, with the first 2 injections being used to elute the residue of the previous sample and balance the whole system, and the last injection being used for background removal.</p>	<p>Analysis was performed using <strong>Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF</strong> mass spectrometer, operated in positive and negative polarity modes, using the following parameters: sheath gas, 40 psi; sweep gas, 0 L/min; auxiliary gas rate, 10 L/min for positive and 7 L/min for negative mode; spray voltage, 3.5 kV; capillary temperature, 320 °C; heater temperature, 350 °C; S-Lens RF level, 50. Scan range was <strong>114-1700 m/z;</strong> automatic gain control target, 3e^6; normalized collision energy, 22, 24 and 28eV for both positive and negative modes; injection time, 100 ms; isolation window, 1 m/z; automatic gain control target (MS2), 2e^5; dynamic exclusion, 6 s.</p>	<p>The raw data files generated by <strong>UHPLC-MS/MS</strong> were processed using the <strong>Compound Discoverer 3.01 (CD3.1, Thermo Fisher)</strong> to perform peak alignment, peak picking and quantitation for each metabolite. The main parameters were set as follows: retention time tolerance, 0.2 min; actual mass tolerance, 5 ppm; signal intensity tolerance, 30%; signal/noise ratio, 3 and minimum intensity, 100,000. After that, peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity.</p>	<p>The normalized data was used to predict the molecular formula based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks and fragment ions. And then peaks were matched with the <a href='http://www.lipidmaps.org' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Lipidmaps</strong></a>, <a href='https://fiehnlab.ucdavis.edu/projects/lipidblast/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Lipidblast</strong></a> and <strong>Mtb LipidDB[1] </strong>databases to obtain the accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results. Background ions were removed using a blank sample. Statistical analyses were performed using the statistical software <a href='https://www.r-project.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>R</strong></a> <strong>(R version R-3.4.3)</strong>, <a href='https://www.python.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Python</strong></a> <strong>(Python 2.7.6 version)</strong> and <a href='https://www.centos.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>CentOS</strong></a><strong> (CentOS release 6.6)</strong>, When data were not normally distributed, normal transformations were attempted using of area normalization method.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Sartain, M.J., Dick, D.L., Rithner, C.D., Crick, D.C., Belisle, J.T. 2011. Lipidomic analyses of Mycobacterium tuberculosis based on accurate mass measurements and the novel 'Mtb LipidDB'. J Lipid Res. 52(5):861-72. doi:10.1194/jlr.M010363. PMID: 21285232; PMCID: PMC3073466.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS711
Study Title	Fecal microbiota transplantation plus anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in advanced melanoma: a phase I trial
Study Description	<p>Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) represents a potential strategy to overcome resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with refractory melanoma; however, the role of FMT in first-line treatment settings has not been evaluated. We conducted a multicenter phase I trial combining healthy donor FMT with the PD-1 inhibitors nivolumab or pembrolizumab in 20 previously untreated patients with advanced melanoma. The primary end point was safety. No grade 3 adverse events were reported from FMT alone. Five patients (25%) experienced grade 3 immune-related adverse events from combination therapy. Key secondary end points were objective response rate, changes in gut microbiome composition and systemic immune and metabolomics analyses. The objective response rate was 65% (13 of 20), including four (20%) complete responses. Longitudinal microbiome profiling revealed that all patients engrafted strains from their respective donors; however, the acquired similarity between donor and patient microbiomes only increased over time in responders. Responders experienced an enrichment of immunogenic and a loss of deleterious bacteria following FMT. Avatar mouse models confirmed the role of healthy donor feces in increasing anti-PD-1 efficacy. Our results show that FMT from healthy donors is safe in the first-line setting and warrants further investigation in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. ClinicalTrials.gov identifier <a href='https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03772899' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>NCT03772899</a> .</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Samples</strong></p><p>The samples were plasma collected serially from human participants in a phase I clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT03772899), exploring the impact of a single healthy donor capsulised fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) prior to anti-PD1 immunotherapy in patients with advanced melanoma. Specifically, these were 20 human adults with advanced melanoma, without prior treatment with anti-PD1 immunotherapy, who - in a single arm, unblinded trial - provided plasma both before and after intervention. The participants were recruited across three academic centres in Canada.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Sampling time points</strong></p><p>S1: Collected just prior to FMT (which was also before anti-PD1 immunotherapy)</p><p>S2: Collected 7 days post FMT, just prior to commencing first cycle of anti-PD1 immunotherapy</p><p>S3: Collected between 3-4 weeks post first cycle of anti-PD1 immunotherapy, just prior to second cycle of anti-PD1 immunotherapy</p><p>S4: Collected two months after second cycle of anti-PD1 immunotherapy, prior to further anti-PD1 immunotherapy cycle</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Sample handling and aliquoting</strong></p><p>Plasma samples were received on dry ice dry ice and transferred for analysis at the National Phenome Centre, Imperial College London where they were immediately stored at -80 °C.</p><p> </p><p>Meta-data variables essential to the design of the study were identified and used to determine sample analysis orders that were uncorrelated with those factors using in-house software, avoiding the potential for confounding critical clinical variables with analytical run order effects. For each study, this order was preloaded into an in-house laboratory information management system (LIMS). Samples were then sorted into sets of 80 using aluminium racks (9x9 position format) cooled by direct contact with dry ice to ensure that all samples remained frozen for the duration of the sorting exercise. Completed sets were stored at -80 °C until required for subsequent steps.</p><p> </p><p>Once sorted, individual plasma sample sets were reformatted into 96-well plates. This step required the overnight thawing of a set of 80 samples at 4 °C, mixing of each individual sample by vortexing, and transfer of the liquid contents of each sample to a well within a 96-deep-well polypropylene plate (2 mL, Eppendorf). During this process, each sample was checked against a LIMS-generated map of sample identifiers and positions in the 96-well plate to validate the batch composition and sample order. Study samples were added only to the wells within the first 10 columns of rows A through H; columns 11 and 12 of the plate were reserved for the later addition of pooled quality control (QC) reference samples. The plate was sealed with a silicone cap mat and centrifuged at 3,486 × g for 10 min at 4 °C using an Eppendorf 5810R centrifuge equipped with an A-2-DWP-AT rotor for the removal of suspended particulate matter such as cryoprecipitate, cellular debris, or crystalline material. The supernatant was aspirated and dispensed in pre-programmed volumes among 96-well polypropylene plates using a multi-channel pipette. The volume dispensed into plates according to assay:</p><ul><li>Hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) profiling: 50 μL to a 0.5 mL plate</li><li>NMR: 350 μL to a 2.0 mL plate</li><li>Reversed-phase chromatography (RPC) for bile acid profiling (BAP): 50 μL to a 0.5 mL plate</li><li>Tryptophan targeted assay: 30 μL to a 0.5 mL plate</li></ul><p>All plates were sealed with a silicone cap mat and returned to -80 °C.</p>	<p><strong>Preparation of Method Reference (MR) and Internal Standards (IS) mixtures</strong></p><p>Mixtures of reference chemicals were generated for LC-MS methods, enabling the targeted assessment of instrument stability (e.g. chromatographic retention, mass accuracy, and signal intensity) and data quality. For further details see Lewis et al. 2022 [Lewis et al. 2022]</p><p> </p><p><strong>Preparation of Quality Control (QC) samples</strong></p><p>QC samples were analysed at regular intervals throughout the run and utilized to support the assessment of data quality within each experiment.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Long Term Reference (LTR)</strong></p><p>A pooled Long-Term Reference (LTR) plasma sample is maintained by the laboratory and is used as an independent sample reference throughout all molecular profiling studies. To create this material, 10 L of bulk plasma were purchased from Seralab, homogenised, centrifuged and aliquoted for long term storage. Plasma LTR samples were mixed with assay-specific HILIC MR mixture in a 5:1 LTR:MR ratio.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Study Reference (SR)</strong></p><p>A pooled Study Reference (SR) sample representing the mean of samples within the study was prepared. The SR was created by thawing the contents of all SR sample plates (with 50 μL of each sample) at 4°C and combining their contents in an 800 mL glass beaker. The pooled sample was homogenized by swirling and portioned in an approximate 3:1 ratio for use with RPC and HILIC assays respectively. The HILIC SR sub-fraction was mixed with HILIC-specific MR mixture in a 5:1SR:MR.</p><p>All SR materials were frozen at -80 °C until required for analytical plate preparation.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Dilution series</strong></p><p>A dilution series of SR samples was prepared for each assay type from thawed SR in the same manner described above except that they were prepared in bulk volume in Eppendorf microcentrifuge tubes and the products were stored in polypropylene sample vials for analysis supporting multiple injections from the same stock. Prior to and immediately following the analysis of each experiment’s samples, dilution series samples were analysed by repeated measurement aimed at weighting the ends of the series (10,10,5,3,3,5, and 10 consecutive replicate injections of the 1%, 10%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% SR samples respectively).</p><p> </p><p>Further details on sample handling and preparation, and QC samples can be found in Lewis et al. 2022 [Lewis et al. 2022]</p><p> </p><p><strong>HILIC sample preparation</strong></p><p>Individual aliquot plates were removed from -80 °C storage approximately 3 h prior to their scheduled analysis and thawed at 4 °C. All study samples were diluted with 25 μL ultrapure water, commensurate with the volume of QC sample dilution caused by the addition of MR. LTR stock and SR stock aliquots were thawed and 75 μL distributed into columns 11 and 12. Finally, the 25 μL IS solution was added to all samples. Acetonitrile (ACN) was added to samples for HILIC analysis in a 3:1 ACN:sample ratio to better match the initial solvent conditions of the chromatographic method. The sample plate was then sealed with Thermo-Seal heat sealing foil sheets using an ALPS 50 V-Manual Heat Sealer (Thermo SCIENTIFIC) prior to mixing for 1 minute at 4 °C using a MixMate (Eppendorf) operating at 850 rpm. The plates were subsequently centrifuged for 10 min at 3486 × g to remove any residual protein precipitated by this addition of organic solvent, and a 150 μL volume of the supernatant was decanted to a microplate for analysis. Once prepared, plates were loaded into the UPLC sample manager where they were held at 4 °C until analysis.</p><p> </p><p><strong>BAP sample preparation</strong></p><p>Reversed-phase (RP) UHPLC–MS was used for bile acid profiling, adapting a previously described protocol [Sarafian 2015]. For bile acid extraction from plasma, 50 µl plasma was mixed with 60 µl water, 10 µl internal standards and 360 µl methanol. The sample plate was then sealed with Thermo-Seal heat sealing foil sheets using an ALPS 50 V-Manual Heat Sealer (Thermo SCIENTIFIC) prior to mixing for 1 minute at 4 °C using a MixMate (Eppendorf) operating at 1400 rpm. Samples were subsequently incubated at −20 °C for 4 hours and then centrifuged for 10 min at 3486 × g. A 150 μL volume of the supernatant was decanted to a microplate for analysis. Once prepared, plates were loaded into the UPLC sample manager where they were held at 4 °C until analysis. Two microliters were injected into the platform.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Tryptophan pathway targeted sample preparation</strong></p><p>A total of 14 tryptophan pathway metabolites were quantified using UHPLC–MS/MS, as previously described [Whiley 2019]. For sample preparation, 30 µl plasma was spiked with internal standards. Protein precipitation and removal was performed using a two-step protocol consisting of addition of methanol followed by pass-through solid phase extraction. Samples were taken to dryness under nitrogen and resuspended in 10 mM ammonium formate with 0.5 % formic acid. Five microliters were injected onto the UHPLC–MS/MS system.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><ul><li>[Lewis et al. 2022] Lewis M, Chekmeneva E, Camuzeaux S, Sands C, Yuen A, David M, et al. The solvent system was ACN with 0.1% formic acid (A) and 20 mM ammonium formate in water with 0.1%formic acid (B) with a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min. After a 0.1 min isocratic separation at initial conditions (95% A), a two-stage gradient was conducted. First, a shallow linear gradient between 95% and 80% A was used followed by a more rapid gradient from 80% to 50% A. Following a return to initial composition, the flow rate was increased to 1.0 mL/min to expedite extended equilibration of the chromatographic system. Further details on the gradient conditions can be found in in Lewis et al. 2016 [Lewis et al. 2016].</p><p><br></p><p><strong>BAP</strong></p><p>For liquid chromatography, an ACQUITY BEH C8 column (1.7 μm, 100 × 2.1 mm) was used at an operating temperature of 60 °C.

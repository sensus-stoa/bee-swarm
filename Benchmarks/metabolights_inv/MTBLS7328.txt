Study Identifier	MTBLS7328
Study Title	A next-generation yeast chassis: comprehensive characterization of a robust fast-growing Saccharomyces cerevisiae
Study Description	<p>Robust and efficient hosts are necessary to facilitate advances in biotechnology. We unveil <em>Saccharomyces cerevisiae</em> strain XP as a promising new microbial chassis owing to its high growth rate and robustness. This strain has a doubling time of 48.2 min, which is 12.8 min faster than the model yeast <em>S. cerevisiae</em> S288C. Strain XP can grow well at low pH (≤ 3.0) and 30% (w/v) glucose under oligotrophic conditions. We establish a genetic manipulation toolbox using URA3 auxotroph and haploid strains, a Cre/loxP-based knocking-out method, and CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing tools. Genomic, transcriptomic and metabolomic data were analyzed in an integrated manner to characterize the strain. The multi-omics analysis reveals that changes in energy and essential nutrient metabolism are linked to rapid growth and robustness. A high-producing strain, XH352-D, was successfully constructed, which produced L-lactic acid with a titer of 52.6 g/L. Here, we comprehensively characterized a robust fast-growing yeast, laying foundation for its potential in industrial production of bulk chemicals.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Strains XP and S288C were cultivated in the YPD medium at 30 °C overnight, then inoculated 1% (v/v) to the FM and FM30 media in flasks. Cells were collected at logarithmic phase (OD600nm = 2), named XP_low sugar, XP_high sugar, S288c_low sugar, S288c_high sugar, respectively.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The reported method was used to extract metabolites in S. cerevisiae<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>The liquid was quickly filtered with a volume of 5 mL to avoid metabolite loss and extracellular metabolite contamination. Then the filter membrane was washed with 10 mL ddH2O. These processes should be complete within 30 s. If the OD600nm values were different, sampling volume was adjusted to make the number of cells consistent. Then the filtered cell masses attached to the filter membrane were mixed quickly with 20 mL of metabolite extraction solvent (acetonitrile:water (1:1, v/v) at -20 °C) and were frozen with liquid nitrogen. When preparing samples, the extraction mixture was thawed on ice, vortexed for 3 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C. The supernatants were frozen in a vacuum dry environment. Finally, the extracts were resuspended in 500 μL of fresh acetonitrile:water mixture (1:1, v/v) on the ice. A total of 6 biological replicates were performed for each sample.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> (69)</p>	<p>Thermo UPLC Q-Extractive (QE) coupled with ESI ion source was applied for untargeted metabolomics data acquisition. Thermo UPLC Q Extractive (QE) Orbitrap system using ESI ion source was applied for data acquisition included full MS, PRM and full MS-ddMS2 instrument methods. RP Zorbax Eclipse XBD-C18 column was applied for positive and negative mode analysis. The mobile phase was used for analysis with 0.1% formic acid in diluted water as aqueous phase A and 0.1% formic acid in pure acetonitrile as organic phase B. The LC gradient elution program was as follows: 0.0 min, 99% A; 5.0 min, 99% A; 5.5 min, 70% A; 9.0 min, 100% B; 11.0 min, 100% B; 12.1 min, 99% A.</p>	<p>The MS parameters of C18-ESI-MS in the analysis of positive ionization mode are as follows: the mass range was set from 80 to 1000 m/z, with a spectra collection rate of 2.0 Hz and capillary voltage of 4500 V; the gas flow rate of the nebulizer was 1.6 bar; the velocity of dry gas at 220 °C was 6.0 L/min; funnel 1 and 2 radio frequencies (RFs) were set as 150 Vpp and 200 Vpp; collision-induced dissociation (CID) energy was set as 0 eV; quadrupole ion energy was 5 eV; low mass was 50 m/z; collision cell energy was 7.0 eV; pre-pulse storage was 5.0 μs; collision RF ramp was from 400 to 800 Vpp; transfer time ramp was from 50 to 100 μs.</p>	<p>Full MS and Full MS-ddMS2 data were transformed to mzXML format by ms converter (ProteoWizard).

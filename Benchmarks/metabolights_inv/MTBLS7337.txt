Study Identifier	MTBLS7337
Study Title	Sequential multi-omics analysis identifies clinical phenotypes and predictive biomarkers for long COVID
Study Description	<p>The post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC), also known as long COVID, is often associated with debilitating symptoms and adverse multisystem consequences. We obtain plasma samples from 117 individuals during and 6&nbsp;mth following their acute phase of infection to comprehensively profile and assess changes in cytokines, proteome, and metabolome. Network analysis reveals sustained inflammatory response, platelet degranulation, and cellular activation during convalescence accompanied by dysregulation in arginine biosynthesis, methionine metabolism, taurine metabolism, and tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle processes. Furthermore, we develop a prognostic model composed of 20 molecules involved in regulating T&nbsp;cell exhaustion and energy metabolism that can reliably predict adverse clinical outcomes following discharge from acute infection with 83% accuracy and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.96. Our study reveals pertinent biological processes during convalescence that differ from acute infection, and it supports the development of specific therapies and biomarkers for patients suffering from long COVID.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The <strong>COVID-19</strong> Surveillance Collaboration (<strong>CoCollab</strong>) Study prospectively enrolled consecutive patients newly admitted to hospital wards designated for <strong>COVID-19</strong> and intensive care units at the University of Alberta Hospital (Edmonton, Canada) between October 15, 2020, and June 29, 2021 (see <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>Figure</strong> <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>S7</strong> in the paper associated with the study). All enrolled patients were ≥ 18 yr of age with a laboratory-confirmed <strong>COVID-19</strong> diagnosis based on a positive <strong>SARS-CoV-2</strong> real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assay from nasopharyngeal swabs or lower respiratory samples. Comparisons were made with age and sex-matched <strong>healthy controls</strong> (n=28) enrolled during the same period. Our study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles for the Declaration of Helsinki with approval from the University of Alberta Health Research Ethics Board (Pro00100319 and Pro00100207). Written and informed consent was obtained from all participants.</p><p><br></p><p>Venous blood sampling was performed in the morning by trained phlebotomists and transported to the Canadian Biosample Repository located at the University of Alberta within 1 h for immediate processing. Samples were collected in tubes containing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and centrifuged at 1500 x g for 10 min at room temperature. <strong>Plasma</strong> was subsequently aliquoted for storage at -80 °C. <strong>Baseline</strong> sampling of acute <strong>COVID-19</strong> was performed<strong> immediately following hospital admission</strong>, while pre-scheduled <strong>follow-up </strong>blood sampling at <strong> 6 mth</strong> was collected either from the patient’s location of residence by the study team or at the Kaye Edmonton Clinic (Edmonton, Canada).</p><p><br></p><p>A custom-made, <strong>targeted</strong> quantitative metabolomics approach was applied to analyze the samples using a combination of <strong>LC-MS/MS</strong> and direct injection mass spectrometry (<strong>DI-MS</strong>). This custom <strong>LC-MS</strong> assay can be used for the targeted identification and quantification of up to 636 endogenous metabolites, including amino acids and amino acid derivatives, biogenic amines, ceramides, cholesterol esters, diacylglycerols, acylcarnitines, glycerophospholipids, sphingomyelins, triacylglycerols, organic acids and nucleotide/nucleosides. The method uses <strong>chemical derivatization</strong> (for<strong> biogenic amines</strong> and <strong>organic acids</strong>), <strong>analyte extraction</strong> and<strong> LC separation</strong> (<strong>or direct injection</strong> for<strong> lipids</strong> and <strong>acylcarnitines</strong>), combined with selective mass-spectrometric detection using multiple reaction monitoring (<strong>MRM</strong>) pairs to identify and quantify metabolites.&nbsp;</p>	<p>A 96-deep-well plate with a filter plate attached via sealing tape was used for extraction of samples, with the first 14 wells of the plate used for a blank sample, 3 zero samples, 7 standard-containing or calibration samples and 3 quality control (QC) samples. To extract and measure <strong>all metabolites except organic acids</strong>, plasma samples were first thawed on ice, then vortexed and centrifuged at 13,000 x g. A total of <strong>10 μL of each plasma sample</strong> was loaded onto the center of the filter on the upper 96-well plate and dried in a stream of nitrogen. Subsequently, phenyl-isothiocyanate (<strong>PITC</strong>) was added for the <strong>derivatization </strong>of all amine-containing metabolites. After incubation, the filter spots were dried again using an evaporator. Extraction of the metabolites was then achieved by adding an <strong>ammonium acetate/methanol</strong> mixture (5 mM ammonium acetate dissolved in 300 μL methanol). Then the extracts were centrifuged into the lower 96-deep-well plate and diluted with the MS running solvent prior to injection into the<strong> LC-MS</strong> system.</p><p><br></p><p>For<strong> organic acid analysis</strong>, <strong>150 μL of ice-cold methanol</strong> and <strong>10 μL of the isotopically labeled ISTD mixture</strong> were added to <strong>50 μL of each plasma sample</strong> for overnight protein precipitation (at 20 °C). After the precipitation step was complete, each sample was centrifuged at 13,000 x g for 20 min at 4 °C and 50 μL of each supernatant was loaded onto the center of a selected well of the 96-deep well-plate, followed by the addition of 3-nitrophenylhydrazine (<strong>3-NPH</strong>), which serves as an organic-acid specific <strong>derivatization reagent</strong>. After incubation for 2h, 10 mg of butylated hydroxytoluene (<strong>BHT</strong>), which is used as a stabilizer, and 50 μL water were added before <strong>LC-MS</strong> injection.</p>	<p>Analysis were performed on an <strong>Agilent 1290 series UHPLC system</strong> (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA). A <strong>ZORBAX Eclipse XDB C18 (3.5 µm, 3 mm x 100 mm; Agilent Technologies) </strong>reverse-phase column with a <strong>SecurityGuard C18 (3 mm x 4 mm; Phenomenex)</strong> guard column<strong> </strong>was used for<strong> LC-MS/MS</strong> analysis. The controlling software for the sample analysis was <strong>Analyst 1.7.2</strong> (Applied Biosystems/MDS Analytical Technologies, Foster City, CA). The HPLC parameters used for the<strong> LC-MS/MS </strong>analysis of the <strong>PITC panel</strong> were as follows - solvent A: 0.2% (v/v) formic acid in water, and solvent B: 0.2% (v/v) formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient profile for this UHPLC solvent run was as follows: 0 min, 0% B; 0.5 min, 0% B; 5.5 min, 95% B; 6.5 min, 95% B; 7.0 min, 0% B; and 9.5 min, 0% B. The column oven was set at 50 °C. The flow rate was 500 μL/min, and the sample injection volume was 10 μL.</p><p><br></p><p>For the analysis of <strong>organic acids</strong>, the solvents used were (A) 0.01% (v/v) formic acid in water and (B) 0.01% (v/v) formic acid in acetonitrile. The gradient profile was as follows: 0 min, 25% B; 6.0 min, 65% B; 6.3 min, 90% B; 6.5 min, 100% B; 7.0 min, 100% B; 7.5 min, 25% B; 12.0 min, 25% B. The column oven was set to 40 °C. The flow rate was 400 μL/min, and the sample injection volume was 10 μL.</p><p><br></p><p>For<strong> direct flow injection-mass spectrometry analysis (</strong><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>DFI-MS/MS) </strong><span style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>of </span>l<strong>ipids</strong>,<strong> acylcarnitines</strong> and <strong>hexose</strong>, the UHPLC autosampler was connected directly to the MS ion source by red PEEK tubing. The <strong>DFI</strong> mobile phase was 0.02% (v/v) formic acid in a 1:29 (v/v) water:methanol solution. The flow rate was programmed as follows: 0 min, 30 μL/min; 1.6 min, 30 μL/min; 2.4 min, 200 μL/min; 2.8 min, 200 μL/min; and 3.0 min, 30 μL/min. The sample injection volume was 20 μL.</p>	<p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed on an <strong>AB SCIEX QTRAP 5500</strong> tandem mass spectrometry instrument (Applied Biosystems/MDS Analytical Technologies, Foster City, CA). Data analysis was done using MultiQuantTM 3.0.3 (Applied Biosystems/MDS Analytical Technologies, Foster City, CA). All analyses were performed in <strong>m/z range 100-1000</strong>. For the analysis of the<strong> PITC</strong> panel, the mass spectrometer was set to a <strong>positive electrospray ionization mode</strong> with a scheduled multiple reaction monitoring<strong> </strong>(<strong>MRM</strong>)<strong> </strong>scan. The IonSpray voltage was set at 5500 V and the temperature at 500 °C. The curtain gas (CUR), ion source gas 1 (GAS1), ion source gas 2 (GAS2), and collision gas (CAD) were set at 20, 40, 50, and medium, respectively. The entrance potential (EP) was set to 15 V. The declustering potential (DP), collision energy (CE), collision cell exit potential (CXP), MRM precursor ion (Q1), and fragment ion (Q3) were optimized and set individually for each analyte and isotope-labeled ISTD.</p><p><br></p><p>For the analysis of <strong>organic acids,</strong> the mass spectrometer was set to a <strong>negative electrospray ionization mode</strong> with scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (<strong>MRM</strong>) scanning. The IonSpray voltage was set at −4500 V and the temperature at 400 °C. The CUR, GAS1, GAS2, and CAD were set at 20, 30, 30 and medium, separately. The EP was set at −10 V, and the DP, CE, CXP, Q1, and Q3 were optimized and set individually for all the analytes and isotope-labeled ISTDs.</p><p><br></p><p>For the <strong>DFI-MS/MS analysis</strong>, the IonSpray voltage was set at 5500 V and the temperature was set at 200 °C. The CUR, GAS1, GAS2, and CAD were set at 20, 40, 50, and medium, respectively. The EP and CXP were set at 10 and 15 V, respectively, for<strong> positive</strong> mode and −10 and −15 V, respectively, for <strong>negative</strong> mode. Likewise, the DP, CE, Q1, and Q3 were optimized and set individually for each analyte and ISTD.</p>	<p>For some of the analysis such as principle component analysis, differentially abundant metabolites, pathway analysis, and machine learning, the <strong>logarithmic transformation</strong> has been done. However, for showing the box and whisker plots we used absolute values of the metabolites.</p>	<p><strong>Isotope-labeled ISTDs</strong> along with isotope-labeled chemical derivatization reagents are used for accurate metabolite quantification. All chemicals used in this study were weighed individually on a Sartorius CPA225D semimicro electronic balance (Mississauga, ON, CA) with a precision of 0.0001 g. Stock solutions, with defined concentrations for each analyte, were prepared by dissolving the accurately weighed chemicals in proper solvents. 7 different calibration curve standards (Cal1 to Cal7), were prepared by mixing and diluting corresponding stock solutions with appropriate solvents, covering different concentration ranges for different analytes according to their known or expected normal/pathological concentrations in human specimen. For analysis of <strong>amino acids, amino acid derivatives, biogenic amines, and nucleotide/nucleosides</strong> and <strong>organic acids</strong>, <strong>3 QC </strong>standards with different concentrations were prepared by diluting the Cal7 standard solution with the same solvents as preparing the calibration standards.</p><p><br></p><p>For <strong>amino acids, amino acid derivatives, biogenic amines, and nucleotide/nucleosides,</strong> up to 92 standards and 48 isotope-labelled internal standards were used. Optima LC-MS grade water (Fisher Scientific, Ottawa, ON, CA) was used as solvent for preparing stock solutions, calibration mixtures, QC mixtures, and a working ISTD solution mixture. The working ISTD solution mixture with defined concentrations of 48 ISTDs in LC/MS grade water was made by mixing all the prepared isotope-labeled stock solutions. </p><p><br></p><p>For <strong>organic acids, </strong>up to 107 standards were used and 75% (v/v) OptimaTM LC-MS grade methanol (Fisher Scientific, Ottawa, ON, CA) in OptimaTM LC-MS grade water was used for preparing stock solutions and calibration mixtures. An ISTD solution mixture with up to 102 standards with defined concentrations was prepared in the same way as their calibration standards. A working internal standard solution mixture was prepared by derivatizing the ISTD solution with isotope-labeled chemical derivatization reagent during the sample preparation procedure.</p><p><br></p><p>For <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>lipids, acylcarnitines and hexos</strong><strong>e</strong>, a working calibration mixture with defined concentrations of 21 standards and a working ISTD solution mixture with defined concentrations of 18 ISTDs in LC/MS grade methanol were also made by mixing all the prepared stock solutions. Chloroform (Sigma-Aldrich, Oakville, ON, CA) was used for preparing stock solutions for lipids. Methanol was used for preparing acylcarnitines and hexose stock solutions.</p>	<p>For direct flow injection-mass spectrometry analysis (<strong>DFI-MS/MS</strong>) of<strong> lipids, acylcarnitines and hexose</strong>, the UHPLC autosampler was connected directly to the MS ion source by red PEEK tubing. The <strong>DFI</strong> buffer mentioned above was used as the mobile phase, while the flow rate was programmed as follows: t = 0 min, 30 μL/min; t = 1.6 min, 30 μL/min; t = 2.4 min; 200 μL/min; t = 2.8 min, 200 μL/min; and t = 3.0 min, 30 μL/min. The sample injection volume was 20 μL.</p><p><br></p><p>Mass spectrometric analysis was performed on an <strong>AB SCIEX QTRAP 5500</strong> tandem mass spectrometry instrument (Applied Biosystems/MDS Analytical Technologies, Foster City, CA).The IonSpray voltage was set at 5500 V and the temperature was set at 200 °C. The CUR, GAS1, GAS2, and CAD were set at 20, 40, 50, and medium, respectively. The EP and CXP were set at 10 and 15 V, respectively, for <strong>positive mode</strong>. Likewise, the DP, CE, Q1, and Q3 were optimized and set individually for each analyte and ISTD.</p>

Study Title	Neuroactive Potential Signatures of the Gut Microbiome Across Prokaryotic Genomes and Neuropsychiatric Conditions Using GBM2
Study Description	<p>Neuropsychiatric conditions impose a substantial global burden, yet the contribution of microbiota–gut–brain communication to their biology remains difficult to interpret in humans. Metagenomic studies could help characterize microbial neuroactive potential, but such analyses remain constrained by incomplete and non-specialized annotation resources. To address this limitation, we developed Gut–Brain Modules 2 (GBM2), a curated, version-traceable computational framework of pathway modules describing prokaryotic transformations of neuroactive compounds. Evaluation against experimentally characterized strains and four single-culture metabolomics datasets recovered known neuroactive activities and identified gaps in current prokaryotic pathway annotation. Applying GBM2 to a catalogue of 1.6 million prokaryotic genomes revealed widespread, strain-variable neuroactive potential, including contributions from uncharacterized species-level genome bins. We further meta-analysed 44 faecal shotgun metagenomic studies comprising 6,165 individuals with neuropsychiatric conditions and 8,914 controls to examine condition-associated and cross-condition signatures.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Bacteria were cultured under anaerobic conditions with different media (please see metadata file for more info) and the supernatant was collected at different time points (please see metadata file for more info) </p>	<p>Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry analysis was carried out as previously described, using two different analytical methodologies to enrich chemical coverage: reverse-phase chromatography in positive mass spectrometry mode and normal-phase chromatography in negative mass spectrometry mode.</p><p>For reverse-phase analysis, chromatographic separation was performed using an Agilent 1200 Infinity UHPLC system with an InfinityLab Poroshell HPH-C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.9 µm particle size) operated at 45°C. Mobile phases were water with 0.1% formic acid (solvent A) and methanol with 0.1% formic acid (solvent B). Five microliters of the sample were injected with a gradient from 5% to 95% B over 6.5 minutes.</p><p>For normal-phase chromatography, separation was acquired using an InfinityLab Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z, 2.1 x 150 mm, 2.7-micron pore size column (Agilent Technologies, U.S.A.). The mobile phases were (a) with 5 mM ammonium acetate (pH 9) and 0.05% (v/v) Agilent Deactivator, and (b) acetonitrile, 5 mM ammonium acetate (pH 9) and 0.05% (v/v) deactivator as solvent B. The column compartment was kept at 50 °C. 5 µl sample were injected at 96% B and 0.250 ml/min flow, followed by 96% B until minute 2, a gradient to 88% B until minute 5.5, 88% B until minute 8.5, a gradient to 86% B until minute 9, 86% B until minute 14, a gradient to 82% B to minute 17, a gradient to 65% B to minute 23, 65% B until minute 24, a gradient to 96% B to minute 24.5 and remaining at 96% B until minute 26.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The mass spectrometry data were centroided and converted from the proprietary format (.d) to the m/z extensible markup language format (.cef) using the peak picking algorithm of Agilent DAReprocessor at the following parameters: minimum peaks height &gt;= 5.000 counts; all ion species; only select common organic compounds (no halogens); isotoping grouping with limit assigned charge states to a range of 1; compound filters based on Quality Score &gt; 70.</p><p>The CEF files were then processed with Agilent Mass Profiler Professional - MPP (version 15.1). In brief, feature alignment allowed mass tolerances of 0.002 AMU or 20 ppm and retention time tolerance of 0.15 min or 2 for C18 and 0.30 min or 3 for HILIC; minimum absolute abundance 10000 counts; minimum number of ions 3; multiple charge states forbidden. The final feature table was also submitted to MS1 feature annotation against the Metabolite and Tandem MS Database (METLIN) (v1233) [DS1] was performed by Agilent MPP. Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) accession numbers were also derived from METLIN, and compound classes were assigned based on the latest HMDB release (v5.0) [DS2] .</p><p>The resulting feature quantification table was processed in R (v4.4.1) and RStudio/2024.09.1, applying an adapted protocol for feature filtering, blank removal, imputation, quantile normalization, and log-transformation [DS3] . In brief, features with specific blank-to-sample ratios and samples with abnormally low mean intensities were flagged as poor-quality and removed from analysis. Moreover, feature filtering was conducted to retain only features that appear in 75% of at least one condition. The remaining data were imputed to address missing values, in which a limit of detection (LOD) threshold was established based on the minimum non-zero intensity in the data. Values below this threshold were imputed to the LOD cutoff. Intensities were then quantile-normalized using the normalize.quantiles function from the preprocessCore package (v1.64.0) [DS4] and subsequently log2-transformed with a pseudo-count (half of the minimum positive value) to stabilize variance. For multivariate analyses such as PCA, the data were also z-scored (mean-centered and unit-variance scaled).</p><p><br></p><p>&nbsp;[DS1]Doi 10.1007/978-1-0716-0239-3_9</p><p>&nbsp;[DS2]https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34986597/</p><p>&nbsp;[DS3]https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.684342</p><p>&nbsp;[DS4]Bolstad B (2023) preprocessCore: a collection of pre-processing functions. R package version 1.64.0. https://bioconductor.org/packages/preprocessCore. Accessed September 2025.</p>	<p>Bacterial supernatants were prepared for LC-MS analysis by organic solvent extraction. In brief, 20 μL of supernatant was mixed with 105 µL of organic solvent (acetonitrile:methanol, 1:1) supplemented with an internal standard mixture. Internal standard mixture consisted of phenylalanine-d5, tryptophan-d5, ibuprofen-d4, tolfenamic acid-d4, estriol-d3, diclofenac-d4, warfarin-d5, oxfendazole-d3, chloramphenicol-d5, nafcillin-d5 and caffeine-d9, each to a final concentration of 80 nM. The material was homogenized and incubated at -20°C to allow protein precipitation. After incubation, samples were centrifuged (3220 rcf, 4°C) for 15 min. For C18 LC-MS acquisition, 15 µL of supernatant was diluted with 30 µL H2O. For HILIC LC-MS acquisition, 15 µL of supernatant was diluted with 15 µL H2O.&nbsp;</p>	<p>For reverse-phased LC-QTOF analysis, the qTOF instrument (Agilent 6550) was operated in scanning mode (50–1,700 m/z) in positive ionization modes at the following source parameters: VCap: 3500 V, nozzle voltage: 2000 V, gas temp: 225 °C; drying gas 13 L/min; nebulizer: 20 psig; sheath gas temp 225 °C; sheath gas flow 12 L/min. Online mass calibration was used throughout the run in a second ionization source and a constant flow of reference ion solution (m/z 121.0508 and 922.0097 for positive mode).</p><p>For normal-pahsed LC-QTOF, the qTOF (Agilent) was operated in negative scanning mode (50-1700 m/z) with the following source parameters: VCap = 3500 V; nozzle voltage = 0 V; gas temp = 225 °C; drying gas flow = 13 l/min; nebulizer = 35 psig; sheath gas temp = 350 °C; sheath gas flow = 10 L/min; fragmentor = 300 V; and Octopole RF Vpp = 750 V. Online mass calibration was performed using a second ionization source and a constant flow of reference solution (reference ions are m/z 112.9857 and 1033.9881).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p>	<p> The area under the curve from significant features was integrated using the MassHunter Quantitative Analysis Software (Agilent, version 7.0) based on the accurate high-resolution mass and RT of the reference analytes and the following parameters: signal threshold of 30,000; mass tolerances of 0.002 amu or 20 ppm; retention time tolerance of 0.2 min. All metabolites annotated in this manuscript were assigned a Level 1 annotation according to the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) classification system. This includes a match in retention time using the same chromatographic system, accurate mass, and MS/MS fragmentation pattern, ensuring unequivocal identification of the compounds.</p>

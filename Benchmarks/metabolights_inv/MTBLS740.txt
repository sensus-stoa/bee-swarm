STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS740
Study Title	Quantitative metabolomic analysis of changes in the lens and aqueous humor under development of age-related nuclear cataract
Study Description	<p><strong>INTRODUCTION:</strong> Metabolites are essential for the proper functioning of the eye lens, they either enter the lens from the aqueous humor (AH), or are synthesized in the lens epithelium. Antioxidants, osmolytes and UV filters are especially important for the lens protection, and their lack may cause the development of ophthalmic diseases.</p><p><strong>OBJECTIVES:</strong> Comparison of the metabolomic compositions of lenses and AH taken from cataract patients with that taken from human cadavers without cataract can shed light onto molecular mechanisms underlying onset of age-related nuclear cataract.</p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> Combined use of 1H nuclear magnetic resonance and high performance liquid chromatography with optical and high-resolution mass spectrometric detection for the identification and quantification of metabolites in the lens and AH extracts.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> The concentrations of 86 metabolites were determined for four groups of samples, including lenses and AH from cataract patients and from human cadavers. In cataractous lens the most abundant metabolites are (in descending order): myo-inositol, lactate, acetate, glutamate, glutathione; in AH-lactate, glucose, glutamine, alanine, valine. The concentrations of the majority of metabolites in normal post-mortem samples of both lens and AH are higher than that in samples from the cataract patients.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> Comparison of metabolite concentrations in lens and corresponding AH reveal that the most important for the lens protection metabolites are synthesized in the lens epithelial cells. The reduced levels of antioxidants, UV filters, and osmolytes were found in the cataractous lenses what cannot be explained by post-mortem changes in normal lens; that indicates that the age-related nuclear cataract development may originate from the dysfunction of the lens epithelial cells.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>All procedures of this study were subjected to the Declaration of Helsinki-ethical principles for medical research involving human subjects and with the ethical approval from International Tomography Center SB RAS. Written informed consent was obtained from the patients (cataractous lenses and aqueous humors (AH)) or next of kin (post-mortem lenses and AH) after explanation of the nature and possible consequences of the study.</p><p><br></p><p>Normal human lenses of the age from 55 to 66 yr (7 lenses total, both genders, the average age 60 yr) and corresponding AH were obtained 7-17 h post-mortem from human cadavers from the Novosibirsk Regional Clinical Bureau of Forensic Medicine. Cataractous human lenses and corresponding AH were obtained from non-diabetic patients of the age from 64 to 81 yr (12 lenses total, both genders, the average age 72 yr) with advanced age-related nuclear cataract at the Novosibirsk Regional Hospital during the extracapsular cataract extraction surgery. The lenses and AHs were frozen immediately after removal and stored at -70 °C until analyzed.</p>	<p>The description for sample preparation is given in detail earlier<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, lenses were weighed and then homogenized in cold ethanol to obtain protein-free extracts. Then lipids were removed from sample by mixing the supernatant with cold CHCl3. The obtained extract was divided in 2 parts for NMR and LC-MS measurements. AH protein-free extracts were analyzed without the delipidation procedure.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Tsentalovich YP, Verkhovod TD, Yanshole VV, Kiryutin AS, Yanshole LV, Fursova AZh, Stepakov DA, Novoselov VP, Sagdeev RZ. Metabolomic composition of normal aged and cataractous human lenses. Exp Eye Res. 2015 May;134:15-23. doi:10.1016/j.exer.2015.03.008. PMID:25773987.</p>	<p>The extracts for NMR measurements were lyophilized, re-dissolved in 500 μl of D2O containing 6 x 10^−6 M sodium 2,2-dimethyl-2-silapentane-5-sulfonate (DSS) as an internal standard and 20 mM deuterated phosphate buffer to maintain pH 7.4.</p>	<p>The 1H NMR measurements were carried out with the use of a NMR spectrometer AVANCE III HD 700 MHz (Bruker BioSpin, Germany) equipped with a 16.44 T Ascend cryomagnet.</p>	<p>The proton NMR spectra for each sample were obtained with 100 accumulations. Temperature of the sample during the data acquisition was kept at 25 °C, the detection pulse was 90º. The repetition time between scans was 30 s to allow for the complete relaxation of all spins. Low power radiation at the water resonance frequency was applied prior to acquisition to presaturate the water signal.</p>	<p>The baseline processing and integration were done using the <strong>ACD/NMR Processor Academic Edition</strong>.</p>	<p>The initial signal assignment was performed with the use of literature data<strong>[1]-[5]</strong> and online databases<strong>[6][7]</strong>, and was then confirmed by the addition of standard compounds to the extract solutions. The concentrations of metabolites in the samples were determined by the peak area integration respectively to the internal standard DSS. The compounds whose NMR signals were too weak in the majority of the samples, or whose signals were strongly overlapped by other NMR peaks impeding the reliable integration, were excluded from the data processing.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Fris M, Tessem MB, Saether O, Midelfart A. Biochemical changes in selenite cataract model measured by high-resolution MAS H NMR spectroscopy. Acta Ophthalmol Scand. 2006 Oct;84(5):684-92. doi:10.1111/j.1600-0420.2006.00716.x. PMID:16965502.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Risa Ø, Saether O, Löfgren S, Söderberg PG, Krane J, Midelfart A. Exp Eye Res. 2014 Aug;125:71-8. doi:10.1016/j.exer.2014.05.016. PMID:24910091.</p><p><strong>[4]</strong> Midelfart A, Dybdahl A, Gribbestad S.

Study Identifier	MTBLS7533
Study Title	Peripheral modulation of antidepressant targets MAO-B and GABAAR by harmol induces mitohormesis and delays aging in preclinical models
Study Description	Reversible and sub-lethal stresses to the mitochondria elicit a program of compensatory responses that ultimately improve mitochondrial function, a conserved anti-aging mechanism termed mitohormesis. Here, we show that harmol, a member of the beta-carbolines family with anti-depressant properties, improves mitochondrial function and metabolic parameters, and extends healthspan. Treatment with harmol induces a transient mitochondrial depolarization, a strong mitophagy response, and the AMPK compensatory pathway both in cultured C2C12 myotubes and in male mouse liver, brown adipose tissue and muscle, even though harmol crosses poorly the blood-brain barrier. Mechanistically, simultaneous modulation of the targets of harmol monoamine-oxidase B and GABA-A receptor reproduces harmol-induced mitochondrial improvements. Diet-induced pre-diabetic male mice improve their glucose tolerance, liver steatosis and insulin sensitivity after treatment with harmol. Harmol or a combination of monoamine oxidase B and GABA-A receptor modulators extend the lifespan of hermaphrodite Caenorhabditis elegans or female Drosophila melanogaster. Finally, two-year-old male and female mice treated with harmol exhibit delayed frailty onset with improved glycemia, exercise performance and strength. Our results reveal that peripheral targeting of monoamine oxidase B and GABA-A receptor, common antidepressant targets, extends healthspan through mitohormesis.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Mice experiments:</strong></p><p>Animal experiments were conducted at the CNIO (Madrid) and performed according to protocols approved by the CNIO‐ISCIII Ethics Committee for Research and Animal Welfare (CEIyBA) (PROEX 161/18). All the mice used in this work were of <strong>C57BL/6JOlaHsd</strong> background and were fed with Teklad Global 18% Protein Rodent Diet (Teklad, 2018S) until reaching 12 weeks of age. Mice experiments were performed with male mice, this sex selection was due to availability of mice and cohort homogeneity. Mice were housed at 22 ºC, with 12-hour light/dark cycles (7:00-19:00) and 55 +/- 15% humidity, and kept in pathogen-free conditions.</p><p><br></p><p>Chronic Harmol treatment-C57BL/6JOlaHsd mice: 12 weeks-old mice were rendered pre-diabetic by feeding them with 45% high-fat diet (HFD, Research Diets D12451i) for 4 months. After this, they were kept on HFD and treated with vehicle (water, W) or 100 mg/kg harmol (Alfa Aesar, Catalog number: J63827, H) in their drinking water for 3 months. After the treatment, mice were sacrificed and <strong>liver</strong> tissue was snap-frozen and kept at -80ºC until <strong>Polyamine (Spermidine)</strong> LC-MS/MS analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>Acute Harmol treatment-C57BL/6JOlaHsd mice: 12 week-old male mice were treated by oral gavage with vehicle (water, W) or 100 mg/kg harmol dissolved in water after removing their food, and kept them in fasting until sacrifice, 3, 7 and 24 hours later. Levels of <strong>harmol</strong> in <strong>plasma, liver and brain </strong>samples (for technical details, see <strong>Table S2</strong>), were measured by LC-MS/MS.</p><p><br></p><p><strong><em>In vitro</em> experiments:</strong></p><p>Undifferentiated C2C12 myoblasts were obtained from the ATCC (code CRL-1772). They were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) with 25 mM glucose (Lonza, 12-604F) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Tico). Cells were kept at 37 ºC and 5% CO2 atmosphere and passages were performed every 2 days in order to avoid differentiation. For C2C12 differentiation to <strong>myotubes</strong>, cells were initially seeded in DMEM + 10% FBS. 16 hours after seeding, cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and medium was replaced with differentiation medium (DMEM supplemented with 2% Horse Serum (HS)). Medium was replaced every 2 days and total differentiation was complete after 5 days.</p><p><br></p><p>Acute Harmol treatment-C2C12 myotubes: C2C12 myotubes were treated <strong>with vehicle (0.1% DMSO)</strong> or with <strong>1.3 mg/ml harmol</strong> during <strong>24 hours</strong>, and then cells were snap-frozen and kept at -80 ºC until <strong>Polyamine (Spermidine)</strong> levels were measured by LC-MS/MS.</p>	<p><strong>Polyamine (Spermidine)</strong> <strong>determinations</strong>:</p><p>For polyamine extraction, <strong>40-50 mg of liver</strong> or <strong>4 x 10^5 cells</strong> were homogenized on ice for 30-60 seconds using an electrically powered Turrax homogenizer (by IKA) in 1,200 µL (tissue) or 600 µL (cells) <strong>5% TCA </strong>(w/v in water). 80 µL (tissue) or 100 µL (cells) of this homogenate were immediately mixed with the stable-isotope-labeled internal standard 13C4-spermidine (13C4-spd, ≥95%). The 13C4-spd stock solution was first prepared in water/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) at 500 ng/ml and then diluted using 5% TCA (w/v in water) to 400 ng/ml, and it was added to the liver and cells homogenates to a final concentration of 100 ng/ml. A quantitative LC-MS/MS-based determination of polyamines was performed as previously described <strong>[1]</strong>, with minor modifications: After derivatizing the TCA extracts using isobutyl as previously described <strong>[1]</strong> <strong>[2]</strong>, polyamine derivatives were extracted offline by <strong>SPE</strong> (Strata-X, Polymeric Reversed Phase, 96 well plate). <strong>SPE</strong> was conditioned with 500 µl acetonitrile, equilibrated with 500 µl distilled water containing 0.2% acetic acid. Derivatized TCA extracts were loaded onto the <strong>SPE</strong> and after two washing steps with 500 µl of 0.2% acetic acid, samples were eluted with 250 µl 80% acetonitrile containing 0.2% acetic acid.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Harmol determinations</strong>:</p><p>For <strong>plasma</strong> samples, 30 µL were placed in a 1.5 mL polypropylene tube. After that, 20 µL of internal standard (harmine-d3 prepared in methanol at 1 µg/mL) was added, followed by 250 µL of methanol for protein precipitation. Samples were mixed using a vortex for 60 seconds, and centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 10 min at 4 ºC. Finally, supernatants were transferred into glass vials, and 1 µL were injected into the liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) system.</p><p><br></p><p>For <strong>tissue</strong> samples, frozen tissues were placed in 5 mL glass tubes (200 mg of brain or 100 mg of liver), together with 1 mL of methanol. Samples were homogenized using an Ultra-Turrax dispersing apparatus for 30 seconds, transferred to 1.5 mL polypropylene tubes, and centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 10 min at 4 ºC. Finally, 280 µL of supernatant were transferred into glass vials together with 20 µL of internal standard (harmine-d3 prepared in methanol at 1 µg/mL), and 1 µL was injected into the LC-MS/MS system.</p><p>Matrix-matched calibration curves were prepared in blank extracts in the range of 0.05 to 250 ng/mL, and injected at least in triplicate.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Magnes, C., Fauland, A., Gander, E., Narath, S., Ratzer, M., Eisenberg, T., Madeo, F., Pieber, T., Sinner, F. 2014. Polyamines in biological samples: rapid and robust quantification by solid-phase extraction online-coupled to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. J Chromatogr A 1331, 44–51(2014). doi:10.1016/j.chroma.2013.12.061.</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Byun, J.A., Lee, S.H., Jung, B.H., Choi, M.H., Moon, M.H., Chung, B.C. 2008. Biomed. Chromatogr. 22 (2008) 73. doi:10.1002/bmc.898.</p>	<p><strong>Polyamine (Spermidine)</strong> <strong>quantification:</strong></p><p>Eluted SPE extracts were subjected to LC-MS/MS. Analyses were carried out following the previously described methodology <strong>[1]</strong>, on an Ultimate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA). The system was controlled by Xcalibur Software 2.1. The oven of the autosampler was maintained at&nbsp;room temperature, and the tray at 5&nbsp;°C.&nbsp;The HPLC column comprised a reversed-phase C18 column (Kinetex reversed phase C18; 100A, 50&nbsp;×&nbsp;2.1&nbsp;mm i.d., 2.6&nbsp;μm particle size) from Phenomenex (California, USA).&nbsp;An isocratic elution with 80% acetonitrile containing 0.2% acetic acid and a 250 µl/min flow rate was used.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Harmol quantification:</strong></p><p>Extracts were analysed using an Acquity UPLC I-Class liquid chromatography system (Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). Chromatographic separation was achieved using an Acquity BEH C18 2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 μm particle size analytical column (Waters Corp, Wexford, Ireland) maintained at 55 °C. Mobile phases consisted on water (solvent A) and methanol (solvent B), both with 1 mM ammonium formiate and 0.1% formic acid, delivered at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min, and changing as follows: 0 min 5% B, 0.5 min 5% B, 7.0 min 40% B, 7.1 min 99% B, 8.5 min 99% B, 8.6 min 5% B, and maintained to 10 min for column re-equilibration. Injection volume was 1 μL.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong>&nbsp;Magnes C, Fauland A, Gander E, Narath S, Ratzer M, Eisenberg T, Madeo F, Pieber T, Sinner F. 2014. Polyamines in biological samples: rapid and robust quantification by solid-phase extraction online-coupled to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. J Chromatogr A. 2014 Feb 28;1331(100):44-51. doi:10.1016/j.chroma.2013.12.061. PMID:24485539.</p>	<p><strong>Polyamine (Spermidine) quantification:</strong></p><p>MS conditions were as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, a quantum TSQ Quantum triple&nbsp;quadrupole&nbsp;mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA), equipped with an&nbsp;electrospray&nbsp;ion source was used. The instrument was operated in multiple reaction-monitoring mode (MRM). The ion source was operated in positive&nbsp;electrospray ionization&nbsp;(ESI) mode. The MRM of the precursor-product ion transitions, collision energies and tube lens offset are 446.4 / 298.1, 15 (%) and 106 (V) for spermidine and 450.2 / 302.1, 14 (%) and 101(V) for 13C4-spd. The optimized conditions were: ESI spray voltage 4.0&nbsp;kV, sheath gas pressure 20&nbsp;AU, auxiliary gas pressure 15&nbsp;AU, capillary temperature 270&nbsp;°C and skimmer offset −24&nbsp;V. The scan was performed in profile mode with scan width (m/z) 0.1, scan time 0.05&nbsp;s, and peak width of Q1 and Q3 0.7 (full width at half maximum). Spermidine data was acquired and processed using the <strong>Xcalibur 4.0 QF1, Version 4.0.27.21</strong> software.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Harmol quantification:</strong></p><p>ESI was operated in positive ionization mode using a capillary voltage of 3.0 kV. Nitrogen was used as desolvation (1200 L/h) and cone gas (150 L/h), while source temperature was set to 150 °C, and desolvation temperature 600 °C. Cone voltage and collision energies, using argon (99.995%) as collision gas, were optimized for each compound. Data were acquired selected reaction monitoring (SRM) acquisition mode, acquiring at least 3 transitions/compound. Dwell times were selected in order to acquire 12 points/peak, being at least 10 ms/transition. LC-MS/MS data were acquired and processed using <strong>MassLynx 4.2</strong> software (Waters Corp, Manchester, UK) and <strong>TargetLynx</strong> application (Waters Corp, Manchester, UK).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong>&nbsp;Magnes C, Fauland A, Gander E, Narath S, Ratzer M, Eisenberg T, Madeo F, Pieber T, Sinner F. 2014. Polyamines in biological samples: rapid and robust quantification by solid-phase extraction online-coupled to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. J Chromatogr A. 2014 Feb 28;1331(100):44-51. doi:10.1016/j.chroma.2013.12.061. PMID:24485539.</p>	<p><strong>Polyamine (Spermidine) quantification:</strong></p><p>No transformation was performed with this data. Raw files were directly processed with&nbsp;the vendor software <strong>Xcalibur 4.0 QF1, Version 4.0.27.21</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Harmol quantification:</strong></p><p>Similarly to spermidine analysis, no data transformation was performed. Raw files were directly processed using <strong>TargetLynx XS</strong> application manager for peak integration, calibration and quantification.</p>	<p>Metabolites were identified based upon retention time (RT) and Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) transitions. For <strong>Spermidine</strong> analysis, MRM transitions were: m/z 446 to 298 and for <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>13C4-spermidine</strong> (internal standard), m/z 450 to 302. For <strong>harmol</strong>, MRM transitions were: m/z 199.2 to 77.0, 103.0, 131.0, 171.0 and 184.1, using these collision energies: 40, 35, 35, 30 and 30 V, respectively. For <strong>Harmine-d3</strong> (internal standard), the selected transitions were: m/z 216.1 to 144.0, 169.0, 170.0 and 198.0, using the following collision energy values: 35, 45, 30 and 25 V, respectively. Also, for analysis of spermidine, calibration curves were made in the range of 3-1500 ng/ml and for harmol, matrix-matched calibration curves were prepared in blank extracts in the range of 0.05 to 250 ng/mL, and injected at least in triplicate. The internal standards 13C4-spermidine and harmine-d3 were used to calculate peak/area ratios for spermidine and harmol respectively.</p>

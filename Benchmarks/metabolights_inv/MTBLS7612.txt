Study Identifier	MTBLS7612
Study Title	Age-, sex- and proximal-distal-resolved multi-omics identifies regulators of intestinal aging in non-human primates.
Study Description	<p>The incidence of intestinal diseases increases with age, but the regulators of gut aging and the molecules linking gut aging and diseases remain largely unknown. By profiling the transcriptome, proteome, phosphoproteome and metabolome of 104 <em>Macaca fascicularis</em> large intestinal tissues, we unveiled asynchronous aging within the proximal and distal colon. The levels of many gene products, phosphosites and metabolites changed with age in a location- and sex-dependent manner, and many of these changes occurred at distinct rates or even in opposite directions at different locations. Assessment of 60 age-related molecules (genes, phosphosites, metabolites) across<em> C. elegans</em>, mice, and cell lines identified 32 crucial regulators of gut atrophy and barrier integrity. Notably, we found that tryptophan metabolism via the kynurenine and serotonin (5-HT) pathways was more active in the proximal and distal colon, respectively. In a mouse colitis model, reducing 5-HT production with a Tph1 inhibitor alleviated distal but not proximal colitis, while adding 5-HT aggravated distal symptoms, indicating that distal colitis is more sensitive to 5-HT fluctuations. Moreover, we identified 24 proteins, particularly HPX, that potentially link gut aging to colorectal cancer (CRC). High levels of HPX in CRC predicted poor prognosis in patients older than 50 yr but not in younger patients, suggesting that an age-linked HPX increase in the gut may contribute to CRC progression. Collectively, this work reveals the heterogeneity of large intestinal aging in non-human primates and offers promising targets for preventing gut aging and diseases.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>To characterize the molecular landscape of large intestine aging, we collected 104 pieces of fresh large intestinal tissues obtained from <strong>26 <em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong> and performed location- and sex-resolved multi-omics analyses, including metabolomics (<strong>see Table S4 </strong>of the paper associated with the study). <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'><em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong><strong>&nbsp;</strong>were housed in a standardized laboratory environment maintained at a temperature of approximately 25 °C and a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle in Canton. Before the experiment, all animals had no clinical or experimental history that might affect physiological aging or increase susceptibility to disease. <strong>Large intestinal tissues</strong> from&nbsp;<strong>9 young</strong> (3-4 yr old, <strong>5 males and 4 females</strong>), <strong>8</strong> <strong>middle-aged</strong> (9-10 yr old, <strong>4 males</strong> and <strong>4 females</strong>) and <strong>9</strong> <strong>aged</strong> (15-16 yr old, <strong>5 males</strong> and <strong>4 females</strong>) <strong><em>Macaca fascicularis</em> </strong>were collected and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen (see<strong> Fig. 1a</strong> and <strong>Table S1a</strong> of the paper associated with the study).</p>	<p><strong>Untargeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Tissues were weighed and hydrophilic metabolites were extracted from <strong><em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong>&nbsp;colon tissues by adding <strong>1 mL</strong> of pre-cooled <strong>80% (v/v) HPLC&nbsp;grade methanol</strong> containing 2 isotopic amino acids (<strong>1 μg/mL&nbsp;of 13C6-lys and 13C6-15N4-Arg</strong> ) per 100 mg of tissue, and homogenizing using the protein_01_01 program with a gentle MACS Dissociator,&nbsp;3 times for 15 s, with 30 s pause in between cycles. Then, the whole lysate was transferred to an Eppendorf tube, and the metabolites were fully extracted by letting stand the samples in methanol at 4 °C for 1 h. The lysate was centrifuged at 14,000 x g for 20 min at 4 °C and the same volume of supernatant of each sample was transferred to a new Eppendorf tube.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p>In addition, <strong>20 μL of each sample</strong> were mixed and used as <strong>QC</strong> for mass spectrometry (MS) detection.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Tissues were weighed (approximately 10 mg per sample), then <strong>200 μL</strong> of&nbsp;pre-cooled <strong>80% (v/v) methanol </strong>were added. The tissues were homogenized with Tissue Prep (Bio-Xplorer) at 3,000 rpm for 10 s, with a 1 min pause between cycles. Then <strong>800 μL of 80% (v/v) methanol</strong> spiked with isotopic chemicals (<strong>120.89 μM 13C6-D-glucose and</strong> <strong>23.12 mM 13C5-15N-L-glutamate</strong>) were added and the samples were kept at -80 °C for 30 min, to fully extract the metabolites. Then, the lysates were sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min and centrifuged at 13,000 x g for 20 min at 4 °C. After this, 600 μL of supernatant were transferred to a new Eppendorf tube and <strong>500 μL&nbsp;of 80% (v/v) methanol</strong> were added to the Eppendorf&nbsp;tubes still containing the pellets. Samples were vortexed for 5 min at 4 °C and the lysates were centrifuged at 13,000 x g for 20 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were then combined with those obtained previously.</p><p><br></p><p>Finally, <strong>20 μL of each sample </strong>were pooled and used as <strong>QC</strong> for mass spectrometry (MS) detection.</p>	<p><strong>Untargeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Dionex Ultimate 3000 UHPLC system</strong> was used to analyze the hydrophilic metabolites extracted from <strong><em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong> large intestinal tissues.&nbsp;For analysis performed in <strong>positive mode</strong>, an <strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>Atlantis Silica HILIC (3 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters)</strong> was used, with the column temperature set at 35 °C. A gradient elution was performed using 95% ACN, 10 μM ammonium formate, 0.0001% formic acid, as mobile phase A, and 50% ACN, 10 μM ammonium formate, 0.0001% formic acid, as mobile phase B and a 300 μL/min flow rate. The linear gradient was as follows: 0-2 min, 1% B; 3.5 min, 20% B; 17 min, 80% B; 17.5-19 min, 99% B; 19.1-22 min, 1% B. In <strong>negative ion mode</strong>, an <strong>ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 (1.7 µm, 1 mm x 100 mm; Waters)</strong> was used, with 95% ACN, 10 μM ammonium acetate, pH = 9, as mobile A and 50% ACN, 10 μM ammonium acetate, pH = 9, as mobile phase B and a 250 μL/min flow rate. The linear gradient was as follows: 0-2 min, 5% B; 4 min, 20% B; 18 min, 85% B; 19-21 min, 95% B; 21.1-25 min, 5% B. Injection volume was 1 μL in each mode.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>A <strong>SCIEX ExionLC AD </strong>and an <strong>ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters)</strong> were used to detect metabolites in tissues. The column temperature was set at 40 °C. The gradient elution was performed at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min, with 90% H2O, 10 mM ammonia acetate, 0.2% acetic acid as mobile phase A and 90% ACN, 10 mM ammonia acetate, 0.2% acetic acid as mobile phase B. The linear gradient was as follows: 1.5-90 min, 90% B; 10 min, 45% B; 12-25 min, 90% B. In positive ion mode, 2 μL&nbsp;was taken and 10 μL&nbsp;was taken in negative ion mode for<strong> MS/MS</strong> analysis.</p>	<p><strong>Untargeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Exactive</strong> mass spectrometer was used to detect hydrophilic metabolites in <strong><em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong>&nbsp;colon tissues. Analysis of polar metabolites was performed by a Q Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo, CA). The spray voltage was 3.5 kV for positive and 2.5 kV for negative. The capillary temperature was 275 °C for positive and 320 °C for negative. The mass range was <strong>70-1050 m/z for positive</strong> and <strong>80-1200 m/z for negative</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>An <strong>AB SCIEX Triple Quad 6500+</strong> was used to detect metabolites in tissues. Mass spectrometry detection parameters were as follows:<strong> Positive</strong> ion mode: Curtain Gas (GUR), 35; Collision Gas (CAD), 8.0; IonSpray Voltage (IS), 5500. <strong>Negative</strong> ion mode: Curtain Gas (GUR), 35; Collision Gas (CAD), 8.0; IonSpray Voltage (IS), -4500. The temperature was set to 550 °C and the Ion Source Gas 1 (GS1) was set to 55. <strong>Only negative ion mode analysis results were considered to report 3-hydroxybutyric acid targeted quantitation</strong>.</p>	<p><strong>Untargeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Metabolites with missing values ≥ 20% or CV values ≥ 30% were removed. Calibration was performed according to the total intensity of the sample, that was, each metabolite was divided by the correction factor (total intensity per sample/average total intensity of all samples). Missing values were filled with half of the minimum value for subsequent analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Metabolites with CV values ≥ 0.2 for longitudinal quality control replicates were removed. To normalize the weight of the samples, the intensity of each metabolite was divided by the corresponding correction factor of that sample (correction factor = weight of the sample/average weight of all samples). Subsequently, the intensity of each metabolite was corrected using the average intensity of the metabolite in QC samples before and after the MS acquisition of the sample. The targeted metabolomic data of <strong><em>Macaca fascicularis</em></strong> large intestines satisfy a normal distribution.</p>	<p><strong>Untargeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Raw files of untargeted metabolomics were searched by using <strong>TraceFinder (Thermo, CA)</strong> based on a homemade metabolite database. The mass tolerance of precursor ions was 10 ppm, and the mass tolerance of fragment ions was 15 ppm. For quantitation, a retention time shift of 0.25 min was allowed.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Targeted metabolomics</strong></p><p>Raw files of targeted metabolomics were analyzed by using <strong>SCIEX OS</strong> software based on a homemade metabolite database. In the Analytics module of <strong>SCIEX OS</strong> software, the peak area value and retention time of each metabolite of each sample were checked manually.&nbsp;We identified the measured metabolites by the ion pairs generated through fragmentation of multiple metabolite standards. </p>

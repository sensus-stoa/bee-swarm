Study Identifier	MTBLS7760
Study Title	Analysis of glucose-independent metabolic pathways associated with anti-proliferative effect of metformin and their reversibility in liver cancer cells
Study Description	<p class='ql-align-justify'>Several studies indicated anti-cancer effects of metformin in liver cancer. This was attributed to the activation of LKB-AMPK axis, which is associated with anti-hyperglycaemic effect and cytotoxicity. However, glucose- and central carbon-independent effects of metformin and their reversibility remain unexplored. The dose-dependent effects of metformin on HepG2 cells were examined in presence and absence of glucose. The longitudinal evolution of metabolome was analyzed along with gene and protein expression as well as their correlations with and reversibility of cellular phenotype and metabolic signatures. Metformin concentrations ≤2.5mM were found to be non-cytotoxic but anti-proliferative irrespective of presence of glucose. Mitochondrial impairment along with derangement of one-carbon, glutathione and polyamine metabolism was associated with non-cytotoxic metformin treatment irrespective of glucose supplementation. Depletion of pantothenic acid, upregulation of fatty acid desaturation and downregulation of essential amino acid uptake, metabolism and purine salvage were identified as novel glucose-independent effects of metformin. These were significantly correlated with cMyc expression and reduction in proliferation. Rescue experiments established reversibility upon metformin withdrawal and tight association between proliferation, metabotype and cMyc expression. Taken together, derangement of novel glucose-independent pathways and concomitant cMyc downregulation coordinately contribute to anti-proliferative effect, which is reversible and may influence therapeutic utility of metformin.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>HepG2 cells were cultured with low glucose (5mM) DMEM media, supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 I.U/mL penicillin and 50 µg/mL streptomycin and maintained in a humidified 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2. All experiments were performed within passage 3-10. <strong>Metformin</strong> (<strong>0.625 to 10mM</strong>) was added to <strong>DMEM with or without 5mM glucose</strong> and effects were analyzed at <strong>3, 6, 12, 24 and 48 h</strong> post-treatment. Based on these,<strong> 2.5 mM metformin</strong> was identified as maximum non-cytotoxic dose of metformin and all metabolomic experiments were conducted with or without <strong>2.5mM metformin</strong> to examine the metabolomic consequences of non-cytotoxic metformin treatment.</p><p><br></p><p>Treatment groups were designated as: <strong>NG</strong> (<strong>5mM glucose DMEM</strong>), <strong>NT</strong> (<strong>2.5mM metformin and 5mM glucose DMEM</strong>), <strong>WG</strong> (<strong>no glucose DMEM</strong>) and<strong> WT (2.5mM metformin and no glucose DMEM)</strong>. For a rescue experiment, cells were cultured under the aforementioned conditions for <strong>24 h,</strong> followed by media change and culture for another <strong>48 h</strong> as described next. The <strong>control group </strong>(<strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>NG</strong>) continued to be cultured with the same <strong>NG media </strong>(5mM glucose). For the cells cultured for <strong>24 h</strong> on <strong>NT</strong> <strong>media </strong>(2.5mM metformin/5mM glucose DMEM), metformin was removed for the next <strong>48 h</strong> by using <strong>NG media</strong> (5mM glucose), or by exposing cells to glucose deprivation, by switching to<strong> WG</strong> <strong>media</strong> (no glucose DMEM). For those cells cultured first with <strong>WG media</strong> (no glucose DMEM), either glucose was replenished using <strong>NG media</strong> (5mM glucose) or they were exposed to metformin treatment in absence of glucose, using <strong>WT media </strong>(2.5mM metformin and no glucose DMEM). For the <strong>WT</strong> group, metformin was removed using <strong>WG media</strong> (no glucose DMEM), glucose was replenished using <strong>NT media</strong> (2.5mM metformin/5mM glucose DMEM), both were done simultaneously using <strong>NG media</strong> (5mM glucose), or cells continued with <strong>WT media</strong> as <strong>control</strong>.</p>	<p>Cells were grown in 6-well plates for up to 60-70% confluency before the treatment. After the indicated time points, the cells were washed with ice-cold 150 mM NaCl twice. Then the cells were quenched and scraped with 1 mL cold metabolite extraction solvent (<strong>Water:Acetonitrile:2-propanol, 2:3:3, v/v/v</strong>) containing <strong>15 µM Homovalinic acid</strong> as an internal standard into 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes. Immediately after, the samples were stored in a -80 °C freezer until further use. Samples were thawed on ice and vortexed vigorously. Then, they were lysed by first freezing them using liquid nitrogen and then thawing them in a 37 °C incubator. This&nbsp;freeze-thaw cycle was repeated 3 times with intermittent vortexing, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 x g RCF for 25 min at 4 °C. Next, 100 µL of supernatant were taken in a 0.6 mL glass crimp microvial and dried under vacuum using a vacuum concentrator. <strong>30 µL of 2% methoxamine </strong>reagent were added to the samples and heated for 1 h at 50 °C in a sealed vial for <strong>oxime derivatization,</strong> followed by adding of <strong>50 µL of MSTFA</strong>. Sealed vials were heated at 65 °C for 1 h for <strong>silylation</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>A pool of supernatants from all the samples was also prepared following the same procedure for <strong>quality control</strong> and feature annotation purposes.</p>	<p>Derivatized samples were analyzed using an <strong>Agilent 7693 Autosampler (G4513A)</strong> and a <strong>7890B Gas-chromatography system,</strong> equipped with a <strong>HP-5MS (0.25 µm, 0.25 mm x 30 m; Agilent Technologies) column</strong>. A total of 1 µL of the sample was injected into the column in splitless mode, using 99.999% pure Helium as a carrier at a constant flow rate of 1 mL/min. A retention time-locked data acquisition method (RT-lock) was developed against homovalinic acid, for superior chromatographic alignment across the samples. The front inlet and MS transfer lines were set at 300 °C. The oven temperature was set to 70 °C and held for 2 min followed by a ramp to 280 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min and held for 1 min. The second ramp was set to 295 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min and held for 5 min. A post-run equilibration was done at 300 °C for 5 min before coming down to the initial state.</p><p><br></p><p>Before the individual sample injection, the pooled samples were injected five times for conditioning the column. Samples for each time point were prepared in a single batch and injected in GC-MS in randomized order with intermittent pooled and blank sample injections.</p>	<p>An <strong>Agilent 5977B MSD</strong> single-quadrupole mass spectrometer was used. MS source and MS quad temperature were set to 230 and 150 °C, respectively. Measurements were done using electron impact ionization (<strong>EI</strong>) at 70 eV was used to generate fragments. Spectra were acquired in full scan mode in the <strong>m/z range of 50-600</strong> with a focus m/z of 150 at a speed of 30 Hz/s. </p>	<p>Data were imported in <strong>MassHunter quantitative analysis software (Agilent, Santa Clara, USA) </strong>and annotated by searching against the <strong>NIST14 library</strong> and standards if available. The area of each metabolite from each sample was normalized with the area of internal standard (i.e., Homovalinic Acid) from the same sample. Then the features which are more or equal in abundance to that of samples or pools present in the blank were deleted. The features/ metabolites with &gt;30% coefficient of variation (% CV) in the pooled sample were also excluded for further analysis. The samples were sum-normalized and multiple derivatives of the same compounds were added together.</p>	<p>Features were annotated by searching against the <strong>NIST14 library</strong> and validated with authenticated standards if available. Putative metabolites were annotated based on matching score &gt;700 against <strong>NIST14 library</strong>. Unidentified peaks were annotated with X_ followed by their retention time. The integrated peak area table was exported in an Excel file for further processing.</p>

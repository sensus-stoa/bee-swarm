Study Identifier	MTBLS7776
Study Title	Identification of pre-diagnostic lipid sets associated with liver cancer risk using untargeted lipidomics and chemical set analysis – a nested case-control study within the ATBC cohort
Study Description	<p>In pre-disposed individuals, a reprogramming of the hepatic lipid metabolism may support liver cancer initiation. We conducted a high-resolution mass spectrometry based untargeted lipidomics analysis of pre-diagnostic serum samples from a nested case-control study (219 liver cancer cases and 219 controls) within the Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta-Carotene Cancer Prevention (ATBC) Study. Out of 462 annotated lipids, 158 (34.2%) were associated with liver cancer risk in a conditional logistic regression analysis at a false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05. A chemical set enrichment analysis (ChemRICH) and co-regulatory set analysis suggested that 22/28 lipid classes and 47/83 correlation modules were significantly associated with liver cancer risk (FDR &lt; 0.05). Strong positive associations were observed for monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA), triacylglycerols (TAGs), and phosphatidylcholines (PCs) having MUFA acyl chains. Negative associations were observed for sphingolipids (ceramides and sphingomyelins), lysophosphatidylcholines, cholesterol esters and polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) containing TAGs and PCs. Stearoyl-CoA desaturase enzyme 1 (SCD1), a rate limiting enzyme in fatty acid metabolism and ceramidases seems to be critical in this reprogramming. In conclusion, our study reports pre-diagnostic lipid changes that provide novel insights into hepatic lipid metabolism reprogramming may contribute to a pro-cell growth and anti-apoptotic tissue environment and, in turn, support liver cancer initiation.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Baseline visit blood samples were obtained from Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta-Carotene Cancer Prevention (<strong>ATBC</strong>) Study participants after an overnight fast (usually at least 12 h). Blood samples were allowed to clot for 30 min and then centrifuged for 15 min at 3000 rpm (1500 rcf). Serum was aliquoted and stored at -70 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>The lipidome analysis was performed at the <strong>West Coast Metabolomics Center (University of California Davis Genome Center)</strong>, using 50 μl of serum as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Barupal DK, Fan S, Wancewicz B, Cajka T, Sa M, Showalter MR, Baillie R, Tenenbaum JD, Louie G; Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative; Alzheimer's Disease Metabolomics Consortium; Kaddurah-Daouk R, Fiehn O. Generation and quality control of lipidomics data for the alzheimer's disease neuroimaging initiative cohort. Sci Data. 2018 Nov 20;5:180263. doi:10.1038/sdata.2018.263. PMID:30457571.</p>	<p>Extraction solvents methanol (MeOH) and methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) were sonicated for 5 min, then stored at -20 °C prior to use. Serum and plasma (<strong>QC</strong>) samples were thawed at room temperature and placed on ice. Samples were vortexed for about 5 s to obtain homogenized samples. For each sample, a 20 μL aliquot was transferred to an Eppendorf tube and 225 μL of -20 °C cold MeOH containing a mixture of internal standards was added. Samples were vortexed for 10 s. Then 750 μL of -20 °C MTBE with Cholesteryl ester (CE, 22:1) was added, vortexed for 10 s and shaken for 6 min at 4 °C. LC-MS grade water (188 μL) was added and the mixture was vortexed for 20 s. Samples were centrifuged for 2 min at 14,000 rcf. The upper organic phase was divided into 2 aliquots of 350 μL. Solvents were removed under vacuum to complete dryness in a speed-vacuum dryer. 1 aliquot was kept at -20 °C as backup, the other dried-down sample was re-suspended in 100 μL of a MeOH/toluene (9:1, v/v) mixture containing 12-[[(cyclohexylamino)carbonyl]amino]-dodecanoic acid (CUDA) as internal standard (Cayman Chemical, Michigan, USA) and vortexed for 15 s.</p><p><br></p><p>For quality controls and for normalizations, 1 <strong>blank </strong>negative control extraction was prepared per 10 actual samples from empty Eppendorf tubes as starting material. Also, 1<strong> Bioreclamation plasma</strong> (HMPLEDTA, BioIVT, Westbury, NY) was used as <strong>QC</strong> sample per 10 actual study samples and <strong>NIST SRM 1950 plasma</strong> (Standard Reference Material 'metabolites in human plasma') was also employed as<strong> QC</strong>. In addition to these, <strong>Pooled QC serum samples</strong> were prepared by pooling 10 μL of each study sample.</p>	<p>An <strong>Agilent 1290 Infinity UHPLC</strong> equiped with an <strong>Acquity charged surface hybrid technology (CSH) C18 (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm column, Waters)</strong> and <strong>Acquity VanGuard CSH C18 (5 x 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm, Waters)</strong> pre-column, were used.&nbsp;Mobile phase A (60:40 ACN:water + 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid) was prepared by mixing 600 mL ACN, 400 mL water, 1 mL formic acid and 630 mg of ammonium formate. Mobile phase B solvent (90:10 IPA:ACN + 10 mM ammonium formate + 0.1% formic acid) was prepared by mixing 900 mL IPA, 100 mL acetonitrile, 1 mL formic acid, 630 mg ammonium formate previously dissolved in 1 mL of H2O. Both solvents were mixed and sonicated for 10 min (twice) before their use. For ESI (-) the composition of mobile phases was identical but 10 mM ammonium acetate (771 mg per 1 L) was used instead as modifier. The liquid chromatography gradient used a 0.6 mL/min linear velocity flow rate. The gradient started at 15% B, ramped to 30% at 2 min, 48% at 2.5 min, 82% at 11 min, 99% at 11.5 min and kept at 99% B until 12 min before ramping down to 15% B at 12.1 min which was kept isocratic until 15 min to equilibrate the column. The total run time was 15 min.&nbsp;Injection volume was 1.7 μL for the ESI (+) mode and 5 μL for the ESI (-) mode.</p><p><br></p><p>Before starting each batch of sample analyses, 1 empty injection, 5 solvent injections (MeOH/toluene 9:1 with CUDA internal standard), 2 extraction blanks and 2 Bioreclamation pool plasma samples (<strong>QCs</strong>) were injected. Instrument performance was monitored for column backpressure and signal intensities of the reference compounds.&nbsp;</p>	<p>An <strong>Agilent 6530 Accurate-Mass Q-TOF</strong> and an <strong>Agilent 6550 iFunnel Q-TOF</strong> were used. The mass spectrometer was tuned before each batch as per the vendor’s instructions using Agilent tune mix (mass resolving power ~10000 Full width at half maximum (FWHM) and ~20000 FWHM for the Agilent 6530 and 6550, respectively). A reference solution of purine and HP-0921 (<em>m</em>/<em>z</em>&nbsp;121.0509,&nbsp;<em>m</em>/<em>z</em>&nbsp;922.0098 in electrospray ionization (ESI) (+) and&nbsp;<em>m</em>/<em>z</em>&nbsp;119.0360 and&nbsp;<em>m</em>/<em>z</em>&nbsp;980.0164 (acetate adducts) in ESI (-)) was used to correct small mass drifts during the acquisition. The quadrupole/time-of-flight (QTOF) are operated with electrospray ionization (<strong>ESI</strong>) performing full scan in the mass range <strong>m/z 65-1700</strong> in <strong>positive</strong> (<strong>Agilent 6530</strong>, equipped with a <strong>JetStreamSource</strong>) and <strong>negative </strong>(<strong>Agilent 6550</strong>, equipped with a <strong>dual JetStream Source</strong>) modes, producing both unique and complementary spectra. Instrument parameters were as follows for the ESI (+) mode: gas temperature 325 °C, gas flow 8 L/min, nebulizer 35 psig, sheath gas temperature 350 °C, sheath gas flow 11, capillary voltage 3500 V, nozzle voltage 1000 V, fragmentor voltage 120 V and skimmer 65 V. For the ESI (-) mode: gas temperature 200°C, gas flow 14 L/min, fragmentor 175 V, with the other parameters identical to (+) ion mode. Data are collected in centroid mode at a rate of 2 scans/s.</p>	<p>Internal standards were used for the calibration of retention times. Raw peak heights were normalized using systematic error removal using random forest&nbsp;(<strong>SERRF</strong>) to eliminate unwanted systematic variation</p>	<p>Lipids were annotated by matching the mass and retention time against an internal library of validated lipids routinely detected in blood samples at the West Coast Metabolomics Center, UC Davis. In this database, annotated lipids are associated with retention time, adducts,&nbsp;<em>m</em>/<em>z</em>&nbsp;value and InChiKeys, verified by accurate mass, isotope ratio, retention time and MS/MS spectra matching to either commercial lipid standards or to the <strong>LipidBlast library[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Kind T, Liu KH, Lee DY, DeFelice B, Meissen JK, Fiehn O. LipidBlast in silico tandem mass spectrometry database for lipid identification. Nat Methods. 2013 Aug;10(8):755-8. doi:10.1038/nmeth.2551. PMID:23817071.</p>

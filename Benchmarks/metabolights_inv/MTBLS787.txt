Study Identifier	MTBLS787
Study Title	Correlation-based Deconvolution (CorrDec) to generate high quality MS2 spectra from data independent acquisition in multi-sample studies (Chemical standards assay)
Study Description	<p>Data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry (DIA-MS) is essential for information-rich spectral annotations in untargeted metabolomics. However, the acquired MS2 spectra are highly complex, posing significant annotation challenges. We have developed a correlation-based deconvolution (CorrDec) method that uses ion abundance correlations in multi-sample studies using DIA-MS as an update of our MS-DIAL software. CorrDec is based on the assumption that peak intensities of precursor and fragment ions correlate across samples, and exploits this quantitative information to deconvolute complex DIA spectra. CorrDec clearly improved deconvolution of the original MS-DIAL deconvolution method (MS2Dec) in a dilution series of chemical standards and a 224-sample urinary metabolomics study. The primary advantage of CorrDec over MS2Dec is the ability to discriminate co-eluting low-abundance compounds. CorrDec requires the measurement of multiple samples to successfully deconvolute DIA spectra, and our randomized assessment demonstrated that CorrDec can contribute to studies with as few as 10 unique samples. The presented methodology improves compound annotation and identification in multi-sample studies and will be useful for applications in large cohort studies.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Chemical standards assay</strong> is reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS787' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS787</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>Urine assay</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS816' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS816</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Chemical standards of proline betaine, trigonelline, dimethylglycine, trimethylamine N-oxide, tyrosine, betaine, proline, 3-OH-kynurenine were purchased and 5-60 mM stock solutions were prepared as specified in <strong>Table S5</strong> of the paper associated with this study.</p>	<p>Urine matrix for the serial dilutions of the compounds was prepared as follows. Pooled reference urine sample was normalized to specific gravity (SG) of 1.002 using LC-MS grade water and diluted 10-fold with acetonitrile containing internal standards (see <strong>Table S6</strong> in the paper associated with this study). The first dilution was prepared for each of the compounds at a starting concentration of 400 μM. Then, as shown in <strong>Figure S6</strong> in the paper associated with this study, 2 x 10-fold serial dilutions and 10 x 1.5-fold serial dilutions were performed in a 96-well 0.2 ml PCR plate PCR-96-MJ (BMBio, Tokyo, Japan) using 8-channel pipette to obtain end concentrations ranging from 69 to 4000 nM. A pierceable seal 4Ti-0531 (4titude, UK) was used to seal the plate for 4 s at 185 °C using PX1 heat sealer (Biorad, USA). The plate was stored at 4 °C until LC-MS measurement. 2 μl of each dilution were injected with a water blank between each compound dilution series (see <strong>Table S7</strong> in the paper associated with this study).</p>	<p>Solvents used for the LC-MS analysis and sample preparation were of LC-MS grade and purchased from Wako (Japan). LC-MS measurements in AIF mode were performed as described previously<strong>[1][2]</strong>. Briefly, metabolite separation was performed on a HILIC SeQuant ZIC-HILIC (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) column 100 Å (100 x 2.1 mm, 3.5 μm particle size) coupled to a guard column (2.1 x 20 mm, 3.5 μm particle size). Sample analysis was performed using water with 0.1% formic acid (pH 2.63; solvent A) and acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid (solvent B). The elution gradient used was as follows: isocratic step at 95% B for 1.5 min, 95 to 40% B in 12 min, maintained at 40% B for 2 min, then decreasing to 25% B at 14.2 min, maintained for 2.8 min, then returned to initial conditions over 1 min, and a column re-equilibration phase of 7 min. The flow rate was 0.3 ml/min, injection volume 2 μl, and the column oven was maintained at 25 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Naz S, Gallart-Ayala H, Reinke SN, Mathon C, Blankley R, Chaleckis R, Wheelock CE. Development of a Liquid Chromatography-High Resolution Mass Spectrometry Metabolomics Method with High Specificity for Metabolite Identification Using All Ion Fragmentation Acquisition. Anal Chem. 2017 Aug 1;89(15):7933-7942. doi:10.1021/acs.analchem.7b00925. PMID:28641411</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Chaleckis R, Naz S, Meister I, Wheelock CE. LC-MS-Based Metabolomics of Biofluids Using All-Ion Fragmentation (AIF) Acquisition. Methods Mol Biol. 2018;1730:45-58. doi:10.1007/978-1-4939-7592-1_3. PMID:29363064</p>	Data was acquired in positive ionization mode on an Agilent 6550 Q-TOF-MS system in centroid mode. Nitrogen was used as a sheath gas and drying gas at a flow of 8 and 15 l/min, respectively. The drying and sheath gas temperature was set at 250 °C with the nebulizer pressure at 35 psig and voltage 3000 V. Data was obtained with a mass range of 40−1200 m/z in AIF mode including 3 sequential experiments at alternating collision energies: 1 full scan at 0 eV (fragmentor voltage 380 V), followed by 1 MS/MS scan at 10 eV (fragmentor voltage at 175 V), and then followed by 1 MS/MS scan at 30 eV (fragmentor voltage at 380 V). The data acquisition rate was 6 scans/s.	<p>Data was transformed from Agilent .d format to .abf format using <a href='https://www.reifycs.com/AbfConverter/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>Reifycs Abf (Analysis Base File) Converter</a><a href='Reifycs Abf (Analysis Base File) Converter' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>.</a></p>	<p>Data was analysed using MS-DIAL software as described in the publication associated with this study. Abundances have been reported as peak intensities.</p>

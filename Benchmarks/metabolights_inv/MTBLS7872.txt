Study Identifier	MTBLS7872
Study Title	Pathogenic NLRP3 mutants form constitutively active inflammasomes resulting in immune-metabolic limitation of IL-1β production
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:</strong> Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndrome (CAPS) is an autoinflammatory condition consequence of monoallelic variants in the NLRP3 gene that exacerbate IL-1β production. These variants are gain-of-function, but the exact regulatory mechanism of the NLRP3 CAPS-inflammasome is not well yet understood. It is considered as a hypersensitive inflammasome triggered by cell priming, but patients with CAPS and animal disease models present inflammatory flares in the absence of external triggers.</p><p><strong>OBJECTIVES:</strong> To study the regulation and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in CAPS.</p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> CAPS derived blood samples and genetically modified macrophages expressing different NLRP3 CAPS-associated variants were used to assess NLRP3 function dissociated from cell priming and cell metabolism.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> We found that CAPS-associated variants constitutively result in active NLRP3 inflammasomes, that induce a basal cleavage of gasdermin D, IL-18 release and pyroptosis. The constitutive active NLRP3 inflammasome was blocked by MCC950 and was dependent on NLRP3 expression level, being further regulated by deubiquitination. We also showed that activation of NF-κB with lipopolysaccharide or other endogenous host-derived molecules (palmitate, S100A9 or IL-6) further modulated the activation of the NLRP3 CAPS inflammasome and expanded the repertoire of molecules secreted from CAPS macrophages to IL-1β, IL-1α, HMGB1, cystatin B and the P2X7 receptor, identifying novel proteins involved in CAPS pathogenesis. NLRP3 inflammasomes with CAPS associated variants affected the immunometabolism of the myeloid compartment and impaired glycolysis.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> These findings demonstrate that NLRP3 CAPS-associated variants form a constitutive active inflammasome that induce basal pyroptosis and IL-18 release without cell priming, suggesting a priming-independent mechanism for the initiation of CAPS flares characterized with a profound affection in the immunometabolism.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Monocyte purification</strong></p><p>Monocytes were isolated from PBMCs using the MagniSort Human CD14 Positive Selection Kit (Thermofisher) and 2 x 10^6 monocytes were cultured in Opti-MEM Reduced Serum Media (Life Technologies) at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in presence or absence of <strong>500 ng/mL LPS</strong> for <strong>2 h</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Immortalized mouse macrophages generation and stimulation.</strong></p><p>For <strong>doxycycline</strong>-inducible expression of NLRP3 variants in Nlrp3-/- immortalized mouse macrophages (a gift from I. Hafner-Bratkovič, National Institute of Chemistry, Ljubljana, Slovenia), we used the Tet-ON retroviral system (Clontech) with <strong>NLRP3-YFP wild type</strong> or <strong>p.D303N</strong>, p.T348M and p.R260W <strong>mutants</strong> as previously described<strong>[1][2]</strong>. Cells were treated with <strong>doxycycline </strong>(0.25, 0.50 and <strong>1 µg/mL) </strong>to express NLRP3, in the presence or absence of MCC950, ATP, MSU, <strong>LPS</strong> (<strong>100 ng/mL, 4 h</strong>), Pam3-CSK4, S100A9, IL-6 or palmitate at different concentrations and incubation times as indicated in the figure legends in the paper associated with this study. In some experiments, the cells were also incubated with the following deubiquitinase inhibitors G5 (5 µM), PR-619 (10 µM) and b-AP15 (5 µM).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Control cultures</strong> without cells were run in parallel as metabolic control.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Tapia-Abellán, A., Angosto-Bazarra, D., Martínez-Banaclocha, H., de Torre-Minguela, C., Cerón-Carrasco, J.P., Pérez-Sánchez, H., Arostegui, JI., Pelegrin, P. MCC950 closes the active conformation of NLRP3 to an inactive state. Nat Chem Biol. 2019 Jun;15(6):560-564. doi:10.1038/s41589-019-0278-6. Epub 2019 May 13. Erratum in: Nat Chem Biol. 2021 Mar;17(3):361. PMID: 31086329.</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Hafner-Bratkovič, I., Sušjan, P., Lainšček, D.,&nbsp;Tapia-Abellán, A.,&nbsp;Cerović, K.,&nbsp;Kadunc, L.,&nbsp;Angosto-Bazarra,&nbsp;D., Pelegrin, P., Jerala, R. NLRP3 lacking the leucine-rich repeat domain can be fully activated via the canonical inflammasome pathway.&nbsp;<em>Nat Commun</em>&nbsp;9, 5182 (2018). doi:10.1038/s41467-018-07573-4. PMID:&nbsp;30518920.</p>	<p>After stimulation, cells were washed with PBS twice and resuspended in<strong> 2 mL of MeOH</strong> (PanReac AppliChem) to obtain polar metabolites. A total of 4 replicates of each group were processed. Samples were homogenized in a vortex for 1 min, and then centrifuged for 10 min at 6000 x g at 4 °C (Thermo Scientific). The resultant supernatants were filtered through a 0.22 µm PVDF filter prior the injection in the UPLC-ESI-QTOF-MS system.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was carried out using an <strong>Agilent 1290 Infinity LC</strong> system and a <strong>Poroshell 120 EC-C18 (2.7 µm, 3 mm x 100 mm; Agilent Technologies) </strong>reverse phase column at 30 °C, using water with 0.1% formic acid (Phase A) and acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid (Phase B) as mobile phases with a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The following gradient was used: 0-10 min, 1-18% B; 10-16 min, 18-38% B; 16-22 min, 38-95% B. Finally, the phase B content was returned to the initial conditions (1%) for 1 min and the column re-equilibrated then for 5 min.</p>	<p>Analyses were performed with an <strong>Agilent 6550 Accurate-Mass Quadrupole Time-of-Flight (Q-TOF) (Agilent Technologies)</strong> using an electrospray interface (Jet Stream Technology). The mass analyser was operated under the following conditions: gas temperature 180 °C, drying gas 12 L/min, nebulizer pressure 45 psi, sheath gas temperature 350 °C, sheath gas flow 11 L/min, capillary voltage 3500 V, nozzle voltage 250 V, fragmentor voltage 350 V, and octupole radiofrequency voltage 250 V. Data were acquired over the <strong>m/z range of 50-1700</strong> at the rate of 2 spectra/s. The data were acquired in negative and positive polarity.</p>	<p>The raw data files were acquired by the UPLC-ESI-QTOF-MS system in profile file mode and were exported to <strong>MZmine software (Version 2.53, 2005-2015 MZmine Development Team)</strong> to create the data matrix. The raw data generated of both polarities were pre-processed separately by a batch set of parameters including the mass detection, chromatogram builder and deconvolution and alignment algorithm.</p><p><br></p><p>The pre-processing operations from full data set gave a data matrix based on 203 entities and 925 entities in negative and positive polarities respectively. 423 metabolites were found differentially present in monocytes from healthy individuals and CAPS patients. From those metabolites, 46 were upregulated in monocytes from healthy donors upon LPS stimulation, and were also upregulated in both, untreated and LPS-treated monocytes from CAPS compared to untreated monocytes from healthy donors. Then, 121 metabolites were upregulated, and 256 metabolites were downregulated in monocytes from CAPS when compared to monocytes from healthy individuals.</p>	<p>The final list of features obtained after processing the data matrices was used for metabolite identification with databases according to the exact mass, achieving 'level 2' or tentative identification<strong>[1]</strong>.&nbsp;The final list of significate metabolites were exported to MPP (Agilent Mass Profiler Professional) for metabolite annotation by the acquired <a href='https://metlin.scripps.edu/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>Metlin</strong></a> databases, including <strong>Metlin Metabolites</strong> and <strong>Metlin lipids</strong> databases. The match tolerance criteria was set in 2.00 mDa.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Sumner, L.W., Amberg, A., Barrett, D.&nbsp;et al.&nbsp;Proposed minimum reporting standards for chemical analysis.&nbsp;Metabolomics&nbsp;3, 211–221 (2007). doi:10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2. PMID:&nbsp;24039616.</p>

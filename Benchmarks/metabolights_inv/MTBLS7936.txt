Study Identifier	MTBLS7936
Study Title	Comparison of intestinal microbes and metabolites in active VKH versus acute anterior uveitis associated with ankylosing spondylitis
Study Description	<p><strong>BACKGROUND:&nbsp;</strong>It has been reported that the gut microbiome is involved in the pathogenesis of uveitis, but the specific pathogenic microbes and metabolites in different types of uveitis are still unclear.</p><p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>METHODS</strong><strong>:&nbsp;</strong>Microbiome and metabolites were detected using 16S ribosomal DNA and LC‒MS/MS (liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry) in 45 individuals, including 16 patients with Vogt Koyanagi Harada (VKH), 11 patients with acute anterior uveitis (AAU) and 18 healthy controls.</p><p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>RESULT:</strong>&nbsp;The diversity of intestinal microbes among the VKH, AAU and control groups was not significantly different. 13 specific microbes and 38 metabolites were detected in the VKH group, and 7 metabolites (vanillin, erythro-isoleucine, pyrimidine, 1-aminocyclopropanecarboxylic acid, beta-tocopherol, (-)-gallocatechin and N1-methyl-4-pyridone-3-carboxamide) significantly changed only in patients with VKH, which mainly acted on nicotinamide and nicotinamide metabolism and biotin metabolism (p&lt;0.05). Compared with the VKH group, the AAU group had milder intestinal changes. Only 11 specific microbes and 29 metabolites changed in the AAU group, while these metabolites were not specific (p&lt;0.05). These metabolites mainly acted on arachidonic acid metabolism. In addition, 3 microbes and 2 metabolites had the same changes in the VKH and AAU groups (p&lt;0.05). Multiple correlations were found between gut microbes and metabolites in the VKH and AAU groups. 6 microbes (Pediococcus, Pseudomonas, Rhodococcus, Photobacterium, Gardnerella and Lawsonia) and 2 metabolites (pyrimidine and gallocatechin) as biomarkers could effectively distinguish patients with VKH from patients with AAU and healthy individuals, with AUC (area under the curve) values greater than 82%. 4 microbes (Lentilactobacillus, Lachnospiraceae_UCG-010, Cetobacterium, Liquorilactobacillus) could distinguish patients with AAU from patients with VKH and healthy controls with AUC&gt;76%.</p><p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>CONCLUSION: </strong>Significant differences in intestinal microbes and metabolites suggest their different roles in the pathogenesis of uveitis entities. Changes in the metabolism of certain B vitamins may be involved in the pathogenesis of VKH.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>A total of 45 individuals were involved in this study, including <strong>16</strong> patients with active <strong>Vogt Koyanagi Harada</strong> (<strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>VKH</strong><strong> group</strong>, average age: 49.6 ± 13.5 yr, male/female: 0.78:1), <strong>11</strong> patients with <strong>ankylosing spondylitis associated with acute anterior uveitis</strong> (<strong>AAU group</strong>, average age: 40.9 ± 6.7 yr, male/female: 1.75:1), and <strong>18</strong> <strong>healthy controls</strong> (<strong>C group</strong>, average age: 39.1 ± 12.5 yr, male/female: 1.25:1). The criteria for inclusion of patients were as follows: <strong>1)</strong> the patients’ disease was in an active stage and had not received drug treatment; <strong>2)</strong> there was no other immune-related disease, such as inflammatory bowel disease or lupus erythematosus and <strong>3)</strong> there are no infectious diseases. <strong>Fecal samples</strong> were collected from <strong>VKH</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>AAU</strong> patients and <strong>healthy controls</strong>. All samples were frozen immediately and then stored at -80 °C.</p>	<p><strong>10 mg of fecal sample</strong> were weighed into an Eppendorf tube and <strong>1000 μL of</strong> <strong>methanol:water (3:1)</strong> extracting solution were added. After swirling the solution, ultrasound in an ice water bath, and centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and placed into a fresh glass vial for analysis. The following isotopically labeled internal standards were added to samples for <strong>ESI MS positive</strong> ionization mode: L-leucine-5,5,5-d3 (8 µM/L), trimethylamine-d9 N-oxide (2 µM/L) and 4-aminobutyric acid-2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4-d6 (1 µM/L). And L-leucine-5,5,5-d3 (8 µM/L), [13C3]-L-(+)-sodium lactate (4 µM/L) and succinic acid-2, 2, 3, 3-d4 (2 µM/L), were added to samples for <strong>ESI MS negative</strong> ionization mode analysis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>QC</strong><span style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'> samples were prepared by mixing </span>equal amounts of supernatant from all samples.</p>	<p>Analyses were performed using an <strong>Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System</strong> with an<strong> ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters)</strong>. The column temperature was 30 °C. Mobile phase A consisted of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia hydroxide in water (pH = 9.75) and mobile phase B, acetonitrile. The elution gradient was as follows: 0-0.5 min, 95% B; 0.5-7.0 min, 65% B; 7.0-8.0 min, 40% B; 8.0-9.0 min, 40% B; 9.0-9.1 min, 95% B; 9.1-12.0 min, 95% B. The flow rate was set at 0.5 mL/min, the autosampler temperature was 4 °C and the injection volume was 2 μL.</p>	<p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Q Exactive HF-X </strong>mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire <strong>MS/MS</strong> spectra on information-dependent acquisition (<strong>IDA</strong>) mode in the control of the acquisition software (<strong>Xcalibur, Thermo</strong>). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the full scan MS spectrum. The <strong>ESI</strong> source conditions were set as following: sheath gas flow rate as 30 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 25 Arb, capillary temperature 350 °C, full MS resolution as 120,000, MS/MS resolution as 7500, collision energy as 10/30/60 in NCE mode, spray Voltage as 3.6 kV (<strong>positive</strong>) or -3.2 kV (<strong>negative</strong>), respectively and <strong>m/z range 100-1500</strong>.</p>	<p>The raw data were converted to the <strong>mzXML</strong> format using <a href='https://proteowizard.sourceforge.io/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>ProteoWizard</strong></a> and processed with an in-house program, which was developed using <a href='https://www.r-project.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>R</strong></a> and based on <a href='https://xcmsonline.scripps.edu/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>XCMS</strong></a>, for peak detection, extraction, alignment and integration.</p><p><br></p>	<p>An in-house MS2 database (<strong>BiotreeDB</strong>) was applied in metabolite annotation. The cutoff for annotation was set at 0.3.&nbsp;</p>

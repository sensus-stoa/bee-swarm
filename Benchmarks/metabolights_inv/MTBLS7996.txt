Study Identifier	MTBLS7996
Study Title	Exogenous L-Alanine promotes phagocytosis multidrug-resistant bacterial pathogens (V. parahaemolyticus-infected spleen metabolomics)
Study Description	<p>Multidrug-resistant bacteria present a major threat to public health. Therefore, new drugs or approaches are urgently needed to manage and mitigate this threat. Here, we screen the molecular candidates that allow the survival of mice upon <em>Vibrio parahaemolyticus</em> infection by integrated proteomic and metabolomics analysis, where L-Alanine metabolism and phagocytosis are highly correlated. The role of L-Alanine on boosting mouse survival is further confirmed with <em>in vivo</em> bacterial challenge studies on bacteria including <em>V. parahaemolyticus</em>, <em>Escherichia coli</em>, <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em> and <em>Klebsiella pneumoniae</em>. Functional studies demonstrate that exogenous L-Alanine promotes phagocytosis to these different species of multidrug-resistant pathogens. The underlying mechanism involves two events that are L-Alanine-dependently increased TLR4 expression and L-Alanine-enhanced TLR4 signaling via increasing the biosynthesis and secretion of fatty acids such as palmitate. Palmitate enhances the binding of LPS to TLR4 and thereby promotes TLR4 dimmer formation and endocytosis for the subsequent activation of PI3K/Akt and NF-κB pathways and phagocytosis of bacteria. These results suggest that modulation of metabolic environment is a plausible approach for combating infection with multidrug-resistant bacteria.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>V. parahaemolyticus-infected spleen metabolomics</strong> is reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS7996' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS7996</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>L-Alanine-treated macrophage metabolomics</strong> is reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS7986' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS7986</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Study design</strong></p><p>The aim of this study is to identify the metabolite(s) that potentiate innate immunity to kill multidrug-resistant bacteria. We adopted the integrated metabolomic and proteomic approach to profile spleens after being infected with <em>Vibrio parahaemolyticus</em>. The crucial biomarker was identified through multivariate analysis. Metabolites that could potentiate phagocytosis was investigated in primary macrophage, macrophage cell lines and in mouse model. Using biochemical, immunological and cell biology approaches, we revealed the mechanism of metabolite action on phagocytosis.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ethics statement</strong></p><p>This study was conducted in accordance with the recommendations in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the National Institutes of Health and maintained according to the standard protocols. All experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Sun Yat-sen University (SYSUIACUC-2020-B1267).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Preparing of spleen samples for proteomics and metabolomics analysis</strong></p><p>A total of 63 mice were randomly divided into 3 groups with 30 mice for group 1, 30 mice for group 2 and 3 mice for control group. Group 1 and group 2 of the 2 groups was averagely divided into 3 subgroups. For group 1 and group 2, mice were orderly infected by intraperitoneal injection with 2 x 10^8 CFU, 3 x 10^8 CFU and 4 x 10^8 CFU (N = 10 for each dose) of virulent <em>V. parahaemolyticus</em>, while control mice (N=3) were injected with saline buffer as control. Prior to this, pre-test showed that mouse die mostly occurred during 12-48 h and stopped in 72 h when mice were infected the same doses of the pathogen. Thus, mice in group 1 that survived at 72 h post-infection (we obtained survival mice N = 6 for 2 x 10^8 CFU infection, N = 4 for 3 x 10^8 CFU infection and N = 3 for 4 x 10^8 CFU infection) were collected as survival sample, and mice in group 2 (N = 30) at 6 h post-infection, without showing any symptoms, were collected. Thus, a total of 43 infected mice were collected, and their spleens were extracted - 4/5 of the whole spleen was used for proteomics analysis and the remaining was collected for metabolomics analysis<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Chen XH, Liu SR, Peng B, Li D, Cheng ZX, Zhu JX, Zhang S, Peng YM, Li H, Zhang TT, Peng XX. Exogenous l-Valine Promotes Phagocytosis to Kill Multidrug-Resistant Bacterial Pathogens. Front Immunol. 2017 Mar 6;8:207. doi: 10.3389/fimmu.2017.00207. PMID:28321214.</p>	<p>For metabolite extraction of spleen, the 2-step extraction method was used as previously described<strong>[1][2]</strong>. Briefly, spleen tissues were weighed and homogenized with the first solvent (the mixture of chloroform, methanol and water (1:2:1, v/v/v)) for 30 s at 4 °C and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and deposit was re-homogenized with the second solvent (methanol alone) before a second centrifugation. The two supernatants were mixed, and aliquot of sample was transferred to a GC sampling vial containing 5 μL 0.1 mg/mL ribitol (Sigma) as an analytical internal standard and then dried in a vacuum centrifuge concentrator before the subsequent derivatization. In total 2 technical replicates were prepared for each sample.</p><p><br></p><p>Briefly, the cells were quenched with cold methanol containing 10 mL of 0.1 mg/mL ribitol (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), which served as an analytical internal standard. Cells were then lysed by sonication for 10 min and centrifuged for another 10 min at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C. The supernatant was dried in a rotary vacuum centrifuge device (LABCONCO). The dried extracts were derivatized; 80 mL of 20 mg/mL methoxyamine hydrochloride (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was added to the dried samples, mixed, and incubated at 37 °C for 90 min; 80 mL N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide (MSTFA; Sigma-Aldrich) was added, followed by incubation at 37 °C for another 30 min. The derivatized samples were centrifuged and supernatants were collected.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Pan L, Qiu Y, Chen T, Lin J, Chi Y, Su M, Zhao A, Jia W. An optimized procedure for metabonomic analysis of rat liver tissue using gas chromatography/time-of-flight mass spectrometry. J Pharm Biomed Anal. 2010 Aug 1;52(4):589-96. doi:10.1016/j.jpba.2010.01.046. Epub 2010 Feb 4. PMID:20185264.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Jiang M, Chen ZG, Zheng J, Peng B. Metabolites-Enabled Survival of Crucian Carps Infected by Edwardsiella tarda in High Water Temperature. Front Immunol. 2019 Aug 22;10:1991. doi:10.3389/fimmu.2019.01991. PMID:31507599.</p>	<p>GC-MS analysis was carried out with a variation on the two-stage technique as described previously<strong>[1][2]</strong>. In brief, samples was derivatized and then used to firstly protect carbonyl moieties through methoximation, through a 90 min 37 °C reaction with 40 μL of 20 mg/mL methoxyamine hydrochloride (Sigma-Aldrich) in pyridine, followed by derivatization of acidic protons through a 30 min 37 °C reaction with the addition of 80 μL N-methyl-Ntrimethylsilyltrifluoroace-tamide (MSTFA, Sigma-Aldrich). The derivatized sample of 1 μL was injected into a 30 m x 250 μm i.d. x 0.25 μm DBS-MS column using splitless injection and analysis was carried out by Trace DSQ II (Thermo Scientific). The initial temperature of the GC oven was held at 85 °C for 5 min followed by an increase to 330 °C at a rate of 15 °C/min then held for 5 min. Helium was used as carrier gas and flow was kept constant at 1 mL/min. The MS was operated in a range of 50-600 m/z.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Chen XH, Zhang BW, Li H, Peng XX. A retention time (RT) correction was performed for all the samples, and then the RT was used as reference against which the remaining spectra were queried and a file containing the abundance information for each metabolite in all the samples was assembled. Metabolites from the GC-MS spectra were identified by searching in National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) library used the NIST MS search 2.0. The resulting data matrix was normalized using the concentrations of added internal standards which were subsequently removed so that the data could be used for modeling consisted of extracted compound. The resulting normalized peak intensities form a single matrix with Rt-m/z pairs for each file in the dataset. To reduce between-sample variation we centered the imputed metabolic measures for each tissue sample on its median value and scaled it by its inter-quartile range (IQR)<strong>[2]</strong>. In the integration of proteomic and metabolomic data set, the z-score analysis scaled each protein or metabolite according to a reference distribution. The control samples were designated as the reference distribution. Thus, the mean and standard deviation of the control samples was determined for each metabolite or protein. Then each sample was centered by the control mean and scaled by the control standard deviation, per molecule. In this way, we can know how the molecule expressions deviate from the control state.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Jiang M, Gong QY, Lai SS, Cheng ZX, Chen ZG, Zheng J, Peng B.

Study Title	A noteworthy response process of Microcystis aeruginosa induced by exogenous reactive oxygen species in algae-laden water treatment
Study Description	The effectiveness of reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) in inactivating harmful algae in drinking water varies considerably. Therefore, the physiological and biochemical reactions of algae need to be considered to clarify the underlying mechanisms. Herein, we studied the response mechanisms of Microcystis aeruginosa to exogenous ROS.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The method for obtaining Fe-loaded graphite-phase carbon&nbsp;nitride&nbsp;(FeCN) material is described in a previous study[1].&nbsp;<em>Microcystis aeruginosa</em>&nbsp;(no. FACHB-905) were purchased from the Institute of Aquatic Biology, CAS. and cultured at 3000&nbsp;lx with a light–dark ratio of 12&nbsp;h:12&nbsp;h. The algal solution was cultured to an OD680&nbsp;of 0.150 for experimental use. FeCN material and&nbsp;peroxymonosulfate&nbsp;(PMS) were used as the treatment group, and equal doses were added to the algae solution simultaneously (0.1&nbsp;g/L without otherwise specified). Using 0.1&nbsp;M H2SO4&nbsp;and NaOH, the initial pH of the algal solution was adjusted to 7.5&nbsp;±&nbsp;0.2. At different times, the algal solution was mixed and sampled and then centrifuged at 4 ℃, 4000&nbsp;rpm for 10&nbsp;min (5810R, Eppendorf) to collect algal cells for further analysis.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[1] Yu, B., Li, X., He, M., Li, Y., Ding, J., Zhong, Y. and Zhang, H., 2023. Selective production of singlet oxygen for harmful cyanobacteria inactivation and cyanotoxins degradation: Efficiency and mechanisms.&nbsp;Journal of Hazardous Materials,&nbsp;441, p.129940. doi:10.1016/j.jhazmat.2022.129940.</p>	<p>Equal amounts of algal precipitation samples were re-dissolved with 1&nbsp;mL of methanol–water (V/V&nbsp;=&nbsp;4:1), and then 200 uL of&nbsp;chloroform&nbsp;was added. After ultrasonic crushing, the sample was extracted by&nbsp;ultrasonic extraction&nbsp;in an ice-water bath for 20&nbsp;min, then rested at −40&nbsp;°C for 2&nbsp;h. After centrifugation, the sample was filtered using a 0.22&nbsp;μm membrane and analyzed by LC-MS (ACQUITY UPLC I-Class plus). The analytical conditions and methods are presented in Text S2. </p>	<p>Samples were analyzed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class System and a ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm×2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) column. Column temperature was 45 °C; mobile phase: A-water (containing 0.1% formic acid), B-acetonitrile; flow rate: 0.35 mL/min.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>A Waters Vion IMS QTof quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer was used to perform the analysis of samples. </p>	<p>The original LC-MS data were processed by the software Progenesis QI V2.3 (Nonlinear, Dynamics, Newcastle, UK) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integral, retention time correction, peak alignment, and normalization. The main parameters of 5 ppm precursor tolerance, 10 ppm product tolerance, and 5% production threshold were applied. </p><p><br></p><p> &nbsp;</p>	<p>Compound identification was based on precise mass-to-charge ratio (M/z), secondary fragments, and isotopic distribution using The Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Lipidmaps (V2.3), Metlin, EMDB, PMDB, and self-built databases to do qualitative analysis. The extracted data were then further processed by removing any peaks with a missing value (ion intensity = 0) in more than 50% of groups, by replacing the zero value with half of the minimum value, and by screening according to the qualitative results of the compound. Compounds with resulting scores below 36 (out of 60) points were also deemed to be inaccurate and removed. A data matrix was combined from the positive and negative ion data.</p><p><br></p><p>The matrix was imported in R to carry out Principle Component Analysis (PCA) to observe the overall distribution among the samples and the stability of the whole analysis process. Orthogonal Partial Least-Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) and Partial Least-Squares-Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA) were utilized to distinguish the metabolites that differ between groups. To prevent overfitting, 7-fold cross-validation and 200 Response Permutation Testing (RPT) were used to evaluate the quality of the model.</p><p><br></p><p>Variable Importance of Projection (VIP) values obtained from the OPLS-DA model were used to rank the overall contribution of each variable to group discrimination. A two-tailed Student’s T-test was further used to verify whether the metabolites of difference between groups were significant. Differential metabolites were selected with VIP values greater than 1.0 and p-values less than 0.05.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>

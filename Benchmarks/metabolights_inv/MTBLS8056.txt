Study Identifier	MTBLS8056
Study Title	PREDICT: untargeted metabolomic profiling in a Brassica napus (canola) breeding population by GC-MS
Study Description	<p>Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) identified thousands of genetic loci associated with complex plant traits, including many traits of agronomical importance. However, functional interpretation of GWAS results remains challenging because of large candidate regions due to linkage disequilibrium. High-throughput omics technologies, such as genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics open new avenues for integrative systems biological analyses and help to nominate systems information supported (prime) candidate genes. In the present study, we capitalize on a diverse canola population with spring-type 477 lines which was previously analysed by high-throughput phenotyping (Knoch et al., 2020), and by RNA sequencing and metabolite profiling for multi-omics-based hybrid performance prediction (Knoch et al., 2021). We deepened the phenotypic data analysis, now providing 123 time-resolved image-based traits, to gain insight into the complex relations during early vegetative growth and re-analysed the transcriptome data based on the latest Darmor-bzh v10 genome assembly (Rousseau-Gueutin et al., 2020). Genome-wide association testing revealed 61,298 robust quantitative trait loci (QTL) including 187 metabolite-QTL, 56,814 expression-QTL, and 4,297 phenotypic QTL, many clustered in pronounced hotspots. Combining information about QTL colocalisation across omics layers and correlations between omics features allowed us to discover prime candidate genes for metabolic and vegetative growth variation. Prioritized candidate genes for early biomass accumulation include A06p05760.1_BnaDAR (PIAL1), A10p16280.1_BnaDAR, C07p48260.1_BnaDAR (PRL1), and C07p48510.1_BnaDAR (CLPR4). Moreover, we observed unequal effects of the Brassica A and C subgenomes on early biomass production.</p><p><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Here, we investigated a genetically diverse population of 477 spring-type canola lines. We re-analysed our previously generated multi-omics data sets<strong>[1][2]</strong>, including image-derived traits (i-traits) based on daily high-throughput phenotyping, gene expression and metabolite profiles from an early vegetative growth stage, and used them to perform extensive genome-wide association studies.&nbsp;Plants were cultivated under controlled environmental conditions in an incomplete randomized block design in 4 experiments<strong>[1]</strong>. The shoot material from <strong>4 plants per container</strong> was collected at 14 days after sowing (DAS), between 7 to 9 h after illumination, and immediately shock-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Plant material was homogenised in a scintillation vial (Zinsser Analytic GmbH, Eschborn, Germany) for 2 min at -60 °C using 2 x 8-mm steel balls and a cryogenic plant grinding and dispensing system (Labman Automation Ltd., Stokesley, United Kingdom). The material of <strong>4 individual plants per carrier</strong> was combined and equal amounts of plant material from all carriers from the different experiments were <strong>pooled</strong>. Samples from the first phenotyping experiment were omitted, as a breakdown of the cooling system in the glasshouse caused elevated temperatures during this experiment and an increased developmental speed, which might have biased the results. <strong>15 ± 1.5 mg fresh weight</strong> were dispersed in 1.4 ml storage tubes (Micronic, Lelystad, The Netherlands) for metabolite profiling. For transcript profiling, <strong>50 ± 1.5 mg fresh weight</strong> were manually weighed. Material was stored at −80 °C until use.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Knoch, D., Abbadi, A., Grandke, F., Meyer, R.C, Samans, B., Werner, C.R., Snowdon, R.J., Altmann, T.&nbsp;2020. Strong temporal dynamics of QTL action on plant growth progression revealed through high-throughput phenotyping in canola. Plant Biotechnol J 18:68–82. PMID:&nbsp;31125482. DOI:10.1111/pbi.13171</p><p><strong>[2] </strong>Knoch, D., Werner, C.R., Meyer, R.C., Riewe, D., Abbadi, A., Lücke, S., Snowdon, R.J., and Altmann, T. 2021. Multi-omics-based prediction of hybrid performance in canola. Theor Appl Genet, 134, 1147–1165. PMID:&nbsp;33523261. DOI:&nbsp;10.1007/s00122-020-03759-x</p>	<p>Metabolite profiling, quality controls and data normalization are as described in associated publication<strong>[1]</strong>. Polar metabolites were extracted from 15 mg deep-frozen, homogenised plant material using a previously described liquid–liquid extraction protocol<strong>[2]-[4]</strong>. The protocol was adjusted to 96 tubes/rack format and implemented on a liquid handling system (Biomek FXP, Beckman Coulter GmbH, Krefeld, Germany), 0.625 ml chilled extraction buffer (<strong>2.5:1:1 v/v MeOH/CHCl /H2O</strong>) and 0.250 ml H2O after incubation. Extraction was split into 6 batches with 96 samples each and aliquots of 50 μl of polar phase were sampled. The dried extracts were <strong>in-line chemically derivatized</strong> directly, using a two-step <strong>methoxyamination</strong> (20 mg/mL methoxyamine hydrochloride in pure pyridine)and <strong>per-silylation</strong> (N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide, <strong>MSTFA</strong>), prior to the <strong>GC-MS</strong> analysis of the reaction products as previously described<strong>[5]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Knoch, D., Werner, C.R., Meyer, R.C., Riewe, D., Abbadi, A., Lücke, S., Snowdon, R.J., and Altmann, T. 2021. Samples were analyzed in split mode (1:4) using an <strong>Agilent 7890A gas chromatography instrument</strong>,<strong> </strong>equipped with a <strong>DB-35ms GC Column (0.25 µm, 0.32 mm x 30 m; Agilent Technologies) </strong>as previously described<strong>[1]-[3]</strong>. A sample volume of 1 μl was injected using the auto-sampler at 250 °C with helium carrier gas flow set to 2 ml/min. The flow rate was kept constant with electronic pressure control enabled. Inlet temperature was set to 230 °C. The temperature program of the oven was isothermal for 3 min at 85 °C, followed by a 15 °C per min ramp to 340 °C. Temperature was held at this value for 1.5 min. The transfer line temperature was set to 250 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Riewe D, Koohi M, Lisec J., Pfeiffer,&nbsp;M., Lippmann, R.,&nbsp;Schmeichel, J.,&nbsp;Willmitzer, L.,&nbsp;Altmann, T. 2012. The instrument was tuned automatically according to the manufacturer’s instructions.</p>	<p>The metabolite intensities were normalized regarding to <strong>sample weight</strong> and <strong>measuring d/detector response</strong>. In total, 154 analytes of biological origin, 64 of known and 90 of unknown chemical structure, were quantified.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Cuadros-Inostroza, A., Caldana, C., Redestig, H., Kusano, M., Lisec, J., Peña-Cortés, H., Willmitzer, L., Hannah, M.A. 2009.

Study Identifier	MTBLS8062
Study Title	Mitochondrial NAD+ Deficiency in Vascular Smooth Muscle Impairs Collagen III Turnover to Trigger Aortic Disease
Study Description	<p>Aortic disease poses a significant mortality risk in adults, yet many of its underlying factors remain undiscovered. In this study, we identify mitochondrial NAD⁺ deficiency as a causal factor. Metabolomics analysis of 73 surgical aortic specimens revealed impaired NAD⁺ salvage and mitochondrial transport in diseased aortas. Precursor metabolites NMN and NAM were significantly elevated, while NAD⁺ levels were decreased, indicating a disrupted salvage biosynthesis pathway. Using mouse models, we provided mechanistic insights into the pathogenic process. The production of type III procollagen during aortic medial matrix turnover imposes a high demand for proline, an essential amino acid in collagen. A deficiency in the mitochondrial NAD⁺ pool, regulated by NAD⁺ salvage and transport, impairs proline biosynthesis in mitochondria, potentially contributing to the progression of aortic disease.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The thoracic aortic tissues were collected from 73 patients who underwent cardiac surgery with a normally functioning tricuspid aortic valve and a non-diseased ascending aorta (samples named 'Control_') or who underwent ascending aortic replacement (samples named 'TAA_', thoracic aortic aneurysm) at our institution. After excision from the body, the resected ascending aortic specimens were immediately transferred to the laboratory in cold PBS, carefully dissected in a sterile environment, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>In the mouse model, smooth muscle-specific knockouts of Nampt, Nmnat1 and Slc25a51 (samples named 'mouse_nampt/nmnat1/slc25a51'), as well as control mice (samples named 'mouse_myh11cre'), were infused with AngII for 3 days. The entire aorta was then isolated, washed 3 times with PBS and frozen.</p>	<p>Fresh frozen aorta were washed 3 times with PBS and homogenized in 2 mL precooled 80% HPLC-grade methanol. Homogenates were incubated for 2 h at -80 °C. After 10 min 14,000 x g centrifugation at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected and evaporated to dryness in a vacuum concentrator. The lyophilized extracts were resuspended in 100 µL of 1:1 anhydrous acetonitrile:H2O and sonicated 10 min. The mixture was centrifuged at 15,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected for LC-MS/MS.</p>	<p>For human aorta, the supernatant was analyzed on a Shimadzu LC30AD LC liquid chromatography system (Shimadzu). Samples were separated by XBridge BEH Amide columns (100 mm x 4.6 mm i.d., 3.5 μm, Waters) with a 26 min gradient at 300 μL/min. Buffer A and B were 20 mM ammonium acetate, 10 mM ammonium hydroxide in 5% acetonitrile (pH 10) and anhydrous acetonitrile, respectively. The chromatographic gradients were listed below: from 85% to 32% buffer B (3.5 min), from 32% to 2% buffer B (8.5 min) and then held at 2% buffer B for 4.5 min.</p><p><br></p><p>For mouse aorta, MRM analyses were performed to quantify proline using an H-Class HPLC system (Waters). Samples were separated on a Welchrom C18 (5 μm, 2.1 x 250 mm; Welch) column at a flow rate of 200 μL/min over a 20-min gradient. The gradient involved 100% solution A (0.1% formic acid in water) for 1 min, followed by a linear increase from 0% to 90% solution B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile) over 13 min. The column was held at 90% solution B for 2 min, then returned to 0% solution B over 0.1 min, and re-equilibrated with 100% solution A for 3.9 min. A proline standard was injected to quantify the level of proline in each sample.</p>	<p>For human aorta, the supernatant was analyzed using a Triple Quad 6500+ mass spectrometer (SCIEX). The electrospray ionization mass spectra were acquired in positive ion mode and negative ion mode, respectively. Multiple reaction monitoring acquisition method was used to collect MS information simultaneously. The ion spray voltage was set to 4850 V for positive mode and 4500 V for negative mode, and the heated-capillary temperature was maintained at 475 °C. The curtain gas flow, nebulizer and heater gas were set to 25, 33 and 33 arbitrary units, respectively. </p><p><br></p><p>For mouse aorta, MRM analyses were performed to quantify proline using an Triple Quad 6500+ mass spectrometer (SCIEX). The ion spray voltage was set to 5500 V for positive mode, the heated-capillary temperature was maintained at 500 °C. The curtain gas flow, nebulizer and heater gas were set to 40.</p>	<p>All mass spectrometry files from human aorta were detected and aligned using MultiQuan (SCIEX). MQ4 Integration Algorithm Parameters were employed, and all integrated chromatograms were manually inspected to ensure that the software identified and integrated the correct peak for each multiple reaction monitoring ion pair. Based on the in-house spectral library, compounds with signal-to-noise ratio greater than 3 were extracted to generate an identification list. MRM data from mouse aorta were processed using Skyline software (v22.2.0.255) to extract peak areas. Proline levels in mice aortas were quantified using a proline standard curve.</p>	<p>All mass spectrometry files from human aorta were detected and aligned using MultiQuan (SCIEX).

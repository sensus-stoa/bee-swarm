Study Identifier	MTBLS8088
Study Title	Chemodiverse cell systems responses to UV in an algal sister of land plants
Study Description	<p>Plant terrestrialization necessitated overcoming a barrage of stressors. Embryophytes (land plants) use an integrated response network to adjust their molecular physiology in response to terrestrial stressors—one of the important stressors is UV irradiance. The zygnematophytes are the closest streptophyte algal relatives of embryophytes, renowned for their UV resilience and key for inferring the UV response toolkit of the earliest embryophytes. Throughout evolution, specialized metabolism radiated, yielding chemodiverse responses to environmental challenges ranging from UV-shielding flavonoids and coumarins to the polymer lignin of tracheophytes16; homologs of the underpinning core pathway occur in streptophyte algae. Here, we exposed the zygnematophyte Mesotaenium to UV-B irradiation and profiled its physiological, morphological, transcriptomic, and metabolomic features. After UV-B exposure, the cells showed rapid photophysiological responses and progressively growing terminal vacuoles. Our transcriptome data capture dynamic changes in gene expression in (1) core downstream homologs of phenol metabolic enzymes, photophysiological homeostats, and DNA repair factors and (2) upstream components featuring key homologs of kinase-mediated signaling cascades, as well as light quality and abscisic acid-mediated signaling components. To scrutinize the acclimatory chassis, we created a metabolite feature database specifically for the Mesotaenium metabolome. The metabolome displayed pronounced temporal shifts, with several phenolic features that accumulate along the UV-stress–acclimation kinetics. Overall, we capture chemodiverse responses, including various phenolics such as methoxypsoralen-like derivatives and coumarins. We establish an integrated model for UV responses in the closest algal relatives of embryophytes, illuminating the toolkit that allowed the progenitors of embryophytes to move out of a protective water column.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Samples were analyzed using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (<strong>1290 Infinity</strong>, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). A volume of 2 μL was injected onto an <strong>Acquity HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm; Waters Corporation, Milford, USA)</strong> maintained at 40 °C. Metabolites were eluted at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min using a binary gradient of water (0.1% formic acid) and acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid), as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Feussner, K., Abreu, I.N., Klein, M. and Feussner, I., 2023. Metabolite fingerprinting: A powerful metabolomics approach for marker identification and functional gene annotation. In&nbsp;Methods in enzymology&nbsp;(Vol. 680, pp. 325-350). Academic Press.</p>	<p>Raw MS data were processed using <strong>MassHunter Profinder B.08.00 (Agilent Technologies) </strong>employing recursive feature extraction, as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. The resulting dataset was normalized to sample dry weight and subjected to statistical analysis using <strong>MetaboAnalyst 6.0.63</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Kasper, K., Abreu, I.N., Feussner, K., Zienkiewicz, K., Herrfurth, C., Ischebeck, T., Janz, D., Majcherczyk, A., Schmitt, K., Valerius, O. and Braus, G.H., 2022. Multi-omics analysis of xylem sap uncovers dynamic modulation of poplar defenses by ammonium and nitrate.&nbsp;The Plant Journal,&nbsp;111(1), pp.282-303.</p>	<p>Cultures were transferred into Eppendorf tubes and lyophilized overnight. 2 tungsten beads were added to each tube, and the material was pulverized using a Mixer Ball Mill MM400 (Retsch). Approximately 5 mg of dried and homogenized algal material was extracted with 500 μL of 70% (v/v) methanol, following the protocol previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. After centrifugation, 200 μL of the supernatant was transferred to an LC vial and dried using a SpeedVac. Prior to analysis, samples were reconstituted in 20 μL methanol and subsequently diluted with 60 μL Milli-Q H2O. </p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Kelly, A.A., Fulda, M., Aden, M., Abreu, I.N., Feussner, K. and Feussner, I., 2026. Reducing the Sinapine Levels of Camelina sativa Seeds Through Targeted Genome Editing of REF1.&nbsp;Plant Biotechnology Journal,&nbsp;24(3), pp.1839-1865.</p>	<p>The analysis was carried on using a a quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (<strong>QTOF-MS; 6546 UHD Accurate-Mass QTOF, Agilent Technologies</strong>). High-resolution accurate mass spectra were acquired over a mass range of <span style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54); font-style: normal; font-weight: 700;'>m/z 80-1700 </span>using an electrospray ionization source operated in both positive and negative ionization modes.&nbsp;</p>	<p><strong>MS/MS-based metabolite annotation</strong></p><p>Additional Auto-MS/MS and All-Ions MS analyses were performed using collision energies ranging from 10 to 40 V to support systematic annotation of phenolic metabolites in&nbsp;Mesotaenium&nbsp;extracts. Metabolite annotation was conducted as follows: molecular formulas of precursor and fragment ions were calculated based on monoisotopic mass; diagnostic fragments were identified through searches in METLIN, KEGG, and PubChem databases, as well as available literature; conjugated metabolite forms were manually predicted based on MS/MS spectral interpretation, considering neutral losses, retention times, and ionization modes and All MS/MS spectra were imported into an in-house compound database and library (Agilent PCDL; Meso_database). Putative metabolite annotations were assigned according to the guidelines of the Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI), corresponding primarily to Level 2 identification based on accurate mass and MS/MS fragmentation pattern matching.</p><p><br></p><p>To facilitate structural interpretation, newly detected metabolites were further classified according to diagnostic fragment similarity and inferred core scaffolds. Four structural classes were defined: Class I – coumarins (C8H6O3, chromen-2-one core); Class II – furanocoumarins (C12H8O5), Class III – biscoumarins (C12H6O6), and Class IV – phenolic benzochromenone-type metabolites (C13H8O5, Trihydroxy-benzo[c]chromen-6-one-like). This fragment-based classification was used to group structurally related metabolites and highlight potentially novel phenolic scaffolds detected in&nbsp;Mesotaenium&nbsp;extracts under UV stress. All annotated metabolites and associated parameters are listed in&nbsp;<strong>Table S1 </strong>of the paper associated to the study.</p>	<p>The earliest land plants had to dynamically adjust to rapidly changing environmental conditions<strong>[1]</strong>. We worked with the unicellular, genome-sequenced<strong>[2][3]</strong>&nbsp;zygnematophyte&nbsp;<strong>Mesotaenium endlicherianum&nbsp;SAG 12.97</strong> (Me), one of the closest algal relatives of land plants (<strong>Figure 1A</strong> of the paper associated to this study). The algae were grown at 80-90 μmol photons m^2/s&nbsp;for 7 d. 24 h before experimental start, they were transferred to the experimental setup, where they were subjected to 50±10 μmol photons m^2/s&nbsp;(<strong>Figure 1B</strong> of the paper associated to this study). Upon experimental start, <strong>ultraviolet (UV) specimens </strong>were exposed to <strong>3 h of 1.7-2.9 W m^-2&nbsp;UV radiation</strong> with a peak in the UV-B spectrum (at 311 nm,&nbsp;<strong>Figure S1A</strong> of the paper associated to this study).&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Cultivation of algae on solid medium</strong></p><p>For stock culturing,&nbsp;<em>M. endlicherianum</em>&nbsp;was stored on solid Bold’s Basal Medium (BBM) with vitamins and triple nitrate (3NBBM+ V) according to the recipe described before<strong>[4]</strong>(see also<strong>[5][6]</strong>) at 15 °C under full-spectrum fluorescent lamps (5-10 μmol photons m^2/s; constant light). Stock cultures between 21 and 28 d were used for the inoculation of experimental plates.</p><p><br></p><p>For the UV-B exposure experiments, the plates were grown at 80-90 μmol photons m^2/s&nbsp;(Niello LED 300W, 380-740 nm spectrum;&nbsp;Figure S1A) for 7 d with a 16/8 h light/dark cycle, before being transferred to the UV setup location for 24 h acclimation before the experiments.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Experimental set-up</strong></p><p>The UV setup used the same grow lights as previously described in addition to two UV-B lamps used in conjunction, the UV-B Broadband TL fluorescent tube lamp with a UV-B spectrum of 290 to 315 nm, and the UV-B Narrowband TL fluorescent tube lamp with a UV-B spectrum between 305 and 315 nm, which peaks at 311 nm (lamp wattage: 40 W and 20 W; length: 1213.6 mm and 604 mm respectively, Philips, the Netherlands; spectrum&nbsp;Figure S1A). They produced a UV-B intensity ranging from 1.7-2.9 W m^-2, depending on location of the individual petri dish. The photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) values were 40-50 μmol photons m^2/s and 50-60 μmol photons m^2/s&nbsp;for UV-B and control, respectively. While PAR was applied with the previous 16/8 h light/dark cycle, for UV treated samples UVR was applied for 3 h (Figure 1B) To reduce condensation on the lids of the petri dishes, fans were installed to create air circulation. Temperature and humidity were monitored using a Tempo Disc (BlueMaestro). The temperature and humidity ranged from 17.2 °C to 20.6 °C with an average of 19.2 °C and 29.7 % to 51.3 % with an average of 40.4 %, respectively.</p><p><br></p><p>10 min before the experiment started, the micropore tape around all the plates and the lid were removed, to avoid UV-B irradiation being filtered out by the plastic lid, as contamination within the following max. 6 h can be neglected. Sampling was performed as follows: 2 plates at each time point of the experiment were taken out of both settings,&nbsp;Mesotaenium&nbsp;was removed from the cellophane and 1/4 of each plate pooled (depending on setting) for transcriptomics and 3/4 of each plate pooled for metabolite analysis. Pooling was done to balance the varying irradiation intensity.

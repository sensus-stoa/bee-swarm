Study Title	Advanced LC-IMS-MS Protocol for Holistic Metabolite Analysis in Wine and Grape Samples
Study Description	The final aim of metabolomics is the comprehensive and holistic study of the metabolome in biological samples. Therefore, the use of instruments that enable the analysis of metabolites belonging to various chemical classes in a wide range of concentrations is essential, without compromising on robustness, resolution, sensitivity, specificity, and metabolite annotation. These characteristics are crucial for the analysis of very complex samples, such as wine, whose metabolome is the result of the sum of metabolites derived from grapes, yeast(s), bacteria(s), and chemical or physical modification during winemaking. In recent years, a big advantage, in this direction, was the hardware developments on hyphenated instruments that enable the integration of liquid chromatography (LC), ion mobility spectrometry (IMS), and mass spectrometry (MS). This chapter describes an LC-IMS-MS protocol for the analysis of wine and grape samples as well as the use of IMS data in metabolite annotation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Small collection of wine samples of Cabernet Sauvignon, Timorasso and Barbera from diverse wineries. The samples were keep it at 4 °C in dark glass bottle.</p>	<p>To prevent undesirable oxygen-dependent reactions, the wine bottles were opened under an N2 atmosphere. The samples were prepared following a random sequence. The wine samples were diluted 3x times with degasified MiliQ water, filtered (0.22 µm PDVF) into 2 mL dark vial.&nbsp;</p>	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed in an Acquity I-class UPLC with a binary solvent manager, sample manager FTN-1, column manager, and sample organizer (Waters Corporation). The UPLC Column Acquity TM Premier HSS T3 with VanGuard Fit, 1.8 µm, 2.1 x 100 mm was set at 40 °C, at flow rate 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phase A is water with 0.1% of formic acid, and B is methanol with 0.1% of formic acid. The following multistep gradient was used for 2 µL of injected sample:&nbsp;0.0-1.0 min (0% mobile phase B), 1.0-3.0 min (0-10% mobile phase B), 3.0-12.0 min (10-40% mobile phase B), 12.0-16.0 min (40-100% mobile phase B), 16.0-18.0 min (100% mobile phase B), 18.0-18.10 min (100-0% mobile phase B), 18.10-20.0 min (0% mobile phase B).</p>	<p>A Synapt-XS high resolution mass spectrometer (Waters corporation) with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source was in tandem with the UPLC Acquity I chromatographic system. The source parameters are setting as follows: 3.00 and 2.00 kV for capillary, 40 and 30 for sampling cone, for ESI+ and ESI-, respectively. The source temperature and desolvation temperature was set at 120 and 450 °C. The data was acquired in resolution mode using a normal dynamic range with a scan time 0.2 s in continuum mode. The HDMSE method for ESI + and ESI- for the low energy function were disabled in the transfer cell, whereas for the high energy function a ramp of collision energy were applied in the transfer cell device, from 20 to 40 V in ESI+, and from 20 to 50 V in ESI-. The triwave velocity for the trap was 311 m/s and for the transfer 141 m/s with Nitrogen (90 mL/min) as IMS buffer gas. A lock mass solution (leucine enkephalin 100 pg/µL) was infused during the data acquisition at 10 µL/min. The acquisition of the lock mass solution is on an interval of 15 s with a scan average of 3. The capillary voltage applied in the source for lock mass in ESI- is 2.60 kV and in ESI+ is 3.00 kV, respectively. The calibration was performed using CCS Major MIX IMS/ToF solution (Waters Corporation) and lock mass solution.</p>	<p>The output raw data do not have the lock mass correction (leucine enkephalin), for the data analysis is required to perform a lock mass correction.&nbsp;The ESI+ theoretical values for leucine enkephalin are m/z 556.277 (M+H) and a drift time of 6.185 ms, meanwhile for ESI-, the values are m/z 554.262 (M-H) and a drift time of 6.002 ms.</p>	<p>The strategies for metabolite annotation process of the selected features includes resources such as in-house libraries (RT, MSE spectra and TWCCSN2 value) built with analytical standards and managed by UNIFI software.</p>

Study Identifier	MTBLS8336
Study Title	Chlorophyll biosynthesis under the control of arginine metabolism.
Study Description	<p>In natural environments, photosynthetic organisms adjust their metabolism to cope with the fluctuating availability of combined nitrogen sources, a growth-limiting factor. For acclimation, the dynamic degradation/synthesis&nbsp;of tetrapyrrolic pigments, as well as of the amino acid arginine, is pivotal; however, there has been no evidence that these processes could be functionally coupled. Using co-immunopurification and spectral shift assays, we found that in the cyanobacterium <em>Synechocystis</em> sp. PCC 6803, the arginine metabolism-related ArgD and CphB enzymes form protein complexes with Gun4, an essential protein for chlorophyll biosynthesis. Gun4 binds ArgD with high affinity, and the Gun4-ArgD complex accumulates in cells supplemented&nbsp;with ornithine, a key intermediate of the arginine pathway. Elevated ornithine levels restricted <em>de novo</em> synthesis of tetrapyrroles, which arrested the recovery from nitrogen deficiency. Our data reveal a direct crosstalk between tetrapyrrole biosynthesis and arginine metabolism that highlights the importance of balancing photosynthetic pigment synthesis with nitrogen homeostasis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>Experimental model and subject details</strong></p><p><strong><em>Synechocystis</em> sp. PCC 6803 substrain GT-P</strong><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>[1]</strong> was used as the wild type (<strong>WT</strong>) and as a genetic background for all prepared strains listed in<strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'> Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with this study. Unless stated otherwise the <em>Synechocystis</em> strains were grown photoautotrophically in liquid BG-11 medium on a rotary shaker at 28 °C, under continuous, moderate irradiance of 40 µmol photons/m^2/s given by white fluorescence tubes. The plate-drop experiments were performed by pipetting liquid cultures on a BG-11 agar plate. The drops were photographed after 3 days of photoautotrophic growth at 30 °C, under constant illumination with 30 µmol photons/m^2/s. Alternatively, the plates were exposed to 500 µmol photons/m^2/s (high light); or to repeated periods of 5 min illumination with high light and 5 min dark (fluctuating light). The fluctuating nitrogen (N) stress was mimicked by repeated cycles of washing the cells to nitrate-less BG-11 (N0) for 8 h, followed by the addition of 18 mM NaNO3 supplement to the liquid cultures for another 8 h, while agitating at 240 rpm and 28 °C, illuminated by 200 µmol/m^2/s. When amino acids were included to the media the stable pH was ensured by 10 mM TES. The absorption spectra of cells were measured by a UV-3000 spectrophotometer (Shimadzu). The number and average size of cells were assessed by coulter counter (Multisizer 4, Beckman Coulter).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Construction of the Synechocystis model strains</strong></p><p>The<strong> ΔargD</strong> and <strong>ΔcphB</strong> strains were constructed by replacing the <em>slr1022</em> and <em>slr2001</em> genes by erythromycin or spectinomycin resistance cassettes, respectively. The primers in the megaprimer PCR method used for mutagenesis are listed in <strong>Table S5</strong> of the paper associated with the study. The segregation of <strong>ΔargD</strong> locus was achieved in the presence of 5 mM Orn. For the construction of strains expressing the<strong> N-terminally 3xFLAG-tagged ArgD protein</strong> (<strong>f.ArgD</strong>), we purchased a synthetic <em>Synechocystis</em> <strong><em>argD</em> gene</strong> (GenScript, USA) with an optimized codon usage to remove the common restriction binding sites. To express the <strong>AstC enzyme</strong> from <em>Escherichia coli</em> (<em>E.coli)</em> in <em>Synechocystis</em>, the <strong><em>astC</em> gene </strong>of <em>E.coli</em> was amplified by PCR from the genomic DNA. The <strong><em>argD</em></strong> and <strong><em>astC</em></strong> genes were cloned to the pPD-NFLAG plasmid<strong>[2][3]</strong>. The obtained constructs were transformed into <strong><em>Synechocystis</em> <em>ΔargD</em></strong> and/or <strong>ΔcphB </strong>cells. All transformed <em>Synechocystis</em> cells were fully segregated on BG-11 plates with increasing concentrations of relevant antibodies.</p><p><br></p><p>The phenotype of studied strains after feeding with biosynthetic intermediates of Arg was monitored. For this, using metabolic profiling, we checked the ability of&nbsp;Synechocystis&nbsp;to uptake <strong>N-acetylornithine (AcOrn)</strong>, the enzymatic product of <strong>ArgD</strong>, treating <strong>WT</strong> with <strong>100&nbsp;μM AcOrn</strong> or <strong>100&nbsp;μM Orn</strong>, for 40 min<strong>.</strong></p><p><br></p><p><strong>Quantification of selected metabolites</strong></p><p>For the quantification of selected metabolites by combined <strong>GC-MS</strong> and <strong>LC-MS</strong> analysis<strong>[4],</strong> 3 x 10^9 cells were collected by centrifugation and frozen in liquid N2.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Tichý M, Bečková M, Kopečná J, Noda J, Sobotka R, Komenda J. Strain of Synechocystis PCC 6803 with Aberrant Assembly of Photosystem II Contains Tandem Duplication of a Large Chromosomal Region. Front Plant Sci. 12; 7: 648. doi:10.3389/fpls.2016.00648. PMID:27242849.</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Hollingshead, S., Kopecná, J., Jackson, P. J., Canniffe, D. P., Davison, P. A., Dickman, M. J., Sobotka, R., Hunter, C. N. 2012. Conserved chloroplast open-reading frame ycf54 is required for activity of the magnesium protoporphyrin monomethylester oxidative cyclase in Synechocystis PCC 6803. J Biol Chem. 287(33): 27823-33. doi:10.1074/jbc.M112.352526. PMID: 22711541.</p><p><strong>[3] </strong>Chidgey, J.W., Linhartová, M., Komenda, J., Jackson, P.J., Dickman, M.J., Canniffe, D.P., Koník, P., Pilný, J., Hunter, C.N., Sobotka, R. 2014. A cyanobacterial chlorophyll synthase-HliD complex associates with the Ycf39 protein and the YidC/Alb3 insertase. Plant Cell. 26(3): 1267-79. doi:10.1105/tpc.114.124495. PMID:24681617.</p>	<p>Cell pellets were dried and immediately extracted with <strong>400 µL</strong> of a cold extraction medium<strong> MeOH:ACN:H2O (2:2:1 v/v/v) </strong>containing an internal standard, <strong>4-fluorophenylalanine (80 nmol</strong>). The sample was then homogenized using a Tissue Lyser II (Qiagen) at 50 Hz, 0 °C for 5 min. The mixture was then centrifuged (8000 x g, 10 min, 5 °C). The supernatant was removed, and the extraction step was repeated under the same conditions but without the internal standard. The supernatants were combined and the obtained sample extract stored (-80 °C). A <strong>100 μL aliquot</strong> of each extract was mixed with <strong>13C-labeled internal standards</strong> (13C3-Serine, 13C3-Alanine, 13C6-Tyrosine, 13C2-Glutamic acid, 13C6-Arginine, 13C6-Lysine, 13C6-Phenylalanine, 13C4-Asparagine, 13C5-Methionine, 13C2-Threonine, 13C4-2-Oxoglutarate, 13C5-Glutamine, 13C5-Proline, 13C6-Arginine, in a absolute concentration of 2 nmol each) and concentrated in a vacuum concentrator (RVC 2-25 CD Plus combined with ALPHA 1-2 LD Plus, Thermo-Fischer Scientific). Each dried extract sample was then subjected to <strong>derivatization</strong> with <strong>ethanol (EtOH) - ethyl chloroformate (ECF)</strong> reaction medium, <strong>under pyridine catalysis</strong> and simultaneous liquid-liquid microextraction into a lower chloroform layer as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, the following 5 sequential steps were used for the addition of a corresponding medium to the evaporated sample extract:<strong> 1)</strong> <strong>50 μL </strong>of <strong>EtOH:H2O(2:1, v/v)</strong>, <strong>2)</strong> <strong>50 μL </strong>of<strong> EtOH:pyridine (2:1, v/v)</strong>, <strong>3)</strong> <strong>50 μL </strong>of <strong>ECF:chloroform (1:7, v/v)</strong>, <strong>4)</strong> <strong>50 μL</strong> of <strong>1 M NaOH </strong>and <strong>5)</strong> <strong>50 μL</strong> of <strong>ECF:chloroform (1:7, v/v)</strong>. The reaction mixture was stirred before addition of each solvent mix. Finally, 30 μL of the lower chloroform layer was evaporated by a gentle stream of nitrogen and redissolved in <strong>100 μL MeOH:H2O (3:7, v/v)</strong> for <strong>LC-MS analysis</strong>. After this, <strong>50 μL of 1 M HCl </strong>was added to the remaining reaction mixture and stirred. Then, <strong>50 μL of the lower chloroform phase</strong> was used for <strong>GC-MS analysis.</strong></p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Opekar S, Kvíčala J, Moos M, Pejchal V, Šimek P. 2021. Mechanism of Alkyl Chloroformate-Mediated Esterification of Carboxylic Acids in Aqueous Media. J Org Chem. 86(23): 16293-16299. doi:10.1021/acs.joc.1c01546. PMID:34546734.</p>	<p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>An <strong>Accela 600</strong> liquid chromatograph (LC) and a <strong>Accela</strong> autosampler (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were used for quantitative analysis.&nbsp;<strong>Amino acids</strong> were separated on a<strong> Kinetex C18 (2.6 µm, 3 mm x 150 mm; Phenomenex)</strong>,<strong> </strong>with a mobile phase flow rate of 400 μL/min, an injection volume of 5 μL, and a column temperature of 35 °C. Mobile phase A was: 5 mmol/L ammonium format in methanol and mobile phase B: 5 mmol/L aqueous ammonium format. The gradient used was as follows: 0.0 min, 30% A; 10.0 min, 100% A; 11.0 min, 100% A; 11.1 min, 30% A; 14.5 min, 30% A.</p><p><br></p><p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Analysis of <strong>amino acids</strong> were performed using a gas chromatograph <strong>Agilent 5977B MSD</strong> and a <strong>VF-17ms (0.25 µm, 0.25 mm x 30 m; Varian)</strong> capillary column. The instrument settings were: Helium flow rate, 1.2 mL/min; inlet temperature, 280 °C; injection mode, splitless; split flow, 40 mL/min; splitless time, 1.0 min; septum purge flow 3 mL/min; temperature program, 45 °C, hold for 2 min, 16 °C/min to 320 °C, hold for 2 min; transfer line temperature, 280 °C.</p>	<p><strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>LC-MS</strong></p><p>An <strong>Thermo Scientific LTQ XL linear ion trap </strong>mass spectrometer was used for quantitative analysis. Full scan positive ion mass spectra were acquired in a <strong>mass range of 85-850 Da</strong>. LTQ settings were as follows: ion source parameters, 2.5 kV spray voltage, 300 °C capillary temperature, sheath gas at 40 au, aux gas at 10 au, spare gas at 1 au, source temperature 300 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Analysis were performed using the quadrupole mass spectrometer <strong>Agilent 5977B MSD</strong> with an electron ionization source (EI) operated in full-scan mode (<strong>range 40-500 Da</strong>). The instrument settings were: transfer line temperature, 280 °C; EI source temperature, 230 °C; ionization energy, 70 eV.</p>	<p><strong>LC-MS</strong> data were processed using <strong>Thermo&nbsp;Xcalibur&nbsp;software version 4.0</strong> (Thermo Fisher Scientific, RRID:SCR_014593).</p><p><br></p><p>For <strong>GC-MS</strong>, data were processed using <strong>Agilent MassHunter WorkStation - Qualitative Analysis for GC/MS</strong> (Agilent, RRID:SCR_016657) and <strong>Agilent Masshunter Quantitative Analysis software version 10</strong> (Agilent, RRID:SCR_015040).</p>	<p>All quantified metabolites were identified by mass and retention time steps with the respective metabolite standard. The external calibration curve method was used for quantification with 13C-labeled internal standards (13C3-Serine, 13C3-Alanine, 13C6-Tyrosine, 13C2-Glutamic acid, 13C6-Arginine, 13C6-Lysine, 13C6-Phenylalanine, 13C4-Asparagine, 13C5 -Methionine, 13C2-Threonine, 13C4-2-Oxoglutarate, 13C5-Glutamine, 13C5-Proline, 13C6-Arginine).</p>

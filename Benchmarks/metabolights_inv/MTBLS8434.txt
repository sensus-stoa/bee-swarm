Study Identifier	MTBLS8434
Study Title	Multi-omics analysis reveals the mechanism of Lacticaseibacillus paracasei IMAUJBC1 in alleviating hyperlipidemia
Study Description	<p>This study investigated the alleviating effect of Lacticaseibacillus paracasei IMAUJBC1 on hyperlipidemia and explored the associated mechanism by combining microbiome, transcriptomic and lipomic analyses. The high-fat mouse model was established with a high-fat diet (HFD), and the intervention of IMAUJBC1 was conducted for 8 weeks after modeling. The results showed that IMAUJBC1 inhibited weight gain and liver fat accumulation in HFD mice, reduced total cholesterol (T-CHO) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) serum levels, promoted the conversion of liver cholesterol to total bile acid (TBA), and increased fecal T-CHO excretion. The IMAUJBC1 intervention decreased the ratio of Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes, while the abundance of Alistipes was increased in mice gut microbiome. IMAUJBC1 may alleviate hyperlipidemia through the PPAR signaling pathway, fat digestion and absorption, cholesterol metabolism, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. In summary, IMAUJBC1 can be used as a functional lactobacillus to intervene in hyperlipidemia.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples were derived from 8-week-old male ICR mice. Mice were fasted for 12 h and killed after anesthesia. Tissues were separated and liver samples were washed with phosphoric acid buffer, weighed and stored at -80 °C.</p><p><br></p><p>Briefly, 'Z' represents the control group, 'M' represents the model group and 'R' represents the Lactobacillus paracasei IMAUJBC1 group. The animal test groups were as follows: (1) control group (Z), control diet (CD) + 0.9% saline (0.1 mL/10 g body weight/day); (2) model group (M), high-fat diet (HFD) + 0.9% saline (0.1 mL/10 g body weight/day); and (3) L. paracasei IMAUJBC1 group (R), HFD + 1 x 10^9 cfu/mL L. paracasei IMAUJBC1 (0.1 mL/10 g body weight/day).</p>	<p>For lipid extraction, 50 mg solid sample were mixed with 80 µL methanol and 400 µL MTBE. the mixtures were for vortexed for 30 s followed with an ultrasound frequency for 30 min, then allowed to settle at -20 °C a for 30 min. The organic phase was separated by centrifugation (13,000 x g) at 4 °C for 15 min. A total of 350 µL lipid extracts which contained in the upper phase were transferred to EP vials, then dried in an vacuum concentrator. After concentration, lipid extracts was redissolved with 100 µL isopropanol:acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) solution, followed with vortexing of 2 min and an ultrasonic process of 5 min in ice water bath. Finally, 80 µL supernatant were carefully transferred to sample vials for LC-MS/MS analysis.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation of the lipids was performed on a Thermo UHPLC Vanquish Horizon system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm x 100 mm; Waters) column. The mobile phases consisted of 10 mM ammonium acetate in ACN:H20 (1:1, v/v) (0.1% (v/v) formic acid) (solvent A), and 2mM ammonium acetate in ACN:IPA:H2O (10:88:2, v/v/v) (0.02% (v/v) formic acid). The solvent gradient changed according to the following conditions: from 0 to 4 min, 65% (A): 35% (B) to 40% (A): 60% (B); from 4 to 12 min, 40% (A): 60% (B) to 15% (A): 85% (B); from 12 to 21 min, 15% (A): 85% (B) to 0% (A): 100% (B) ; from 21 to 24 min, 0% (A): 100% (B) to 0% (A): 100% (B); from 24 to 24.1 min, 0% (A): 100% (B) to 65% (A): 35% (B); from 24.1 to 28 min, 65% (A): 35% (B) to 65% (A): 35% (B) for equilibrating the systems. The sample injection volume was 2 µL and the flow rate was set to 0.4 mL/min. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C.</p>	<p>The mass spectrometric data was collected using a Thermo Q-Exactive Mass Spectrometer&nbsp;equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operating in either positive or negative ion mode. The optimal conditions were set as followed: Aus gas heater temperature, 370 °C; Sheath gas flow rate, 60 psi; Aus gas flow rate, 20 psi; ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF), -3000 V in negative mode and +3000 V in positive mode, respectively; Normalized collision energy, 20-40-60 V rolling for MS/MS. Data acquisition was performed with the Data Dependent Acquisition (DDA) mode. The detection was carried out over a mass range of 200-2000 m/z.</p>	<p>After UPLC-MS analyses, the raw data were imported into the LipidSearch (Thermo, CA) for peak detection, alignment and identification. The lipids were identified by MS/MS fragments. Mass tolerance for precursor and fragment was both set to 5 ppm. The displayed m-score threshold was set as 2.0 and grades A, B, C and D were all used for ID quality filter. The preprocessing results generated a data matrix that consisted of the lipid class, retention time (RT), mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) values and peak intensity. Lipidomic features detected at least 80%&nbsp;in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, minimum lipid values were imputed for specific samples in which the lipid levels fell below the lower limit of quantitation and each lipid features were normalized by sum. Features which the relative standard deviation (RSD) of QC &gt; 30% were discarded.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>After UPLC-MS analyses, the raw data were imported into the LipidSearch (Thermo, CA) for peak detection, alignment and identification.

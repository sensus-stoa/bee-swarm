Study Identifier	MTBLS847
Study Title	Evaluation of sample preparation protocols for quantitative NMR-based metabolomics
Study Description	<p><strong>INTRODUCTION:</strong> Quantification of metabolites in biological fluids and tissues by NMR spectroscopy is challenged by the presence of abundant macromolecules and lipoproteins in samples, which give broad signals in the NMR spectra. To improve the quality of NMR spectra the different protocols for protein and lipid removal from the sample are used.</p><p><strong>OBJECTIVES:</strong> This work is aimed at the evaluation of the effectiveness of various methods of purification of blood serum from proteins and lipids for 1H NMR metabolomic profiling.</p><p><strong>METHODS:</strong> The advantages and limitations of different methods of the sample preparation for NMR-based quantitative metabolomics have been compared, including ultrafiltration, methanol and ethanol extractions with and without additional lipid removal, and methanol-chloroform extraction.</p><p><strong>RESULTS:</strong> The concentrations of 30 abundant metabolites extracted from human blood serum have been measured. It is found that ultrafiltration provides the best lipid removal, but causes significant and inhomogeneous metabolite losses. Ethanol and methanol extractions demonstrate similar performance with the minimal metabolite losses, and are ideal for fluids and tissues with low lipid content. The additional purification of alcohol extracts from lipids allows for the significant improving of NMR spectra, but causes additional metabolite losses.</p><p><strong>CONCLUSIONS:</strong> The methanol-chloroform extraction seems to be an optimal method for tissues with the high lipid content, providing a satisfactory lipid removal and low metabolite losses. The ultrafiltration leads to large losses of metabolites (up to 60 %) and for this reason is not suitable for quantitative analysis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Human blood from 3 healthy individuals was obtained at the International Tomography Center SB RAS. Blood samples were immediately centrifuged (3,000 rpm, 10 min, +4 °C) to remove the blood cells. The obtained plasma samples were pooled, immediately frozen and stored at −70 °C until analyzed. After the sample defrosting, the fibrin clots were removed to obtain the serum samples for metabolite extraction.</p>	<p><strong>Ultrafiltration (UF):</strong></p><p>Centrifugal filters (3 kDa cutoff, Amicon Ultra -0.5, Merck Millipore Ltd., Ireland) were washed and centrifuged thrice with 500 µl of water at +4 °C, 12,000 rpm for 30 min. 200 µl of serum was placed in the filter tubes and centrifuged at +4 °C, 12,000 rpm for 30 min. Filtrate was collected and vacuum dried. 4 samples were prepared.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Single extraction with alcohols (M1 – methanol and E1 – ethanol extraction):</strong></p><p>200 µl of serum was mixed with 400 µLlof ice-cold (-20 °C) alcohol, vortexed for 30 s and incubated at -20 °C for 20 min, and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm, 2 °C for 30 min. Supernatant was collected and vacuum dried. 4 samples were prepared for each extractive solvent.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Triple extraction with alcohol (M3 – triple methanol and E3 – triple ethanol extraction):</strong></p><p>200 µl of serum was extracted with alcohol as described above. The pellet remaining after extraction was mixed with 800 µl of ice-cold 70% alcohol. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s and incubated at -20 °C for 20 min, and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm, 2 °C for 30 min. Supernatant was collected, while the pellet was extracted 1 more time in the same way. All 3 supernatants (first, second, and third extractions) were pooled and vacuum dried. 4 samples were prepared for each extractive solvent.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Lipid removal (Md – methanol and Ed ethanol extraction with additional lipid removal):</strong></p><p>200 µl of serum was extracted with alcohol as described above (M1 and E1 extractions). The obtained supernatant was mixed with 300 µl of CHCl3, vortexed, and placed in a shaker (1300 rpm, room temperature, 15 min). Then 300 µl of deionized water was added to the sample, vortexed and shaken for 20 min at 1300 rpm at room temperature. The mixture was kept at -20 °C for 30 min, and then centrifuged at 2 °C, 12,000 rpm for 30 min. The top hydrophilic fraction (alcohol-water) was collected to a fresh vial. The procedure of the lipids removal from the sample by mixing with chloroform was repeated 1 more time: the obtained hydrophilic fraction was mixed with 300 µl CHCl3, vortexed, shaken at 1,300 rpm at room temperature for 15 min, placed at -20 °C for 30 min, and then centrifuged at the same conditions as before. The top fraction was collected and vacuum dried. 3 samples were prepared for each extractive solvent.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Methanol-chloroform extraction (MCl):</strong></p><p>200 µl of ice-cold methanol and 200 µl of ice-cold chloroform were added to 200 µl of serum and vortexed for 30 s, and then kept on ice for 10 min. The mixtures were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm, 2 °C for 30 min to pellet proteins. The top hydrophilic fraction was collected to fresh vials and vacuum dried. 3 samples were prepared.</p><p><br></p>	<p>Dried extracts were re-dissolved in 500 µl of D2O containing 6 x 10^-6 M of sodium 4,4-dimethyl-4-silapentane-1-sulfonate (DSS) as an internal standard and 20 mM deuterated phosphate buffer to maintain pH 7.4 and placed into standard 5 mm glass NMR tube.</p>	<p>The 1H NMR measurements were carried out with the use of an NMR spectrometer AVANCE III HD 700 MHz (Bruker BioSpin, Germany) equipped with a 16.44 T Ascend cryomagnet and 5 mm TXI NMR probe at a temperature of 25 °C.</p>	<p>The proton NMR spectra for each sample were obtained with 200 accumulations.. The repetition time between scans was 25 s to allow for the relaxation of all spins and the detection pulse was 90 degree. Low power radiation at the water resonance frequency was applied prior to acquisition to presaturate the water signal. The pulse sequence <strong>zgpr</strong> was applied.</p>	<p>The baseline processing and integration were done using an <strong>ACD/NMR Processor Academic Edition</strong>.</p>	<p>The signal assignment was confirmed by the addition of authentic compounds into samples and using the human metabolome database (<a href='https://www.hmdb.ca' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'>https://www.hmdb.ca</a>)<strong>[1]</strong> and our own experience in the metabolomic profiling of human tissues and biofluids<strong>[2]-[6]</strong>. The concentrations of metabolites in the samples were determined by the peak area integration respectively to the internal standard DSS.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Wishart DS, Feunang YD, Marcu A, Guo AC, Liang K, Vázquez-Fresno R, Sajed T et al.

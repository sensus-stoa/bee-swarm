Study Identifier	MTBLS867
Study Title	Untargeted metabolomics reveals alterations in metabolites of lipid metabolism and immune pathways in serum of rats after long term oral administration of Amalaki rasayana
Study Description	Amalaki rasayana (AR), a traditional preparation is widely used by Ayurvedic physicians for the treatment of inflammatory conditions, cardiovascular diseases, cancer and for rejuvenating aged people. Metabolic alterations induced by Amalaki rasayana intervention are unknown. We aimed to investigate modulations in serum metabolomic profiles following long term oral Amalaki rasayana administration in Wistar rats. A global metabolic profiling was performed of the serum of rats administered with Amalaki rasayana or ghee+honey (GH) for 18 months and control animals which were left untreated. Amalaki rasayana components were identified from AR extract using HR-LCMS analysis. Significant reductions in prostaglandin J2, 11-dehydrothromboxane B2 and higher levels of reduced glutathione and glycitein metabolites were observed in serum of AR administered rats compared to the control groups. Pathway analysis suggest that altered metabolites in AR administered rats are those associated with different biochemical pathways of arachidonic acid metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, leukotriene metabolism, G-protein mediated events, phospholipid metabolism and the immune system. Our study identifies the AR components that induce alterations in lipid metabolism and immune pathways in animals which consume AR for a long period.

Study Protocol Description	Serum samples of rats were stored at -80 °C until analysis. Frozen cryovials were thawed, vortexed and centrifuged for 10 mins at 2000 rpm, 4 °C. Quality control samples were prepared by pooling equal volume of aliquots from all the samples (10 μl) to a fresh Eppendorf tube and mixing with methanol (4 times the volume of total sample in Eppendorf tube). For sample preparation, 100 μl of sample was added to a fresh Eppendorf tube and mixed with 400 μl methanol. Samples and quality control samples were then incubated for 1 hr at -20 °C, vortexed and then centrifuged for 20 mins at 14000 rpm, 4 °C. Supernatant was collected in fresh Eppendorf tubes and vacuum dried. Dried samples were stored at -80 °C until analysis. Samples were dissolved in 100 µl of solvent containing 50% methanol and 0.1% formic acid.	The Quadrupole - Time of Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (MS/MS, SYNAPT-G2, Waters) was operated in resolution mode (MSE) with electrospray ionization (ESI). The selected mass range was from 50 to 1200 m/z in full scan mode with a scan time of 0.25 secs and an inter-scan delay of 0.024 secs. Ion source and desolvation gas (nitrogen) temperatures were kept at 112 °C and 350 °C respectively. The sampling cone and desolvation gas flow rates were 60 l/h and 875 l/h respectively. Capillary voltage was set at 2.85 kV. Sampling cone voltage was set at 40 V. The lock mass acquisition was made every 10 secs by Leucine enkephalin (MW= 555.62) for accurate on-line mass calibration. Initially 10 QC sample runs were used for the stabilisation of the system. QC samples were then acquired after every 5th sample run.	Animal experiments were conducted after approval from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of Rajiv Gandhi Center for Biotechnology (RGCB). During all the animal experiments, we strictly followed the rules and regulations of the Committee for the purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA), Government of India. Three months old male Wistar rats were procured from the animal house and kept at 12 hrs day/night cycle at controlled humidity and temperature with food and water ad libitum. Animals were then divided into three groups, each group having 8 animals. Amalaki rasayana or GH (500 mg/kg, orally) was administered for 18 months to two groups of rats. One group of rats was left untreated. Body weights were monitored on a weekly basis until the sacrifice of the animals. Body weights were recoded for all the rats on a weekly basis. At 21 months of age (after 18 months of AR administration), all the animals were sacrificed and blood samples were collected.	MassLynx4.1 SCN781 (Waters) was used for data acquisition. Progenesis QI (Non-linear dynamics, Waters) was used for raw data analysis. Progenesis produced a feature matrix that contained accurate mass (m/z), retention time (RT), and chromatographic peak area. The features detected in <70% of the QC samples were removed with a relative standard deviation of < 30% in the QC samples were used for further statistical analysis. The quality of the data was evaluated by principal component analysis (PCA) of QC samples and study samples as described earlier.	An ultra-performance liquid chromatography system (ACQUITY UPLC, Waters) attached to a mass spectrometer was used for the analysis. The Hydrophilic Interaction Chromatography (HILIC) method was performed with a BEH column (ACQUITY BEH HILIC column, 130 A°, 1.7 μm, 2.1mm X 150mm, Waters). For the HILIC mobile phase, A was 95% Acetonitrile in 10mM ammonium acetate and B was 50% Acetonitrile in 10mM ammonium acetate. Both A and B contained 0.1% formic acid and 0.1% acetic acid in positive ion mode and negative ion mode respectively. The HILIC positive ion gradient was 0 min, 1% B; 5 min, 20% B; 10 min, 50% B; 14 min, 95% B; 16 min, 95% B; 17 min, 1% B; and 20 min, 1% B. Reversed-Phase Liquid Chromatography (RPLC) technique was employed with a C18 column (ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column, 100 A°, 1.8 μm, 2.1mm X 100mm, Waters). In the RPLC Method, mobile phase consisted of aqueous (A) and organic (B) solvent components, where A was 0.1% formic acid in water and B was 0.1% formic acid in methanol. The gradient was 0 min, 0% B; 1 min, 0% B; 16 min, 100% B; 20 min, 100% B, and 22 min, 0% B. The injection volume was 5 µl with a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min and the column temperature of 40°C.	Progenesis QI (Waters, Nonlinear Dynamics, and the USA) was used for the identification of metabolite features. Metabolites identification search was performed using the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB2016) within a mass error of 10 ppm. Identifications matched with fragmentation database were labeled as MSI level 2 and remaining annotations were labeled as MSI level 3 as described earlier.

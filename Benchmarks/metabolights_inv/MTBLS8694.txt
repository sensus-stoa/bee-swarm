STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS8694
Study Title	Polyketide synthase-derived sphingolipids determine microbiota-mediated protection against pathogens in C. elegans
Study Description	Protection against pathogens is a major function of the gut microbiota. Although bacterial natural products have emerged as crucial components of host-microbiota interactions, their exact role in microbiota-mediated protection is largely unexplored. We addressed this knowledge gap with the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans and its microbiota isolate Pseudomonas fluorescens MYb115 that is known to protect Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) infection. We find that MYb115-mediated protection depends on sphingolipids that are encoded by the iterative type I polyketide synthase (PKS) biosynthetic gene cluster, thereby describing a noncanonical pathway of bacterial sphingolipid production. We provide evidence that MYb115-derived sphingolipids affect C. elegans tolerance to Bt infection by altering host sphingolipid metabolism. This work establishes sphingolipids as structural outputs of bacterial PKSs and highlights the role of microbiota-derived sphingolipids in host protection against pathogens.

Study Protocol Description	<p>Liquid <em>P. fluorescens cultures of</em> 5 mL were incubated for 24 h at 30 °C in two different grown conditions (with arabinose or with glucose) for two different bacterial strains (Wild type (WT) and mutant (MYb115)).</p>	<p>We adapted the extraction method from Brown et al. (2019) for the bacterial lipidomics experiment. The equivalent of 1 mL OD600 nm of 5 was harvested by centrifugation (1 min, 20 °C, 17,000 x g). The cell pellet was resuspended in 0.4 mL H2O. 1.5 mL CHCL3/MeOH (1:2) were added and the extracts were mixed by vortexing. The cell mixture was incubated at 30 °C with gentle shaking, after 18 h 1 mL CHCl3/H2O (1:1) was added. After phase separation, the organic phase was dried using a nitrogen evaporator and stored at -20 °C. </p><p><br></p><p>An internal standard mixture was added to all of the samples at the beginning of the extraction. The mixture contain the following lipids: LPE 13:0, LPG 13:0, PG 20:4_20:4, PE 20:4_20:4, CL 14:1_14:1_14:1_14:1, SM 18:1;O2/6:0, HexCer 18:1;O2/8:0 and Cer 18:1;O2/4:0.</p>	<p>The chromatographic separation was performed using a Acquity Premier CSH C18 column (2.1 x 100 mm, 1.7 μm particle size, Waters) a constant flow rate of 0.3 mL/min with mobile phase A being 10 mM ammonium formate in 6:4 ACN:water and phase B being 9:1 IPA:ACN (Honeywell, Morristown, New Jersey, USA) at 40 °C. The injection volume was 5 µL. The mobile phase profile consisted of the following steps and linear gradients: 0.0-1.5 min constant at 37% B; 1.5-4 .0 min from 37 to 45% B; 4.0-5.0 min from 45 to 52% B; 5.0-8.0 min from 52 to 58% B; 8.0-11.0 min from 58 to 66% B; 11.0-14.0 min from 66 to 70% B; 11.0-14.0 min from 66 to 70% B; 14.0-18.0 min from 70 to 75% B; 18.0-20.0 min from 75 to 98% B; 20.0-25.0 min constant at 98% B; 25.0-25.1 min from 98 to 37% B; 25.1-30.0 min constant at 37% B.</p>	<p>For the measurement, a Thermo Scientific ID-X Orbitrap mass spectrometer was used. Ionization was performed using a high-temperature electrospray ion source at a static spray voltage of 3500 V (positive) and a static spray voltage of 2800 V (negative), sheath gas at 50 (Arb), auxiliary gas at 10 (Arb), and ion transfer tube and vaporizer at 325 and 300 °C, respectively. Data-dependent MS2 Top 10 measurements were conducted by applying an orbitrap mass resolution of 120 000 using quadrupole isolation in a mass range of 200 – 2000 and combining it with a high energy collision dissociation (HCD) with a relative collision energy of 25%. Fragments were detected using the orbitrap mass analyser at a predefined mass resolution of 15 000. Dynamic exclusion with an exclusion duration of 5 seconds after 1 scan with a mass tolerance of 10 ppm was used to increase coverage</p>	<p>For lipid annotation, a semi-quantitative comparison of lipid abundance and annotated peaks were integrated using Compound Discoverer 3.3 (Thermo Scientific) and FreeStyle 1.7 (Thermo Scientific) for the manual annotation.</p>	<p>The long chain sphinganines (SPB 26:0;O2, SPB 24:0;O2 and SPB 28:0;O2) were annotated based on the information we had the measure individual standards and verify the corresponding retention time (RT) and MS2 fragmentation pattern. Also, we found compounds with masses 154 Da heavier than those of the three sphinganine (m/z 568.43420 [M+H]+, RT 8.45 min; m/z 540.40295 [M+H]+, RT 6.73 min; m/z 596.46564 [M+H]+, RT 10.234 min). The masses did not correspond to any known lipid species in the MS-DIAL LipidBlast (version 68) dataset. Hence, we used the exact mass and tried different lipid headgroups, and we concluded that the compounds are most likely phosphoglycerol sphingolipids (PG-sphingolipids).</p>

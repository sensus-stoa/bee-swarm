Study Identifier	MTBLS874
Study Title	Marine Biodiscovery Ireland 2017 Samples
Study Description	Over the last 60 years, marine natural products have had major economic and commercial importance. The development of a National Marine Biodiscovery Laboratory in Ireland (NMBLI), located at the Marine Institute (Oranmore), aims to strengthen Ireland’s capacity as research leaders in marine biotechnology and in particularly marine natural products chemistry. The NMBLI houses an Irish marine repository derived from marine macro-organisms collected in Irish coastal waters. The aim of this project is to collect organisms for the marine repository and identify samples of interest for chemical investigation. Other than the development of the laboratory at the marine institute (Oranmore) with the appropriate facilities and procedure to undertake biodiscovery research, the outcomes of this project are to identify bioactive marine natural products from Irish marine life.

Study Protocol Description	<div>Marine macro-organisms were collected in the coastal waters of Ireland. Samples were divided into three parts: <br></div><div>1. voucher specimen located at the Marine Institute Oranmore Co. Galway</div><div>2. taxonomy and genetics specimen for identification</div><div>3. biomass freeze dried and stored in -80 degree freezer accessible to partners</div>	<p>Three different analytical LC methods were used, with one for each type of fraction that was developed for each sample.Fwm was seperated on a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH Amide 1.7 µm column. The column was first eluted with 100% MeCN for 2 mins followed by a linear gradient to 20% MeCN/80% H2O over the next 6 mins. </p><p><br></p><p>The column was further eluted with 20% MeCN/80% H2O for 5 mins before a linear gradient back to 100% MeCN over 2 mins.Fm was seperated on a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH C18 1.7 µm column. The column was first eluted with 5% MeCN/95% H2O for 2 mins followed by a linear gradient to 100% MeCN over the next 6 mins. </p><p><br></p><p>The column was further eluted with 100% MeCN for 5 mins before a linear gradient back to 5% MeCN/95% H2O over 2 mins.Fmd was seperated on a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH Phenyl Hexyl 1.7 µm column. The column was eluted with 100% MeCN for 2 mins followed by a linear gradient to 20% MeCN/80% H2O over the next 6 mins. The column was further eluted with 20% MeCN/80% H2O for 5 mins before a linear gradient back to 100% MeCN over 2 mins.</p>	MS/MS spectral data was recorded on an Agilent 6540 q-ToF mass spectrometer. The MS/MS scan method in the mass hunter work package was used with a single collision energy of 35V for three selected ions.<br>	<p>For transparency the data uploaded has not been transformed and is in it crude form.</p>	Samples were extracted three times under ultrasonication with a solvent mixture of 1:1 MeOH:DCM. <br>	Identification of metabolites was through extensive 1D and 2D NMR structure elucidation of purified molecules or through MS-MS dereplication of fractions.<br>	<p>NMR was performed on pure compounds only. The solvent and parameters used were based on the physicochemical properties and concentration of the compounds.</p>	NMR spectroscopy was performed on a Varian Inova 500 MHz spectrometer. <br>	<p>Three different NMR experiments were performed on the pure compounds. These include a proton (1HNMR), carbon (13CNMR) and heteronuclear singular quantum correlation (HSQC).</p>	The combined extract was dried, bound to C18 silica and subject to SPE on a C18 cartridge. The SPE cartridge was eluted with varying solvent mixture to produce four fractions: Fw (H2O), Fwm (1:1 H2O:MeOH), Fm (MeOH) and Fmd (1:1 MeOH:DCM). The Fw fraction was discarded and the Fwm, Fm and Fmd was subject to LC-MS/MS.</span></span></span></span>	Pure compounds were isolated from the fractionated extracts using repetitive reverse and normal phase HPLC. Other methods also included ion exchange chromatography and size exclusion chromatography.<br>

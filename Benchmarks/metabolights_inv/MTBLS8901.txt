Study Identifier	MTBLS8901
Study Title	Hif1α-Dependent Uncoupling Of Glycolysis Suppresses Tumor Cell Proliferation
Study Description	Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF1α) attenuates mitochondrial activity while promoting glycolysis. Paradoxically, it remains unknown why lower glycolysis is compromised in human clear cell renal cell carcinomas (ccRCC), in which HIF1α acts a tumor suppressor by inhibiting cell autonomous proliferation. Here we found that unexpectedly, HIF1α suppresses lower glycolysis after the glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) step, leading to reduced lactate secretion in different tumor cell types when cells encounter a limited pyruvate supply such as that typically found in the tumor microenvironment in vivo. This is because HIF1α-dependent attenuation of mitochondrial oxygen consumption increases the NADH/NAD+ ratio that suppresses the activity of NADH-sensitive GAPDH glycolytic enzyme. This is manifested when pyruvate supply is limited since pyruvate acts as an electron acceptor that prevents the increment of the NADH/NAD+ ratio. Furthermore, this anti-glycolytic function provides a molecular basis to explain how HIF1α can suppress tumor cell proliferation by increasing the NADH/NAD+ ratio.

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Cell models</strong></p><p>The <strong>786-O-LUC/HIF1α/HIF1α-bHLH*[1]</strong>, <strong>G55</strong>, <strong>MDA-MB-231</strong> and <strong>A549</strong> cell lines, and the murine embryonic fibroblasts (<strong>MEFs</strong>) were all maintained in pyruvate free Dulbecco's high glucose modified Eagle´s Medium (DMEM, SH30022.01, Cytiva HyClone) supplemented with 100 units/mL of penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (P/S, DE17-602E, Lonza), 20 mM HEPES buffer (17-737E, Lonza) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, SV30160.03, Cytiva HyClone). In the experiments, <strong>786-O-LUC/HIF1α/HIF1α-bHLH*</strong> cells were treated with <strong>doxycycline</strong> (1 μg/mL, D9891, Sigma-Aldrich) to induce vector expression. Cells were kept at 37 °C in an atmosphere with 5% of CO2 and 95% air for normoxic conditions. To induce hypoxia the cells were maintained at <strong>0.5% O2</strong> in an Invivo2 400 workstation (Ruskinn) or exposed to dimethyloxalylglycine (<strong>DMOG</strong>, <strong>0.1 - 1 mM</strong>, BML-EI347, Enzo Life Sciences, Inc.), a pharmacological inhibitor of prolyl- 4-hydroxylase. All experiments were carried out in 5 mM glucose unless specifically stated.</p><p><br></p><p>To measure metabolites, cells were plated in pyruvate-free high glucose DMEM supplemented with P/S, HEPES and 10% dialyzed FBS (dFBS, 10,000 Mw cutoff, F0392, Sigma-Aldrich). Then cells were subjected to <strong>0.5% hypoxia</strong> or treated with<strong> DMOG (0.1 mM-1 mM)</strong> or <strong>metformin</strong> (<strong>2 mM</strong>, D150959, Sigma-Aldrich), and supplemented as necessary with <strong>sodium pyruvate</strong> (<strong>1 mM</strong>, BE13-115E, Lonza) or <strong>sodium α-ketobutyrate</strong> (<strong>αKB</strong>,<strong> 1 mM</strong>: K0875, Sigma-Aldrich) for <strong>24 h</strong>. Subsequently, the cells were maintained for <strong>2 h</strong> in a <strong>5 mM glucose pyruvate-free DMEM medium</strong> (SH30022.01, Cytiva HyClone and 11966025, Gibco) <strong>with the corresponding treatments</strong>. Finally, this media was changed to the same <strong>5 mM glucose pyruvate-free DMEM media </strong>(<strong>with the corresponding treatments</strong>) and cells and supernatants were collected after <strong>15</strong> or <strong>60 min</strong> to measure intracellular metabolites and extracellular lactate. For <strong>L-Glutamine-5-13C</strong> tracer analysis, the <strong>786-O-LUC/HIF1α</strong> and <strong>G55</strong> cells were incubated as above but with pyruvate- and glutamine-free DMEM (11960044, Gibco) supplemented with<strong> 4 mM L-Glutamine-5-13C </strong>(CLM-1166, Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc.), after which the medium was collected. All the samples collected were quenched in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until they were processed.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Meléndez-Rodríguez F, Urrutia AA, Lorendeau D, Rinaldi G, Roche O, Böğürcü-Seidel N, Ortega Muelas M et al. HIF1α Suppresses Tumor Cell Proliferation through Inhibition of Aspartate Biosynthesis. Cell Rep. 2019 Feb 26;26(9):2257-2265.e4. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2019.01.106. PMID:30811976.</p>	<p>A cold two-phase <strong>methanol:water:chloroform (5:3:5, v/v/v) </strong>solution was used for metabolite extraction. The <strong>polar (methanol-water) phase</strong> and protein interphase was collected after centrifugation at 4 °C and dried using a vacuum concentrator. Dried polar and protein fractions were stored at -80 °C until use. The protein fraction was dissolved in 200 μL sodium hydroxide (0.2 M), and the protein concentration was then quantified using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (23225, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for further normalization.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>Dried polar metabolites were resuspended in 50 μL of 80% methanol and the pentose-phosphate intermediates, fructose bisphosphate (<strong>FBP</strong>) and dihydroxyacetone phosphate(<strong style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>DHAP</strong><span style='color: rgb(54, 54, 54);'>), </span>glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (<strong>GAP</strong>) were analyzed by liquid chromatography mass spectrometry (<strong>LC-MS</strong>).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Polar metabolites were derivatized with <strong>20 μL methoxyamine</strong> (<strong>20 mg/mL in pyridine</strong>, 270970 and 226904, Sigma-Aldrich,) for 90 min at 37 °C. Subsequently, <strong>15 μL </strong>of N-(tert-butyldimethylsilyl)-N-methyltrifluoroacetamide, with 1% tert-butyldimethylchlorosilane (<strong>MTBSTFA + 1% TBDMSCl</strong>: 375934 Sigma-Aldrich) were added to 7.5 μL of each derivative and incubated for 60 min at 60 °C.</p>	<p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>The pentose-phosphate intermediates (<strong>FBP</strong>,<strong> DHAP</strong>,<strong> GAP</strong>) were analyzed using a <strong>1290 Infinity II LC System (Agilent Technologies)</strong>, with a thermal autosampler set at 4 °C. A sample volume of 2 or 10 μL was injected on a <strong>Poroshell HILIC column (Agilent InifinityLab Poroshell 120 HILIC-Z, 2.1 mm x 150 mm, 2.7 μm, PEEK-lined, Agilent Technologies)</strong>. The separation of metabolites was achieved with a column temperature of 50 °C and a flow rate of 0.25 mL/min. Solvent A was 10 mM ammonium acetate in water with 2.5 μM InfinityLab Deactivator Additive, pH = 9 and Solvent B was 10 mM ammonium acetate in water/acetonitrile 15:85 (v:v) with 2.5 μM InfinityLab Deactivator Additive, pH = 9. The following 32 min gradient was applied to separate the metabolites: 0-2 min, 96% B; 5.5 min, 88% B; 5.5-8.5 min, 88% B; 9 min, 86% B; 9-14 min, 86% B; 19 min, 82% B; 25 min, 65% B; 25-27 min, 65% B; 28 min, 96% B; 28-32 min, 96% B.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Intracellular glycolytic intermediates (<strong>GAP</strong>, <strong>DHAP</strong>,<strong> 2/3-PG</strong>, <strong>PEP</strong>), and <strong>lactate</strong> as well as <strong>extracellular lactate</strong> and <strong>glutamine</strong> were detected using a <strong>7890 A GC system (Agilent Technologies)</strong>, as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. A total of 1 μL of each sample was injected into a <strong>DB35MS column</strong> in splitless or in split mode (ratio 1 to 9) using an inlet temperature of 270 °C. The carrier gas was helium with a flow rate of 1 mL/min. Upon injection, the GC oven was kept at 100 °C for 1 min, increased up to 105 °C with a gradient of 2.5 °C/min followed by 2 min at 105 °C, then ramped to 240 °C with a gradient of 3.5 °C/min, and after that ramped up to 320 °C with a gradient of 22 °C/min, which was finally followed by 2 min at 320 °C.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Rossi M, Altea-Manzano P, Demicco M, Doglioni G, Bornes L, Fukano M, Vandekeere A, Cuadros AM et al. PMID:35585241.</p>	<p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>A <strong>6546 Quadrupole-Time of Flight (Q-TOF, Agilent Technologies) </strong>was used for the analysis. The MS operated in negative full scan mode (<strong>50-1100 m/z</strong>) using a gas temperature of 225 °C, sheath gas flow of 12 L/min, sheath gas temperature of 350 °C and capillary voltage of 3.5 kV.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>The measurement of metabolites was performed using a <strong>5975C Inert MS system (Agilent Technologies) </strong>under electron impact ionization at 70 eV using a selected-ion monitoring (<strong>SIM</strong>) mode, as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Rossi M, Altea-Manzano P, Demicco M, Doglioni G, Bornes L, Fukano M, Vandekeere A, Cuadros AM et al. PMID:35585241.</p>	<p>No transformations were applied to the data other than natural abundance-correction of mass distribution vectors in all stable isotope-tracer measurements using <strong>GC-MS</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>LC-MS</strong></p><p>Data was collected using the <strong>MassHunter Workstation LC/MS Data Acquisition Version 10.1 (Agilent Technologies)</strong> and was analyzed using the <strong>Agilent MassHunter Workstation Profinder version 10.0.2</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>GC-MS</strong></p><p>Isotopologue distributions were extracted from the raw ion chromatograms using a custom <strong>Matlab m-file</strong>, which applies consistent integration bounds and baseline correction to each ion.

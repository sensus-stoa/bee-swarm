Study Identifier	MTBLS8958
Study Title	Delineating molecular regulatory network of meat quality of longissimus dorsi indicated by transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomics analysis in rabbit (SPME-GC-MS assay)
Study Description	<p>This study aims to investigate the potential regulatory network responsible for the meat quality using multi-omics to help developing better varieties. Slaughter performance and meat quality of Shuxing No.1 rabbit outperformed Yila rabbit. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and differentially abundance proteins (DAPs) were involved in meat quality-related pathways, such as PI3K-Akt and MAPK signaling pathway. Only SMTNL1 and PM20D2 shared between DEGs and DAPs. Olfactory-sensitive undecanal, a differentially abundant metabolite (DAM) in volatilomics (vDAMs), correlated with all of the remaining 11 vDAMs and most of 12 vDAMs were associated with amino acid metabolism. Integration revealed that 829 DEGs/DAPs were associated with 15 DAMs in four KEGG pathways, such as melatonin (a DAM in widely targeted metabolomics) was significantly positively correlated with ALDH and negatively correlated with RAB3D and CAT in tryptophan metabolism pathway. This study sheds light on the potential mechanisms that contribute to the improved meat quality and flavor.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>SPME-GC-MS assay</strong> is reported in the current study <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS8958' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS8958</strong></a>.</p><p><strong>UPLC-MS/MS assays</strong> are reported in <a href='https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS8957' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>MTBLS8957</strong></a>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Equal numbers of male and female 70-day-old <strong>Shuxing No.1 meat rabbits (n = 8)</strong> and <strong>Yila rabbits (n = 8)</strong> were provided by Sichuan Academy of Animal Science. All rabbits were healthy and had not been treated with any medication. All rabbits were kept in the same breeding environment and had free access to food and drinking water. After euthanasia, the <em>Longissimus dorsi</em> tissues were harvested and frozen in an -80 °C freezer. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sichuan Academy of Animal Science. <em>Longissimus dorsi</em> samples about 20 mg were thawed on ice and homogenized by a grinder (30 Hz) for 20 s at 4 °C.</p>	<p>Appropriately 0.2 g sample was transferred into a 20 ml head-space vial (Agilent, Palo Alto, CA, USA), containing 0.2 g NaCl powder and 10 μl internal standard solution. The vials were sealed using crimp-top caps with TFE-silicone headspace septa (Agilent).</p>	<p>Samples were isolated using fully automated <strong>Headspace Solid-Phase Microextraction (HS-SPME)</strong> for <strong>GC-MS</strong> analysis. During <strong>SPME</strong> analysis, each vial was shook in 60 °C for 5 min, next, a 120 µm DVB/CWR/PDMS fibre (Agilent) was exposed to the headspace of the sample for 15 min at 60 °C. After sampling, at 250 °C for 5 min in the splitless mode, desorption of the VOCs from the fibre coating was performed in the injection port at 250 °C of an Agilent Model 7890B GC coupled to an Agilent 7000D mass spectrometer. VOCs were separated by a DB-5MS (5% phenyl-polymethylsiloxane) 30 m x 0.25 mm x 0.25 μm capillary column with helium as carrier gas at 1.2 ml/min. The oven temperature was designed from 40 °C (hold for 3.5 min), increasing to 100 °C at 10 °C/min, then increasing to 180 °C at 7 °C/min, finally, increasing to 280 °C at 25 °C/min (hold for 5 min).</p>	<p>VOCs were identified and quantified using an Agilent 7000D mass spectrometer. Mass spectra were monitored in electron impact (EI) ionisation mode at 70 eV. The temperatures of the quadrupole mass detector was set at 150 °C, ion source was set at 230 °C, and transfer line was set at 280 °C. The scan mode of MS was selected ion monitoring (SIM) mode, which applied to identify and quantify analyses according to the GB 23200.8-2016. The temperature of the detector was maintained at 280 °C.</p>	<p>No data transformation information was supplied for this study.</p>	<p>Raw data in MS were analyzed using <strong>Agilent MassHunter</strong> software. All ions in each group that need to be detected are detected separately in the order of their peaks and in different time periods. If the retention time detected is consistent with the standard reference and the selected ion appears in the sample MS after deducting the background, then the metabolite is identified. To identify differentially abundant metabolites in <strong>volatilomics (vDAMs)</strong> between <strong>Shuxing No.1 rabbit</strong> and <strong>Yila rabbit</strong>, the screening criteria was determined as follows: <strong>VIP ≥ 1</strong>, and <strong>FC ≥ 2</strong> or <strong>FC ≤ 0.5</strong>. <strong>VIP</strong> values were produced by <strong>OPLS-DA</strong> result using <strong>OPLSR.Anal</strong> function in <strong>R</strong> package <strong>MetaboAnalystR</strong> with a permutation test (200 permutations) to avoid over-fitting, which contain score plots and permutation plots.</p>

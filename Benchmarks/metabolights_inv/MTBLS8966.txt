Study Identifier	MTBLS8966
Study Title	L-serine enables reducing the virulence of Acinetobacter baumannii and modulating the SIRT1 pathway to eliminate the pathogen
Study Description	<p>The emergence of high-virulent <em>Acinetobacter baumannii</em> strains increases the mortality of patients and seriously affects their prognosis, which motivates us to explore novel ways to control such infections. In this study, gas chromatography-mass spectrometry was adopted to explore the metabolic difference between high- and low-virulent <em>A. baumannii</em> strains, and the decreased L-serine levels were identified as the most crucial biomarker in low-virulent <em>A. baumannii</em> strains. <em>In vitro,</em> L-serine reduced the virulence of <em>A. baumannii</em> to Beas 2B cells and inhibited the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome via decreasing the generation of ROS and mtROS and the release of inflammatory cytokines (IL-18 and IL-1β) through upregulating SIRT1. <em>In vivo</em>, the <em>Galleria mellonella</em> model was adopted. L-serine downregulated the levels of virulence genes (ompA, carO and omp33-36), reduced the mortality of <em>A. baumannii</em> to <em>G. mellonella</em>, and decreased the blacking speed as well as the degree of <em>G. mellonella</em> after infection. Taken together, we found that L-serine can reduce the virulence of <em>A. baumannii</em> and enhance the host’s defense against the pathogen, providing a novel strategy for the treatment of infections caused by <em>A. baumannii</em>.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p><strong>Bacterial strains, cell lines and culture conditions</strong></p><p><em>A. baumannii</em> strains were collected from patients at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, China, from May 2021 to August 2022. The ATCC 19606 strain was obtained from laboratory storage, and they were all stored at -80 °C. The strains were cultured in fresh LB media at 37 °C. The overnight bacterial culture was collected, washed with saline and suspended in M9 medium for subsequent experiments. The high-virulent A. baumannii strain AB9 was identified by cell infection in our study.</p><p><br></p><p>Human alveolar epithelial cells (Beas 2B) were obtained from laboratory storage. They were cultured in RPMI (GIBCO) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin and streptomycin. The cells were cultured in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 °C and passaged 3x times a week to maintain optimal growth. To observe the infection process, the 6-well plates were planted with Beas 2B cells (about 1 x 10^6 each well); 10 µL of <em>A. baumannii</em> solution (1 x 10^7) was added 24 h later. To evaluate the effect of L-serine (Sigma Aldrich), the cells were treated with L-serine (4 mg/mL) for 24 h. EX527 (MCE), a specific inhibitor of SIRT1, was dissolved in 1% DMSO (Beyotime) and added to the culture medium to reach a concentration of 10 µM. The blank control group, <em>A. baumannii</em> infection group, L-serine+<em>A. baumannii</em> group and L-serine+EX527+<em>A. baumannii</em> group were set separately for the subsequent experiments.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Bacterial sample preparation and GC-MS analysis</strong></p><p>Both the sample bacterial preparation and GC-MS analysis were performed as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>. Briefly, 10 high- and 10 low-virulent strains were cultured in the LB medium with 200 rpm at 37 37 °C until reaching an optical density at 600 nm (OD600) of 1.0. Every sample was analyzed in duplicate.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Kuang SF, Feng DY, Chen ZG, Liang ZZ, Xiang JJ, Li H, Peng XX, Zhang T. Inactivation of Nitrite-Dependent Nitric Oxide Biosynthesis Is Responsible for Overlapped Antibiotic Resistance between Naturally and Artificially Evolved Pseudomonas aeruginosa. mSystems. 2021 Oct 26;6(5):e0073221. doi:10.1128/mSystems.00732-21. Epub 2021 Sep 21. PMID:34546070.</p>	<p>An aliquot of 10 mL cells was quenched with precooled methanol (Sigma Aldrich) and ultrasonication. Then, the cells was quenched by ultrasonication. Ribitol (0.1 mg/mL, Sigma Aldrich) was added as an internal standard. The aliquot of the 500 μL supernatant was separated with 12,000 x g at 4 °C for 10 min and dried by a vacuum centrifugation dryer (Labconco). Methoximation-pyridine hydrochloride (Sigma Aldrich) was added to the dried fraction above and continuously shaken with 200 rpm at 30 °C for 90 min. A total of 80 μL of N-methyl-N-trimethylsilyltrifluoroacetamide (Sigma Aldrich) was added and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min.</p>	<p>The data were analyzed using an Agilent 7890A GC and an Agilent 5975C VL MSD detector (Agilent Technologies). Column manufacturer, model number and dimensions were Agilent 30 m in length and 250 mm in width (inner diameter) with a 0.25 mm DBS-MS column.</p><p><br></p>	<p>The test data need quality control.

Study Identifier	MTBLS9118
Study Title	Excoriation Disorder Is Characterized by Systemic Glutamatergic Dysfunction
Study Description	<p>Excoriation disorder (ED), also known as neurotic excoriations, is a psychocutaneous disorder characterized by repetitive, compulsive picking of the skin leading to secondary tissue damage. ED carries an estimated prevalence of 1.4%, and is associated with significant social dysfunction, as patients often spend several hours each day performing compulsive behaviors leading to absence from work, school or other social events. Despite the significant impact on quality of life, there is still a lack of understanding of the factors involved in disease precipitation or progression, highlighting a need for identification of novel biomarkers of disease. The study of metabolic alterations, through plasma metabolomics, may lead to a greater understanding of disease pathogenesis, as metabolic alterations have been identified in related conditions such as obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and in animal models of compulsive behavior. However, the metabolomic abnormalities present in ED have yet to be described. The purpose of this study is to perform an untargeted comparative plasma metabolomics analysis on ED patients and healthy controls to characterize the metabolic alterations in ED. Mass spectrometry quantified 76 total metabolites, 20 of which were identified as significantly different between ED and healthy controls, with four being increased and 16 being decreased in ED.</p><p><br></p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>This study was approved by the Johns Hopkins Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from participants. Metabolite measurements were performed on plasma from <strong>4 excoriation disorder </strong>(<strong>ED) patients</strong> and <strong>4 age-, race- and sex-matched controls</strong> recruited from the outpatient dermatology clinics. Plasma was isolated from whole blood by centrifugation and frozen at -80 °C. All <strong>ED</strong> patients were diagnosed by a board-certified dermatologist and met the diagnostic and statistical manual of mental health disorders, fifth edition, criteria for the disorder. Controls lacked a diagnosis of<strong> ED</strong> or other chronic skin diseases. The mean age of <strong>ED</strong> patients was 59.3 ± 7.3 yr, while the mean age for controls was 57.5 ± 14.9 yr (<strong>Figure 1A</strong> and <strong>Table S1</strong> in the paper associated with the study). <strong>ED</strong> patients and controls were 75% non-Hispanic White and 25% African American.</p><p><br></p><p><br></p>	<p>Metabolomics was performed at the <strong>Johns Hopkins Institute for Clinical &amp; Translational Research Biomarker Development Metabolomics Facility</strong>. Metabolites were extracted by adding <strong>100%</strong> high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)-grade <strong>methanol</strong> (Fisher Scientific) to plasma to achieve a final concentration of 80% (v/v) methanol. Speed vacuum was used to evaporate methanol, and samples were lyophilized and re-suspended in 50% (v/v) acetonitrile.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was performed using a <strong>Thermo Scientific Vanquish UHPLC System</strong>, with a<strong> Thermo Scientific Vanquish Split Sampler HT</strong> auto-sampler, kept at 4 °C. A <strong>Discovery HS F5 (3 µm, 2.1 mm x 150 mm; Supelco)</strong> column and a <strong>Discovery HS F5 Supelguard (3 µm, 2.1 mm x 20 mm; Supelco)</strong> guard column were used and maintained at 35 °C. Reverse-phase chromatography separations were achieved using 0.1% formic acid in mass spec-grade water as mobile aqueous phase and 98% acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid as the mobile organic phase. The total runtime was 13 min and the injection volume was 2 µL.</p>	<p>A <strong>Thermo Scientific Q Exactive Plus</strong> mass spectrometer interfaced with heated electrospray ionization (<strong>HESI-II</strong>) source and Orbitrap mass analyzer operated at 35,000 mass resolution was used. The mass range was <strong>50-6000 m/z</strong> and the MS analysis alternated between <strong>MS</strong> and <strong>data-dependent MSn scans</strong> using dynamic exclusion.</p>	<p><strong>Compound Discoverer</strong> and<strong> TraceFinder (Thermo Scientific) </strong>software were used for data analysis. Intensities were normalized based on protein concentration. The max protein concertation of all samples was divided by the protein concentration of each sample to calculate a scaling factor. Raw intensities were multiplied by the scaling value.</p>	<p>Metabolites were only selected for analysis if they had a compound name annotation, a mass accuracy within 0.001 of the theoretical value, and an MS2 fragmentation pattern matching to the<strong> </strong><a href='https://www.mzcloud.org/' rel='noopener noreferrer' target='_blank'><strong>mzCloud</strong> </a>database with a score above 60<strong>[1][2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Zhang, C., Hoang, G., Attarwala, N., Cooper, A.J.L., Asaka, R., Le, A.

Study Identifier	MTBLS9432
Study Title	Chronic exposure to tire rubber-derived contaminant 6PPD-quinone impairs sperm quality and induces the damage of reproductive capacity in male mice
Study Description	<p>The study investigates the effects of 6PPDQ on male fertility in adult mice. The findings of this study are essential as the effects of low-dose environmental factors of 6PPDQ on adult male infertility are unclear, with the increasing in male infertility. The results suggest that 6PPDQ adversely affects sperm quality and male fertility possibly through affecting the proliferation of germ cells and sperm function. Future study is warranted to investigate the molecular mechanisms by which 6PPDQ affects germ cell and mature sperm functions. This study provides essential information for further study on the influence of 6PPDQ on sperm function and protective measures</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>mice were anesthetized before the sacrifice, and the anesthesia was performed by 100 % carbon dioxide inhalation for euthanasia. Tissues and serum were collected and stored at 80 °C after liquid nitrogen quick freezing</p>	<p>Each mice testicular samples (6PPDQ vs. control) were weighed an appropriate amount and put into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, add 1000 µL tissue extract, add steel balls; Then, put it into the tissue grinder, grind it at 50 Hz for 60 s, and repeat the above operation twice; The mixture was ultrasound for 30 min at room temperature and ice bath for 30 minutes; and centrifuged for 10 min at 12,000 rpm and 4, take all the supernatant, transfer it to a new 2 mL centrifuge tube, concentrate and dry it; Then, add 200 µL 50% acetonitrile solution with 2-chloro-l-phenylalanine (4 ppm) to redissolve the sample, and filter the supernatant by 0.22 μm membrane and transfer into the detection bottle for LC-MS detection</p>	<p>The LC analysis was performed on a Vanquish UHPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Chromatography was carried out with an ACQUITY UPLC&nbsp;HSS T3 (2.1× 100 mm, 1.8 µm) (Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The column maintained at 40 . The flow rate and injection volume were set at 0.3 mL/min and 2 μL, respectively. </p>	<p>Mass spectrometric detection of metabolites was performed on Orbitrap Exploris 120 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with ESI ion source. Simultaneous MS1 and MS/MS (Full MS-ddMS2 mode, data-dependent MS/MS) acquisition was used. The parameters were as follows: sheath gas</p><p>pressure, 40 arb; aux gas flow, 10 arb; spray voltage, 3.50 kV and -2.50 kV for ESI(+) and ESI(-), respectively; capillary temperature, 325 ; MS1 range, m/z 100-1000; MS1 resolving power, 60000 FWHM; number of data dependant scans per cycle, 4; MS/MS resolving power, 15000 FWHM; normalized collision energy, 30%; dynamic exclusion time, automatic</p>	<p>The untargeted metabolic profiling of testicular samples was performed and analyzed by Bio-Deep (Suzhou, China). Metabolites with variable importance in projection (VIP) of 1 and p &lt; 0.05 were regarded as statistically significant (differentially expressed metabolites). Pathway analysis was performed by integrating hypergeometric test and topology analysis. The pathway impact value was calculated by an MetaboAnalyst tool, to measure the degree of involvement of various metabolic pathways</p>	<p>After the raw data is converted into other format using software, a self-developed R package is used for peak recognition, peak extraction, peak alignment, and integration processing. Then, it is matched with the secondary mass spectrometry database for substance annotation.</p>

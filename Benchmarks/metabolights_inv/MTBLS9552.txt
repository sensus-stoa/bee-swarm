Study Title	The circadian clock of Populus affects physiological, transcriptional and metabolomic responses to osmotic and ionic components of salt stress
Study Description	<p>The circadian oscillator is an innate timing mechanism present in most organisms, including plants. In this study,&nbsp;<em>Populus tremula</em>&nbsp;×&nbsp;<em>P. tremuloides</em>&nbsp;(<em>Populus</em>) trees with reduced expression of circadian clock components were exposed to gradually increases in the osmotic and ionic components of salt stress. Reduced levels of the morning components&nbsp;<em>PttLATE ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 1</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>2 (PttLHY1,2)</em>&nbsp;or of the evening components&nbsp;<em>PttPSEUDO-RESPONSE REGULATOR 7a</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>b</em>&nbsp;(<em>PttPRR7a,b</em>) and&nbsp;<em>PttGIGANTEA1,2</em>&nbsp;(<em>PttGI1,2</em>) affected growth adaptation under stress conditions.&nbsp;<em>PttLHY1,2</em>&nbsp;regulated growth under NaCl treatment&nbsp;<em>via</em>&nbsp;the control of&nbsp;<em>PttCyclin D3</em>&nbsp;expression.&nbsp;<em>PttPRR7</em>a,b and&nbsp;<em>PttGI</em>1,2 were instrumental in maintaining growth in roots by enabling effective adaptation of the metabolome. Major changes in the root metabolome under prolonged stress included alterations in carbohydrate, amino acids, and fatty acids. This study places the circadian clock at the centre of adaptation to adverse conditions in trees and will help the development of stress-resistant trees.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>In vitro cultivated cuttings from four genotypes; wild-type&nbsp;Populus&nbsp;tremula x P.&nbsp;tremuloides (Ptt)&nbsp;trees cv. T89 (Nilsson et al., 1997), and lines of RNA interference (RNAi) in this background with reduced expression of&nbsp;PttLATE ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 1&nbsp;(PttLHY1), and&nbsp;PttLHY2&nbsp;combined (line&nbsp;lhy-10; Ibanez et al., 2010),&nbsp;PttGIGANTEA 1&nbsp;(PttGI1) and&nbsp;PttGI2&nbsp;combined (line&nbsp;gi-13; described in this study), and&nbsp;PttPSEUDO-RESPONSEREGULATOR7a (PttPRR7a) and&nbsp;PttPRR7b&nbsp;combined (line&nbsp;prr7-5; described in this study) were potted and grown in a fertilized peat: perlite (3:1) mix in 3L pots, under controlled long day (18 h light/6 h dark) conditions at 22°C day/18°C night, 70 % humidity, and watered daily. After four weeks, roots from three plants of each of the four genotypes were harvested at treatment point 0 (T0). Plants were divided up in three treatment groups containing each of the four genotypes. The treatments were group I, NaCl (ionic stressor), group II with a comparable osmotic concentration of mannitol (osmotic stressor), and a control group III receiving no stressor (water). The treatment strategy was to have a gradually increasing stress, until a target concentration was reached in group I of 150 mM NaCl, or group II 300 mM mannitol, and group III with water. Thus, group I received a solution of 25 mM (25 mL) of NaCl every 12 hr until reaching 150 mM and group II received a solution of 50 mM (25 mL) of mannitol every 12 h until reaching 300 mM. In both cases, the maximum concentrations were reached after 72 h. Following this point, 80 h after treatment started at Zeitgeber time 8 (8 h after dawn) the first treatment (T1) sampling of roots took place. Specifically, four plants, randomly selected from each genotype and treatment was sampled. The treatment continued with remaining plants and plants from all four genotypes receiving in Group I; 150 mM of NaCl (25 ml), and group II; 300 mM (25 ml) of mannitol, and group III of water (25 ml) every 12 h until 184 hours after the treatment commenced. At the end of this time, again at ZT 8, roots from four plants from each treatment and four genotypes were harvested, considered as Treatment point 2 (T2). In both, T1 and T2, plants watered with water were considered as control treatment.</p><p><br></p><p>In summary, for metabolomics we analysed three plants per genotype only receiving water at T0. At T1 after 80 h, four plants per genotype and treatment were harvested at the point when target concentrations of stressor had been reached. Similarly, at the end of the experiment (at T2) four plants per genotype and treatment were harvested. All 109 samples except one wild type treated with NaCl (number 78), were successfully analysed by GC-TOF-MS, following Papazian et al. (2016) and Papazian et al. (2019) procedures.</p><p><strong>&nbsp;</strong></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Ibáñez, C., et al. (2010). Circadian clock components regulate entry and affect exit of seasonal dormancy as well as winter hardiness in Populus trees. Plant Physiol. 153, 1823–1833. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2923903/</p><p><strong>[2]</strong> Nilsson, O. et al. Spatial pattern of cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter luciferase expression in transgenic hybrid aspen trees monitored by enzymatic assay and non-destructive imaging. Transgen. Res. 1, 209–220 (1992). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02524751.</p><p><strong>[3]</strong> Papazian, S et al. 2016. Central metabolic responses to ozone and herbivory affect photosynthesis and stomatal closure, Plant Physiol. 172, 2057–2078, https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.16.01318</p><p><strong>[4] </strong>Papazian, S.. et al. Leaf metabolic signatures induced by real and simulated herbivory in black mustard (Brassica nigra). Metabolomics 15, 130 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11306-019-1592-4.</p><p>&nbsp;</p>	<p>Sample preparation was performed according to Gullberg et al [1].&nbsp;In detail, 1000 µL of extraction buffer (20/20/60 v/v chloroform:water:methanol) including internal standards were added to 9 - 12 mg of plant material. The sample was shaken with a tungsten bead in a mixer mill at 30 Hz for 3 minutes, the bead was removed and the sample was centrifuged at +4 °C, 14 000 rpm, for 10 minutes. 200 µL of supernatant were transferred to a micro vial and solvents were evaporated.&nbsp;Derivatization was performed according to Gullberg et al [1]. In detail 30 µL of methoxyamine (15 µg/µL in pyridine) were added to the dry sample and the sample was shaken vigorously for 10 minutes before left to react in room temperature for 16 hours. 30 µL of MSTFA were additionally added, the sample was shaken and left to react for 1 hour in room temperature. 30 µL of methyl stearate (15 ng/µL in heptane) were added before analysis. The GC-MS internal standards were: L-proline-13C5, alpha-ketoglutarate-13C4, myristic acid-13C3, cholesterol-D7 from Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc. (CIL; Andover, MA, USA) and succinic acid-D4, salicylic acid-D6, L-glutamic acid-13C5,15N, putrescine-D4, hexadecenoic acid-13C4, D-glucose-13C6, D-sucrose-13C12 from Sigma (St. Louis, MO, USA).</p><p><br></p><p>Refs:</p><p>[1]&nbsp;Gullberg J, Jonsson P, Nordström A, Sjöström M, Moritz T. Design of experiments: an efficient strategy to identify factors influencing extraction and derivatization of Arabidopsis thaliana samples in metabolomic studies with gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Anal Biochem. 2004 Aug 15;331(2):283-95. doi:10.1016/j.ab.2004.04.037. PMID:15265734.</p>	<p>GCMS analysis was performed as described previously<strong>[1]</strong>. In detail, 1 µL of the derivatized sample was injected in splitless mode by a CTC Combi Pal autosampler (CTC Analytics AG, Switzerland) into an Agilent 6890 gas chromatograph equipped with a 10 m x 0.18 mm fused silica capillary column with a chemically bonded 0.18 µm DB 5-MS UI stationary phase (J&amp;W Scientific).&nbsp;The injector temperature was 270 °C, the purge flow rate was 20 mL min-1 and the purge was turned on after 60 seconds. The gas flow rate through the column was 1 mL min-1, the column temperature was held at 70 °C for 2 minutes, then increased by 40 °C min-1 to 320 °C, and held there for 2 minutes.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong>&nbsp;Gullberg J, Jonsson P, Nordström A, Sjöström M, Moritz T. Design of experiments: an efficient strategy to identify factors influencing extraction and derivatization of Arabidopsis thaliana samples in metabolomic studies with gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Anal Biochem. 2004 Aug 15;331(2):283-95. doi:10.1016/j.ab.2004.04.037. PMID:15265734.</p>	<p>GCMS analysis was performed as described previously [1]. The column effluent was introduced into the ion source of a Pegasus III time-of-flight mass spectrometer, GC/TOFMS (Leco Corp., St Joseph, MI, USA). The transfer line and the ion source temperatures were 250 °C and 200 °C, respectively. Ions were generated by a 70 eV electron beam at an ionization current of 2.0 mA, and 30 spectra s-1 were recorded in the mass range m/z 50 - 800. The acceleration voltage was turned on after a solvent delay of 150 seconds. The detector voltage was 1500-2000 V.</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[1]&nbsp;Gullberg J, Jonsson P, Nordström A, Sjöström M, Moritz T. Design of experiments: an efficient strategy to identify factors influencing extraction and derivatization of Arabidopsis thaliana samples in metabolomic studies with gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Anal Biochem. 2004 Aug 15;331(2):283-95. doi:10.1016/j.ab.2004.04.037. PMID:15265734.</p>	<p>For the GC-MS data, all non-processed MS-files from the metabolic analysis were exported from the ChromaTOF software in NetCDF format to MATLAB R2016a (Mathworks, Natick, MA, USA), where all data pre-treatment procedures, such as base-line correction, chromatogram alignment, data compression and Multivariate Curve Resolution were performed using custom scripts. The extracted mass spectra were identified by comparisons of their retention index and mass spectra with libraries of retention time indices and mass spectra [2].</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[2]&nbsp;Schauer, N., et al. (2005), 'GC-MS libraries for the rapid identification of metabolites in complex biological samples', Febs Letters, 579 (6), 1332-37.</p>	<p>Mass spectra and retention index comparison was performed using NIST MS 2.0 software. Annotation of mass spectra was based on reverse and forward searches in the library. Masses and ratio between masses indicative for a derivatised metabolite were especially notified. If the mass spectrum according to SMC’s&nbsp;experience was with highest probability&nbsp;indicative of a metabolite and the retention index between the sample and library for the suggested metabolite was ± 5 (usually less than 3) the deconvoluted “peak” was annotated as an identification of a metabolite.&nbsp;</p>

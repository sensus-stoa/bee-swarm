STUDY
Study Identifier	MTBLS9657
Study Title	DNA nanomachines reveal an adaptive energy mode in confinement-induced amoeboid migration powered by polarized mitochondrial distribution
Study Description	<p>Energy metabolism is highly interdependent with adaptive cell migration <em>in vivo</em>. Mechanical confinement is a critical physical cue that induces switchable migration modes of the mesenchymal-to-amoeboid transition (MAT). However, the energy states in distinct migration modes, especially amoeboid-like stable bleb (A2) movement, remain unclear. In this report, we developed multivalent DNA framework-based nanomachines to explore strategical mitochondrial trafficking and differential ATP levels during cell migration in mechanically heterogeneous microenvironments. Through single-particle tracking and metabolomic analysis, we revealed that fast A2-moving cells driven by biomimetic confinement recruited back-end positioning of mitochondria for powering highly polarized cytoskeletal networks, preferentially adopting an energy-saving mode compared with a mesenchymal mode of cell migration. We present a versatile DNA nanotool for cellular energy exploration and highlight that adaptive energy strategies coordinately support switchable migration modes for facilitating efficient metastatic escape, offering a new perspective for therapeutic interventions in cancer metastasis.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>The samples were collected from HeLa cells (HeLa Kyoto with LifeAct-mCherry, a kind gift of Matthieu Piel, Institut Curie, France). For acquiring metabolomic samples, HeLa cells pretreated with calyculin A (10 nM) were cultured in 3-um confined microenvironments (Experimental groups) and FN-modified glass substrates (Control groups) at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator for 7 h, respectively. The counted proportion of HeLa cells in A2 shape was about 65% from dynamic cell tracking. The samples were then washed with 1 x PBS (pH 7.4) for next extraction.</p>	<p>After washing with 1x PBS (pH 7.4), the cellular metabolism was immediately quenched with 80% methanol (v/v, pre-cooled at -20 °C). The collected cell suspension was then centrifugated at 800 x g for 5 min followed by processing with pre-cooled 80% methanol (v/v). Subsequently, the obtained samples were kept in liquid N2 for 30 s and thawed on dry ice followed by 10 s of vortex. After 3 replications, the samples were centrifugated at -9 °C, 11,500 x g for 10 min and finally freeze-dried overnight. For analysis, samples were redissolved in 100 uL acetonitrile/methanol/water(1:1:2, v/v) solvent.</p>	<p>The extracted metabolites were analyzed with a TripleTOF 4600 mass spectrometer (SCIEX, Boston, MA, USA) coupled with a LC-20ADXR high performance liquid chromatography system (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan). The sample separation was achieved through an Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (100 x 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm, Waters, Elstree, UK) in binary gradient mode at 25 °C.</p>	<p>The extracted metabolites were analyzed with a TripleTOF 4600 mass spectrometer (SCIEX, Boston, MA, USA) coupled with a LC-20ADXR high performance liquid chromatography system (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan). Scan m/z range of 50-1000 Da.</p>	<p>For untargeted analysis, the XCMS R package (R version 2.13.2 and package ‘xcms’ version 1.26.1) was employed to analyze raw datasets. Statistical analysis for metabolomics and integrative networks were performed using MetaboAnalyst 5.0 with FC &gt; 2.0 and p value &lt; 0.05 as thresholds. Logarithm transform and data normalization were performed.</p>	<p>The identification of metabolites was processed using Metabolite identification and Dysregulated Network Analysis (MetDNA) version 1.</p>

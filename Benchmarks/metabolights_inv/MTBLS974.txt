Study Protocol Description	For the Leiden Longevity Study, long-lived siblings of European descent were recruited together with their offspring and the partners of the offspring. Families were recruited if at least 2 long-lived siblings were alive and fulfilled the age criterion of 89 yr or older for males and 91 yr or older for females, representing less than 0.5% of the Dutch population in 2001[1][2]. In total 421 families were included consisting of long-lived siblings with a mean age of 94 yr (range 89-104), their offspring (61 yr, 34-81) and partners there of (60 yr, 30-79). </br></br> The current dataset Leiden Longevity Study Sibs consists of 965 nonagenarian siblings(aged between 89 and 107 yr; mean age 92.7 yr ± 3.6; Nfemale=594 (61.6%)) with available Leiden NMR spectra available. </br></br> The current dataset Leiden Longevity Study Partner-Offspring consists of 2,405 descendants (offspring) of nonagenarian siblings and their partners (aged between 30 and 80 yr; mean age 59.2 yr ± 6.8; Nfemale=1,312 (54.6%)) with available Leiden NMR spectra available.</div> </br></br> Ref:</br> [1] Schoenmaker M, de Craen AJ, de Meijer PH, Beekman M, Blauw GJ, Slagboom PE, Westendorp RG. Evidence of genetic enrichment for exceptional survival using a family approach: the Leiden Longevity Study. Eur J Hum Genet. 2006 Jan;14(1):79-84. doi:10.1038/sj.ejhg.5201508. EDTA plasma samples were thawed at 4 °C and were mixed by inverting the tubes 10 times. Next, samples (300 μl) were mixed with 300 μl 75 mM disodium phosphate buffer in H2O/D2O (80/20) with a pH of 7.4 containing 6.15 mM NaN3 and 4.64 mM sodium 3-[trimethylsilyl] d4-propionate using a Gilson 215 liquid handler in combination with a Bruker SampleTrack system. Samples were transferred into 5-mm SampleJet NMR tubes in 96 tube racks using a modified Gilson 215 tube filling station and kept at 6 °C on a SampleJet sample changer while queued for acquisition.	All proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H-NMR) experiments were acquired on a 600 MHz Bruker Avance II spectrometer (Bruker BioSpin, Karlsruhe, Germany) equipped with a 5-mm triple resonance inverse (TCI) cryogenic probe head with Z-gradient system and automatic tuning and matching. The spectrometer is part of a Bruker metabolic profiler platform, which also includes 2 Gilson liquid handler robots. All experiments were recorded at 310 K. Temperature calibration was done before each batch of measurements using a previously published method[1]. The duration of the π/2 pulses were automatically calibrated for each individual sample using a homonuclear-gated nutation experiment on the locked and shimmed samples after automatic tuning and matching of the probe head. </br></br> Ref:</br> [1] Findeisen M, Brand T, Berger S. A 1H-NMR thermometer suitable for cryoprobes. Magn Reson Chem. 2007 Feb;45(2):175-8. doi:10.1002/mrc.1941. PMID:17154329</br>	A 1D-version of the NOESY experiment was performed with a relaxation delay of 4 s. A NOESY mixing time of 10 ms was used during which the water resonance was irradiated with the presaturation RF field. For water suppression, presaturation of the water resonance with an effective field of γB1 = 25 Hz was applied during the relaxation delay. After applying 4 dummy scans, a total of 98,304 data points covering a spectral width of 18,029 Hz were collected using 16 scans. </br></br> For T2-filtered 1H NMR spectra, a standard 1D Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) pulse sequence was used with a relaxation delay of 4 s. A pulse train of 128 refocusing pulses with individual spin echo delays of 0.6 ms was applied resulting in a total T2 filtering delay of 78 ms. For water suppression, presaturation of the water resonance with an effective field of γB1 = 25 Hz was applied during the relaxation delay. After applying 4 dummy scans, a total of 73,728 data points covering a spectral width of 12,019 Hz were collected using 16 scans. </br></br> J-resolved spectra (JRES) were recorded with a relaxation delay of 2 s and a total of 1 scan for each increment in the indirect dimension. For water suppression, presaturation of the water resonance with an effective field of γB1 = 25 Hz was applied during the relaxation delay. A data matrix of 40 x 12,288 data points was collected covering a sweep width of 78 x 10,000 Hz.	The raw time domain data was processed in the KNIME-based KIMBLE workflow.	Metabolites were identified using the reference spectra in the Human Metabolome Database, spiking experiments, JRES multiplicities and interpeak correlations. Areas under the peak were calculated using the KIMBLE metabolomics workflow.

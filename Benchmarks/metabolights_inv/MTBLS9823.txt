Study Identifier	MTBLS9823
Study Title	Defective mitochondrial COX1 translation due to loss of COX14 function triggers ROS-induced inflammation in liver
Study Description	Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) fuels cellular ATP demands. OXPHOS defects lead to severe human disorders with unexplained tissue specific pathologies. Mitochondrial gene expression is essential for OXPHOS biogenesis since core subunits of the complexes are mitochondrial-encoded. COX14 is required for translation of COX1, the central mitochondrial-encoded subunit of complex IV. Here we generated a COX14 mutant mouse corresponding to a patient with complex IV deficiency. COX14M19I mice display broad tissue-specific pathologies. A hallmark phenotype is severe liver inflammation linked to release of mitochondrial RNA into the cytosol sensed by RIG-1 pathway. We find that mitochondrial RNA release is triggered by increased reactive oxygen species production in the deficiency of complex IV. Additionally, we generated a COA3Y72C mouse, affected in an assembly factor in early COX1 biogenesis, which displayed a similar yet milder inflammatory phenotype. Our study provides mechanistic insight into how defective mitochondrial gene expression causes tissue-specific inflammation.

Study Protocol Description	<p>The guidelines from the German Animal Welfare Act and approved by the Landesamt für Verbraucherschutz und Lebensmittelsicherheit, Niedersachsen, Germany (AZ: 33.9-42502-04-14/1720) were followed for the maintenance of animals and performance of animal experiments. The animals were housed either in high barrier (SPF-specified pathogen free) areas in IVC (individually ventilated caging) on standard rodent chow to WT C57BL/6N mice, with restricted access for animal care staff only.</p><p><br></p><p>The<strong> COX14M19I</strong> mice (C57BL/6N-Cox14em83Cecad), in which methionine 19 of the cDNA of Cox14 (Ensembl ENSMUST00000023761) is exchanged to isoleucine, were generated by pronuclear injection of CRISPR/Cas9 reagents essentially as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>. For maintenance, the mice were fed ad libitum with Ssniff chow V1534-300 (ssniff®&nbsp;R/M-H autoclavable Complete feed for rats and mice - maintenance, fortified), and kept under a 12:12 dark-light cycle with lights on at 4:30 am. The room temperature where the mice are kept is maintained at a constant temperature of 21 °C (+/- 1 °C) and the humidity is about 50% (+/- 5%).&nbsp;Liver tissue samples were collected and transferred into 1&nbsp;mL of methanol and homogenized using Precellys 24 (CK-14 2&nbsp;ml tubes, 3 Cycles, 50&nbsp;s, 5500&nbsp;rpm, 30&nbsp;s breaks, VWR International, Delaware).</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Ref:</strong></p><p><strong>[1]</strong> Tröder, S.E., Ebert, L.K., Butt, L., Assenmacher, S., Schermer, B. and Zevnik, B. 2018. An optimized electroporation approach for efficient CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing in murine zygotes. PloS One 13(5). doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0196891. PMID: 29723268; PMCID: PMC5933690.</p>	<p>Lipid&nbsp;extractions in the presence of internal lipid standards were performed using an acidic liquid-liquid extraction (ABD) method, except for plasmalogens, which were extracted under neutral conditions[1]. To ensure that similar amounts of lipids were subjected to extractions, a test extraction (ABD) was performed to determine the concentration of PC as a bulk membrane lipid and to adapt extractions volumes to similar total lipid amounts. On average, a total lipid amount of&nbsp;3000&nbsp;pmol was used for extractions.</p><p><br></p><p>Lipid extractions were performed in the presence of internal lipid standards for each lipid class, with the standards resembling the structure of the endogenous lipid species. Following this approach, a relative quantification of lipid species was performed. Lipid standards were added prior to extractions, using a master mix consisting of 50&nbsp;pmol phosphatidylcholine (PC, 14:1/14:1, 20:1/20:1; 22:1/22:1, Avanti Polar Lipids), 50&nbsp;pmol sphingomyelin (SM,&nbsp;d18:1/13:0,&nbsp;d18:1/17:0, d18:1/25:0,&nbsp;semi-synthesized[1], 100&nbsp;pmol deuterated cholesterol (D7-cholesterol, Avanti Polar Lipids), 28&nbsp;pmol phosphatidylinositol (PI, 17:0/20:4, Avanti Polar Lipids), 25&nbsp;pmol phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and 25&nbsp;pmol phosphatidylserine (PS) (both 14:1/14:1, 20:1/20:1, 22:1/22:1, semi-synthesized as previously described<strong>[1]</strong>), 25&nbsp;pmol diacylglycerol (DG, 14:0/14:0, Larodan), 25 pmol cholesteryl ester (CE, 19:0 Avanti Polar Lipids, 9:0, 24:1, Sigma), and 24&nbsp;pmol triacylglycerol (TG, d5-mixture, LM-6000, Avanti Polar Lipids), 5 pmol ceramide (Cer d18:1/18:0-D3, Matreya) and 5&nbsp;pmol glucosylceramide (HexCer, (GlcCer(d18:1/17:0), Avanti Polar Lipids), 5&nbsp;pmol lactosylceramide (Hex2Cer,&nbsp;LacCer (d18:1/17:0), Avanti Polar Lipids&nbsp;), 10&nbsp;pmol phosphatidic acid (PA, 17:0/20:4, Avanti Polar Lipids), 10&nbsp;pmol phosphatidylglycerol (PG, 14:1/14:1, 20:1/20:1, 22:1/22:1, semi-synthesized[1]) and 5&nbsp;pmol lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC, 17:1, Avanti Polar Lipids). The phosphatidylethanolamine plasmalogen (PE P-) standard mix consisted of 22&nbsp;pmol PE&nbsp;P-Mix 1 (16:0p/15:0-d3, 16:0p/19:0), 31&nbsp;pmol PE&nbsp;P- Mix 2 (18:0p/15:0-d3, 18:0p/19:0), 43&nbsp;pmol PE&nbsp;P-Mix 3 (18:1p/15:0-d3, 18:1p/19:0). Semi-synthesis of PE&nbsp;P- was performed as previously described<strong>[2]</strong>.</p><p><br></p><p>The final chloroform phase was evaporated under a gentle stream of nitrogen at 37 °C. Samples were either directly subjected to mass spectrometric analysis, or were stored at -20 °C prior to analysis, which was typically done within 1-2 days after extraction. Lipid extracts were resuspended in 10&nbsp;mM ammonium acetate in 60&nbsp;µL methanol. For QTRAP6500+ measurements, 2&nbsp;µL aliquots of the resuspended lipid extracts were diluted 1:10 in 10&nbsp;mM ammonium acetate in methanol in 96-well plates (Eppendorf twin tec 96). For TG and DG measurements on a Q Exactive, 5&nbsp;µL aliquots of the reconstituted lipid extracts were diluted with 15&nbsp;µL 7.5&nbsp;mM ammonium formate in isopropanol:methanol:chloroform (4:2:1, v:v:v). For cholesterol determinations, the remaining lipid extract was again evaporated and subjected to acetylation as previously described<strong>[3]</strong>.&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Refs:</strong></p><p><strong>[1] </strong>Özbalci C, Sachsenheimer T, Brügger B. PMID:16458590.</p>	<p>Direct infusions were performed using Triversa Nanomate units (Advion Interchim Scientific). </p>	<p>Cholesterol was analyzed on a QTRAP5500 (Sciex) mass spectrometer with Chip-based direct injection (nano) via a Triversa NanoMate system. Derivatized cholesterol and cholesterol standard were measured in positive ion mode. Scan type: Neutral loss scanning selecting for a neutral loss of m/z 77. Mass range: 442-480 m/z.</p><p><br></p><p>TG and DG lipid species were analyzed in positive ion mode on a Q Exactive (Thermo Scientific) coupled to a robotic with chip-based (HD-D ESI Chip, Advion&nbsp;Interchim Scientific) electrospray infusion and ionization via a Triversa Nanomate (Advion&nbsp;Interchim Scientific).&nbsp;Scan types: FullMS: 1.0-1.5 min; targeted SIM: 1.5-9.5 min; PRM: 10-30 min.</p><p><br></p><p>All other lipids were analyzed on QTRAP6500+ (Sciex) mass spectrometer with Chip-based direct injection (nano) via a Triversa NanoMate system. GPL, SL and SE lipid were measure in positive ion mode. Scan types: precursor ion and neutral loss scanning[1].</p><p><br></p><p>Ref:</p><p>[1] Özbalci C, Sachsenheimer T, Brügger B. PMID:23996167.</p>	<p>.wiff data files were processed using Analyst 1.6.3 and LipidView 1.3 (beta) and raw data files were converted using PeakStrainer (DOI&nbsp;10.17617/1.47; 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b00794) to .mzXML files.</p>	<p>Except for TG and DG, identification of lipid species was based on precursor ion or neutral loss scanning as previously described[1]. Data evaluation was done using LipidView and lipid class-specific target lists, including isotopic correction factors. Mass tolerance was set to 0.3 Da, smoothing and deisotoping functions were activated.</p><p><br></p><p>Identification of TG and DG lipid species was performed using LipidXplorer[2][3] .

Study Identifier	MTBLS9853
Study Title	Modulating the developing gut microbiota with 2’-fucosyllactose and pooled human milk oligosaccharides
Study Description	<p>Synthetic human milk oligosaccharide (HMO) structures are used to supplement infant formula despite limited understanding of their impact on the developing gut microbiota. Here, we assess the impact of 2-fucosyllactose (2’FL) and pooled (p)HMOs on the composition and activity of cultured microbial communities derived from seven infants. Bacterial isolates spanning 157 species were derived from these communities and individually evaluated for their responses to HMOs.&nbsp;Exposure to pHMOs resulted in shifts in the composition and metabolic output of the communities, including increased absolute abundance of several genera, notably Bacteroides, and production of health-associated metabolites. In contrast, the addition of 2’FL alone had no measurable effect.&nbsp;Over 100 non-Bifidobacterium species showed enhanced growth with pHMOs treatment, and a high degree of intraspecies variation in HMO metabolism was noted. Our study warrants further investigation into the significance of incorporating 2’FL alone into infant formula, especially in the context of gut microbiota modulation.&nbsp;</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>Stool samples donated by seven young children, aged 18-24 months old, enrolled in the DIABIMMUNE birth cohort were evaluated for this study. The children’s stool was first collected at home and stored in residential freezers until their next visit to the DIABIMMUNE study centre in Helsinki, Finland, where samples were stored at -80°C. Subsequently, the samples were shipped on dry ice to the Hospital for Sick Children then to the University of Guelph where they were stored at -80°C until used. Fecal slurries were prepared under anaerobic conditions (10% H2, 10% CO2, balanced with N2) from 0.5 g stool in sterile, pre-reduced bioreactor media. Each slurry was used to inoculate a 400 mL Multifors bioreactor systems (Infors AG, Bottmingen/Basel, Switzerland) designed to model the physiological conditions of human distal colon i.e. vessels were maintained at 37°C and pH 7 under anaerobic conditions with constant stirring at 50 rpm and a media turnover rate of 24 h. Samples of culture were collected and used to inoculate 200 mL vessels containing bioreactor media and operated under similar conditions. Vessels were maintained for 14 days to allow the microbial communities to achieve compositional and metabolic steady state before culture was harvested and immediately transferred into an anaerobic chamber. In triplicate, 18 mL of each microbial community was treated with 4.0 g/L pHMOs (previously extracted and purified from pooled milk), 0.5 g/L 2’FL, or a no-treatment (water) control, to final volumes of 20 mL. Communities were incubated at 37°C for 60 h during which samples were collected for cell counting, taxonomic and metabolic profiling, and HMO structure profiling.</p>	<p>Cell-free supernatant was obtained from thawed samples by centrifuging at 14,000 rpm to 15 min and filtering through sterile 0.22 μm-pore size filters. </p>	<p>The resulting filtrates were diluted with an internal standard, 99.9% D2O with 5 mM 4,4-dimethyl-4-silapentane-1-sulfonic acid (DSS) and 0.2% sodium azide, to a final concentration of 0.5 mM DSS. Samples were transferred to 5 mm glass NMR tubes (535-PP-7, Wilmad, Wineland, NJ, USA) and stored at 4°C overnight before scanning on NMR instrument. pH of each sample was determined using litmus paper following NMR scan.</p>	<p>Samples were scanned using a Brüker Avance 600 MHz NMR spectrometer (NMR Centre, AAC, University of Guelph, ON, Canada) equipped with a Bruker “TCI” 5 mm HCN cryoprobe. </p>	<p>Spectra were acquired using the first increment of a 1D 1H NOESY pulse sequence with tmix of 100 ms, 4 s acquisition time, 1 s relaxation delay, and a spectral width of 12 ppm.</p>	<p>Chenomx NMR Suite 7.0–7.7 (Chenomx Inc., Edmonton, AB, Canada) was used to process spectra.</p>	<p>Chenomx NMR Suite 7.0–7.7 was used to identify metabolites using the software’s 600 MHz compound library. Metabolites were quantified from the area of the projected signal by using the known concentration of DSS and the area of the DSS peak.</p>
